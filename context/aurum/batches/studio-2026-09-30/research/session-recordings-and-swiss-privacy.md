# session-recordings-and-swiss-privacy

## Verified

- Session replay and heatmap tools process information that can identify a person or describe behaviour linked to a visit; treat them as personal-data processing under the Swiss FADP, not as «anonymous analytics»: https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de
- A privacy notice must describe processing, purposes, and recipients in plain language (Art. 19 FADP): https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de
- Aurum security article on privacy notice duty in Switzerland (linked from calendar crossLinks): `/security/blog/privacy-notice-requirement-switzerland` and `/de/security/blog/datenschutzerklaerung-pflicht-schweiz`
- EDÖB guidance on cookies and similar technologies (2025): consent when profiling is high risk, for specially protected data, when data pass to a third party for their own purposes, and for cross-site tracking and retargeting; otherwise information and a way to object (Art. 45c FMG): cited in studio WRITER-BRIEF with EDÖB guide link (re-check URL on edoeb.admin.ch before print)
- Microsoft Clarity is named on Aurum service pages as part of analytics and session recording from day one in validation builds (internal offer fact, not a vendor compliance statement)
- Supabase Zurich region is separate from where a US session vendor stores replays: https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions

## Not verified

- Whether a specific MVP’s Clarity or Hotjar configuration meets EDÖB expectations without a lawyer reading the notice and vendor DPA
- Exact retention defaults in each vendor’s free tier at publish time

## Do not claim

- Orientation, not legal advice (calendar `apart`)
- That turning on session recording makes an MVP «DSG-compliant» or «FADP-complete»
- That session replay alone proves product-market fit
- Invented conversion or «good UX» benchmarks from recordings
