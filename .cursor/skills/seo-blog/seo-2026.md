# How search treats this work

Checked against Google Search Central in September 2026. Third-party "ranking factor" percentages are not rules here.

## The policy in Google's words

- Helpful, reliable pages are written for people. Pages made mainly to gain rankings are what the ranking systems are built to demote. Source: [Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).
- The spam policies describe **scaled content abuse**: many pages produced to manipulate rankings rather than help users, whether a person, a model, or both produced them. Source: [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).
- AI-assisted content works when it is useful; producing it primarily to manipulate rankings is spam. Source: [Google's guidance on AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content).
- The helpful-content signal is site-wide, so one thin locale or one rushed cluster affects the rest of the domain.
- People-first checks that matter while drafting: original information, a clear who-it's-for, evidence for claims, and a reason to exist beyond "this keyword had volume".
- The words people search for belong in the title, headings, anchor text, and alt text.

## The shape that works

- A cluster of URLs with different jobs (define, compare, choose, implement), each with its own examples, limits, and internal links.
- Each row earns its place with its own primary query and decision. Synonyms of one query are one article. A row that duplicates a URL, swaps a keyword, or targets a query the site cannot honestly answer gives way to a better row.
- Each locale is a native article for its own search behavior.
- A `pubDate` schedules the post; an `updatedDate` marks a real update.
- A listicle is strong when it ends in a decision.

## One host, several blogs

Aurum's studio, prozess-check, security and web3 blogs are sections of one domain, each with its own publishing calendar. Two sections publishing on the same day does not change the policy: the test is whether each page is distinct and useful to the person searching. Because the quality signal is site-wide, the per-article bar is the same in every section.

## Citations

Research the query, then write the page. For queries where an answer box or an AI summary may quote the page, put a direct answer in the first paragraph and again under the H2 that asks the question. Comparisons, limits, and sourced numbers are easier to quote than slogans. This is a writing practice, not a published ranking weight.

Cover the adjacent questions on the same URL when they serve the same intent (price, limits, who should skip). Split them onto a new URL only when the intent changes.

## Experience

Prefer a concrete walkthrough from the product or the brand's own process over a generic explainer. Where the brand has no first-hand detail for a claim, narrow the claim or cite a source. The brand's own process and numbers carry the most weight.
