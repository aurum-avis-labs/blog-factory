GoldCrew agents collaborate on a shared taskboard inside one workspace. You brief a role, agents pick up and assign related work, and you can step in at any point. For inbound mail, the product shows a drafted reply queued for your review. Do not treat the crew as cleared to publish, send, or set a price without that read.

## Why separate AI tools leave you as the glue

Most AI tools are one job in one window. Writing sits in one product, research in another, files in a third. None of them holds the decision you made on Tuesday.

You become the copy-paste layer. A research note gets summarised into a brief. The brief gets summarised into a caption. The caption gets summarised into an email. Each summary drops a constraint, so the email offers a thing the research note already ruled out.

GoldCrew's answer is one workspace and a taskboard the agents share. The product describes agents that assign work to each other, pick tasks up, and update progress while you watch. That is the collaboration. It is not a promise that six models secretly agree in the background with no record you can read.

## Who is on the crew you can brief today

The public roster is six roles, plus two marked as joining later. Sales and legal are listed as coming soon. This article only describes the roles that are on the roster now.

- **Miles** is the general manager. He is the single contact for the crew: he coordinates tasks and is meant to keep the others aligned. Start here when you do not yet know which role owns the next step.
- **Dash** is the business analyst. He turns an idea into a strategy, with market analysis and a business model you can argue with.
- **Quill** is research. Market notes and data analysis sit with Quill, so Dash is not also your clipping service.
- **Jay** is brand and design. Identities and visual systems come from Jay, not from the marketing draft.
- **Rio** is marketing. Campaigns and cross-channel work start from what Jay and Quill already put on the board.
- **Sophie** is operations. Schedules, communications, and the follow-through on a workflow sit here, after the offer exists.

You can message one of them directly, the way you would message a teammate. A logo is a chat with Jay. A campaign question is a chat with Rio. The product also describes group chats when two roles need the same thread, with file sharing in that chat.

## How a launch actually moves across the board

Here is one chain, a new offer, in the order the roles are built for. You can stop after any step.

<ol class="blog-flow">
  <li><strong>Brief</strong><p>Tell Miles the offer in one place: who it is for, what it is not, and the date you care about.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Strategy</strong><p>Dash writes the model and the bet. If the bet is wrong, stop. Later agents will inherit it.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Research</strong><p>Quill checks the market around that bet and leaves notes on the board.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Brand</strong><p>Jay sets the identity and the visual system from those notes, not from a blank chat.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Campaign and ops</strong><p>Rio drafts the campaign in that voice. Sophie lines up the schedule and the messages. You read both before they go out.</p></li>
</ol>

On the taskboard, the product says agents can assign and pick up tasks themselves, with progress you can see. You are not required to open five chats and paste. You are required to notice when a task moved on a bad assumption. Oversight is part of the feature, not a backup plan: the same page says you can intervene at any time.

A smaller version of the same pattern is the automation flow the product shows for inbound mail. An email comes in, a support ticket is created, an agent drafts a response in your brand voice, and the draft waits in a queue for your review. The business can keep receiving work outside office hours. The reply still waits until someone approves it.

## What the agents share, and what they do not

They share the workspace and the taskboard. Rio's campaign is supposed to sit on Jay's brand work and Quill's notes, because those outputs live in the same place, not because you remembered to paste them.

They do not share a licence to invent facts about your company. If the pricing model, the buyer, or the offer is not in the brief, the next agent will guess. Put the constraint on the board before you hand the task on. A sentence you are unwilling to say in public does not belong in the brief, because the next role will treat it as source material.

Role boundaries are the other half. Quill is not your designer. Jay is not the person who should rewrite the business model after Dash has already written it, unless you send it back. When two outputs clash, that is a briefing problem or a step you skipped, and the board is where you see it. Correct it there. A group chat with the two roles is for that correction, not a third draft in a private window they cannot see.

## Integrations, as the product states them

GoldCrew's site says the integration layer uses the same engine as Pipedream, with 2,800+ apps, and names Gmail, Slack, Google Drive, Notion, and Salesforce. Setup is described as no-code. That is the vendor's claim about coverage, not a test of every app.

Use an integration when the handoff is already clear: a file that should land in Drive, a message that should sit in Slack until you approve it, mail that should become a ticket. Do not connect a tool so that an agent can write into a live customer record before you have read a sample run. Connect one system, watch one real item go through the queue, then add the next.

The same caution applies to the "if it has an API, we connect to it" line on the site. An API existing is not the same as the field you care about being mapped. Check the one object you would be angry to see wrong: a customer name, a price, a send-to address.

## Who should use a different tool

Stay on a single chatbot if you have one writing task and no second role will touch it. GoldCrew's cost, in time, is the crew and the board. One caption does not need Miles.

Do not brief a sales agent or a legal agent against this roster. Both are marked as joining later, so they are not roles you can hand a pipeline or a contract to today. Hiring and finance are not listed as live specialists either. Do not assume a seat exists because a general model will talk about contracts or cash. For a signature, a filing, or a number a buyer will rely on, use the person who is allowed to own it. An agent draft can sit next to that work. It does not replace it.

If your operational headache is stock, suppliers, and a kitchen rather than a launch crew, that is a different job. <a href="/blog/the-hidden-cost-of-manual-inventory-management-in-restaurant-kitchens">The hidden cost of manual inventory</a> covers that one. It is not a tour of this taskboard.

## When to stop the chain

Stop when the next action leaves the building: a send, a publish, a price, a promise to a buyer. Read the draft against the brief you gave Miles. Check that Jay's line and Rio's line still describe the same offer Quill was asked to research.

The board tells you which role touched which piece. It does not tell you whether the offer is true. That read is the job you kept.

A sensible first run is one offer, not the whole company. Brief Miles, let Dash and Quill leave something you can disagree with, and only then call Jay. If you cannot point at a sentence you changed, you have not reviewed anything. You have watched a board move.
