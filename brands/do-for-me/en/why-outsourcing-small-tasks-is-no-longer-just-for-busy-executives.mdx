Outsourcing small tasks used to mean a personal assistant or an agency, so people treated it as something for busy executives. That picture is outdated for a parcel, a stack of boxes, or a dog walk in Zürich. You can get local help for one clearly described job without a long contract.

This page is for households, students, and anyone who still hears "outsourcing" and pictures a status symbol. The job in front of you is probably smaller than that word.


## The old picture was built for a different workload

<Image src={img2} alt="Professional assistant stereotype" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

When people hear "outsourcing", they picture:

- a full-service agency,
- a contract that runs for months,
- or an assistant covering a whole calendar.

That shape fits a large, ongoing workload. It is the wrong shape for:

- carrying boxes on a moving day,
- a few errands while you are in a seminar,
- walking or sitting a dog for two hours,
- help with homework,
- or setup and cleanup for a small gathering.

For those, the old model is overkill. The word stuck. The need did not.


## Everyday life got tighter, and the habit did not

<Image src={img3} alt="Busy household evening" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

A normal week in Zürich already stacks work or studies, family, appointments, and the travel between them. Many people still try to do every practical task themselves, including ones a person nearby could do in an hour.

The result is familiar. Evenings and weekends disappear into chores that do not need your judgement. You are not failing at adulthood. You are using a habit that matched a time when help meant hiring someone for a job, not for an errand.

## What changed is the size of the commitment

<Image src={img4} alt="Friendly local help interaction" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

The useful shift is access. You can describe a task in plain language, find someone already in the city, and stop when the task stops. There is no assistant to retain for the month.

This is not a replacement for professional services. A tradesperson, a cleaning company with a route, or a specialist still belongs on specialised work. Affordable help with tasks, in the everyday sense, is for work that is short, practical, and easy to explain. Think of it as structured local help. The corporate word is the part that aged badly.

People use local task services when the task is straightforward, timing matters more than a perfect process, and they would rather keep the evening than stretch it around one more stop. Many of those tasks used to be postponed because "it is not enough to hire someone".


## Who actually does this now

Not a corner office. A student moving between shared flats at the end of a semester. A parent whose afternoon is already a pickup. Someone working shifts who cannot reach a counter during opening hours. A newcomer whose family is not in the canton.

The people doing the helping are often students: hours that move around lectures, a reason to earn beside a degree, and enough practical sense for a defined job. The exchange is simple. A household gets the task done. A student gets work that fits around a timetable. Nobody has to pretend the errand is a career.

  <p>Short local help is a poor fit for ongoing employment, sensitive personal data, or work that needs a licence. Those still belong with a proper arrangement or a qualified trade.</p>

## A practical choice, at an ordinary size

You do not need to rebrand your life as "delegated". You need to notice when a small task is eating a block you care about.

If you have thought "this should not take my whole evening" or "I just need someone in the city for this", the old executive picture is the wrong test. Start from the task. If a nearby student could follow your instructions once, you are already in the group this kind of help was made for.

The hours that disappear are laid out in [the opportunity cost of doing everything yourself](/en/blog/the-opportunity-cost-of-doing-everything-yourself). The ways to get the help, including what to skip, are in [what your options are when you need help with small tasks](/en/blog/what-are-your-options-when-you-need-help-with-small-tasks).

  <h3>Is this only worth it if I earn a high salary?</h3>
  <p>No. The comparison is the evening or the study block, not a day rate. A one-off job can be worth handing off even when you are a student yourself.</p>
