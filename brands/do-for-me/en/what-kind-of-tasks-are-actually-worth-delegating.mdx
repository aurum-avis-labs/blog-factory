The tasks worth delegating are the ones a nearby person can finish from a short description, while you use the hour for something that actually needs you. In Zürich that is often carrying, collecting, walking a dog, or a basic homework session. It is rarely your decisions, your private data, or a job you like.

If you can say the task in one or two sentences, it is a candidate. If the sentence turns into a judgement call, keep it.


## Simplicity matters more than how "important" the task feels

<Image src={img2} alt="Simple task being handled by someone else" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

People skip help because the chore feels too minor, then spend the evening on it anyway. The best tasks to outsource are usually repetitive and practical, not high status.

A good candidate has three traits, and you do not need all the ceremony around them:

- you can explain it without a meeting,
- the result is visible (the box is upstairs, the dog is walked, the parcel is in the flat),
- your personal judgement is not the product.

Importance is a weak test. Walking the dog is "unimportant" until you are in a seminar and the dog is not.


## Tasks that usually clear the test

<Image src={img3} alt="List of everyday tasks" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

Short-term help fits everyday situations like these:

- carrying boxes on a moving day, or up the stairs in a building without a lift,
- errands and pickups: groceries, a pharmacy bag, dry cleaning, a parcel you cannot reach before the counter closes,
- dog walking or sitting for a defined stretch of time,
- basic tutoring or homework, where the subject is clear and you are not handing over a diagnosis,
- setup or cleanup for a small gathering.

These small tasks to outsource do not need a standing arrangement. They need an extra pair of hands at a named time. Household tasks to delegate often look like "logistics": the same run each week, bags that must move, a hallway that will not clear itself. They interrupt a schedule without moving your actual work forward.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Write the task in one sentence a stranger could follow.</li>
  <li>Name the place in Zürich and the time window.</li>
  <li>Say what "done" looks like in one more sentence.</li>
  <li>If that took longer than the task, do it yourself or rethink the scope.</li>
</ul>


## Tasks that are usually better kept

<Image src={img4} alt="Household chores taking time" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

Keep these with you:

- decisions that depend on what you want (which flat, which offer, how to reply to a difficult message),
- work that involves sensitive personal data, passwords, or access to your accounts,
- anything you genuinely like and that restores you,
- work that needs a licence: electrical, gas, anything a trade would refuse to let an amateur touch.

Delegation should remove a trip or a load. If it adds a briefing, a risk, or a mess you then have to undo, it was the wrong task.

## A worked example: one Saturday move

You are leaving a shared flat in Kreis 4 for a room in Höngg. Friends can take the evening. Saturday morning is the problem: two bulky pieces, recycling that must go to the right point, and a key handover at 11:00.

Worth handing off: a student for two hours to carry with you, or to run the recycling while you wait for the handover. You can explain it. The outcome is obvious. You stay for anything that needs the tenancy, the contract, or the meter reading.

Not worth handing off: "sort my whole life and tell me what to keep." That is a decision, and it will take longer to brief than to do.

The tradeoff is coordination. You spend ten minutes writing the message so you do not spend the morning stuck. Stop delegating when the next item on the list is the part only you can sign.


## When it starts to make sense, and when to stop

Hand a task off when it keeps returning, when it blocks study or family, or when the mental list is louder than the task. At that point the cost of doing it yourself is the hour plus the attention.

Stop when you are outsourcing things you wanted to do, or when every listing is really "please run my week." One or two clear jobs is the useful size. A life run by other people is a different service, and it is not what short local help is for.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is cleaning a good task to delegate?</h3>
  <p>A defined tidy or a laundry run can be, if you say which rooms and what to leave alone. An open-ended "make the flat nice" is vague, and vague tasks come back half done.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>What about IT help?</h3>
  <p>Setting up a device, or sitting with you while you learn a step, can be a clear task. Handing over passwords, or asking someone to "fix my whole digital life", is not. Keep access to your accounts with you.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>How many tasks should I hand off in a week?</h3>
  <p>Start with one. If writing the request is easy and the result matches what you asked, add another later. There is no quota that makes you efficient.</p>
</div>
