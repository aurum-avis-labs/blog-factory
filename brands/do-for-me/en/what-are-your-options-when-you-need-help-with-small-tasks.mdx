When you need help with small tasks in Zürich, you have four real options: do it yourself, ask someone you know, book a company, or ask a student nearby for one defined job. The hard part is picking, because the alternatives feel either too casual or too formal.

This is a comparison, not a ranking with a single winner. The right choice depends on urgency, how often the task returns, and whether a stranger can follow your instructions.


## Option 1: Do it yourself

<Image src={img2} alt="Person handling chores alone" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

This is the default, and it is often right.

People choose it when they want no coordination and no payment, and when the task is already on their route. The cost shows up as time. Small tasks eat evenings, and the mental list stays open. Doing everything yourself stays reasonable until the same chore repeats or the route is across the city.

**Keep this option** when you enjoy the task, or when you are the only person who can decide it. **Leave it** when "I'll do it tonight" has already failed twice this week.


## Option 2: Ask friends or family

<Image src={img3} alt="Friends helping with a task" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

The next step is usually someone you know.

It feels informal, often costs nothing, and trust is already there. Availability is the catch. A favour is hard to put on a clock, and people hesitate to ask a second time. In Zürich that matters if your family lives in another canton. Friends have their own seminars, shifts, and moves.

**Keep this option** for a one-off that someone offered, or a true emergency. **Leave it** if you would be embarrassed to ask again next month. A repeating errand is a poor use of goodwill.


## Option 3: Hire a professional service

<Image src={img4} alt="Professional service worker" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

Companies are built for reliability and a defined craft: a mover with a van, a cleaner on a route, an electrician, a dog sitter who does it as a job.

You get clear expectations and less personal awkwardness. You also often get a minimum booking, a process, and a bill sized for that business. For carrying boxes for an hour, a single pickup, or help at a small gathering, that process can be larger than the task.

**Keep this option** when the work needs a licence, insurance that the firm actually carries, tools you do not have, or a guarantee. **Leave it** when you need a pair of hands this week and the job is easy to explain. Check the firm if you need a number.


## Option 4: Ask a student nearby

<Image src={img5} alt="Local task platform interface" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

Local student help sits between a favour and a company. You describe the task, the place, and the time. A student in the city, often around ETH, UZH, or another local university, can take a short job. DO-4-ME is one way to do that in Zürich: you post the task, you talk to the person, you agree what you will pay them directly. Posting on DO-4-ME has no platform fee. The amount for the work is between you and the student.

People choose it because the task stays one task. Coordination stays in a chat. There is no subscription.

The tradeoff is trust without a personal history. You are not asking a cousin. You look at a profile, you write in plain language, and you confirm before anyone comes over. That is more structure than a text to a friend, and less machinery than a bidding marketplace.

**Keep this option** for errands, pickups, dog walks, moving help, basic homework support, or a few hours at an event. **Leave it** for sensitive data, licensed trades, or a job that is really a part-time role.

| | Yourself | Friend or family | A company | A student nearby |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | Tasks on your route, or ones you like | A one-off someone offered | Licensed or equipped work | A clear job of a few hours |
| Skip it when | The evening keeps disappearing | You would owe a repeating favour | The minimum is bigger than the task | The work needs a licence or private data |
| Commitment | Your time | Goodwill | A booking | One task, then stop |


## How to pick for one task

Start with the job, not the tool.

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>If it is on your way home</h3><p>Do it yourself. Asking someone else to detour can take longer than the stop.</p></li>
  <li><h3>If it needs a van, a licence, or a guarantee</h3><p>Call a company. A student with a tram pass is the wrong hire.</p></li>
  <li><h3>If you can explain it in two sentences and you need it this week</h3><p>A student nearby is the middle option. Write the place, the time, and what "done" looks like.</p></li>
</ul>

Urgency, frequency, and how much explaining you can stand all matter. There is no single right answer for every chore in a household. There is a right size for the chore in front of you.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Can I mix these options?</h3>
  <p>Yes. Use a friend for the thing they offered, a company for the licensed job, and a student for the box of books that still needs to cross the city. Mixing is normal.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is a student less reliable than a company?</h3>
  <p>A company is built to repeat a service. A student is a person with a timetable who agreed to one job. Reliability comes from a clear description and a confirmation in chat, not from a brand name. If you need a guarantee or specialised cover, use the company.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>What if I only need help once?</h3>
  <p>Once is a good size. You should be able to stop afterwards. If a service wants a subscription for a single errand, it is the wrong size.</p>
</div>
