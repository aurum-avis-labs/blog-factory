You don't need a personal assistant to delegate everyday tasks. You need one clear job done by someone already in Zürich: an errand, boxes, a dog walk, basic homework help, or a few hours at a small gathering.

A personal assistant is a role. Most people who feel behind are missing a spare hour, not a staff member.


## A personal assistant is a role, not a spare hour

<Image src={img2} alt="List of small daily tasks piling up" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

The list that eats the week is rarely one project. It is grocery runs, a package, carrying, the dog, homework, a last-minute stop. Each piece looks manageable. Together they push real plans into the late evening.

Hiring an assistant is one answer, and it assumes a recurring workload, a budget for a person, and tasks that need someone who knows your life. That is a real job. It is a strange response to "the parcel counter closes at 18:30 and I am still at ETH".

Most people hesitate because they picture the assistant, the service contract, or an awkward long commitment. Then they keep stacking small tasks into nights and weekends, including jobs someone else could do from a two-sentence brief.


## What people actually need on a Tuesday

<Image src={img3} alt="Person hesitating to ask for help" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

The useful unit is the task.

- It is simple and it takes time.
- It does not need your professional judgement.
- It happens in the city, once, or for a defined hour.
- You want an extra pair of hands, then you want them gone.

Students nearby fit that unit. Their week already bends around lectures. A short practical job is a normal way to earn. You are not employing a household. You are getting local task help for something you can point at.

DO-4-ME is one place that stays at that size: you post the task, you choose a student, you chat, you pay them directly when it is done. There is no platform fee to post, and no subscription that keeps billing after the dog has been walked. You agree the pay with the student. This page does not set it.

## How to hand off one task

<Image src={img4} alt="Friendly local help interaction" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

You can do this the same day you notice the evening filling up.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Name one job</h3><p>Not "help me with life". One outcome: the boxes upstairs, the shopping in the kitchen, the dog back by 16:00.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Add place and time</h3><p>A quarter is not enough if they need a door. Say where in Zürich, and the window that still helps you.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Say what to leave alone</h3><p>Other rooms, accounts, decisions. If it needs a password, it is the wrong task.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Confirm in a chat before anyone travels</h3><p>Who, when, and what you will pay them. Then stop coordinating.</p></li>
</ol>

The goal is to free the block, not to build a system you then have to run.


## A worked example

You have a seminar until 17:00 at Zentrum. The dog has been in a flat in Seefeld since lunch. A friend is not free. An assistant for one walk is absurd.

You write: walk the dog from the flat to the lake path and back, 16:30 to 17:15, harness by the door, no food, text when you are back inside. A student who is already around the university district can do that. You stay in the seminar. You are home to a walked dog, not to a new employee.

If the brief starts to include "also decide what to cook and answer my mail", you have left the useful size. Cut back to the walk.


## When you really do need someone standing in for you

Get a proper arrangement if the work is every day, if it includes private information, or if you need a person who already knows your preferences without a fresh brief. A student listing is the wrong tool for a nanny contract, a bookkeeper, or a licensed trade.

For the rest, the thought "I just need someone for this one thing" is enough. You do not need a personal assistant. You need the right size of help.

> You don't need a personal assistant.
> You need the task written clearly enough that a local student can do it once.

What belongs on that list is in [what kind of tasks are actually worth delegating](/en/blog/what-kind-of-tasks-are-actually-worth-delegating). If you want to check the fit before you post, use [the student-help checklist](/en/blog/is-a-student-based-task-platform-right-for-you-a-simple-checklist).
