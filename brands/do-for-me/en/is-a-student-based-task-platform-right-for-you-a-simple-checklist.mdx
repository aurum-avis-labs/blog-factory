A student-based task platform is right for you when the job is short, local, and easy to explain, and you are fine paying a student directly for that one job. If you need a licensed trade, a long employment, or someone who holds your private data, it is the wrong tool.

If you are wondering whether to hire students for small tasks in Zürich, run the checklist on a real chore from this week. Curiosity is not a decision. A yes on most lines is.


## What this kind of help is

<Image src={img2} alt="Student helping with an everyday task" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

Student task help, including DO-4-ME, is for:

- short, practical tasks,
- local help with a clear end,
- one-off or tightly timed needs in Zürich and nearby.

It is not for:

- hiring someone for months,
- highly specialised professional services,
- open-ended projects ("sort out my life"),
- or work that is illegal or unreasonable.

DO-4-ME students are verified with a university email and the product's vetting. Many study at ETH, UZH, or another local university. You chat before you confirm. Posting is free. You pay the student, not a platform commission. That is the shape. The checklist tells you whether your task fits the shape.


## The checklist

<Image src={img3} alt="Checklist visual" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

Answer for one task, not for your personality. More yes answers means a better fit.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>You can describe the task in one or two sentences.</li>
  <li>It needs time and care, not a professional certificate.</li>
  <li>It is a few hours, a day, or a single moment.</li>
  <li>The person needs to be in Zürich, not on a call from somewhere else.</li>
  <li>You would rather coordinate in a chat than sign a contract.</li>
  <li>You can say what you will pay the student, or you are willing to agree it with them.</li>
  <li>The task does not need your passwords, medical files, or bank access.</li>
  <li>You can be specific about the place and the time window.</li>
</ul>

### 1. Is the task simple to explain?

If a stranger could start from two sentences, student help is usually a good option. If you need a meeting to define "done", shrink the task or keep it.

### 2. Does it need time, not a licence?

Practical work fits. A certified repair does not. Do not use a student listing as a cheaper electrician.

### 3. Is it short or one-off?

A move-in Saturday, an exam-week dog walk, cleanup after a birthday: these fit. "Be my household admin every week with no end" does not. That is a job.

### 4. Does local availability matter?

If you have looked for student help near you, you already know the constraint. A helper who understands the tram and the building is more useful than a specialist on another continent. On DO-4-ME that means Zürich and nearby.

### 5. Do you prefer a chat to a formal process?

The coordination is: profile, message, agree the time and the pay, then the task. If you want a tender, a legal review, and a service-level agreement, you want a firm.

### 6. Can you pay a person, not a platform fee?

There is no platform fee to post, and no commission taken off the student's pay. You still pay the student directly, often cash or Twint, after the work. If you wanted a free favour, ask a friend. If you wanted a company invoice with a minimum, call a company.

### 7. Can the task happen without secrets?

Leave accounts and private records out. A shopping list is fine. Your mailbox password is not.

## Walk one example through the list

<Image src={img4} alt="Examples of student-helped tasks" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

Task: "On Saturday from 9:00 to 11:00, help carry boxes from a third-floor flat in Altstetten to a van parked outside. No packing decisions. I will be there."

- Explainable: yes.
- Time, not a licence: yes. You are not asking them to rewire the kitchen.
- Short: yes.
- Local: yes.
- Chat, not a contract: yes.
- Pay the student: you can agree an amount before they travel.
- No secrets: yes, if you do not hand over a folder of contracts "while you are there".

That task should go ahead. Change it to "and also set up my e-banking on your phone" and the same morning becomes a no.

People often use student task help for carrying on moving day, errands, dog walking, basic tutoring, and setup or cleanup. Those examples work because the end state is obvious.


## How to read your answers

Five or more yes answers, including the "no secrets" line: post the task, or read [how DO-4-ME works from posting to done](/en/blog/how-do-4-me-works-from-posting-a-task-to-getting-it-done) and then post.

A no on licence, secrets, or length: choose another option. A company, a friend for a true one-off, or you. Forcing a bad fit makes the chat long and the result messy.

Two or three yes answers: shrink the task until it is one job, then score it again. "Help me move" is vague. "Carry from 9 to 11 while I am there" is a listing.

There is no perfect score. A fit that is good enough for this task is the decision you needed. You can still do the next chore yourself.
