DO-4-ME keeps everyday help simple, local, and human by staying with one task in Zürich: you describe it, you talk to a student nearby, they do it, you pay them directly. It is not a heavy gig system, and it is not a personal assistant.

People hesitate because help happens at home, or with a dog, or around a child doing homework. The useful question is whether the setup stays clear enough that asking feels ordinary.


## Local means Zürich, not a city you pick from a menu

<Image src={img2} alt="Clear task expectations" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

DO-4-ME is focused on Zürich and the surrounding area. Helpers are mostly students at ETH, UZH, and other local universities, so the person is already moving around the same trams and quarters you are.

Proximity does practical work. A student in Oerlikon can reach a flat in Oerlikon between lectures. A profile from another country cannot collect your parcel before the counter closes. Messages stay about the door, the time, and the bags, which is what you wanted to talk about.

That is **local help for small tasks**: errands, carrying, a dog walk, basic tutoring, setup for a gathering. The context is a neighbourhood, not a global catalogue.


## Trust starts with who is coming, and what they will do

<Image src={img3} alt="Local neighborhood help" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

Ambiguity is what makes task tools feel uncomfortable. DO-4-ME pushes the opposite: a clear description, a time, a place, and a chat before anyone confirms.

Students verify with a university email and go through DO-4-ME's vetting. You can read a profile, including reviews other people left. You write to the person before you agree. That is the trust model. It is not anonymity, and it is not a claim that a rating number replaces your own judgement. Read the profile, then decide.

Safety here is structure at a small scale. You know the scope before the visit. You are not buying a complicated compliance pack. If the job needs a licensed trade, this is the wrong door.

  <p>Do not post passwords, medical files, or access to your accounts. A clear household task does not need them. If the work only works with secrets, keep it to yourself or hire a specialist.</p>

## Human means a person you can talk to

<Image src={img4} alt="Human-centered interaction" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

A lot of marketplaces feel like transactions first: bids, badges, and a queue. DO-4-ME frames the same hour as help. You chat directly. The tone can stay normal, because the job is normal.

Students fit that tone. Their schedules move. They want short work that can sit beside a degree. The interaction is closer to asking a capable neighbour than to briefing a vendor. Many people experience that as safe task help. The word "outsourcing" makes the same hour sound larger than it is.

Friendly still needs a boundary. Say what is in and what is out. A warm chat that never names the time will waste both of you.

## Simple means one task, then you are done

Posting is free. There is no platform fee. You pay the student directly for the work, often in cash or with Twint, after it is done. There is no subscription sitting behind the errand.

You use it when you need the help. You do not keep a retainer for the weeks you do not. That is the whole product: get the thing done, then leave.

DO-4-ME says most tasks get a response within a few hours. If yours is tight, you can mark it as time-sensitive so students who are free sooner see it first. That is the product's claim about replies, not a promise that every chore in the city finishes on your ideal clock.


## What to try first

Pick a small task you can explain without your life story. A pickup. A pile of boxes. A dog walk with a route. Post it once.

Most of the hesitation is before that first listing. After you have seen a clear chat and a finished job, the next one is easier to judge. You still should not use it for ongoing employment or for work only a qualified trade should touch.

The step-by-step of posting is in [how DO-4-ME works, from the task to done](/en/blog/how-do-4-me-works-from-posting-a-task-to-getting-it-done). If you want to decide before you write anything, use [the checklist for student-based help](/en/blog/is-a-student-based-task-platform-right-for-you-a-simple-checklist).

### Ready when you are

If you are curious, try one small task. You do not have to commit to a second. You do not have to organise your household around it. You need something done.
