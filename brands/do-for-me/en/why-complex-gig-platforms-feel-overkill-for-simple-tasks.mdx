Complex gig platforms feel like overkill when the job is a short local task: a pickup, boxes, a dog walk, an hour of help at home. Those sites are often built for ongoing freelance work. Searching "small job help near me" and landing in a system of bids, profiles, and categories is a mismatch, not a personal failure to understand the app.

If you want simple task help in Zürich, judge the tool by how many steps stand between the sentence and the doorbell.


## Simple tasks do not need a project system

<Image src={img2} alt="Simple task being done quickly" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

An everyday task is short, local, and easy to explain. "Carry two boxes from a ground-floor room in Wiedikon to a first-floor room in Höngg on Saturday at 9:00" is a complete brief.

Large marketplaces often treat that sentence as a project: a category, a bid, a negotiation, a profile you are supposed to study like a hiring panel. For boxes or an errand, the overhead is the product. You came to remove a trip, and the site handed you a process.


## What those platforms are built for

<Image src={img3} alt="Marketplace designed for large tasks" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

Large gig marketplaces tend to optimise for:

- people looking for ongoing freelance work,
- clients running longer jobs,
- prices that move through offers and counter-offers.

That is a fair design when the work is complex, the commitment is long, and the result needs a spec. A logo, a month of design, a renovation quote: the extra steps earn their place.

The same steps get in the way when the outcome is "the dog had a walk" or "the bag is in the kitchen". Everyday tasks do not get better because more people competed to do them.


## Where the friction shows up

<Image src={img4} alt="Friction points highlighted" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

People notice the mismatch in a few places:

- the form asks for detail you do not have, because the task is simple,
- replies arrive after the useful window (a counter, a handover, a lecture),
- the price is a negotiation, so a one-hour job feels like a contract discussion,
- messages feel impersonal, which matters when someone is coming to your home.

At that point many people close the tab and do the task themselves. The platform did not fail at being a marketplace. It failed at being the right size.

## When a large marketplace is still the right tool

Keep the heavy platform if:

- the work runs for weeks,
- you need specialists who are not "a student who can carry things",
- you want competing proposals and a paper trail sized for a project,
- or the outcome is hard to judge from a doorway.

Do not force a dog walk into that shape, and do not force a multi-week freelance job into a neighbourhood errand tool. The point of the comparison is fit.
