You can **record Webex on Mac** without asking Webex to record the meeting, as long as the call audio is playing on that Mac and you are allowed to keep a copy. A menu-bar app takes the microphone and the system audio. It does not join Webex as a participant, and it does not depend on the host pressing Record.

Webex's own recorder is still the better tool when you need the host's official file, video, or a recording that exists even if your laptop is muted. This page is the local path, and the cases where you should not use it.

## What record Webex on Mac means here

Webex plays the meeting through the Mac's audio devices. If you can hear the other people, a recorder that captures system audio can hear them too. Your microphone captures you. Those are two local tracks. Nothing is added to the Webex participant list.

  <li data-step="2"><h3>Allow System Audio Recording</h3><p>Audio-only tap for the call track.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Start from the menu bar</h3><p>No extra participant appears in Webex.</p></li>

You start and stop the recording. If you join late, you miss the start. If you leave, the recording stops with you. A host recording in Webex's cloud, when the site allows it, can cover the meeting without your laptop staying open. Those are different products.

Cisco's current click-path for the in-meeting Record button changes with the site and the client. Use Webex help for the host control: [Record a meeting in Webex](https://help.webex.com/en-us/article/nh2sgm6/Record-a-meeting). Whether a site even allows cloud or local recording is an admin setting. Do not promise a guest that the Record button will be there.

The local path does not need that button. It needs the sound.

## Webex's recorder and yours

| | Webex record control | Mac menu-bar capture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who starts it | Someone with permission in the meeting, usually the host | You, on your Mac |
| Visible in Webex | The meeting can show that it is being recorded | No extra participant |
| Video of the stage | When the site records video | No. Audio only. The screen is not captured |
| You step away | The host recording can continue | Your capture stops |
| File location | Webex cloud or a local Webex file, depending on the site | On your Mac, in the meeting library |
| Works if the site blocks recording | No | Yes, if audio still plays and you are allowed to record |

## A local recording from the menu bar

Vemoir is a Mac app that does this for Webex the same way it does it for Teams or Zoom. The meeting brand does not matter. The audio device does.

1. Install from the [Mac App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794693630). Apple silicon, macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, 8 GB of memory, 10 GB free. 16 GB is the recommendation for long meetings. Intel Macs are not supported.
2. Allow the microphone and System Audio Recording when macOS asks. The capture is an audio-only tap. It does not request Screen Recording, and it does not record pixels.
3. Pick the project if this Webex belongs to a client, then press record in the menu bar before people start the real conversation.
4. Join Webex as you usually do. Use speakers or headphones. Headphones are fine: system audio is the call, not the room.
5. Speak into the selected microphone so your side is on its own track.
6. Stop when the call ends. The transcript that counts is built from the finished audio, not from the live preview.

Price, once, in the App Store: CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US ([pricing](/#pricing)). No per-meeting fee.

Transcription runs on the Mac. Speaker separation runs on the Mac. Summaries, decisions, and action items stay on the Mac unless you export them or you turn on your own OpenAI or Anthropic key for text only. Audio does not go with that key. Webex's cloud does not receive a copy from this path.

If the download of the speech model has not finished, you are not offline yet. Internet is for setup and model downloads. After that, the call can be processed without a network.

## After you stop

Read the decisions before you send anything. A Webex chat decision that happened in the side panel is not in the audio. Add it as a manual note, marked as yours, or type it into the decision row. Do not pretend the tape said it.

Playback exists if you kept the audio. Storage settings choose whether future meetings retain audio after processing succeeds. You can purge audio later and keep the transcript. You can delete the meeting if the whole record should go. A recording that failed halfway keeps recovery audio until you retry or discard it.

Copy the minutes, or export to an Apple Notes folder you picked on the project. There is no PDF or DOCX file from the app. Paste into whatever Webex's follow-up channel expects.

A Webex recording you already downloaded is a different job. Import the audio file (MP3, M4A, WAV, AIFF, CAF, or FLAC). If you only have a video, convert to audio first. A file of 15 minutes or less can also go through [transcribe recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) in the browser: three runs a week, no upload, no speaker labels.

Language: the app interface includes English, German, French, Italian, and Dutch. Parakeet v3 transcribes 25 European languages. That is standard speech, not a dialect mode. A Webex call in Swiss German will not be promised as dialect text.

## Where Webex's own file is the better record

Use the host's recording when any of these matter.

The official record must be the one Webex stores, with the site's retention and the admin's policy. A personal Mac file is not that record, even if the words match.

You need video: slides, who was on camera, a shared screen. Local capture here is audio. It will not reproduce the stage.

You will not stay in the meeting. Training sessions, hearings, or a call you organize and then leave need the host control or a bot you are not using here. A menu-bar recorder leaves when you do.

The other people are in a conference room and you are remote. You get the mix Webex sends to your Mac. You do not get a mic on their table. Names for people sharing one far-end microphone will be weak.

Your company forbids local copies and only allows the Webex recording. Follow that. A technically possible capture is not a permitted one.

If you already have the Webex file and only need text, do not re-record the meeting. Transcribe the file. Re-recording a playback through speakers is a worse copy.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I record Webex on Mac if I am not the host?

You can capture the audio your Mac plays, if you are allowed to. You cannot press Webex's Record button unless the site gave you that control. The two are separate.

### Will other people see a bot?

Not from this path. No participant is added. They will not see a recording indicator from your Mac app. If your policy is that people must know, say so in the meeting. Absence of a bot is not the same as consent.

### Does this grab Webex's cloud recording automatically?

No. If the host recorded in Webex, download that file from Webex when you are allowed to, then import the audio. The menu-bar capture is only what your Mac heard while you were in the call.

### What if I use headphones?

System audio still receives the call. The microphone still receives you. You do not need speakers. If system-audio permission is missing, you will get your mic and little or none of the remote side.

### Is the screen recorded?

No. The permission is System Audio Recording. Webex's window is not captured.

### Can I do this on Windows?

Not with this app. It is Apple silicon Macs only. Webex's own recorder is the cross-platform path.

### What does it cost?

A one-time App Store purchase: CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US. Webex recording controls are part of whatever Webex subscription the host already has. They are not included in the Mac app price.

Tell people if you are keeping a copy. Then record from the menu bar, or use Webex's own control when the official file is the one that counts. The app is on the [Mac App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794693630), and the audio path is described under [capture](/#capture).
