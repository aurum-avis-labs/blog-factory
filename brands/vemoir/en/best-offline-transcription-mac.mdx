## What has to be true before a tool is offline

An offline transcription path has four checks.


**The model is already installed.** The first download needs the internet. Whisper weights, Parakeet, or whatever speech model the app uses are not magic inside the binary unless the developer bundled them. Vemoir does not silently download or substitute a model during a recording. You pick, you see the size, you wait, you can resume a failed download. After that, transcription does not require a connection.

**Audio does not leave for a speech API.** Transcription and speaker separation, where the product has them, run on the Mac. A text model in the cloud is a different step. If an app “falls back” to a cloud transcript when the local one is slow, it is not offline on the day you needed it. Vemoir’s rule is the opposite: if you have not explicitly activated your own OpenAI or Anthropic key, text generation stays local, and a failure does not silently switch providers. Audio never goes to that key. The key is optional. Leave it unused if offline is the point.

**Live preview is not the record.** A partial line on screen during a call can exist without being the transcript you file. The authoritative text is built from the finished audio after you stop. If you quit mid-call, do not expect a complete transcript from the preview.

**The machine can run it.** Apple silicon, enough memory, enough disk. Intel Macs are outside the Vemoir requirement. A huge Whisper model on an 8 GB Mac can be installed and still be a poor daily driver. The app shows hardware fit. Believe it. 16 GB is the recommendation for long meetings. 8 GB and 10 GB of free storage are the minimums for Vemoir, on macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, M1 or newer.

Offline does not include Swiss German dialect as dialect text. Plan on Standard German when the speech was Swiss German, for the public Whisper models and for any promise you are tempted to make. No date belongs on a future dialect feature.

## Best offline transcription for Mac if you only have a file

[OpenAI Whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper) is the reference local model a lot of Mac tools wrap. You can run it yourself. Once the weights are downloaded, the transcription step does not need the network. There is no 15-minute product cap in the open-source project. There is also no meeting, no action list, and no speaker names unless you add them.


That path, or a Finder-first app wrapped around the same idea, is the **better choice** when:

- You drag in files and you never record a live call.
- You do not want a meeting library, projects, or follow-up drafts.
- You are fine in Terminal, or you prefer an app whose whole interface is “drop a file.”

MacWhisper is the usual example of that Finder-first shape. Use it, or anything like it, when the deliverable is a transcript file. This article does not quote its price or a feature from a page we are not linking as a spec sheet. Read the developer’s current page. Do not expect it to be a system of record for client decisions with owners and dates. That is a different product.

DIY Whisper is also where you go if you need a model size the meeting app does not list, and you accept the setup time. You own the failure modes: a wrong sample rate, a file the command did not open, a transcript with no timestamps because of a flag you skipped.

You do not go to raw Whisper when the next hour is a Teams call you have not recorded yet. The model does not hear the call. Something else has to capture system audio first.

## The free browser tool is a short-file cousin

[Transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) downloads a public Whisper model into the browser and can run later with the network off. The file is not uploaded. That is offline in a narrow sense, and it is free.

It is not the best offline transcription for a Mac user with real meetings, because:

- 15 minutes maximum.
- 3 successful runs a week.
- No speaker labels.
- No live capture. The call has to be a file already.
- No minutes workflow beyond what you copy out.

Use it for a short clip on a machine where you will not install an app. Do not describe it as the offline meeting recorder. It is not one.

## Where cloud is still the better system

Cloud wins when several people must open the same recording from a browser, an admin must revoke a leaver, or the organization has already standardized on a seat-based notetaker and a data-processing agreement. Offline on one laptop is a poor team archive. Pay for the shared product. Their pricing pages change. Read them. A local app will not grow a colleague login because you wanted it to.

Cloud also wins when you refuse to manage model downloads and you accept that the audio goes to the vendor. That is a preference, not a failure. It is the wrong preference on a train with no signal, ten minutes after a client said the recording must stay on your computer.

Live captions built into macOS are a different feature. They help you read along. They are not a meeting file with decisions and a retention switch. Do not archive captions from a strip on the screen and call it the transcript.

Video editors’ caption tracks are better when the product is a published video. They are worse when the product is client minutes. Pick the output you were hired to deliver.

## Setup you do once, on purpose

Download models on a stable network, not guest wifi that drops mid-file. In Vemoir, choose speech and local text models before the first client call and read the size on screen. On an 8 GB Mac, take the smaller speech model the app recommends instead of Large-v3 by reflex. Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages. Neither pick ships with a benchmark you should paste into a proposal.

Same day: grant microphone and System Audio Recording, then run a one-minute test so the system track is not silent. Set retention in Storage before the meeting file exists. If the client contract is stricter, note that in your project folder. Offline describes processing without the network, not keeping audio forever.

A reinstall or second Mac needs the weights again. They do not hide in iCloud. After a macOS update, transcribe a one-minute file with wifi off before you travel. If the app asks for the network then, fix it before you tell the client the recording never left the Mac.

When someone else needs the text, export the note. A laptop password is not a sharing method, and exporting is still an upload in the everyday sense even when transcription was local.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best offline transcription for Mac if I only drop files in Finder?

A local Whisper install or a file-first app such as MacWhisper. You do not need a meeting library for that job. Confirm the current app price with its developer.

### Does offline include the first launch?

No. You download models while you have a connection. Offline starts after those files are on disk.

### Will Vemoir switch to the cloud if the local model fails?

No. There is no silent fallback. An external text model runs only after you explicitly activate your own key, and it never receives the audio.

### Which Macs are supported for Vemoir?

Apple silicon, M1 or newer, macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, 8 GB of memory, 10 GB free. 16 GB is recommended for long meetings. Intel is not supported.

### How much does the offline meeting app cost?

CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US, once. No subscription.

### Can the free site tool run offline?

After its model download, yes, for files up to 15 minutes and three successful runs a week. No speakers, no live meeting.

### Does System Audio Recording capture the screen?

No. It is the permission used to hear other apps. The recording is audio.

### Does offline mean Swiss German dialect transcripts?

No. Do not claim dialect output. Expect a standard-language draft and correct it.
