System audio recording on a Mac is how Vemoir hears the meeting app. macOS asks for **System Audio Recording**. That permission lets the app take the audio other apps play, through an audio-only capture. Vemoir does not ask for Screen Recording, and it does not record the screen, a window, or the camera image. If a guide tells you to enable Screen Recording for Vemoir, that advice is out of date.

You still need the microphone if you want your own voice as its own track. The two captures are separate: the mic is your side, system audio is the call.

## Why the old name keeps showing up

For a while, the practical way for a Mac app to hear another app was the Screen Recording permission, even when the app never saved video. Guides were written around that checkbox. macOS now has a permission whose job is system audio. Vemoir uses that one. Searching "screen recording permission meeting" still lands on articles for the old path. Follow the prompt Vemoir shows, and the toggle named System Audio Recording.

The capture is a Core Audio tap. It takes meeting audio and leaves pixels alone. It also excludes Vemoir's own output so the app is not recording itself.

## Turn on system audio recording

1. Install Vemoir from the [Mac App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794693630). Requirements are Apple silicon, macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, and room for the local models. Intel Macs are not supported.
2. When you record meeting sound, allow **System Audio Recording** if macOS asks. Allow the **Microphone** as well if you want your voice.
3. If you dismissed the prompt, open System Settings, Privacy and Security, **System Audio Recording**, and turn Vemoir on. Do the same for Microphone. Quit and reopen the app if you just changed a toggle.
4. Do not enable Screen Recording for Vemoir to "finish" setup. That row is not the permission this version uses.
5. Start the recording from the menu bar when the call starts, and stop when the call ends. Nothing joins as a bot. See [platforms](/#platforms) and [capture](/#capture).

Headphones do not change the permission. The tap hears Mac output whether sound goes to speakers or AirPods. If remote voices stay silent after you allow system audio, check mute in the meeting app, the wrong output device, and whether you only armed the microphone. Those cases are covered in [meeting recording with no sound on a Mac](/blog/meeting-recording-no-sound-mac).

## What gets stored

Vemoir keeps microphone and system audio as separate local tracks. After you stop, the transcript is built from the finished audio on the Mac. A live line on screen while you record is a preview, not the record you keep.

Transcription and speaker separation stay on the machine. Notes run on the machine by default. If you turn on your own API key for text, the audio still does not go out for that step. Once the models are installed, the meeting can be processed offline. Internet is for the first download.

You choose whether to keep the audio after a successful pass. Sharing a summary does not attach the tracks unless you deliberately share those files. See [exports](/#exports).

Speaker labels from this capture are produced on the Mac, then you rename them. The browser cannot listen to system audio at all. A file of 15 minutes or less is the browser trial for files you already have, not live system audio.

## What this permission is not

- Not a screen capture, screenshot tool, or camera.
- Not a bot in the participant list.
- Not permission to skip telling people they are being recorded. Consent stays with you.
- Not Windows, iPhone, or an Intel Mac.
- Not the browser. No Vemoir page records system audio in a tab.

QuickTime and some other Mac apps use screen capture when you want a movie of the display. Audio can ride along as a side effect of video. If your goal is a transcript of a call, a screen movie is the heavy way to get sound, and you still have no minutes until something transcribes it. System audio recording, as Vemoir uses it, skips the movie.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Vemoir need Screen Recording to hear a Teams or Zoom call?

No. It asks for System Audio Recording and uses that for audio only. Older instructions that send you to the Screen Recording list describe a permission this app does not request.

### Why do I also see a microphone prompt?

System audio is the other people's audio, as the Mac plays it. The microphone is you. They are separate tracks. You can record system audio without the mic if you only need the remote side.

### I enabled Screen Recording and the call is still silent. What now?

Screen Recording is the wrong checkbox for this app. Check System Audio Recording and the microphone, then reopen Vemoir. Also unmute the meeting app and confirm you started the recording from the menu bar.

### Does system audio recording upload the call?

Not by default. The tracks stay on the Mac unless you export them or you explicitly send text to a model with your own key.

### Can a browser tab record system audio the same way?

No. The free tools take a file you already have, up to 15 minutes, or text you paste. They do not capture live system audio.

### Will this record a phone that is not playing through the Mac?

No. System audio is sound the Mac is playing. A handset at your ear, or a phone on speaker the Mac never hears, is outside this tap. The microphone might pick up a loud speakerphone in the room. That is room audio, not the system-audio track.

### Does the permission cover every meeting app?

It covers audio those apps play on the Mac. It is not a Teams integration or a Zoom plugin. If the app's audio is audible in your headphones or speakers, the tap can take that output. If the app is muted, there is nothing to take.

### Is the screen ever stored by mistake?

No. The capture path does not take screen images or video. If you also use another app to record the display, that file is from that app, not from this permission.
