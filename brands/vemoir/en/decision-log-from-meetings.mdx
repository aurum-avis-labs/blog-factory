A **decision log from meetings** is a short list of choices: the wording, the person responsible, and the date. It is not the transcript, and it is not the task list. Someone reading only the log should see what changed, not who talked the longest.

If you already keep minutes, the decisions block is the part people look up three weeks later. This page is how to keep that block honest, whether you fill a sheet by hand or let a local recorder draft the rows.

## What a decision log from meetings records

Write a row only when the room chose something. "We discussed pricing" is not a decision. "Standard support stays at the current rate through 31 March. Lea owns the client note. 11 Nov 2026." is a decision.


Each row needs three fields:

- **Wording.** What was chosen, in a sentence a person who missed the call can act on.
- **Responsible person.** Who carries it. A team name is weaker than a name. If nobody owned it, the row is not finished.
- **Date.** The day the choice was made, not the day you typed the log. If the choice applies later, put that date in the wording and keep the decision date as the day of the meeting.

Optional, and useful: the meeting it came from, and whether it replaced an older row. Do not paste the transcript under the row. If you need the line later, keep a pointer to the passage. The log itself stays short.

A worked example, labeled as an example, not a client story:

```text
Decision: Keep the March pilot on the existing plan. No new tier.
Person: Samir Shah
Date: 2026-11-11
Meeting: Acme weekly
Replaces: none
```

The blank layout for a whole sitting, including decisions next to actions, is the [meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template). Use that when you need the full record. Use the log when the only question is "what did we decide."

## One line that still means something later

A log fails in a few repeatable ways.

**The row quotes the debate.** "Noah preferred monthly, Priya preferred annual, we talked for twenty minutes." That is a transcript note. The decision is the choice at the end, or an explicit "no decision, revisit on 18 Nov."

**The person is "the team."** Three weeks later nobody sends the note. Put a name. If the name is you, say so.

**The date is missing.** Two rows about the same topic then look simultaneous. The later one should show that it replaced the earlier one, with both dates.

**The wording softens the choice.** "We are leaning toward pausing hiring" will be read as a pause. If you only leaned, write "No decision. Revisit 2 Dec. Owner: Jo."

**A passing remark becomes policy.** Someone said "we should probably stop sending the Friday deck." That is not a decision until the room accepted it. Leave it in the transcript or in your manual notes. Manual notes stay marked as yours. They are not a verbatim line and they are not a decision until you promote them.

## Decisions are not action items

An action is work: description, owner, due date, status. A decision is a choice. The same meeting produces both, and mixing them makes the log useless.

| | Decision | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question it answers | What did we choose? | Who does the next piece, by when? |
| Fields | Wording, person, date | Description, owner, due date, status |
| Example | Stay on the current plan through March. | Email the client the March date. |

You can have a decision with no immediate task ("we will not bid"). You can have a task that does not change a policy ("book the room"). The log is the first column. The task list is the second. Copying actions into the decision log makes both longer and less trustworthy.

On a project that spans many calls, the useful view is recent decisions across those calls, not a fresh summary of the last hour. A decision from 11 Oct still governs 11 Nov unless a later row replaces it. Tasks from the same call live in a separate list; see [action items from meetings](/blog/action-items-from-meetings) when owners and due dates are the question.

## A sheet you fill without a recording

If you have no audio, or you are not allowed to record, write the log while the call is still warm. The free [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) is a blank record in the browser: attendees, decisions, actions with owner and due date, open questions. Nothing is uploaded. There is no model on that page, so a starter does not spend a weekly transcription run. You copy Markdown when you are done.

A minimum decisions block inside that sheet:

```text
Decisions
- Wording:
  Person:
  Date:
- Wording:
  Person:
  Date:
```

Leave a row blank rather than invent an owner. "Open: no owner" is a better log than a guessed name.

This sheet is not a formal minute for a board or a Verwaltungsrat. Those still need the person who is responsible for the official record. The sheet is the working log you can send the same day.

It is not displayed as if the speaker said those exact words.

The project dashboard shows recent decisions next to open work. That is the log for the engagement, grouped by date, without you reconstructing it from chat.

Regenerating the notes compares the transcript, your manual notes, and what is already written. Edits you made, and rows you maintain yourself, are kept. The app does not silently replace a decision you corrected.

Speaker names on the row come from labels you set after the call, or from a voice profile you explicitly approved. Unknown speakers stay anonymous. Do not put an anonymous label in the "person" field and call it ownership.

Audio stays on the Mac unless you export or you turn on your own API key for text. Transcription does not use that key. Deleting the audio later does not delete the decision row. Deleting the meeting does.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a decision log from meetings the same as minutes?

No. Minutes usually include attendees, agenda, decisions, actions, and open questions. The log is only the decisions, with a person and a date. The [minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template) covers the wider record.

### What if we decided not to decide?

Write that. "No decision. Revisit on {date}. Person: {name}." An empty row looks like you forgot, which is a different fact.

### Can one decision cover several meetings?

Yes. Keep the date of the meeting that made it, and point later meetings at that row instead of copying a fuzzier version. When a later meeting changes it, add a new row that says what it replaces.

### Does the browser sheet transcribe the call?

No. The minutes sheet is a form. It does not upload what you type, and it does not take audio. Use it when you already know the wording.

### Who should be named if two people agreed?

Name the person who will answer for the choice next time it is questioned. Agreement in the room is not the same field as ownership.

### Can I fix a decision after I read the transcript?

Yes. Edit the wording, the person, or the date. Keep the transcript as the evidence of what was said. The row is your record of what was chosen.

### Does this replace a legal minute?

No. A working log is not a certified record, and it is not a formal board minute. The person responsible for that record still writes it.

Fill the decisions block on the [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) when you have the wording and no recording. When the call is on the Mac, keep the row next to the meeting it came from.
