A **Bluedot alternative** is the right search when you like a notetaker that does not join the call, and you still need to decide where the audio lives afterward. Bluedot's homepage says it captures and transcribes meetings, online or in person, without a bot, and that your data is not used to train AI models. Its security page says the data is stored in AWS. A Mac app that keeps the recording on the machine is a different product, even when both are "bot-free."

This page uses Bluedot's own pricing and security pages, checked September 2026. A bot-free team workspace still wins for several jobs. The point is to name them.

## What Bluedot documents

[Bluedot](https://www.bluedothq.com/) describes an AI note taker that captures, transcribes, and summarizes meetings without a bot joining the call. The homepage lists Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, phone calls, and in-person meetings, and says notes can sync toward a CRM, Notion, and other tools. It also says customer data is never used to train or improve AI models.

The [security page](https://www.bluedothq.com/security), checked September 2026, repeats the no-training line and adds that data is not stored, logged, or retained by AI providers after generation. It states AES-256 and TLS, that deleted data is removed immediately with backups cleared within 30 days, and that data is stored in AWS. It says Bluedot maintains SOC 2 Type II controls and GDPR-compliant controls. Those are Bluedot's statements. They describe a cloud service with admin features, not a file that never leaves the laptop. They are not a ruling on whether your call may be recorded.

Workspace admins, on that page, can set retention for recordings, transcripts, and notes. That control exists because the library is the company's, not only yours.

## Bot-free is not the same as on-device

"No bot in the participant list" answers one complaint. It does not answer "where is the file."

Bluedot's design, from the pages above, is a product your team can share: links, comments, clips, CRM and ATS hooks on the higher plan, Slack and Notion even on the free tier. The security page places production data in AWS. If your rule is "transcription happens on this Mac unless I export," that page is the reason to look at a local app instead.

If your rule is "no stranger in the Zoom tile, and the team can open the note tomorrow," Bluedot is aimed at that rule. Replacing it with a single-user Mac library removes the share model. You would export on purpose, one note at a time.

In-person, Bluedot's homepage says it captures conversations in the room. A Mac can also sit on the table with its microphone on. That is the microphone you already have. It is not a separate in-room product mode, and a single mic in a noisy room will not label people the way two tracks (your mic and the call audio) can on a video call.

## What Bluedot charges

Checked September 2026 on [Bluedot pricing](https://www.bluedothq.com/pricing). Annual prices shown on the page:

- **Free:** $0 per user. 5 meetings for the lifetime of the account, Chrome extension, desktop and mobile apps, Slack, Notion, Zapier, and Make.
- **Basic:** $14 per user a month, billed annually. Unlimited audio-only meetings, unlimited storage, max 1 hour per recording, webhook access, MCP integration.
- **Pro:** $20 per user a month, billed annually. Video as well as audio, unlimited recording duration, custom note templates, Zoom and Google Drive imports.
- **Business:** $32 per user a month, billed annually. CRM and ATS integrations, advanced admin, SSO and SCIM, custom data retention, org-wide MCP and webhooks, unlimited audio and video imports.

The monthly column on the same page lists Basic at $18, Pro at $25, and Business at $39 per user. The page also mentions a flat company price. Treat the live toggle as the number you will actually pay.

The free tier is five meetings total, not five a month. A week of standups ends the trial. Basic's one-hour cap matters if your calls run long. Video, imports, and CRM sit on higher tiers. MCP appears on the paid rows. That is Bluedot's integration surface. It is not a reason to assume every notetaker ships the same connector.

## How to choose a Bluedot alternative

1. **Who else must open the note without you exporting it?** If the answer is a team with logins, you want a workspace. If the answer is "I will paste the decisions," a local record is enough.
2. **May the audio sit in AWS?** Bluedot's security page says it does. A local Mac app does not, unless you export or you turn on a text API key you control.
3. **Do you need CRM field updates, ATS push, or SSO?** Those are Business-plan jobs on Bluedot's page. A notes app that stops at Markdown will not replace them.

A recording you already have, 15 minutes or under, can skip both products. [Transcribe recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) runs in the browser, does not upload the file, and allows three successful runs a week. No speaker labels. No live meeting.

## Bluedot alternatives by the job

### Vemoir, when the Mac is the library

Vemoir records from the Mac menu bar. Microphone and system audio are separate local tracks. You start and stop. Nothing joins the call. The permission is System Audio Recording, audio only. The screen is not captured.

After you stop, transcription and speaker separation run on the Mac. Summaries, decisions, actions, documents, and questions stay on the Mac by default. An OpenAI or Anthropic key is optional, text only, off until you turn it on, with no silent fallback. Audio does not go out for transcription.

Decisions have wording, a person, and a date. Actions have an owner and a due date. You can point a claim at a transcript line. You can copy the note, write a Markdown document, or update the same Apple Note on a later export. There is no shared team login, no CRM write-back, and no SSO. There is no PDF or DOCX export. There is no MCP connector in the app.

Price on the Mac App Store ([pricing](/#pricing)): CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US. One-time, every current feature included, no seats. Apple silicon, macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, 8 GB of memory, 10 GB free, 16 GB recommended for long meetings. Intel Macs, Windows, and phones are not supported. [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794693630).

The interface is in English, German, French, Italian, and Dutch. Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages for transcription. That is not Swiss German dialect output.

### Keep Bluedot when the workspace is the product

If the sales or recruiting team needs the note in a CRM or an ATS, and an admin needs retention and SSO, Bluedot's Business row is the thing you evaluated. A local app can draft the note you paste in. It will not update the CRM field or the candidate record. Do not buy it expecting that.

## Where a bot-free team product still wins

Bluedot is the better fit when the absence of a bot is necessary and the team library is also necessary.

**Several people must search the same calls.** A link, a comment, a clip, a workspace login. Vemoir search covers the meetings on that Mac. It does not give a colleague an account.

**CRM or ATS is the system of record.** Business lists those integrations. Pasting a summary into HubSpot or a recruiting tool by hand is slower and gets skipped. If the skip is the problem you are solving, the integration wins.

**You need SSO, SCIM, and a retention timer.** Custom retention is a Business line, and the security page tells admins they can set it. A personal Mac has a storage setting for whether audio is kept after processing. That is not an org policy.

**Windows, Chrome, and a phone are in the fleet.** Pricing lists a Chrome extension and desktop and mobile apps on the free row. Vemoir does not install there. A mixed fleet should not be told to wait for a Mac.

**You want video, not only audio.** Pro adds video and removes the one-hour cap. Vemoir is audio. It never records the screen.

**Five free meetings are enough to see the flow.** Then you pay per seat or you stop. That is a cleaner trial than guessing from a homepage. It is a poor archive. Five lifetime meetings disappear in a busy Monday.

**MCP, webhooks, Slack, Notion, Zapier.** If an automation already expects those, Bluedot's pricing page is listing the hooks. A local notes app will not drop into that chain. Build the note, then hand it to the tool you already pay for, or keep the tool that already emits the hook.

## A practical switch, not a rip-out

Do not move a team off a workspace in one afternoon because a laptop can record. List the last ten meetings. Mark which ones someone other than the recorder opened later. Mark which ones wrote a CRM or ATS field. Mark which ones were sensitive enough that an upload was the actual objection.

If most of the ten were opened by other people, or landed in a CRM, Bluedot (or another team product) is still the system. Use a local recorder only on the calls where the upload was the problem, and paste a short note into the workspace if the team still needs the outcome. Two tools, named in the invite, beat a silent swap.

If almost none of the ten were opened by anyone else, you are paying seats for a library of one. A one-time Mac app is the closer match. Export the decisions you still need before the seat lapses. Five free meetings will not hold the archive.

Write the rule in one sentence your colleagues can repeat: shared calls stay in the workspace; calls that must not be uploaded stay on the Mac. The sentence is the rollout. The feature grid is not.

## Limits to plan for

On the local side, live recording is the Mac app. The website transcribes a file you already have, with the 15-minute and weekly caps, and without speaker names. Swiss German speech usually comes out as Standard German in the browser tools. The Mac app does not add a dialect claim.

A room recording on a Mac is the selected microphone, with optional on-device noise suppression (off, balanced, or strong) for steady hum and keyboard noise. People far from the laptop will be faint. Do not describe that as a dedicated in-person mode. Speaker labels from one room mic are weaker than labels from a call where your voice and the remote audio are separate tracks.

Offline use waits until the models are downloaded. There is no silent model swap if a download fails.

Consent and how long you keep a record stay with you. Neither SOC 2 language nor a local database makes the recording allowed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Bluedot alternative just another bot-free app?

Some are. The useful split is whether the audio is in their cloud for the team, or on your Mac until you export. Bluedot's security page puts data in AWS. Read that before you treat "no bot" as "private to this laptop."

### Does the free plan cover a month of standups?

No. The pricing page says 5 meetings for the lifetime of the free account, not per month. A daily standup uses that up immediately.

### Can Bluedot update a CRM?

The Business plan lists CRM and ATS integrations. Lower rows list Slack, Notion, Zapier, and Make. Check which plan you would actually buy. The Mac app does not write to a CRM.

### What is the one-hour limit?

Basic, on the pricing page, caps a recording at 1 hour and is audio only. Pro lists unlimited duration and video. If your calls run past an hour, Basic is the wrong row even before you compare vendors.

### Does Vemoir join the meeting?

No. It records what your Mac hears. If you are not in the call, and the audio is not playing on that Mac, there is no recording. Bluedot's bot-free claim is about their own apps. It does not put a listener on a machine where nobody opened one.

### Can I import a Zoom file?

Bluedot lists Zoom and Google Drive imports on Pro, and unlimited imports on Business. Vemoir imports local audio files on the Mac, any length the machine can process, without a monthly import cap. The browser tool stops at 15 minutes.

### Is MCP a reason to pick one?

Only if you already have a workflow that calls it. Bluedot's pricing page lists MCP on paid plans. Vemoir does not offer MCP. Do not pick either product for a connector you will not use.

### Which one for a single consultant on a Mac?

If you do not need a shared login or a CRM write, a one-time local app matches the constraint better than a per-seat cloud. If the client team must browse the calls themselves, the workspace wins and the local app is the wrong promise.
