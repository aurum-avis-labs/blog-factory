**Evidence in meeting notes** is a pointer from a claim back to the line that supports it. The summary can be short. The pointer is what lets someone who was not in the room, or who was and remembers it differently, check the wording without replaying the whole call.

A polished paragraph with no trail is easy to forward and hard to defend. This page is about that gap: what counts as evidence, what only looks like it, and why the original transcript has to stay untouched when the notes get cleaner.

## What evidence in meeting notes actually is

Evidence, here, is the timestamped passage in the transcript that a later sentence depends on. It is not the sentence itself. It is not your impression of the tone. It is the words that were captured, in order, from the finished audio.

That split matters because notes and transcripts do different jobs. The transcript is long and uneven. The notes are what you send. Between them sits a judgment: which lines became a decision, which became a task, which stayed small talk. Evidence is the way back from that judgment to the lines.

Without the way back, the note has to be trusted on style. With it, a reviewer can open the passage, read it, and decide whether the note overreached.

Vemoir keeps the raw timestamped transcript separate from generated or edited prose. Summary, action, and decision rows can reveal the passages they rest on. The control opens the source meeting, switches to the transcript, centers the passage, and marks it. A reference that does not resolve is not shown as a link. A broken pointer should look like a gap, not like proof.

## A summary is a claim

Read a summary as a set of claims, even when the prose is smooth.

  <span class="blog-kicker">Note</span>
  <p>Evidence links are not a legal certification. Retention and consent stay with you.</p>
</aside>


- "We agreed to pause hiring" claims agreement, not just a suggestion.
- "Lea will send the draft by Friday" claims an owner and a date.
- "The client rejected the second option" claims a rejection, not a hesitation.

Each of those can be true and still be hard to find in a forty-minute transcript. Evidence is the few lines that make the claim checkable. It is not a dump of every time the topic was mentioned.

A good pointer is short. It lands on the moment the choice was made, or on the moment it clearly was not. Ten highlights on the same topic teach less than one highlight on the sentence that settled it.

Ask-style questions over a meeting work the same way. An answer can point at supporting lines. In Vemoir those links are optional, capped, and chosen as the strongest passages rather than every paragraph the search touched. A long answer that cites everything is a second transcript. It does not help you check a claim.

## What is not evidence

Several things get treated as proof and are not.

**Your manual notes.** Notes you type during the call are valuable. A figure, a spelling, a constraint that never got said clearly. They should feed the minutes. They are still your words. They must stay visually distinct from a verbatim line. If the note and the transcript disagree, both can be true in different ways: you heard a number the model missed, or you wrote down a hope. Neither one silently replaces the other.

**A glossary.** Preferred terms and aliases help later drafts use the name the client uses now. They do not rewrite the original transcript. The evidence stays the words that were spoken, even if the summary uses the preferred name.

**A regenerated paragraph.** Running the notes again can tidy wording and pick up edits. The new paragraph is still generated text. It is evidence only if it still points at a passage that resolves. If the trail breaks, the polish remains a draft.

**A follow-up email.** The email is what you told the other party. That matters. It is not a substitute for the line in the transcript if the dispute is about what was said, rather than about what you later wrote.

**Speaker confidence you did not check.** Unknown speakers stay anonymous until you rename a label or approve a remembered profile. An anonymous label next to a sharp quote is still usable evidence of the words. It is weak evidence of who said them. Do not upgrade it by guesswork inside the note and then treat the note as the source.

**A live preview.** While the call is running, a preview can be incomplete. The transcript that counts is the one built from the finished local audio after you stop. Evidence should point there.

## How a check works on the day

You do not need a special review meeting to use evidence. You need one habit on the same day the minutes land.

1. Read the decision or the action, not the whole summary.
2. Open the cited passage. If there is no citation, the row is a draft until you find the line or you mark it as your own note.
3. If the passage supports the row, leave it.
4. If the passage is softer than the row, edit the row. Do not edit the transcript to match the row.
5. If you cannot find a passage, either demote the row to an open question or write it as your note, with your name on it.

That habit is most useful when two people remember the call differently. You stop arguing about memory and look at the line. If the line is ambiguous, the honest record is "not decided" or "your reading," not a smoother sentence.

On a Mac, those passages stay with the meeting library. Search can find the words. A scoped question can return an answer with links. Archived and deleted meetings drop out of that picture, which is another reason to resolve the citations before you archive a touchy call. How that question-and-answer surface is set up is on the [chat section](/#chat). How the record is structured is on [the record](/#record).

If you write the note by hand, the [meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template) is the wider canvas for decisions and actions. A hand-written row still deserves the same habit: you should be able to say which moment it came from, even if the pointer is a timestamp in the margin rather than a link.

## When the line and the note disagree

Disagreement is the point of keeping both.

Sometimes the transcript is the weaker source. Crosstalk, a name said once, a number spoken quickly. Your manual note may be the better capture of that detail. Keep it as a manual note. Do not paste it into the transcript and call it audio.

Sometimes the note is the weaker source. The summary says the budget was approved. The passage says "we can look at it next week if finance is in the room." Fix the summary. Leave the passage.

Sometimes both are incomplete. The decision happened in the room after the recording stopped, or in chat after the call. Then the transcript cannot be the evidence. Write the source as the chat or the hallway, and do not imply the tape said it.

None of this is a certified transcript, and it does not settle a legal record. It is a practical way to keep meeting notes checkable. Consent to record, and what you are allowed to keep, stay with you and with the people on the call.

Audio on this path is local by default. A pointer inside the library does not upload the passage. If you later export a document without evidence links, the recipient sees the note and not the trail. Send the trail only when they need to check the claim. Copying the whole transcript "just in case" is a different decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does evidence in meeting notes mean the summary is quoted speech?

No. The summary is still prose. Evidence is the passage you can open to see what the prose rests on.

### What if the link does not land on a real line?

It should not be shown as evidence. A dangling reference is a gap. Treat the row as unchecked until you repair it or mark it as your own note.

### Can my typed notes count as evidence of what was said?

They count as evidence of what you wrote. They are not a verbatim transcript. Keep that difference visible.

### Will cleaning up names rewrite the original line?

A glossary can change temporary input to a draft and the names in generated text. The original transcript stays the evidence, old wording included.

### Is a citation the same as agreement?

No. A line can show that an idea was said. Agreement is a decision row with a person and a date, and it still should point at the moment the room chose.

### Do I have to send the evidence with every email?

No. Many follow-ups only need the decision and the action. Keep the pointer in your record so you can open it if the email is questioned.
