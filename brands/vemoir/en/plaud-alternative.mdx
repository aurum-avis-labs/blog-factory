You are looking for a **Plaud alternative** because you want to know whether you still need the hardware or whether software is enough. Plaud Note and Plaud NotePin are pocket recorders: press a button, the app transcribes. Vemoir is Mac software for calls that play on the computer. Zoom, Meet, and Teams send audio through the Mac; for that, the menu bar is often enough. Conversations in a room with no laptop still need a device on the table.

Pages below were checked in September 2026. Device prices and minute bundles change. Read Plaud’s product page before you order.

## What Plaud does well

[Plaud](https://www.plaud.ai/products/plaud-note-ai-voice-recorder) sells a physical note-taker, not a bot that joins a video call. The Note product page describes a thin recorder with onboard storage, long battery life, and a Starter plan of transcription minutes included with the device. Recording on the hardware does not depend on a meeting app. You can sit it in a room, or use the phone-call mode the product describes, without asking Zoom for permission to record.


Plaud’s pages say AI transcription and summaries are part of the membership story. A free monthly minute allowance comes with the device. More minutes are a subscription. The same product pages list compliance language (ISO 27001, GDPR, SOC 2, HIPAA, and others) as Plaud’s own claims. Treat those as claims to verify on Plaud’s trust materials, not as a reason another app is certified. Plaud does not make your recording lawful.

Third-party write-ups of Plaud’s privacy FAQ, and Plaud reseller FAQ pages, describe a split that matters: audio can live on the device and in the app, and transcription sends audio to servers, with cloud retention tied to whether you turn sync on. Confirm the current wording on Plaud’s own privacy pages before you tell a client “it never uploads.” A hardware recorder that transcribes in the cloud is still a cloud transcription product for the text step.

Plaud fits when the microphone must be an object on the table. Software like Vemoir fits when the sound already comes from your Mac speakers.

## How to choose a Plaud alternative

Ask where the mouths are.


1. **Is everyone on a video call that plays on your Mac?** Then system audio plus your microphone is enough. A credit-card recorder in your pocket does not hear the remote side unless the call is also on a speaker in the room.
2. **Is the conversation in the room, or on a phone, with no computer?** Hardware, or a phone app, is the tool. A Mac menu bar cannot hear a meeting it is not connected to.
3. **After you have a file, where may it go?** Plaud’s AI step, as described on its own materials, is a service with a minute quota. A local Mac app or a browser tool can transcribe a file you export, within their limits, without a second subscription.

For a short file you already exported, Vemoir’s [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) tool runs in the browser. No upload. 15 minutes, 3 successful runs a week, MP3, M4A, WAV, or MP4, no speaker labels. Live meetings on the Mac are the Mac app, not that tab. To capture the next headset call without a second device, start from the menu bar ([capture](/#capture)).

## Seven Plaud alternatives

### Vemoir (Mac app)

**Best for:** online meetings on a Mac, with audio staying on the machine by default, and no extra device.

Vemoir is software from Aurum Avis Labs GmbH in Oberägeri, Switzerland. You start and stop from the menu bar. It captures microphone and system audio as separate local tracks. Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, Slack huddles, and Discord are in scope because they play sound, not because of a partner badge ([platforms](/#platforms)). No bot joins. You tell people you are recording.

Transcription and speaker separation run on the Mac. Minutes, decisions, actions, and a follow-up are generated on the Mac unless you explicitly send text to OpenAI or Anthropic with your own key. Audio is not part of that text request. There is no silent cloud fallback. After models are installed, the workflow can run offline.

You can keep or drop meeting audio in Storage settings. Each meeting keeps the choice from when it was created. Notes you type during the call fold into the record ([the record](/#record)). macOS asks for Microphone and System Audio Recording. The app does not record the screen.

The Mac App Store price is one payment: CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US ([pricing](/#pricing)). Apple silicon, macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, 8 GB of memory, 10 GB free. 16 GB is recommended for long meetings. It does not run on iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows, or Intel Macs. It is not a hardware recorder.

### MacWhisper

**Best for:** a file you already have, including an export from a recorder, transcribed locally in Finder.

MacWhisper uses local models by default ([Keeping transcriptions private](https://docs.macwhisper.com/article/52-keeping-transcriptions-private), updated July 27, 2025). Pro is listed at €64 once on the site (checked September 2026). If Plaud’s cloud minute quota is the part you dislike, export the audio you are allowed to keep and drop it on MacWhisper. That is a stronger file workflow than buying another meeting bot. Live menu-bar capture with structured minutes is a different job, and MacWhisper’s meeting detection is a beta that is not in the Mac App Store build ([Record meetings](https://docs.macwhisper.com/article/30-record-meetings)). The file-versus-live-call split is the subject of the [MacWhisper alternative](/blog/macwhisper-alternative) comparison.

### Jamie

**Best for:** bot-free software capture, including in-person meetings Jamie says it covers, with EU processing on a subscription.

Jamie describes recording without a bot, EU storage, and deletion of audio after transcription ([Jamie on data handling](https://www.meetjamie.ai/blog/llm-info), checked September 2026). You do not buy a piece of hardware. You do accept Jamie’s cloud for the text. Pricing is on [Jamie pricing](https://meetjamie.ai/pricing). For a conversation in a room, Jamie’s phone or in-person path may replace a dedicated recorder. Confirm that path on Jamie’s current site. Vemoir does not offer a separate in-person room mode.

### Granola

**Best for:** live notes from a computer, no bot, notes in Granola’s cloud.

Granola does not store meeting audio long term, according to its security page, and it does not join as a participant ([Granola security](https://www.granola.ai/security), checked September 2026). It does not replace a pocket recorder when the Mac is closed. Its privacy FAQ covers model-improvement opt-out on some plans ([Granola privacy FAQ](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs)).

### Fathom

**Best for:** video calls on Zoom, Meet, and Teams, with clips and a free recording tier, not for a tabletop microphone.

Fathom’s 3.0 help describes bot-free modes on Mac for English meetings and a bot path for audio and video ([Fathom 3.0](https://help.fathom.video/en/articles/11577345), checked September 2026). If you bought Plaud to avoid a bot, Fathom’s bot mode is the thing to skip. If you bought Plaud because you were away from the desk, Fathom does not hear the room either.

### Otter.ai

**Best for:** a phone-and-web notetaker with a bot for scheduled calls.

Otter joins meetings and offers mobile apps ([Otter pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing), checked September 2026). It can import audio, which is closer to “I already recorded this” than to “this device is my microphone.” The notetaker still appears when you use the meeting path ([Otter help](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/39339238308503-Recording-Permissions-with-Otter)).

### Fireflies.ai

**Best for:** a team library of video calls, per seat, not for a hardware mic.

Fireflies stores recordings in its cloud and states that meeting content is not used to train models ([Fireflies privacy policy](https://fireflies.ai/privacy-policy), checked September 2026). It does not sit on the table. If the problem was “our Plaud files never get into the CRM,” Fireflies may be the library you wanted. If the problem was “I will not upload this interview,” it is not.

A short export can also go to [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) before you pick a paid path.

## Where Plaud is still the better choice

Buy Plaud, or keep it, when the microphone has to exist without a computer. A phone call you are not taking on the Mac, a conversation across a table in a café, a site visit: the Mac app is silent there. Plaud’s battery and onboard storage are the product. Software that assumes system audio will miss the entire meeting.

Plaud is also the better object when you want one obvious recording gesture for people in the room. A physical device is easier to point at than a menu-bar icon. Consent is still your job. A visible recorder can make that conversation shorter. It does not replace it.

If you already standardized on Plaud templates, mind maps, and a shared minute pool, moving every habit onto a Mac utility throws away the workflow you paid for the hardware to get. Do that only if the cloud transcription step is the part you need to leave.

## Honest limits to plan for

A hardware recorder does not label remote Zoom participants by itself, and it does not hear them unless their voices are in the room or on a speakerphone the device can pick up. For a normal headset call, the remote side is on system audio. That is the Mac app’s job, not the card in your pocket.

Vemoir will not be your phone recorder. There is no iOS app in this product. There is no "walk around the office" mode to describe. In a room, the Mac microphone can hear people near the computer. That is ordinary mic capture, not a dedicated wearable.

Plaud’s AI minutes depend on the plan’s minute quota and on whatever processing the current privacy page describes. If that upload is unacceptable, the hardware can still be a recorder and the transcription can happen later on the Mac, if you can export the file. MacWhisper is the better Finder tool for that file. The browser tool covers a short one. Neither recreates Plaud’s templates automatically.

Vemoir does not claim Swiss German dialect text. Browser tools using public Whisper models usually write Standard German from Swiss German speech. No product here is “compliant” because it is Swiss, local, or hardware. You tell people, and you decide retention.

Disk, model downloads, and Apple silicon limits apply to Vemoir. 8 GB is the minimum, 16 GB is the recommendation for long meetings. Intel Macs are out.

## If you already own the recorder

Keep Plaud for the conversations it can hear and stop asking it to attend your headset calls. For those, record with Vemoir from the menu bar ([how it works](/#how)). You get system audio and your mic, speaker labels you can correct on the Mac, and minutes you can export ([exports](/#exports)).

For a file Plaud already produced, use [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) if it is under 15 minutes and you have a run left this week. Longer exports go to the Mac app’s import (MP3, M4A, WAV, AIFF, CAF, FLAC) or to MacWhisper. Import on the Mac runs through the same local pipeline as a live recording. The browser tool does not separate speakers.

Say in the calendar invite which device is recording. Two mics on one table create two files and an argument about which transcript is the record.

If you test both for a week, do not record the same allowed call twice. Hardware in the room one day, menu bar on a headset another. Otherwise you are comparing two microphones and thinking you compared two products.

After a test week, note four answers: Can you hear the remote side on the recording? Where does the audio live, on the Mac, on the device, or in a cloud, according to the vendor page? Can you delete the transcript without support? Does a colleague on Windows need the note without your file? Those answers beat recording the same allowed call twice on one day.

The Mac app import accepts MP3, M4A, WAV, AIFF, CAF, and FLAC. If Plaud only offers a closed format with no export in those types, the local path stays blocked. Check export options before you cancel a plan because you want local transcription.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Plaud software or hardware?

Hardware, with an app and a web side for transcription and summaries. A Plaud alternative that is only a Mac app solves a different problem: calls whose audio is already on the computer.

### Can Vemoir replace a Plaud Note in a meeting room?

Only for people the Mac can hear. It records the selected microphone and system audio. It does not ship as a wearable. If the laptop stays in a bag, use a device that is actually in the room.

### Does Plaud transcribe on the device?

Recording is on the device. Plaud’s product materials put AI transcription in the app and membership, with a monthly minute allowance and paid upgrades. Read the current privacy page for whether audio is uploaded and how long cloud copies remain.

### What if I will not upload the interview?

Export the audio you are allowed to keep and transcribe it locally. The [browser tool](/tools/transcribe-recording) covers short files without an upload and without speaker labels. The Mac app and MacWhisper cover longer files on the machine.

### Does a Plaud alternative still need consent?

Yes. Local software, a bot, and a recorder on the table are different visibility stories. None of them replaces telling people. Vemoir does not announce itself inside Zoom.

### How much does Vemoir cost next to a device plus a plan?

One Mac App Store purchase: CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US. You do not buy hardware. You also do not get a pocket recorder. Compare that to Plaud’s device price plus whatever minute plan you would pay, on [Plaud’s page](https://www.plaud.ai/products/plaud-note-ai-voice-recorder).

### Will speaker names appear automatically?

On the Mac app, speaker separation produces labels you can rename. Remembering a speaker is opt-in and local. Plaud’s materials describe speaker labels as something you work with in the app, often as a higher-tier feature. Confirm the current plan. The browser tool does not label speakers.

### Who should not switch?

Anyone whose work is away from a computer, anyone who wants one physical record button in the room, and anyone whose files already live in Plaud’s templates and minute pool. Switch the headset calls. Leave the room recorder if the room is the job.

For calls on a Mac, start at [capture](/#capture) or the [Mac App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794693630). For a short file from a recorder you already own, open [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording).
