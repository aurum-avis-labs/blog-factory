**AI notetaker pricing** falls into three shapes you can check on a vendor page: a seat, a usage meter, and a one-time purchase. The sticker price is the easy part. The part that changes the bill is the minute pool, the credit pack, and whether every teammate is a paid seat. Figures below are as of September 2026. Open the linked page before you put a number in a budget.

This is a comparison of published prices, not a ranking of transcription quality. Nobody here has a percentage that would survive a second test set.

## AI notetaker pricing by how the invoice is built

A **seat** means you pay for a person who can log in. Five people in the same workspace are five times the per-user rate, unless the vendor publishes a volume quote. That is Otter’s Pro and Business rows, Fireflies’ paid plans, and Granola Business and Enterprise.

A **usage** line sits beside the seat. It might be transcription minutes, imported files, storage, or a credit that spends when you ask the assistant for a custom summary. The seat can look cheap until the month you host long calls.

A **one-time** price is a purchase of software, usually on a store, with no seat and no renewal for the app. Vemoir’s Mac app is that shape. The amount is on the [pricing](/#pricing) section of the homepage. This article does not repeat it, because the useful question here is which shape you are buying.

Cloud notetakers also differ in whether a bot joins the call. Pricing does not tell you that. [Otter as an alternative](/blog/otter-ai-alternative) and [Fireflies as an alternative](/blog/fireflies-alternative) cover the join behavior. This page stays on the bill. How seats add up over twelve months is in [meeting notes subscription cost over a working year](/blog/meeting-notes-subscription-cost).

## Three billing models compared

| Model | What you buy | Typical invoice | Who it often fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seat | Login per person | Month or year times users | Team with a shared library |
| Usage | Minutes, imports, credits | Seat plus extras | Long calls, heavy summaries |
| One-time | Mac app on the store | Once, no seat | One person, audio stays local |

The table does not replace a pricing page. It sorts the question before you compare a number.

## Monthly price or annual charge on the invoice

Vendors often show a monthly rate and a lower annual rate beside it. The annual rate is usually prepayment for twelve months, not a forever fee. If you read $16.99 but pay yearly, find the "billed annually" row on the same page. If you need to cancel month to month, budget the higher monthly column.

Want capture without a bot on the Mac? Vemoir records system and microphone audio locally; see [how capture works](/#capture).

## Otter: a seat and a minute pool

Otter’s pricing page, checked September 2026, lists Pro at **$16.99 per user per month** and Business at **$30 per user per month** ([Otter pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing)). The same page shows lower figures for annual billing and for promotions marked as first-time or multi-month offers. Use the column that matches how you would actually pay. A blog that quotes only the annual equivalent will understate a month-to-month invoice.

On that page, Pro includes **1,200 in-app recording minutes** and **10 monthly audio or video file imports**. A per-conversation figure of **90 minutes** is shown on the Pro row. Business lists **unlimited meetings and in-app recordings**, unlimited file imports with a footnote, and a **4 hour** conversation figure. Those limits are part of the price. A freelancer who imports recordings can hit the import count before they hit the dollar amount.

Otter also publishes a student and teacher discount on Pro for `.edu` addresses. The pricing page states the discounted Pro annual and monthly amounts. If you do not have that address, ignore the footnote.

Enterprise is custom. Do not invent a fifth-person discount from the self-serve table.

## Fireflies: a seat, then AI credits

Fireflies’ own billing article lists the seat prices as of the page we checked in September 2026 ([billing terms](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/9606045468-fireflies-billing-terms)):

- Free: $0
- Pro: $10 per user per month billed annually, or $18 per user per month billed monthly
- Business: $19 per user per month billed annually, or $29 billed monthly
- Enterprise: $39 per user per month, billed annually

A seat is created when a teammate is added. Removing them reduces the count going forward and does not refund the current cycle. That rule is in the same article.

AI credits are a second price. The article says they can be bought monthly or annually, annual credit plans are priced lower, and unused purchased credits do not carry over. Business-tier notes on Fireflies’ help center say the Business price excludes AI credit charges for AskFred, AI Skills, custom summaries, and similar actions ([Business tier](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/2063312779-fireflies-business-tier-pricing-and-features)). If your team’s habit is to re-summarize every call, budget the credits, not only the seat.

Secondary write-ups disagree about how many storage minutes the free plan includes. This article does not pick a storage number that Fireflies’ billing table did not state in the price row. Read the current pricing page for storage.

## Granola: a seat with a free history cap

Granola’s pricing page lists three public tiers ([Granola pricing](https://www.granola.ai/pricing), September 2026):

- Basic: $0 per user per month, AI notes, and **limited meeting history**
- Business: $14 per user per month, unlimited notes and history, plus integrations
- Enterprise: $35 per user per month, with SSO and admin controls listed on the page

The free row is a real product with a history limit, not a timed trial the page calls a trial. The page does not, in the text checked for this article, publish an exact note count for that limit. "Limited" is the claim this article repeats. Third-party articles sometimes name a count. Use Granola's page if you need the current cap.

Business at $14 is a seat. Four people are $56 a month if every person is on Business. There is no minute price on that page to quote beside it. The tradeoff is product fit, which [the Granola comparison](/blog/granola-alternative) treats separately from the invoice.

## One-time purchase as the third shape

A one-time Mac app does not bill a seat next March. You pay the store once, you install models on the Mac, and the meeting record stays on that computer unless you export it or opt into a cloud text model with your own key. Live recording, speaker separation, and the notes workflow are in the app, not in the free browser tools.

The free tools on vemoir.ch are a different product. They transcribe a file you already have, in the browser, up to **15 minutes**, with **3 successful runs a week**. They do not record the live call and they do not label speakers. Use them when the clip is short. Use the Mac app, or a seat-based notetaker, when the call is the whole morning.

Pricing for the Mac app is one-time and listed on [pricing](/#pricing). Requirements: Apple silicon (M1 or newer), macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, at least 8 GB of memory and 10 GB of free storage. 16 GB is recommended for long meetings. Intel Macs are not supported.

If you want the seat-versus-one-time numbers beside each other on the product site, start at [pricing](/#pricing). The rest of this page is how the other vendors build their invoices.

## Where a seat plan is the better choice

Buy the seat when several people must search the same calls, an admin must turn a leaver’s access off, or a CRM integration is the point of the notes. A laptop app does not replace a team workspace. Fireflies and Otter document those team features because that is the product they sell.

Stay off the seat when you are one consultant, the client forbids a bot, and you already have the audio on the Mac. Paying per month so a vendor can host a file you did not need in their cloud is a choice, not a requirement. [Meeting notes without a bot](/blog/meeting-notes-without-a-bot) is the longer version of that constraint.

Do not mistake a lower annual price for privacy. Annual billing changes when you pay. It does not change where the audio is processed. For that, read the privacy policy, for example [whether Otter fits your file rules](/blog/is-otter-ai-safe).

Count the seat for the person who reads the notes later, not only for the one who records. If read access is tied to a login, every reader can be a seat, even if they never press record. Ask the billing page whether a read-only account counts. If it does, that number goes into the annual budget, not into a note about "power users only." A login for a client at another company is often a seat nobody put in the project budget.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do two AI notetaker pricing pages disagree on the annual price?

Vendors show a monthly rate, an annual rate, and sometimes a promotion. Quote the row that matches your checkout. Dates matter. These notes are as of September 2026.

### Is Otter Pro $16.99 or the smaller annual number?

The pricing page shows both: $16.99 per user per month and a lower annual figure, plus promotional footnotes. Business is listed at $30 per user per month with its own annual and promotional figures ([Otter pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing)).

### Does Fireflies’ seat include every AI feature?

The billing article separates AI credits from the seat. Unused purchased credits do not roll over. Check which buttons spend a credit on your plan.

### How much is Granola Business?

$14 per user per month on [Granola’s pricing page](https://www.granola.ai/pricing), as of September 2026. Enterprise is listed at $35 per user per month. Basic is $0 with limited history.

### Is a free plan enough for client work?

Use it to learn the product. Do not assume history, imports, or minutes will hold a year of client decisions. Granola says history is limited on Basic. Otter meters minutes and imports on the paid Pro row, and the free row has its own caps on the same page.

### Does a one-time app have a seat count?

No. The Mac app is one purchase, not a per-user renewal. The people who can open a given meeting are the people who have the exported note you chose to share, not a seat you added in a billing console.

### Where is the price of Vemoir?

On the homepage [pricing](/#pricing) section, and in the Mac App Store listing. This interest article describes the shape. It does not paste the amount.

### What if the page changes next month?

Believe the vendor page dated the day you buy. Keep a screenshot in the procurement file if finance will ask later.

### How do I add up a full year of subscription cost?

Multiply seats by months and add credits. The walkthrough is in [meeting notes subscription cost over a working year](/blog/meeting-notes-subscription-cost).
