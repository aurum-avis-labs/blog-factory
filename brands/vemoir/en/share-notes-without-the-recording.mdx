You share meeting notes without the recording when the other person needs the decision and the owner, and does not need the audio. The recording is the evidence and the risk. The notes are the page. Sending both "just in case" expands the audience of the file you were careful about. Send the page. Keep the file where you already decided it should live.

This is a sharing choice, not a claim that notes are a legal substitute for a tape. Consent and retention stay with you. Nothing here makes a recording lawful.

## Share meeting notes without the recording

A recording is the microphone track, the system-audio track, or both, plus whatever a meeting vendor stored in their cloud. A transcript is the words. Notes are the short record: summary, decisions, actions, open questions. Those are different objects. You can copy one without copying the others.


People ask for "the notes" and receive a link to a Zoom cloud recording because that was the easiest thing in the chat. The recording includes the aside, the wrong number, and every other client's name that slipped out. If the request was "what did we decide," the notes answer it. If the request was "I need the exact sentence," then a transcript, still not the whole video, may be the right scope. Ask which one they meant before you attach a file.

On the Mac, sharing is contextual. From a meeting you can copy or share the summary, the actions, the decisions, or the transcript, depending on what you are looking at. You do not have to share the audio with the text. Export and share are described on [exports](/#exports). To share outcomes without attaching a file, copy only the section you mean and leave the recording in the meeting storage. macOS sharing hands that part to the app you pick. It does not upload Vemoir's library to a team workspace. There is no shared team room in the app. If a colleague needs the notes, you send the notes.

## A page that stands without the audio

Write the notes so a person who will never hear the call can act.


- Date, and who was there if that matters for the decision.
- The decision in one or two sentences, including a condition if there was one.
- Actions with one owner and a due date.
- Open questions, unmarked as actions.
- Nothing that only makes sense if you play minute 36.

The [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) is that page in the browser: you type it, you download Markdown, and no recording is attached because you never gave it a recording. A draft from a transcript you already have can come from [meeting notes from a transcript](/tools/meeting-notes-from-transcript). Strip the follow-up email down to the keepers before you send it. Do not paste the VTT underneath "for context."

If the only clean copy of the outcome is inside a vendor's recap that also embeds the player, copy the text out. Do not forward the player link and hope they will not press play.

## What to leave on the machine

Leave the audio. Also leave, unless someone with a reason asked:

- The full transcript, when the notes already carry the decision and the reader is not checking a quote.
- Other people's names from a different project that the model or the captioner left in.
- Your private notes typed during the call, if they were for you and not for the room. On the Mac those notes stay marked as yours. Fold them into the minutes only when you mean to. They are not the same thing as a quote.
- A voice profile or any speaker memory. That is not an export for a colleague.

Storage on the Mac is a separate switch from sharing. You can keep the audio for yourself, or drop it after processing, and still copy the summary. Deleting the audio later does not recall a file you already emailed. Decide the audience before you press share.

Apple Notes export writes summary, decisions, actions, and generated documents into a note, not the audio file. Pick the folder on purpose, especially if it is shared.

## How to send it

1. Read the notes against the transcript if you have one. Cut any owner or date the room did not accept.
2. Copy the summary, the actions, or the decisions. On a Mac that is the toolbar copy or share for the section you are in, or a Markdown file from the minutes sheet.
3. Paste into the mail, the ticket, or the doc the reader already uses. Name the meeting and the date in the first line so the note is not an orphan paragraph.
4. Do not attach `.m4a`, `.mp4`, or a cloud recording URL in the same message unless the recipient asked for the recording and is allowed to have it.
5. If they reply "can I hear the part about the date," send the sentence, or a short transcript excerpt, not the two-hour file. If you truly must send audio, send the smallest part you can and say what it is.

A browser cleaner only makes a VTT readable. The readable text is still a transcript. Treat it as evidence you might quote, not as the attachment default.

## When they really do need the recording

Sometimes the notes are the wrong object. A dispute about wording, a research transcript, a journalist's interview, a quote you might publish: the words are the work. In those cases you share a transcript under whatever consent you already have, and you still do not need the video of everyone's face. Interview files that should not be uploaded are covered in [transcribe an interview without uploading](/blog/transcribe-interview-without-upload).

A client who says "send the recording" may mean "send something I can skim." Offer the notes first. If they insist on audio, you are back in a consent and retention decision, not a button decision.

Local capture does not remove the vendor's copy. If the meeting was also recorded in Zoom or Teams, that cloud file remains until the host deletes it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I share notes without the recording from a Teams or Zoom recap?

You can copy the text out of the recap and send that. Forwarding the recap link usually forwards access to the recording or the transcript the vendor stored. If you want only the notes, paste the notes into your own message.

### Does copying the summary include the audio?

No. Copy and share on the Mac follow the section you are in: summary, actions, decisions, or transcript. The audio stays in the meeting's storage unless you export that file yourself. Check the message before you send it if you also dragged a file in.

### What if the notes are wrong without the tape?

Fix the notes before you share them, using the transcript as the check. If you cannot defend a line, cut it. Do not attach the whole recording so the reader can "verify" a sentence you did not read.

### Can I put the notes in Apple Notes and leave the audio out?

Yes. The Apple Notes export is the written record, not the audio. A later export updates the same note instead of making a second one. Confirm the folder, especially if that folder is shared.

### Should absent teammates get the transcript by default?

No. Give them the decisions and the actions. Offer the transcript if they need a quote or they were supposed to be in the room and will actually review it. A default of "everyone gets the file" is how a side comment becomes a document.

### Is a follow-up email enough?

It is enough when it carries the decision, the owners, and the dates, and it does not contradict the notes you filed. It is not enough when it says "as discussed" and attaches the recording.

### Do browser tools upload the notes I paste?

The minutes sheet is a form in the tab. The notes tool drafts from text you paste in the tab. Neither page shares a file with your team. You copy the result out yourself.

### What about people who were recorded and were not asked?

Do not share further. Fix the consent problem before you distribute notes or audio. This page is not a workaround and not legal advice.
