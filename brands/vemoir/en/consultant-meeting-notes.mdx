**Consultant meeting notes** are the page you send after the call: what the client decided, what is still open, and who does the next piece of work. They are not the recording, and they are not a transcript with every aside about budget politics. Clients remember the follow-up email. They rarely ask for the audio unless something later conflicts.

If you already have a transcript, [meeting notes from a transcript](/tools/meeting-notes-from-transcript) is the drafting surface. You still edit before you send.

## What the client actually needs in the note

Write so a person who missed the call can act. A useful consultant note has:


- Date, attendees, and the project name the client uses on the invoice, not your internal code alone.
- Decisions, each in one or two sentences, with the option that was rejected if they might reopen it.
- Actions with a name and a date. “The team will look into it” is not an action.
- Open questions that would block the next workshop.
- What you explicitly did not decide.

Leave out the play-by-play and the joke at minute twelve. Leave out another client’s example if it was only an analogy and could identify them. Leave out your internal margin comment. The note is a client document the moment you send it.

If you bill from the meeting, the commercial terms live in the engagement letter. You can keep an internal line on how long the call ran. The [meeting cost calculator](/tools/meeting-cost) turns headcount, minutes, and hourly rates into one franc sentence and does not upload audio. Put that sentence in the internal note if it helps. Do not surprise the client with a cost line they did not expect in a recap.

## Consultant meeting notes from a call you recorded

Recording is fine when the client knows. Say it at the start. A bot in the participant list is obvious and often unwelcome on a client call. [Meeting notes without a bot](/blog/meeting-notes-without-a-bot) is the longer version. A local recording on your Mac hears the meeting app and your microphone, and no extra guest appears. You still have to say you are recording. The software will not say it for you.


After the call:

1. If you only need one passage and the file is under 15 minutes, transcribe in the browser. The file is not uploaded. You get no speaker labels. Three successful runs a week.
2. For the whole workshop, use a local transcript so you are not slicing the call into weekly allowances.
3. Draft the note from the transcript with [notes from a transcript](/tools/meeting-notes-from-transcript), or from the minutes you typed live. Live notes you trust beat a transcript you will not read.
4. Check every name, date, and figure against what you heard. Then send the note, not the raw text.

Swiss German will tend to show up as Standard German. Your client in Zurich does not want their recap to “correct” their wording into a dialect transcript anyway. They want the decision spelled in standard language. Verify numbers. Those fail quietly.

Scope changes belong in a sentence the project manager can see: “Client asked to add the French entity. Not in the current statement of work. We will send a change note.” If you bury that in a narrative paragraph, you will do the work and argue the invoice later.

## Share the note, hold the audio

Send the note through the channel the engagement already uses. Attach the audio only when the client asked for it or the contract says they receive recordings. An audio file in a CC’d thread is a second distribution you cannot pull back from someone else’s inbox.

On the Mac, share through the system share sheet and pick the note. Exports are a choice, not a default upload of every meeting. A cloud text model is opt-in and text-only if you use your own key. Leave it off when the client’s vendor list does not include that provider. Audio and speaker separation stay on the machine either way.

Redact before you send. Another workstream’s name, a candidate’s salary that came up as an example, a throwaway medical comment: cut them. The transcript contains them. The email should not.

If several consultants were on the call, agree who sends the note. Two recaps that disagree are worse than a late one. The second email should say “addition,” not silently contradict the first.

[Export and share](/#exports) on the homepage is the product description of choosing what leaves the Mac. The client-facing habit is older than the app: one note, one owner, no surprise attachment.

Workshops with a whiteboard produce decisions the transcript never heard, because someone pointed. Photograph the board only if the client agreed, and type the decision into the note in words. A photo without a sentence is not an action. The note should stand alone for a colleague who cannot see the photo.

If you promised a recap within a day, the draft you send can say “unconfirmed” on one figure and still be on time. Waiting for a perfect transcript until Thursday violates the habit the client hired. Send the decisions you are sure of. Follow with a correction in the same thread, not in a new thread nobody links.

## Where a team workspace is the better system

A shared workspace wins when five consultants must see the same client history and people join and leave the account. One laptop’s archive fails the day that person is on holiday. Pay for the shared system when that is the job. A seat-based notetaker is a reasonable part of that system if the client has agreed to the vendor. It is an unreasonable part if the client banned extra tools and you joined one anyway.

A CRM coaching product is the better fit when the point of the call is a sales scorecard and a pipeline field. Project meeting notes are a different artifact. Do not force a pipeline tool to be your workshop recap, and do not force a recap to update a forecast it does not understand.

Slideware is the better deliverable when the client paid for a readout, not for minutes. The notes feed the slides. They are not a substitute when the contract says “workshop document.”

Handwritten notes still win when you cannot get a clean recording, the room is loud, or the client declined. Build the recap from what you wrote down. Do not reconstruct a confident transcript from memory and label it as the record.

Put the project name the client uses in the subject line of the recap, not only your internal code.

If the recap slips to the next morning, say so in the first line instead of backdating the decisions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I attach the recording to the recap?

Only if the client expects it. Default to the note. The audio stays with you under whatever retention the engagement states.

### Can I draft consultant meeting notes from a transcript in the browser?

Yes, if you already have text. [Notes from a transcript](/tools/meeting-notes-from-transcript) does not upload a new requirement of its own beyond the text you paste. The audio transcription tools, if you still need them, cap files at 15 minutes and three successful runs a week and do not label speakers.

### Will a bot look more professional?

It will look like a bot. Many clients have asked consultants to remove them. Ask before you add one. Local capture does not add a guest, and you still disclose it.

### What if two people send notes?

Pick one sender before the call ends. Conflicting recaps create a scope argument.

### Does the note replace the statement of work?

No. A scope change in the notes is a flag to update the commercial document. It is not the commercial document. Send the change note as its own page, with the date of the call that triggered it.

### Can I keep Swiss client audio in a foreign cloud notetaker?

Only if the contract and the client allow that vendor. Otherwise keep the recording local and send the note.

### How do I handle a figure I am unsure about?

Mark it as unconfirmed or check the audio before you send. Do not let a fluent sentence invent a number.

### Is there a price in this article?

No. This is the workflow. Store pricing sits on the product page when you are choosing software. Typing the note by hand follows the same habit.
