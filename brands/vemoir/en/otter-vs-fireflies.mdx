**Otter vs Fireflies** is a choice between two cloud meeting products that can put a notetaker in the call and keep the library on their servers. They are not the same on training, and they are not a local Mac recorder. This page uses their own documents as of September 2026. Prices move. Follow the pricing links before you buy a seat.

If the requirement is that audio stays on the Mac, neither is the tool. Vemoir is a one-time Mac App Store purchase (CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US) that records microphone and system audio without a bot. That is a third option. It does not compete with Otter or Fireflies on team features.

## What both products are

Otter is a meeting assistant built around a calendar notetaker for Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and Google Meet, with transcripts, summaries, and team sharing on paid plans. Fireflies joins meetings as a notetaker, stores recordings and transcripts in its workspace, and sells per-seat plans plus a free tier. Both assume the conversation will be processed in their cloud. Both can be the right buy when the team wants one searchable library and has accepted that shape.


A wider list sits in [Otter alternatives](/blog/otter-ai-alternative) and [Fireflies alternatives](/blog/fireflies-alternative). The privacy reading of Otter's own pages is in [is Otter.ai safe](/blog/is-otter-ai-safe). This article is the head-to-head.

## Otter vs Fireflies on storage and training

Otter's privacy policy describes meeting audio and transcripts processed to provide the service, and names cloud providers including Amazon Web Services in the United States. The privacy and security pages describe AWS storage with server-side encryption. They also describe training proprietary AI on de-identified audio and transcriptions. A more careful walk through those sentences, with the help-center links, is the Otter safety article above. Do not compress it to "Otter never trains." Their pages distinguish de-identified training of Otter's models from a claim about third-party model providers.


Fireflies' privacy policy says it does not use personal information for AI model training and that it contractually prohibits vendors from using that information for their own model training. A Zero Data Retention section says meeting content (audio, video, transcripts, summaries) is not stored by a third-party vendor after processing, is not accessed by that vendor once the service is done, and is not used to train internal or external AI models ([privacy policy](https://fireflies.ai/privacy-policy), [security](https://fireflies.ai/security), checked September 2026). Its storage article says the default public cloud is in the United States, on AWS and Google Cloud, with private storage and bring-your-own storage described for Enterprise ([data storage and transfer](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/9596505232-learn-about-data-storage-and-transfer)).

So the practical split, as of those pages: both default to US cloud. Fireflies states a harder "we do not train, and vendors do not train" line. Otter documents de-identified training of its own models. If that difference is why you are comparing them, read the policies again rather than this summary, because the wording is the product.

Fireflies also describes SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and HIPAA on its security pages. Those are Fireflies' claims about Fireflies. They are not a certification of your meeting, and they are not claims about any other app.

## Bots and who sees them

Otter documents a visible notetaker. It does not join anonymously. Fireflies documents a notetaker that joins the meeting, and has also described a Chrome extension as a capture path for some Google Meet calls. Check the current pricing and help pages before you assume every Fireflies plan uses a bot or that any plan can do without one. A bot is visible. That is useful when you want everyone to see that notes are being taken. It is a problem when the invite says no third-party notetakers.

Neither vendor's bot is the same as recording system audio on your Mac. The people on the call see the bot. They do not see a menu-bar recorder. Whether they consent is still your responsibility either way.

## Where Otter is the better choice

Otter is the better choice when you want its real-time collaborative transcript, its mobile apps, and a workspace your team already knows, and you have read the de-identified training language and accepted it or chosen an enterprise agreement that changes the terms. Otter is also the better choice when what matters is a simple bot your colleagues already have accounts for. Migrating a year of Otter conversations to Fireflies only to chase a training sentence is a poor trade if the Enterprise paperwork already covers your Otter use.

Otter is the worse choice when the training sentence is a hard no and Fireflies' policy matches the no more closely, or when a bot is banned. Do not pick Otter because a roundup called it private. Its own pages describe a US cloud product with a visible notetaker.

## Where Fireflies is the better choice

Fireflies is the better choice when you want the training language in its current privacy policy, conversation search across a team, and admin options such as private storage on Enterprise. It is the better choice when CRM-style workflows and a bot the company already approved are the job. It is the worse choice when you need the recording to remain on one Mac, when Enterprise private storage is outside your budget and default US hosting is not allowed, or when the meeting cannot show a guest.

Seat price is on [Fireflies pricing](https://fireflies.ai/pricing). Confirm it. A one-time Mac app does not include their team search.

## Where neither is the better choice

Neither Otter nor Fireflies is the better choice when the audio must not be uploaded. Record on the Mac. The App Store app does that without a bot. System Audio Recording takes the call audio. It does not record the screen. Speech models stay on device: Parakeet v3 or Whisper. Writing models stay on device unless you activate a text-only key. There is no silent cloud transcript if a local model is missing. You download the model, or you do not transcribe locally.

For one short file you already exported, [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) stays in the browser tab, 15 minutes, three successful runs a week, no speaker labels. It will not build an Otter or Fireflies workspace.

A broader privacy list is in [best private meeting notes](/blog/best-private-meeting-notes). Use it when the question has already left "which cloud seat" and become "which design keeps the file here."

Mac requirements if you take that path: Apple silicon, macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, 8 GB of memory, 10 GB free. Intel Macs, Windows, and phones are not that app. If the team is on Windows, stay with Otter or Fireflies and do the policy work there. A local recording still helps the one person on a Mac who is allowed to keep a private copy, as long as their contract permits a second copy. It does not replace the system of record the company already chose.

## A seat-by-seat check before you switch

List the last ten meetings, not the last marketing email. How many had a bot that nobody questioned? How many were external, with a client who would have stopped the call if Otter or Fireflies had appeared? The internal majority can stay on the cloud seat. The external minority is where Otter vs Fireflies stops being the right question and a local recorder becomes the better tool for that hour only.

Then open both pricing pages and write down the seat count you would actually pay, not the free tier you hope will hold. Free tiers are for trying the product. A team that records every day will hit limits or a checkout page. Put that number next to the one-time Mac price only if the Mac path replaces seats. If it does not replace seats, you will pay both, and you should budget both.

Finally, delete a test recording in each product and see what the help article says about trash, backups, and the delay. Otter's documented trash window and Fireflies' account deletion language are not identical. The person who owns retention in your company should do this once, on a test account, before the tool is on a client calendar.

## Questions to send the vendor, not the blog

If a security review is open, send these to the vendor and keep the reply. A blog cannot close a review.

1. Which subprocessors see audio, and for how long? Fireflies describes a zero-retention rule for meeting content at vendors. Otter describes its own de-identified training path. Ask each account team which plan you are on, because enterprise paperwork can differ from the public policy.
2. Where is the primary store, and is there a region option on the plan you will buy? Both public pages described here default to the United States. Fireflies documents private storage on Enterprise. Do not assume a lower tier inherited it.
3. What does delete do to backups, and on what clock? Run the test mentioned above and match it to the help article, not to a sales call.
4. Can an admin stop auto-join for one external domain? If the answer is no, your bot problem is operational, and Otter vs Fireflies will not fix the client invite by itself.
5. Who inside the company can open the recording by default? Visibility defaults matter as much as the vendor's training sentence. A private-to-you library and a team-visible library are different products wearing the same logo.

Write the answers next to the plan name and the date. September 2026 is when the public pages for this article were read. A reply from the vendor in a later month replaces the blog.

## What you still do on the Mac either way

Otter vs Fireflies does not change the Mac underneath the call. Close other apps that play audio if you are also testing a local recorder the same day, or the local take will include a podcast you forgot. Headphones keep your microphone from swallowing their bot's playback twice. If you run a local recording beside either bot, tell the room. Two copies are two retention problems. Decide which copy is the one you will delete at the end of the month, and put that decision in the same note as the vendor answers above.

Speaker names inside Otter or Fireflies come from their cloud pipelines. They are not the anonymous Mac clusters you rename yourself, and they are not the speaker profiles you manage on your own Mac. Do not describe them as a local voice profile. If a name is wrong in their transcript, fix it in their product, using their help article, and do not assume the fix trained or untrained a model. Their training sentences are the policies you already read, not a side effect of editing a label.

When the review is done, write one line: which tool is allowed for internal calls, which tool is forbidden on client calls, and who turns auto-join off. Without that line, Otter vs Fireflies was a reading exercise. The calendar will keep joining the meeting you meant to keep local.

Match paid seats to the headcount that actually uses the cloud library. Remove spare logins the week you write the rule, and date the note so next year's review does not restart the same thread.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Otter or Fireflies more private?

Read their pages. As of September 2026, Fireflies says it does not train on personal information and forbids vendors from training on it. Otter documents de-identified training of its own models. Both describe US cloud storage by default.

### Do both use a bot?

Both document a notetaker that can join the meeting. Otter says it cannot join anonymously. Confirm Fireflies' current Meet options on their site before you assume a bot-free path.

### Which is cheaper?

Both use free tiers and paid seats. The numbers are on their pricing pages and change. A local Mac app is a one-time purchase, not a seat, and it does not copy their team library.

### Can I keep using one of them for internal calls only?

Yes. Many teams do. Turn the bot off for the external invite that bans it, and use a local recording for that call.

### Does a SOC 2 badge settle the choice?

No. It is a statement about the vendor's program. Your client contract can still forbid US hosting or guest bots.

### Will either app run the transcript only on my Mac?

Not as their default design. Their value is the cloud library. Local transcription is a different product.

### What if I already have an Otter export?

A file you downloaded can be transcribed locally or in the browser tool, within the browser's limits. The history inside Otter stays in Otter until you delete it there.

### Is this legal advice?

No. Consent and retention are yours.

If the team library is the point, open the Fireflies and Otter policy links in this article and pick the training sentence you can defend. If the file should stay on the Mac, the install is the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794693630) and the capture map is [here](/#capture).
