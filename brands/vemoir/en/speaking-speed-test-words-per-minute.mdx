A speaking speed test in words per minute tells you how fast you are talking, not how good the update is. Open the [speaking speed test](/tools/speaking-speed-test), pick a mode, and get a pace you can rehearse against. Read-aloud times a fixed passage with the mic. The calculator turns words and duration into words per minute, or plans how long a slot will run. Timed file uses SRT or VTT cue times for one overall pace. Nothing is uploaded, and no speech model runs.

Treat the number as a rehearsal aid. It is not a grade, and it is not talk-time by person.

## How to run a speaking speed test (words per minute)

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Pick a mode</h3><p>Read aloud to rehearse, Calculator to plan a slot, or Timed file for an SRT or VTT export.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Run the check</h3><p>Start the mic, enter words and time, or paste a caption file with real cue times.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Use the result</h3><p>Read the pace, the band, and the planning table for one, three, five and ten minutes.</p></li>
</ol>

Words per minute is words divided by speaking time in minutes. In read-aloud mode, speaking time runs from the first detected speech to the last, including short pauses. The page does not check that you read every word, and it does not store or play back your voice after you finish.

## Planning a pitch or a short update

In the calculator, pick a target pace to see duration or how many words fit. Rough planning numbers many teams already use:

| Pace | About 5 minutes |
|------|-----------------|
| 120 wpm | about 600 words |
| 150 wpm | about 750 words |
| 180 wpm | about 900 words |

Use your own text in the calculator instead of forcing these numbers into a script that does not fit your voice.

## What this tool does not do

It does not split pace by speaker. Speaker names in a caption file are ignored. For share of words by name after the transcript already labels people, use [who spoke most in a meeting](/blog/who-spoke-most-in-meeting).

It does not replace a mic check. If the input is wrong, fix that first with the [microphone test](/tools/microphone-test). Guide: [test your microphone online before a call](/blog/test-microphone-online-before-call).

It does not invent Swiss German dialect spelling, and it does not claim a certified speaking rate. The bands on the page are starting points for calm, conversational and fast delivery.

## When a timed file is the better path

Meeting notes without timestamps belong in the calculator. An SRT or VTT with real cue times belongs in Timed file. After you strip scaffolding from a Zoom or Teams export, you can still keep a timed copy for pace. Cleaning VTT to plain text is a different job; see [Zoom VTT to plain text](/blog/zoom-vtt-to-plain-text) when you need readable notes instead of pace.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is speaking pace measured?

Words per minute is words divided by speaking time in minutes. Read-aloud timing starts at the first detected speech and ends at the last.

### Is my voice uploaded or stored?

No. The microphone is used only while you read. The audio stays in this tab and is released when you finish.

### What is a good speaking pace?

It depends on language and context. The bands on the page are starting points, not a grade.

### Why is my pace lower in German than in English?

German often uses longer compound words. The same speaking speed can produce fewer words per minute than in English.

### Can I measure pace from a meeting transcript?

Yes, when you have an SRT or VTT with real cue times. Notes without timestamps belong in the calculator. This page does not split pace by speaker.

### Does this use a free transcription run?

No. The speaking speed test does not download Whisper or spend one of the three successful model runs in the rolling 7-week window.

### Does this work on a phone?

Yes in a current mobile browser, if the browser allows microphone access for the read-aloud tab.

### Is this HIPAA or GDPR certified?

No. It is a browser calculator and rehearsal aid. Vemoir does not claim compliance certification for this page.
