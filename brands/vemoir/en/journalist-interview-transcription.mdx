**Journalist interview transcription** is how a recording becomes a draft you can quote from, search, and check. It is not a publish button. Off the record stays off the record because you keep it off the page, not because a model noticed the phrase. The file should live where you decided it would live before the interview, which for a lot of sensitive work is the computer in front of you and nowhere else.

The browser path is [transcribe an interview](/tools/transcribe-interview): file not uploaded, **15 minutes**, **3 successful runs a week**, no speaker labels.

## Agree the terms before you need the text

On the record, on background, and off the record are agreements with a person, not modes in an app. Write them down in your notes with a timestamp if the status changes mid-interview. A transcript of the whole file will not color-code those stretches for you. If you recorded after they said the next part was off the record, that stretch does not become publishable because it is now text.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Agree on recording and quote use before you press record.</li>
  <li>Keep the raw audio until the story is filed.</li>
  <li>Mark uncertain words instead of guessing in print.</li>
</ul>


Tell people you are recording when the law or your newsroom requires it, and when you would want to be told. A menu-bar app on a call does not inject a disclosure into the other person’s phone. Say the sentence.

Source protection is your newsroom’s practice: who can open the folder, whether the laptop is encrypted, what happens if you are searched, how you reply to a legal demand. Local transcription reduces one copy (a vendor’s servers). It does not reduce the copies you make yourself by emailing the transcript to a colleague or dropping it in a synced Desktop folder. If Desktop is in iCloud, the “local” file is not only local. Move sensitive interviews to a disk your newsroom actually chose.

This is not legal advice, and it is not a claim that a workflow complies with the nDSG or with shield laws. Those are questions for your editor and counsel.

## Journalist interview transcription without an upload

1. Save the audio as MP3, M4A, WAV, or MP4. Phone voice memos often need an export step. Take that step on a machine you trust.
2. For a slice of 15 minutes or less, open [transcribe an interview](/tools/transcribe-interview). The model downloads into the browser. The audio is not posted to Vemoir. You can confirm that in the Network panel. Three successful runs a week means a long reporting day of many short calls will not all transcribe on the site. Rank the interviews. Do the one you will quote first.
3. Longer interviews belong on a local model or a Mac app that imports the file. The browser cap will not stretch because the story is important.
4. There are no speaker labels in the browser output. A two-person interview is usually recoverable by hand. A group around a table is not. Mark speakers as you listen, or use a local diarization path and still check every name before publication.
5. Keep the audio. The text is for navigation. The quote you print should match the recording, including a pause or a false start you decide to clean with an ellipsis your style guide allows. Do not clean meaning.

The same no-upload file workflow is spelled out in [transcribe an interview without upload](/blog/transcribe-interview-without-upload). Use it as the checklist. This article is the newsroom version of what the text is for.

For long tapes, live capture from system audio, and speaker separation on the Mac, use [Vemoir](/#capture). No bot joins the call.

Swiss German and other dialect speech will usually appear as Standard German from the public Whisper models. Do not publish a dialect quote from that text as if the person used textbook spelling. Listen, and follow your newsroom’s rule on dialect. If you write in French or Italian, the transcript language follows the model you run, and you still edit.

A live call on the Mac can be recorded from system audio and the microphone without a bot in the participant list. That is a different step from transcribing a file you already have. Phone interviews where the audio is only on the handset will not show up in system audio. Use a recorder that actually hears the call, or put the phone on speaker into a microphone and accept the room noise.

## Quotes, dialect, and the sentence you almost used

Pull quotes into a note with the timestamp. A timestamp is how you get back to the audio in a hurry when an editor asks “did they really say that?”

Check names, titles, and numbers against a second source when the claim matters. Transcription is worse at proper names than at ordinary words. A confident misspelling reads as a fact on a page.

Do not feed an off-the-record passage into a shared document “so the team has context” unless the team is inside the agreement you made. Context is how off the record leaks. Cut the file, or lock the folder.

If you use a cloud text model to tidy a draft, you have sent the text to that provider. Leave that off for sensitive interviews. Local notes do not need a polish pass that exports the source’s words.

Background agreements sometimes allow use of the information without the name. Your transcript will still contain the name. The published sentence is a separate document you write. Do not paste the transcript into the CMS.

Remote interviews on a laptop are the opposite case. System audio hears the guest. Your microphone hears you. If you take the call on a phone and only use the laptop for notes, the guest’s voice never hits the Mac. Say which device actually recorded, in your own log, so a colleague does not hunt for a file that was never on the machine you handed them.

Long investigations produce many files with similar names. Put the date and the source’s initials you are allowed to store in the filename before you transcribe. A folder of `audio.m4a` copies is how quotes get attributed to the wrong person. The model will not rescue the filename.

## Where a field recorder is the better tool

A recorder in a cafe, a rally, or a car is the right device when there is no Mac and the interview is in person. Vemoir does not turn a laptop lid into a field kit, and it does not offer an in-person mode to describe. If you later have a file in a supported format, you can transcribe it. The recording itself had to happen on a device that was there.

A newsroom system that several producers can log into is better when the story is a team production and the sensitive-source problem does not apply. One laptop is a bottleneck and a single point of loss. Sync the transcript on purpose. Do not find out later that it synced because Documents sits in the cloud.

Automatic captions in a video editor are better when the publishable object is the video and the transcript is a caption file. Meeting-style minutes are the wrong shape for a broadcast script. Use the tool that outputs the format your edit bay expects.

Otter-style cloud notetakers are a reasonable speed tool when the source consented to that vendor and the story is not sensitive. They are a bad tool when the source agreed to talk because you said the tape stays with you. Do not quietly substitute a cloud account.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is journalist interview transcription the same as publishing?

No. It is a working draft. Publication is an editorial decision, and off the record is not a setting in the file.

### Does the interview tool upload the recording?

No. Up to 15 minutes, three successful runs a week. Longer tapes need a local path.

### Will it identify the second speaker?

No. Browser output has no speaker labels. Add them from your notes or from a careful listen.

### Can I record a phone call on speaker?

Only if a microphone or a recorder hears it. System audio on the Mac does not capture a handset held to your ear.

### What about Swiss German quotes?

The browser model tends to write Standard German. Verify dialect quotes against the audio. Do not treat the transcript as the speaker’s spelling.

### Should I email the transcript to my editor?

Only under the agreement you made with the source and your newsroom’s file rules. Email creates another copy.

### Is there a certified transcript for court?

No. This is not a certified transcript. If you need one for a proceeding, that is a different service with its own rules.

### Where do I start with a short file?

[Transcribe an interview](/tools/transcribe-interview), then check every quote you intend to use against the audio.

### How is this different from qualitative research transcription?

Newsroom work centers on **quotes, off the record, and publication**. Study work centers on **coding, pseudonyms, and the ethics protocol**. The same browser cap applies. The file rules differ. For the research path, see [qualitative research transcription](/blog/qualitative-research-transcription).
