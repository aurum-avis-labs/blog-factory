**How to take meeting minutes** is a practice you run in the room, not a document you decorate afterwards. You listen for what changed, you write a short dated record, and you leave the word-for-word talk in the transcript if you have one. Minutes that read like a novel do not get used. Minutes that name decisions, owners, and open questions do.

## How to take meeting minutes in the room

Treat the minutes as a filter. Most of the meeting is context. A few lines are the record: who was there, what was decided (including what was refused), which tasks have one owner and a date, and which questions stayed open.


If you try to capture every sentence, you will miss the decision. If you wait until tomorrow and write from memory, you will keep the loud topics and drop the quiet ones. The useful habit is in between: a sparse page during the call, cleaned the same afternoon.

You do not need special software to do this. A notes app and a shared agenda are enough. A [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) gives you the same sections in the browser so you are not inventing headings under pressure. It does not record the room and it does not transcribe audio.

## Before the sitting

Do this in ten minutes, before anyone joins:


1. **Put the skeleton on the page.** Title, date and time with timezone, place or link, facilitator, minute-taker. Paste the agenda with a timebox on each item. Leave empty rows for decisions and actions.
2. **Name the official copy.** If this sitting will be recorded, write where that recording will live (Teams OneDrive, a local folder, nowhere). One official copy beats three surprise ones.
3. **Agree who writes.** The facilitator should not also be the only minute-taker on a hard call. If you are both, say so, and keep the notes even shorter.
4. **Know the audience.** A weekly team sync can be blunt. A Verwaltungsrat sitting or a client call needs names, roles, and language the absentees can use without a recap call.

Open the invite. Copy attendees. Mark apologies before the call starts so you are not typing names during the first agenda item.

## What to write while people talk

Do not transcribe. Listen for four shapes:

**A decision.** Someone closed a choice. Write the choice in one sentence, including the condition if there was one. "We go with vendor A if legal signs by 12 October" is a decision. "We had a good discussion about vendors" is not.

**A refusal.** The room said no, or not yet. Write that. Future-you will otherwise relitigate it.

**An action.** A person accepted work that happens after the meeting. Write one owner, one outcome, one calendar day. "We should follow up" is not an action. If nobody accepted, it is an open question.

**An open question.** The room left a gap. Write the question and, if possible, who will resolve it. Do not invent an owner to make the page look tidy.

Ignore jokes, status theatre, and restated slides unless they changed a decision. If you need a quote, mark it as a quote and keep it short. The transcript, if you have one, is the place for the long version.

Practical tricks that survive a messy call:

- Write in the agenda order, not in the order people interrupted. Park a side thread under the agenda item it belongs to, or under open questions.
- When two people talk at once, wait one breath, then ask "what did we just decide?" That sentence is often the minutes.
- If you are lost, type a timestamp and a few words ("14:12 budget, no close"). Fill it after, from memory or from the recording.
- Type your own observations as notes, not as decisions. On the Mac, notes typed during a recording stay marked as yours when they fold into the generated record. In a plain doc, prefix them with your initials so they do not look like a group decision.

You can take minutes without recording. You can also record and still take minutes by hand. The recording does not replace the filter.

## After the call, the same day

Block thirty minutes on the same calendar day. The job is to make the page sendable, not to write a second meeting.

1. Fix names and spelling. Check titles against the invite.
2. Turn every action into the three-field shape: description, owner, due date. Split combined lines. Remove tasks that were already finished in the room and put those under notes or decisions if they closed something.
3. Move anything still fuzzy to open questions. A wrong owner is worse than a blank.
4. Read the decisions out of context. Could someone who missed the call act on them? If not, add the missing condition or date.
5. Send the minutes, not the recording, unless someone asked for the audio. Say where the official recording is, if there is one.

If you have a transcript, use it as evidence. Search for names next to verbs. Do not paste the transcript into the minutes. A twelve-page dump is how decisions disappear.

The Mac app can keep actions as structured rows (description, owner, due date, done) and decisions as wording plus a responsible person and a date. You still review them. Generated prose is a draft. See [how the record is built](/#record).

## Use a minutes sheet, not a transcript dump

When you do not want to rebuild sections in a blank doc, open the [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet). It is a form: date, attendees, agenda, decisions, actions with owner and due date, open questions. You can start from a team, client, or board sitting and download Markdown. It does not spend a transcription run and it does not invent speakers.

Fill it from your sparse notes, or paste only the lines that survived the filter. If you need the full copyable layout and filled examples, switch to the [meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template). That page is the form. This page is the habit.

Want the next sitting's record ready before people join? Open the [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet), paste the agenda, and leave the decision rows empty.

## When a recording helps, and when it does not

A recording helps when the sitting was fast, bilingual, or disputed, and you need to check a number or a name. It does not help when nobody will listen to it and the minutes were never written. The recording is evidence. The minutes are the working copy.

If you already have a short audio file (15 minutes or less), you can turn it into text in the browser with [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording). The file is not uploaded. You still write the minutes yourself from that text. Longer files belong on the Mac as an import, or as a live capture next time. The browser tool does not label speakers and does not record live meetings.

Local capture on the Mac records microphone and system audio without a bot in the call. You start and stop. If the reason you avoid cloud notetakers is the extra guest in the roster, [meeting notes without a bot](/blog/meeting-notes-without-a-bot) maps that choice to where the audio sits. After processing, you still apply the same filter: decisions and actions, not a paste of the transcript. Consent and retention stay with you. A clean protocol does not make a recording lawful.

Skip the recording when the sitting is a short standup you can minute in real time, or when the client forbade any capture. In those rooms the practice above is the whole method.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should meeting minutes include?

Date and timezone, who was there, the agenda, decisions (with conditions), actions with one owner and a due date, open questions, and the next sitting if you set one. Not a full transcript.

### Should I write a transcript instead?

No. A transcript is evidence of what was said. Minutes are the short page people act on. Keep both if you need both. Do not file the transcript under "minutes."

### When do I write during the meeting versus after?

Write the skeleton and the decision and action lines during the call. Clean names, dates, and send the page the same day. Waiting until next week is how quiet commitments vanish.

### Who should take the minutes?

Someone who can interrupt once to confirm a decision, and who is not also running a difficult facilitation. On a light team sync, the facilitator can do both if the notes stay short.

### How do I handle a bilingual meeting?

Write the official minutes in the language the absentees must use. If a decision was spoken in the other language, keep that wording in parentheses so nobody "translates" the commitment into something weaker.

### Can I use a minutes sheet after the call?

Yes. The [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) is for typing or pasting. It does not listen to the room. After the call is a normal time to fill it, as long as it is the same day.

### What if I have a recording?

Use it to check names, numbers, and disputed lines. Write the minutes as a short record. Do not send the file in place of the minutes unless someone asked for the audio.
