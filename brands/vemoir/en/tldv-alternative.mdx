A **tl;dv alternative** fits when you want searchable notes from Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, yet the recording should not sit in a vendor cloud, even if that cloud is in Europe. tl;dv (short for “too long; didn’t view”) is a team meeting library with clips, summaries, and CRM hooks, and it offers both a bot and a bot-free desktop app. The tools below keep part of that stack and drop the rest.

Facts below come from vendor pages and are as of October 2026. Open the links before you roll a tool out. Plan names move.

## What tl;dv does well

[tl;dv](https://tldv.io/) records and transcribes Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, then turns the call into notes, clips, and follow-ups you can search later. The product is aimed at teams that rewatch sales and customer calls, not at someone who only needs a private transcript of one conversation.


The security commitment on tl;dv’s own site, as of October 2026, states that no customer data is used to train the AI, that tl;dv partners with Anthropic for that processing, and that you can choose whether the AI is hosted in Europe or in the US ([tl;dv security commitment](https://tldv.io/features/security-commitment/)). The same page says the software is hosted on Google Cloud, Amazon Web Services, and Hetzner, and that the data centers it lists are in Europe. It also says tl;dv is GDPR-compliant for all users and points you to the privacy policy for the rest.

That is a clearer residency story than many US notetakers publish. It is still a cloud product. The recording is processed on tl;dv’s infrastructure so the team library can exist. “Europe” is not “this file never left the laptop,” and “no training” is not “no processor.” Read the current privacy policy next to the marketing page, because the two pages answer different questions.

On Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, tl;dv works in two ways. A notetaker bot joins the call, or the desktop app for Mac and Windows records system audio and your microphone with no bot ([tl;dv desktop app](https://tldv.io/desktop-app/), as of October 2026). The bot-free path removes the extra name from the participant list, and tl;dv remains a cloud product. Pricing tiers, including what is free and what needs a seat, belong on [tl;dv pricing](https://tldv.io/app/pricing/). Do not budget from a screenshot in a review.

## Seven tl;dv alternatives

### Vemoir (Mac app)

**Best for:** a personal or small-team record that stays on the Mac by default, with no bot in the call.

Vemoir is made by Aurum Avis Labs GmbH in Oberägeri, Switzerland. You start and stop recording yourself. Microphone and system audio are separate local tracks. The app is not tied to Zoom, Meet, or Teams. Any meeting app that plays audio on the Mac is in scope, including Slack huddles and Discord ([platforms](/#platforms)).

Transcription and speaker separation run on the Mac. Minutes, decisions, action items, Ask Vemoir, and documents run on the Mac unless you explicitly route text generation to OpenAI or Anthropic with your own key. There is no silent cloud fallback. Audio is not sent with that text. After the models are installed, the workflow can run offline. Internet is needed to set up and to download models.

You can type notes during the call. They fold into the minutes. You share through macOS sharing, only what you pick ([the record](/#record)). macOS asks for Microphone and Screen Recording permission, because that is the only way an app can capture other apps’ audio. Vemoir uses it for meeting audio only and does not record the screen.

The Mac App Store price is a one-time purchase: CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US ([pricing](/#pricing)). No per-meeting fee and no seat count. You need Apple silicon, macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, 8 GB of memory, and 10 GB free. 16 GB is recommended for long meetings. Intel Macs, Windows, iPhone, iPad, and Android are not supported.

### Jamie

**Best for:** bot-free desktop capture with a vendor that says storage and processing stay in Europe, on a subscription.

Jamie describes an app that does not add a meeting participant, deletes audio after transcription, and hosts data in the EU ([Jamie on data handling](https://www.meetjamie.ai/blog/llm-info), as of October 2026). That is closer to tl;dv’s European story than a US notetaker, and like tl;dv’s desktop app it adds no bot. The notes still live in Jamie’s service, not only on your disk. Limits and prices are on [Jamie pricing](https://meetjamie.ai/pricing).

### Fathom

**Best for:** a generous free plan, clips, and CRM features when a bot or a documented Mac path is acceptable.

Fathom covers Zoom, Meet, and Teams. Its help article on the 3.0 desktop experience describes bot-free modes on Mac for English-language meetings, plus a bot path for audio and video ([Fathom 3.0 upgrade](https://help.fathom.video/en/articles/11577345), as of October 2026). Windows and other languages should not assume that bot-free path. Confirm the free-plan limits on [Fathom pricing](https://fathom.ai/pricing). The [Fathom alternative](/blog/fathom-alternative) comparison covers it in full.

### Granola

**Best for:** live notes from computer audio without a bot, if you accept that transcripts live in Granola’s cloud.

Granola says it does not store meeting audio long term and that notes sit in encrypted, US-hosted cloud infrastructure ([Granola security](https://www.granola.ai/security), as of October 2026). Its privacy FAQ says anonymized data may be used for Granola’s own model improvements on Free and Business, with an opt-out, and that third-party model providers are not allowed to train on customer data ([Granola privacy FAQ](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs)). You get no team clip library of the kind tl;dv sells.

### MacWhisper

**Best for:** transcribing a file that is already in Finder, locally.

MacWhisper uses local models by default. Cloud transcription, translation, and AI prompts are optional and send data only if you turn them on ([Keeping transcriptions private](https://docs.macwhisper.com/article/52-keeping-transcriptions-private), updated July 27, 2025). The [MacWhisper site](https://macwhisper.com/) lists Pro as a one-time license at €64 (as of October 2026). Meeting recording exists. Automatic detection is documented as a beta that needs Screen Recording permission and is not in the Mac App Store build ([Record meetings](https://docs.macwhisper.com/article/30-record-meetings)). For a file you already exported from Meet or Zoom, MacWhisper is the stronger tool. A live team archive is not its job.

### Otter.ai

**Best for:** a calendar bot, mobile apps, and shared channels when the team has already standardized there.

Otter joins Zoom, Teams, and Meet and sells subscription plans with team features ([Otter pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing), as of October 2026). Its Mac and Windows desktop app records without a bot, like tl;dv’s desktop app ([Otter desktop app](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/35973988280215-Otter-Desktop-App-Mac-Windows)). Otter also documents how the notetaker appears ([Otter recording permissions](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/39339238308503-Recording-Permissions-with-Otter)). The transcripts still live in Otter’s cloud, so leaving tl;dv for Otter changes the vendor and keeps the cloud.

### Fireflies.ai

**Best for:** conversation intelligence, admin controls, and CRM-oriented workflows in a cloud workspace.

Fireflies keeps its notes in a cloud workspace and prices per seat ([Fireflies pricing](https://fireflies.ai/pricing), as of October 2026). Its desktop app for Mac and Windows has a mode that captures device audio without a bot ([Fireflies desktop app](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/1208704416-getting-started-with-the-fireflies-desktop-app)). The privacy policy says personal information is not used for AI model training and that vendors are prohibited from training on it ([Fireflies privacy policy](https://fireflies.ai/privacy-policy)). The terms still allow derived usage and statistical data for internal purposes ([Fireflies terms](https://fireflies.ai/terms-of-service)). Quote the policy itself, not a paraphrase from a comparison site.

A short file after the call can go to [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording). That tab will not join a meeting the way tl;dv does.

## Where tl;dv is still the better choice

Stay with tl;dv when the work product is a shared library: clips a manager can watch, search across many calls, and CRM fields someone else updates. A one-time Mac app does not give the rest of the company that library. Asking six people to export notes into Slack is a process, and it fails the week someone is out.

tl;dv is also the better fit when you specifically want the vendor’s written choice of EU or US AI hosting, a European company story, and a stack already wired to the CRM. Moving that history onto laptops throws away the reason the team bought the product.

Windows users, and anyone who must capture meetings they do not host on their own Mac, should not switch to a Mac-only app just to avoid a vendor name. If a bot is allowed, tl;dv’s join path covers Zoom, Meet, and Teams without installing a native recorder on every machine. Where a bot is not allowed, tl;dv’s own desktop app records without one on Windows as well as Mac.

## Honest limits to plan for

EU hosting does not remove the processor. Someone at the company still has to answer where the file lives, who can download it, and how long it stays. tl;dv’s security page is the source for those answers, and it will not match a laptop that never uploaded the audio.

Vemoir will not build the clip reel or the multi-meeting sales report. Ask Vemoir answers questions about meetings on that Mac, with links back to the transcript ([chat](/#chat)). It does not search your colleagues’ laptops. There is no team workspace in the product you can buy today.

The free browser tool is easy to oversell. It does not record the live call, does not label speakers, and stops at 15 minutes and 3 successful runs a week. Swiss German speech usually comes out as Standard German in those public Whisper models. The Mac app does not claim dialect transcription either.

Models take disk space. The first meeting waits on a download. Long meetings want 16 GB of memory. You tell participants you are recording, because no bot announces itself. None of these tools makes the recording lawful, and Vemoir does not claim to be compliant, HIPAA-ready, or GDPR-certified.

MacWhisper remains the better file tool. If your tl;dv habit is “export the recording and transcribe it,” you may not need another meeting product at all.

## How a Swiss team should test it

Pick two calls you already know you are allowed to record. On the first, use whatever you use today, including tl;dv if it is installed, and write down who could see the notes the next morning. On the second, record with Vemoir from the menu bar ([how it works](/#how)) and export only the minutes.

Then compare the questions your counsel or your client actually asks: did a bot appear, did the audio leave the Mac, can you delete it, and can a colleague in another city find it without you sending a file. tl;dv wins the last question. Vemoir wins the first two, if the Mac app is what you ran. A browser trial on a 10-minute export wins neither the live meeting nor the shared archive. It only answers “can I get text without an upload” ([transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording)).

If the calls mix German and English, choose the transcription model on purpose. Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages. Other local models have their own lists. The app language and the transcript language are separate settings ([languages](/#languages)).

Write down the corrections you make by hand after the second call: a name, a decision, an ownerless task. If those corrections are the real work, a more expensive team plan will not remove them. If the real work is a manager watching ten calls, keep tl;dv.

Before you cancel, one more check. Export from tl;dv only what you are legally or contractually allowed to keep, and delete the rest in the account, not just on the local disk. A Mac app does not replace an archive that already lives in the cloud. It changes where the next conversation goes. If you run both paths side by side, write in the invite which tool records that day. Otherwise the bot and the menu bar capture the same meeting twice, and later it is unclear which version is the record.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a tl;dv alternative without a bot?

Yes. tl;dv’s own desktop app records without a bot on Mac and Windows. So do Vemoir, Jamie, Granola, and MacWhisper, and Otter and Fireflies offer bot-free desktop modes too. Fathom documents bot-free modes on Mac for English meetings. Read where each one stores the transcript after capture. “No bot” and “no cloud” are different sentences.

### Does tl;dv train on customer recordings?

tl;dv’s security commitment says no customer data is used to train the AI, and that you can choose EU or US hosting for that AI (as of October 2026). Confirm the sentence on [their security page](https://tldv.io/features/security-commitment/) before you repeat it in a policy.

### Does EU hosting mean the file stays in Switzerland?

No. tl;dv describes data centers in Europe, not a guarantee that processing stays inside Switzerland. A Mac app that never uploads the audio is a different design. Exporting or turning on a cloud text model changes that design for the text you send.

### Can I transcribe a tl;dv export without uploading it again?

If you already have a short audio or video file, the [browser tool](/tools/transcribe-recording) runs locally in the tab, up to 15 minutes, 3 successful runs a week, with no speaker labels. Longer files go to the Mac app or to MacWhisper.

### Does Vemoir replace tl;dv’s CRM updates?

No. Vemoir writes minutes, decisions, actions, and a follow-up on your Mac. It does not push fields into a CRM. If that push is the product you bought, tl;dv or a peer built for it remains the better tool.

### What does Vemoir cost?

One Mac App Store purchase: CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US. No subscription and no fee per meeting.

### Which platforms does a local recorder cover?

Any app that plays the call on the Mac: Zoom, Meet, Teams, and others ([platform support](/#platforms)). Coverage comes from system audio, so no partner integration is involved.

### Who should not switch?

Teams that live in a shared clip library, Windows-only groups, and anyone whose main requirement is CRM coaching across many reps. Also anyone who needs a phone recorder when no computer is in the room.

For the next meeting on a Mac, read [how capture works](/#capture) or install Vemoir from the [Mac App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794693630). To try a short file first, open [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording).
