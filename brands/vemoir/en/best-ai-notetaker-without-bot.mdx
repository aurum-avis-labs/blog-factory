## What "without a bot" means

A bot notetaker joins Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams as a named participant. Otter's help center describes [OtterPilot](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360019225632-Getting-started-with-OtterPilot) joining meetings you schedule. Fireflies documents its assistant joining as a participant ([getting started](https://guide.fireflies.ai/hc/en-us/articles/4403764950156-Getting-started-with-Fireflies-ai)). Everyone can see the extra guest. Many client invites and IT rules ban that guest even when a human host is allowed to record.


"Without a bot" means the capture path does not add that guest. Typical paths:

- The app records microphone and system audio on your computer. You start and stop it.
- You already have a file and you transcribe it afterward. Nothing joins the call that already ended.
- A vendor offers both a bot and a desktop capture mode. The desktop mode counts only if you actually use it. The calendar bot is still a bot.

A browser page that accepts a file you drop on it is not a live notetaker. It cannot hear a call in progress. Vemoir's free tools are that second kind of path: after the call, in the tab, file not uploaded.

## How to choose the best AI notetaker without a bot

Ask four questions before you compare brand names.


1. **Must the audio stay on the Mac?** Local apps transcribe on the device by default. Desktop apps that "do not join" can still send audio to a cloud speech service. No bot is not the same as no upload.
2. **Do you need notes during the next live call, or text from a file you already saved?** Live capture and after-the-file transcription solve different days.
3. **Who else must see the notes?** A personal record on one laptop is a different product from a shared team library with CRM fields.
4. **What breaks if the network dies?** Offline use only exists when the models are already on the machine.

Hardware still matters for a local Mac app. Vemoir needs Apple silicon (M1 or newer), macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, at least 8 GB of memory, and about 10 GB free for models. 16 GB is a more comfortable floor for long meetings. Intel Macs are not supported. Windows and phones are not supported.

Price belongs in the decision. Subscription notetakers charge per user, often with minute pools or meeting caps. A one-time Mac app does not charge per meeting or per seat. Read the current number on the vendor page. The amounts in this article are the ones published in September 2026.

## Eight options that can stay off the roster

### Vemoir (Mac app)

**Best for:** live calls on a Mac when the audio, the transcript, and the generated record should stay on that Mac unless you export them.

Vemoir records from the menu bar. It captures the microphone and system audio as separate local tracks. No bot joins, and no extra tile appears. You start and stop each recording. It works with any meeting app that plays audio on the Mac: Teams, Zoom, Meet, Slack huddles, Discord, and others ([meeting apps](/#platforms)).

macOS asks for **System Audio Recording**. That permission is an audio-only capture. Vemoir does not request Screen Recording and does not record the screen. If you denied the prompt, turn Vemoir on under System Settings, Privacy and Security, System Audio Recording, and allow the microphone as well. Details sit on [capture](/#capture).

Transcription and speaker separation run on the Mac. Summaries, decisions, action items, and Ask Vemoir run on the Mac by default. You can opt into OpenAI or Anthropic for text only, with your own API key, after an explicit confirmation. Audio does not go out for that option, and there is no silent switch to a cloud model if the local one fails. Once the models you chose are installed, the meeting workflow can run offline. Internet is for setup and downloads.

The app turns a call into minutes, decisions, action items, and a follow-up draft ([the record](/#record)). You share only what you choose, through macOS sharing. Speaker names can be renamed after the call. Remembering a voice is opt-in, encrypted, and deletable. Unknown speakers stay anonymous.

**Price, Mac App Store, September 2026:** one-time purchase, no subscription. CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US. Every feature is included. [Pricing](/#pricing) and the [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794693630).

**Weaker when:** you need Windows, a shared cloud workspace for a whole sales team, or automatic capture without someone pressing record. MacWhisper is the cleaner tool if you only drop files in Finder and do not want a meeting library.

**After the call, short file:** the free [transcribe recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) tool runs in the browser. Up to 15 minutes, three successful runs a week, MP3, M4A, WAV, or MP4. No speaker labels. No live capture. The model downloads once. You can check the Network tab to confirm the file is not posted.

### Granola

**Best for:** a notepad that feels live, on Mac and Windows, with mobile capture, if you accept cloud transcription.

[Granola](https://www.granola.ai/) does not join as a participant. On desktop it captures microphone and meeting audio from the computer, transcribes through third-party speech providers, and summarizes with third-party models ([Granola security](https://www.granola.ai/security), September 2026). Granola states it does not store meeting audio long term and keeps transcripts and notes in encrypted cloud infrastructure on AWS in the US. The same page documents SOC 2 Type 2 and enterprise defaults for model training. Pricing on the homepage is a free tier plus paid plans for history and teams. It is not an offline Mac workflow.

Granola wins when you already live in its calendar flow, want the same notes on a phone, or your team is fine with US-hosted transcripts. It is a worse fit when the file itself must not leave the laptop. A closer look is in [Granola alternative](/blog/granola-alternative).

### MacWhisper

**Best for:** transcribing files you already have, locally, with a one-time Mac license.

[MacWhisper](https://www.macwhisper.com/) transcribes with local models by default and can record online meetings without a bot (site, September 2026). Pro adds meeting recording from system audio and speaker recognition on that page. The Pro license was listed at €64, one-time, in September 2026. App Store prices can differ from the site. Summaries can use local models or cloud AI you connect yourself.

Choose MacWhisper when the job is "this file, on this Mac, export the text." Choose Vemoir when the job is a meeting library with minutes, owners, and decisions, recorded from the menu bar. Both can stay off the roster. They are not the same product.

### Jamie

**Best for:** no bot, and a vendor that documents EU hosting, if a subscription and cloud processing are acceptable.

[Jamie](https://www.meetjamie.ai/) runs on your device, states that no bot joins, documents EU data residency, and says it does not store audio after transcription (homepage, September 2026). The homepage also states ISO 27001. That is Jamie's claim about Jamie, not a certification of your meeting. [Pricing](https://www.meetjamie.ai/pricing) in September 2026 included a free tier (about 10 meetings a month, with a short duration cap), Plus and Pro subscriptions, and team plans. Notes on lower tiers can stay locked until you upgrade, per [Jamie's plan docs](https://docs.meetjamie.ai/billing-pricing/subscriptions-plans). You need a network for their processing. It is not an offline local-model app.

Jamie is the fair pick when procurement wants an EU processor on paper and nobody will accept a bot. Vemoir is the fair pick when "EU cloud" is still more sharing than you want.

### Fathom, bot-free path

**Best for:** a generous free cloud record, if the Mac bot-free beta is actually what you turn on.

[Fathom](https://www.fathom.video/pricing) (September 2026) advertises a free tier with unlimited recordings and transcriptions, summaries, and a choice of bot capture or a bot-free beta on Mac. Paid Premium was listed around $16 to $20 a month depending on billing, with team plans above that. If you leave the calendar bot on, Fathom is a bot product. Count it in this list only for the desktop path you really use. Audio and notes still live in Fathom's cloud, not on the Mac by default.

### Fireflies desktop, without the bot

**Best for:** people who already pay for Fireflies and need one Meet or call without Fred in the room.

Fireflies is famous for a notetaker that joins. The desktop app also has a Take Notes mode that captures system audio without the bot. Fireflies' own article says that path does not save audio or video and does not provide speaker labels ([bot vs no bot](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/7066927951-bot-vs-no-bot-which-capture-am-i-using), September 2026). Paid plans are per seat ([pricing](https://fireflies.ai/pricing)). Fireflies states it does not use customer meeting content to train its models ([privacy policy](https://fireflies.ai/privacy-policy)).

Use this mode when the team library is already Fireflies and a single call forbids a guest. Do not expect the same speaker labels or recording file as the bot path. A full comparison is in [Fireflies alternative](/blog/fireflies-alternative).

### tl;dv desktop capture

**Best for:** teams that want tl;dv's library, but a system-audio recording instead of the tl;dv Notetaker guest.

Default calendar capture uses a visible notetaker. The desktop app can record without that bot by capturing system audio on Mac or Windows ([help article](https://intercom.help/tldv/en/articles/14433337-recording-without-a-bot), September 2026). [Pricing](https://tldv.io/pricing) in September 2026 included a free tier with recording and transcription limits on the AI features, Pro around $18 per seat per month billed annually, and Business around $29. The vendor describes EU hosting options and states that customer recordings are not used to train models ([features](https://tldv.io/features/)). Processing is still their cloud.

### Otter, bot-free desktop path

**Best for:** an Otter workspace you already have, on the calls where OtterPilot is not allowed.

Otter's main product is a calendar agent that can join Zoom, Teams, and Meet. Otter also advertises bot-free recording from Mac or Windows desktop apps and Chrome, so "Otter" is not only the bot ([pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing), September 2026). Subscription tiers that month started from a free Basic plan, with Pro listed from about $8.33 per user per month billed annually and Business higher. Otter's privacy materials say it may use de-identified audio and transcripts to improve its models ([privacy policy](https://otter.ai/privacy-policy)). Read the current wording. A safety-oriented read is [is Otter safe](/blog/is-otter-ai-safe).

If the rule is "no Otter guest," the desktop path is the one to test. If the rule is "audio never leaves the Mac," Otter's cloud is the wrong tool even without a bot.

## Where a bot is still the better choice

A visible bot is the better product when the company has already agreed to it.

- Every host should be captured even if they forget to press record.
- Sales or success needs one searchable library, CRM fields, and coaching across many people. Gong-style conversation intelligence is a different job from a personal Mac record. A local app does not replace that.
- IT has a contract, a retention setting, and an admin who can delete a workspace. A laptop does not.
- The call is internal, nobody objects to the guest, and the notes must appear for people who were not in the room without a forward from the host.

Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, and tl;dv earn their seat in that setup. Forcing a no-bot tool on a team that wants automatic join creates missed meetings, not privacy.

The honest reverse: if the invite says no AI notetakers, check whether a silent cloud upload is banned too. Sometimes only the guest is forbidden. Sometimes any third-party processor is.

## Limits you should plan for

No tool on this list makes a recording lawful. Tell people you are recording. Consent and retention stay with you. Nothing here is legal advice, and nothing here is a compliance certification.

Local apps need disk, a first download, and a person who remembers to press record. Vemoir will not join a call you did not start. Cloud desktop capture fails when the laptop sleeps, the permission is denied, or the vendor changes the plan cap.

The browser trial is not a stand-in for an hour-long client meeting. Fifteen minutes is the cap. Splitting a long file into pieces still spends a weekly run on each successful transcription. Cleaning a VTT, filling a minutes sheet, and counting who spoke do not spend a run, because they do not run the speech model. They also cannot invent speaker names that were not in the file.

Speaker separation, a project glossary, and a full meeting library are Mac-app work. The browser does not offer them.

If you only need text from a short file you already hold, skip the subscription comparison and use [transcribe recording](/tools/transcribe-recording). If the next call is live and must stay on the Mac, the menu bar app is the relevant product ([how it works](/#how)).

## An afternoon to test without buying seats

Do not buy five annual seats to answer a question a single file could settle. Run three checks in one afternoon on a call you are allowed to record yourself.

**Check 1, the participant list.** Start the path you would actually use, not the demo with the bot enabled beside it. Open the list during the call. If a guest appears that you did not invite, that path is not bot-free for this list, whatever the homepage claims. Screenshot the list while the call runs. Memory fades after hang-up.

**Check 2, where the audio goes.** Read the security or privacy page linked from this article as it stands today. Write one line in your notes: «audio stays on the Mac» or «audio goes to the vendor». If the page does not say, do not assume local processing. For Vemoir’s browser tool you can use the Network tab to confirm the file is not posted. That proves the tab workflow, not every desktop app on the market.

**Check 3, a decision you can find again.** Record two minutes where you state a condition, a date and a name that owns an action. Open the record the tool produces. Is the condition still there, or only «people agreed»? That test survives marketing counts on homepages. This article does not quote accuracy percentages.

If all three checks pass and audio must stay on the Mac, Vemoir and MacWhisper stay the narrow candidates, with the split from above: library and minutes, or Finder files. If EU paperwork is enough and the bot is the only ban, run Jamie through the same three checks. If US cloud and a notepad are acceptable, run Granola. Test the other names only when the team library already lives there and you can enable desktop capture without the calendar bot.

Spend a second afternoon on retention: where you delete the recording, the note and a saved voice profile.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best AI notetaker without a bot for a client call?

If the audio must stay on the Mac, Vemoir or MacWhisper. Vemoir if you want minutes, decisions, and actions from a live menu-bar recording. MacWhisper if the job is local file transcription. If EU cloud processing is enough and a bot is the only ban, Jamie is built for that story. If US-hosted notes are fine, Granola is the notepad many teams already know.

### Does "no bot" mean the audio stays on my computer?

No. Granola, Jamie, Fathom, Fireflies, tl;dv, and Otter can avoid a roster guest and still process audio in their cloud. Check the security page, not the phrase "bot-free."

### Is there a free AI notetaker that does not join the meeting?

Granola, Fathom, Otter, Fireflies, tl;dv, Jamie, and MacWhisper publish free tiers with different caps (September 2026). Vemoir's browser tool is free for files up to 15 minutes, three successful runs a week, with no account. The Mac app is a one-time purchase, not a free live recorder.

### Can I use these offline?

Vemoir and MacWhisper can, after local models are installed. The cloud products on this list need a network during capture or processing. The browser tool can run again after its model is cached. Closing the tab does not upload the file. Reopening the page later still depends on the browser keeping that cache.

### Which one should I avoid if the client banned AI notetakers?

Any calendar auto-join: OtterPilot, Fireflies' notetaker, Fathom's bot, tl;dv's notetaker. Then ask whether a silent cloud upload is also banned. If it is, stay with a local Mac transcription.

### Do these tools label speakers without a bot?

Vemoir and MacWhisper describe on-device speaker separation for their live or imported audio. Fireflies' own no-bot desktop mode does not provide speaker labels. Vemoir's browser tools never invent speakers. Names survive only if the file already has them.

### Why not just use the meeting app's own transcript?

Teams and Zoom transcripts depend on license, host settings, and language coverage. Zoom's cloud audio transcript is a VTT on paid cloud recording, not on a local recording. Those transcripts live in the vendor's cloud. They are a good source when you are allowed to use them. They are a different product from a recorder that never adds a guest and keeps the file on the Mac.

### Does Vemoir train models on my meetings?

Transcription stays on the Mac. A cloud language model is used only if you add your own key and confirm it. There is no silent fallback. You still decide what you export or share.
