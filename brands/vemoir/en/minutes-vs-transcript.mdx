Meeting minutes vs a transcript is a choice of record, not of software. The transcript is what was said, in order. The minutes are the short page someone can act on: decisions, owners, dates, open questions. Send the transcript as minutes and nobody reads it. Keep only a polished summary and you cannot check a disputed line later.

Most professional sittings need both, stored as different things. The copyable layout is in the [meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template).

## What a transcript actually is

A transcript is speech as text. It keeps false starts and retractions. Common shapes:

- A Zoom cloud `.vtt` file, with a WEBVTT header, cue times, and sometimes a speaker tag.
- A Teams download, `.vtt` or `.docx`, if transcription was turned on and you are allowed to download it.
- A local speech-to-text file from a Mac app or from a browser tool, often with timestamps and without names.
- A research transcript that a person corrected by hand.

It is evidence of what the recording contains, not a decision. It is not a certification, a privileged record, or an accuracy percentage. Length is a problem only when you file it under "minutes". Browser tools for files you already have do not add speaker names.

## What minutes are for

Minutes are the record of the sitting, written so a person who was not there can see the outcome. A usable page has a date, the attendees, the agenda, the decisions, the action items with one owner and a due date, and the open questions. That shape is what the [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) holds in the browser. You fill it. It does not transcribe the room, and it does not spend a weekly speech-model run.


A decision is different from an action. "We will ship in April" can be finished in the room. "Mara tells the client on Tuesday" happens afterward. Mixing them turns the page into a diary.

Tone is short on purpose. Minutes do not quote every joke, and they do not copy the hedge words if the room actually closed the point. If the room did not close the point, the honest line is an open question, not a fake decision.

On a Mac, Vemoir keeps a summary, actions, and decisions as separate parts of the meeting record, and the raw timestamped transcript stays beside them. Generated lines can point back at the passage they came from, so you can check the words before you share the page. Your own notes typed during the call stay marked as yours. ## Meeting minutes vs a transcript in one sitting

| | Transcript | Minutes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question it answers | What was said? | What was decided, and who does what? |
| Length | Close to the length of the talk | A page or two |
| Speakers | Named only if the source had names, or a diarization pass added labels | Attendees listed once. Actions name one owner |
| Edits | Fix recognition errors without changing the meaning of the words | Rewrite so the outcome is clear |
| Who reads it | The person checking a dispute, a researcher coding the talk, someone who missed a phrase | The people who must act, a board, a client, a future you |
| What goes wrong | You paste it into the follow-up mail | You "clean up" a real disagreement into a decision nobody made |

A board packet, a client recap, and a weekly team note all want the right-hand column. An interview study, a quote you might publish, and a fight about whether someone agreed want the left-hand column.

If you want both records from the same call without a bot in the participant list, [Vemoir keeps transcript and minutes separate on your Mac](/#record).

## When the transcript is the better artifact

Keep the transcript, and do not compress it yet, when:

- Someone may dispute the wording. The minutes can say "the client asked for a delay." The transcript shows the sentence.
- You are coding an interview or a research conversation. The hedge words are data. A smooth summary throws them away. For a file you cannot upload, the working pattern is in [transcribe an interview without uploading](/blog/transcribe-interview-without-upload).
- The automatic summary dropped a condition. "We agree, if legal signs off" is not "we agree."
- You need quotes, not tasks.
- The meeting was a status round with no decisions. Minutes can be three lines. The transcript is still the place you look if a number was wrong.

A transcript is a poor artifact when the only audience is busy and the only need is "what do I do on Monday." They will not scrub a VTT.

## When minutes are the better artifact

Write minutes, and do not attach the whole transcript, when:

- People who missed the call need the outcome today.
- A client should see decisions and dates, not the internal aside.
- You are sharing notes and you are not allowed, or not willing, to share the recording. The text you chose is the thing that moves. The audio can stay on the machine.
- A Verwaltungsrat-style sitting, a project review, or a client checkpoint needs a page that can be filed. The Swiss habit of a short protocol exists for this reason. The words of the debate are not the protocol.

Minutes fail when they are written from memory the next day and the quiet decision is gone. Then the transcript, if you have one, is how you repair the page. It is not the page.

## How to move from one to the other

1. Start with the source you have. If it is a VTT or an SRT full of cue numbers, strip the timestamps first. Names stay only when they were already there.
2. Read for decisions and for verbs with a person. Do not highlight the whole file.
3. Put decisions in one list. Put open questions in another. Put actions in a third list, one owner and one date each.
4. Leave the transcript intact as the evidence. Do not "fix" it to match the minutes. If the minutes and the words disagree, fix the minutes or mark the line as disputed.
5. Share the minutes. Link or store the transcript only for the people who are allowed to see it.

The blank page for step 3 is the [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet). Three starters cover a team sync, a client call, and a board sitting. You can download Markdown when the fields are filled.

Files up to 15 minutes can start in the browser with [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) without upload. Longer audio, live capture, and speaker separation are the Mac app. You still tell people you record, and you still decide retention.

## One call, filed twice

Picture a twelve-minute client call. The client says the revised contract goes out Thursday if clause four stays. You say you will ask legal on Tuesday. Someone says «we are aligned» and the topic changes.

The transcript keeps «if clause four stays», your «I will ask legal», and the «aligned» line. It also keeps the earlier sentences where another clause was dropped. That is the evidence, hedges included.

The minutes have two lines. Decision: revised contract Thursday only if clause four is unchanged. Action: you contact legal by Tuesday. The dropped clause is not a decision. «Aligned» is not its own row.

If you send only the smooth paragraph «revision goes out Thursday», the condition disappears and legal drops off the page. If you send only the VTT, nobody finds Thursday between the cues. Keep both files side by side. Send the minutes in the follow-up mail. Store the transcript with the recording.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can minutes replace a transcript?

Only if nobody will need the wording later. For a client commitment or board decision, keep both.

### Can a transcript replace minutes?

It does not tell an absent person what to do. Someone still writes owners and dates.

### Is a summary the same as minutes?

A smooth paragraph that hides a disagreement is not minutes. Pull decisions, owners, and dates into fields.

### Do minutes have to include who spoke most?

No. Talk time needs names in the text and belongs in research notes, not in a protocol.

### Should I edit the transcript to match the minutes?

Fix misheard names. Do not delete a retraction or a condition.

### What if the transcript has no speaker names?

You can still write minutes from context you remember, and you should mark anything you are unsure about. You cannot honestly assign quotes to people from an unlabeled file. The browser does not invent those names. On the Mac, speaker separation can add anonymous labels you then rename.

### How long should minutes be?

Short enough to read. A team sync is often under a page. A long board sitting can be longer because the decisions are longer, not because you pasted the debate.

### Where do I keep each one?

Minutes go where the work is tracked and where absentees look. The transcript stays with the recording, with access limited to people who should have it. You do not have to circulate both.
