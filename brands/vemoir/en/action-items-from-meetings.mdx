Action items from meetings are the small promises that die when nobody owns them. A sentence like "we should follow up" is not an action. An action names one person, one outcome, and a date you can check. If the call produced ten vague intentions and no names, you do not have a list yet. You have a transcript of good intentions.

This guide shows how to hear an action in ordinary talk, write it so a colleague can do it without replaying the call, and file it on a minutes sheet. It is practice, not a blank template. If you want the copyable record with date, attendees, and decisions, use the [meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template).

## What counts as an action item

An action item is work that someone will start or finish after the meeting ends. It is not the decision itself, and it is not a remark. "We will use vendor A" is a decision. "Mara sends the contract redline by Thursday" is the action that makes the decision real.


Use three fields, every time:

1. **Description.** One outcome, in plain language. "Send the revised scope to the client" is enough. "Look into the scope" is not.
2. **Owner.** One person. A team name is a way to lose the item. If two people must contribute, name the person who will close it and mention the helper in the description.
3. **Due date.** A calendar day, not "soon" or "next sprint" unless you also write the day that sprint ends.

Status comes later: open, in progress, done. Do not invent a status during the call unless someone said it.

Open questions belong in their own column. "Can finance approve this?" is not an action until someone agrees to ask finance. If the room left it hanging, write it as a question with the person who will ask, or leave it as an open question so you do not pretend it was assigned.

## Where action items from meetings hide

People rarely say "action item." They say "I'll ping them," "we need a draft," "can you take the deck," or "let's not drop this." Listen for a verb with a person attached, then for a time.


Useful patterns:

- "I'll..." or "I can..." from a named person. Capture it while they are still talking. If they did not give a date, ask before you leave the topic.
- "Can you..." addressed to someone who did not refuse. Silence is a weak acceptance. If the item matters, confirm the owner out loud.
- "We should..." with no name. That is a wish. Stop and ask who will do it. If nobody answers, it is an open question, not an action.
- A decision with no next step. "We are going with the April launch" still needs someone to tell the customer, update the plan, or book the review.

A transcript will bury these lines between small talk. Search the text for names next to verbs: send, book, draft, confirm, review, share, call, update. Then read a few lines above the hit so you do not pull a joke or a rejected idea.

The Mac app keeps actions as structured records with a description, an owner, a due date, and a done state, and a decision stays separate: wording, a responsible person, and a date. You can also type your own notes during the call. Those notes stay marked as yours and can be folded into the minutes. See [how the record is built](/#record).

## Write the line so it can be done

Write the description as if the owner missed the meeting. They should know what "done" looks like without asking you.

| Weak line | Usable line |
| --- | --- |
| Follow up with the client | Email the client the agreed scope and the Friday deadline |
| Look at the numbers | Send the Q3 margin table to the project channel by Wednesday |
| Discuss pricing later | Book a 30-minute pricing review with finance before 18 October |
| Update the doc | Add the decision and the new date to the project page today |

Keep one outcome per line. "Update the doc and tell the client and fix the invoice" is three items. Split them, even if one person owns all three. Combined lines get half-done and then marked complete.

Match the date to the work. "Today" is fine if you write the actual date when you leave the room. "ASAP" is not a date. If the owner cannot name a day, write the question "Owner to propose a date by tomorrow" so the gap is visible.

If you copy actions into chat, keep the same three fields. A Slack message that only says "as discussed" forces everyone back into the recording.

## A short record you can fill after the call

Paste this under your decisions. It is the action block only. The full minutes layout, including a board sitting and a client call, is in the [meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template).

```text
Actions
- Description:
  Owner:
  Due:
  Status: open

- Description:
  Owner:
  Due:
  Status: open

Open questions
- Question:
  Who will resolve it:
  By:
```

Fill it the same day. A list written the next morning from memory keeps the loud topics and drops the quiet ones. If you have a transcript, use it as the evidence, not as the minutes. Minutes are shorter than the talk on purpose.

## From a messy transcript to the minutes sheet

A raw transcript is a bad action list. It repeats, it includes every "yeah," and it rarely writes "owner" on a line. Use it as a source, then write the short record yourself or with a notes pass.

1. Export or save the text you already have (Teams `.vtt` or `.docx`, a Zoom cloud VTT, or a local transcript).
2. If the file is still full of cue numbers and timestamps, clean it first. Names stay only when they were already in the file. The cleaner does not invent speakers.
3. Read for verbs and names. Write candidate actions in the three-field shape.
4. Put decisions in the decisions section and anything unassigned in open questions.
5. Confirm owners in writing if the call was fuzzy. A wrong owner is worse than a blank one.

If you only have audio and no text yet, a file of 15 minutes or less can go through the [transcribe recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) tool in the browser. The file is not uploaded. Longer meetings are a Mac job: import MP3, M4A, WAV, AIFF, CAF, or FLAC, or record the next call from the menu bar. Live capture is not in the browser.

Want the blank record without rebuilding it in a doc? Open the [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) and paste only the actions that survived the filter.

## Why action lists fail after a good meeting

The meeting felt clear. The list still failed. The usual reasons are boring:

- **No owner.** "The team will" means nobody will.
- **No date.** Items without dates get sorted under "later" forever.
- **The transcript was pasted as the minutes.** Nobody reads twelve pages to find two tasks.
- **Owners were added by the note-taker after the fact.** If the person did not accept the work, write a question, not an assignment.
- **Decisions and tasks were mixed.** A decision can be done in the room. An action happens after.
- **The list lived only in the recording.** People who did not attend will not play the file. Share the notes, not the audio, unless they asked for the audio.

A summary written by a model will also miss actions when the model "cleans up" hedging into a smooth paragraph. Treat generated text as a draft. Check every owner against the transcript before you send it. On the Mac, action and decision lines can point back at the transcript passage they came from, so you can verify the wording before you share.

Consent and retention stay with you. A tidy action list does not make a recording lawful.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are action items the same as meeting minutes?

No. Minutes are the short record of the sitting: who was there, what was decided, which questions stayed open, and which actions were assigned. Action items are one section of that record. A transcript is the longer text of what people said.

### What if nobody accepts the action?

Leave it as an open question, or assign "propose an owner" to the person who runs the next meeting. Do not write a name the person never agreed to.

### Can one action have two owners?

Pick one person who is accountable. Name the second person in the description if they must contribute. Shared owners tend to wait for each other.

### Should I include tasks that were already finished during the call?

Usually no. Put them in the notes or under decisions if the room closed them. The action list is for work that is still open when the meeting ends.

### Do I need a transcript to write action items?

No. A careful note during the call is often cleaner. A transcript helps when you missed a name or a date. It is the evidence, not the list.

### Does the minutes sheet invent owners from an audio file?

No. The [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) is a form you fill. It does not transcribe audio and it does not guess who volunteered. Speaker labels, if you need them, come from a transcript that already has names, or from the Mac app.

### How many action items belong in a meeting?

As many as were actually assigned, and no more. A weekly team sync often has a handful. A page with thirty lines usually means decisions and remarks were copied in as tasks.

### Where does the list go once it is written?

Wherever the people who must do the work already look: the minutes you share, the task tracker you already use, or both, with the same owner and the same date. If the only copy lives in the recording, it will sit there.
