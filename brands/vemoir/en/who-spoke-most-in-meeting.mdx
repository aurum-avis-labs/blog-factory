Who spoke most in a meeting is only a number you can stand behind when the transcript already says who said each line. Paste that text into [who held the floor](/tools/who-held-the-floor) and you get each name's share of the words. No names in the paste means the page stops. The tab does not split voices, and it will not invent "Speaker A" so a chart looks finished.

That share is not minutes on the mic. Someone who talks fast can rank above a slower speaker who held the floor longer. Treat the output as a reading of the text you pasted, not a stopwatch.

## What you need before you count

You need speakers marked in a form the page can read:

- `Name: what they said` on its own line
- `Speaker 1:` or `SPEAKER_01:` if an earlier tool left diarization labels
- a WebVTT voice tag, `<v Name>text</v>`, including the kind Teams and some Zoom cloud files use

A local Zoom `audio_only.m4a` has none of that. Browser transcription of a file does not add labels. If you only have audio, the count waits until you have text with names. Anonymous labels you rename happen in the Mac app after you record or import. How capture works is on [capture](/#capture). This page does not record or diarize.

Cleaning timestamps is fine and does not use a weekly run. The [transcript cleaner](/tools/transcript-cleaner) keeps names that were there and tells you when they were missing. For a VTT, clean first, then paste plain text into who-held-the-floor. Counting word share does not download a speech model and does not spend a transcription run.

Want labels without hunting exports? [Vemoir](/#capture) records the next meeting on your Mac and separates speakers locally.

## Word share is not talk time

Searchers often mix up "who spoke most" with longest airtime. Here you only get words in the paste. Pauses, crosstalk and silence are invisible. A two-minute monologue beats ten minutes of short replies. If you need seconds of audio, use an editor or a tool built for timing, not this page.

## How to read who spoke most in the meeting

You get a name and a percentage of words. Read it against the meeting you actually had.

- Sixty percent for one person in a status update may mean they had the slides. That is not automatically a problem.
- A workshop meant to hear everyone, with one name holding most of the words, is a facilitation signal. The chart does not explain why; it shows uneven text.
- `Speaker 2` is not a colleague until you map it. You can count the label; do not write "Speaker 2 dominated" in email as if it were a person.
- `Conference Room` or `iPhone` is often a device label from the meeting app. That row can swallow several people's words.
- Short agreements add little weight. A long monologue adds a lot because the tool counts words.

`Jon` and `Jonathan` stay separate until you fix the paste. The page does not know your org chart.

## When the number is the wrong question

Minutes need decisions, owners and dates. Talk share is a weak stand-in for who decided. The quiet person may have been the one whose "no" closed the option. Put that on the decision line in the [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet). Leave word share out of the board pack unless participation was the agenda.

In research you sometimes code who held the floor. Code only names you trust. A chart from a nameless automatic transcript is not coding; it is a mixed voice, or nothing if the tool correctly refuses.

Summary models are the wrong counter. They write "Alex led the discussion" because the prose felt that way. That is not a word count. If the draft names someone the file never named, delete the name.

## If the file has no labels

Stop. Do not assign percentages from memory to a tool.

1. On a short call you attended, prefix a copy with `Name:` where you are sure. Leave gaps unlabeled. Count the copy, not the export.
2. On a longer recording, record or import on the Mac. Diarization gives anonymous labels; you rename them. Remembering a voice is optional for one meeting.
3. Skip the chart. Write minutes you can sign. Unsupported attribution does not belong in shared notes.

Do not split one nameless paragraph into fake turns to force a pie chart.

## A small procedure

1. Keep the original transcript unchanged.
2. For VTT or SRT, clean it and check names survived.
3. Normalize spellings on a copy.
4. Paste into [who held the floor](/tools/who-held-the-floor).
5. If the page says there are no speaker names, believe it.
6. Say one defensible sentence: "In this text, Jordan has the largest share of words." Do not upgrade it to "Jordan talked over everyone" unless you saw that.
7. File participation separately from decisions. The [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) holds decisions.

Live capture still needs people to know they are recorded. A participation chart is not consent by itself, not a performance review, and not a compliance report.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does who spoke most mean the most minutes?

Not on this page. You get each name's share of words in the paste, not seconds of audio.

### Why does the page refuse my transcript?

It found no speaker prefixes it recognizes. Timestamps alone are not names. You need labels in the text, diarization on the Mac, or hand labeling.

### Can I count speakers in a Zoom local recording?

Only after it is text with names. `audio_only.m4a` is audio. Cloud VTT sometimes has names; local audio does not. See [transcribe a local Zoom recording](/blog/zoom-local-recording-transcript) if you still need the words.

### Does this use one of my three weekly runs?

No. Word share and VTT cleaning do not run the speech model. Transcribing audio or drafting notes from a transcript does.

### What if someone joined from a conference room?

The export may label every word as the room name. Treat that row as a group, or do not report it as one person.

### Should I put the chart in the notes I send out?

Only if participation was the point and the names are real. Otherwise it distracts from owners and dates, and a wrong label becomes someone else's fact.

### Can the Mac app do this without the browser?

The app separates speakers into labels on the machine. The browser page counts a paste you already have. Use the app for labels; use the page for the share.

### Is a high share a problem?

Only when you wanted more voices. In a briefing, one speaker should have most of the words.
