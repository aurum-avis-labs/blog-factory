A **meeting minutes template** gives you the same headings every time so decisions and follow-ups do not get lost in chat threads. Good minutes are short, dated, and built around what changed: who agreed to what, by when, and what stays open. They are not a word-for-word transcript.

Below is a copyable blank template, filled examples for a team sync, a board (Verwaltungsrat) session, and a client call, plus mistakes to avoid. If you record the meeting on your Mac, [Vemoir](/) can draft these sections from audio on the device ([minutes and record](/#record), [export and share](/#exports)). If you already have plain text from elsewhere, the [meeting notes from transcript tool](/tools/meeting-notes-from-transcript) helps structure it in the browser without uploading your file.

## Blank meeting minutes template

Copy the block below into Notes, Google Docs, or your wiki. Replace the bracketed placeholders before the meeting starts so you only type outcomes during the call.

```text
Meeting title:
Date / time (timezone):
Location or link:
Facilitator:
Minute-taker:

Attendees (name, role / team):
Apologies:

Agenda (topic + timebox):
1.
2.
3.

Discussion summary (by topic, not verbatim quotes):
- Topic 1:
- Topic 2:

Decisions (what was agreed):
| # | Decision | Owner | Effective date |
|---|----------|-------|------------------|
| 1 |          |       |                  |

Action items:
| # | Action | Owner | Due date | Status |
|---|--------|-------|----------|--------|
| 1 |        |       |          | Open   |

Open questions / parked topics:
-

Next meeting:
Materials / links shared:
```

Keep one row per decision and one row per action. If a line does not have an owner and due date, move it to **Open questions** until it does.

## Example: weekly team sync

**Meeting title:** Product team weekly sync  
**Date / time:** 2026-10-06, 09:00–09:30 CET  
**Attendees:** Maya (PM), Jonas (Eng lead), Sara (Design), Alex (Support)

**Agenda:** (1) Release status 10 min, (2) Support themes 10 min, (3) Next sprint priorities 10 min.

**Discussion summary:**

- Release 2.4 is on track; one open bug in billing export blocks the build.
- Support sees repeated questions about SSO setup; docs update is in progress.
- Sprint goal: finish export fix, ship SSO guide, defer mobile polish to the following sprint.

**Decisions:**

| # | Decision | Owner | Effective date |
|---|----------|-------|----------------|
| 1 | Ship 2.4 only after export bug is closed | Jonas | 2026-10-08 |
| 2 | Mobile polish moves to sprint 48 | Maya | 2026-10-06 |

**Action items:**

| # | Action | Owner | Due date | Status |
|---|--------|-------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Patch billing export and post release notes | Jonas | 2026-10-08 | Open |
| 2 | Publish SSO setup guide | Sara | 2026-10-09 | Open |
| 3 | Send weekly support summary to product channel | Alex | 2026-10-07 | Open |

**Open questions:** Do we need a short Loom for SSO, or is the written guide enough? Revisit next week.

This shape fits a **meeting minutes template** for internal standups: decisions stay in the table, actions stay owned, and narrative stays in three bullets per topic.

## Example: Verwaltungsrat / board meeting

**Meeting title:** Verwaltungsrat Q4 planning  
**Date / time:** 2026-10-06, 14:00–16:00 CET  
**Location:** Zug (hybrid link for observers)

**Attendees:** Chair, three board members, CEO, CFO (guest for finance section)

**Agenda:** Approval of prior minutes, CFO report, budget 2027, risk register update, closed session if needed.

**Discussion summary:**

- Prior minutes approved without correction.
- CFO presented cash runway and hiring plan; board asked for a scenario with delayed enterprise deal.
- Risk register: two vendor concentration items flagged; mitigation owners assigned.

**Decisions:**

| # | Decision | Owner | Effective date |
|---|----------|-------|----------------|
| 1 | Prior minutes accepted as written | Chair | 2026-10-06 |
| 2 | Budget 2027 draft approved for shareholder circulation | CEO | 2026-10-20 |
| 3 | Enterprise deal scenario added to next CFO report | CFO | 2026-11-03 |

**Action items:**

| # | Action | Owner | Due date | Status |
|---|--------|-------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Circulate approved budget pack to shareholders | CEO | 2026-10-20 | Open |
| 2 | Update risk register with vendor mitigations | CFO | 2026-10-27 | Open |

**Open questions:** Closed session topic noted separately per governance rules; not repeated in shared minutes.

Board minutes often need a formal tone and explicit **Decisions** rows. The template stays the same; the facilitator reads decisions aloud so the minute-taker can capture them cleanly.

## Example: client call

**Meeting title:** Acme AG – phase 2 scope review  
**Date / time:** 2026-10-06, 11:00–11:45 CET  
**Attendees:** Client PM (Acme), your lead consultant, your delivery manager

**Agenda:** Recap phase 1, confirm phase 2 scope, agree on timeline and communication.

**Discussion summary:**

- Phase 1 deliverables accepted with one documentation tweak.
- Phase 2 adds reporting module; client prefers phased rollout.
- Weekly status email plus shared action log agreed.

**Decisions:**

| # | Decision | Owner | Effective date |
|---|----------|-------|----------------|
| 1 | Phase 2 starts 2026-10-20 | Client PM | 2026-10-06 |
| 2 | Reporting module delivered in two milestones | Your lead | 2026-10-06 |

**Action items:**

| # | Action | Owner | Due date | Status |
|---|--------|-------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Send updated SOW and milestone dates | Your lead | 2026-10-08 | Open |
| 2 | Client shares sample report layouts | Client PM | 2026-10-10 | Open |
| 3 | Schedule weekly status slot | Delivery manager | 2026-10-07 | Open |

**Open questions:** Which SSO IdP Acme will use in production (client IT to confirm).

Client-facing minutes should avoid internal jargon and mark anything confidential before you share. Export only the sections your contract allows ([export and share](/#exports) in Vemoir follows the same idea: you choose what leaves the Mac).

## Common mistakes when writing minutes

**Treating minutes as a transcript.** Readers want outcomes, not every filler word. Capture phrasing only when the exact wording matters (for example a formal board resolution). Otherwise summarize by topic in a few lines.

**Action items without owners.** "The team will look into it" creates follow-up meetings. Every action row needs a name and due date, or it belongs under **Open questions**.

**Decisions buried in narrative.** If the group agreed to something, put it in the **Decisions** table. Narrative explains context; the table is what people search for next month.

**Missing date, attendees, or next meeting.** Without metadata, the note is hard to trust in audits or client disputes. Fill the header before you start.

**Sharing the wrong version.** Keep draft minutes internal until the facilitator approves them, especially for board and client settings.

## When a template is not enough

A **meeting minutes template** does not record the meeting, prove consent, or replace your retention policy. You still need to tell participants when you record, store files where your organization allows, and edit generated text before you send it.

Templates also do not add speaker names by themselves. If diarization matters, you need a workflow that labels speakers (Vemoir on Mac does this locally with speaker profiles, not the free browser tools).

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a meeting minutes template include?

At minimum: title, date, attendees, agenda, a short discussion summary by topic, a decisions table, an action table with owners and due dates, open questions, and the next meeting date. The blank template above is a practical default.

### How long should meeting minutes be?

Often one to two pages for an hour-long operational meeting. Board and client minutes can be longer when formal resolutions need exact wording, but narrative should still stay shorter than a transcript.

### Are minutes the same as a transcript?

No. Minutes record outcomes and commitments. A transcript captures nearly every word. Use a transcript as source material, then distill it into the template tables.

### Who should write the minutes?

Usually a designated minute-taker who is not leading the discussion. On small teams, the facilitator may capture decisions live in the template during the call.

### Can I use this template for board / Verwaltungsrat meetings?

Yes. Keep decisions in their own table, note approvals explicitly, and follow your governance rules for closed sessions and circulation. The Verwaltungsrat example above shows the tone many Swiss boards expect.

### How do action items differ from decisions?

A **decision** states what the group agreed. An **action item** is work someone will do, with an owner and due date. One decision can spawn several actions.

### Does Vemoir replace my template?

It fills the same sections from a recording on your Mac, then you edit and export. You still choose wording, remove sensitive lines, and align with your policy. See [minutes and record](/#record).

### Can the browser tool write minutes from my transcript?

The [meeting notes from transcript tool](/tools/meeting-notes-from-transcript) drafts structured notes from text you already have, without uploading the file to Vemoir. It does not record live meetings or label speakers. Long or sensitive workflows belong in the Mac app.

### Do I need consent to record before using Vemoir?

Recording laws and workplace rules depend on your situation. Participants should know when a meeting is recorded, and you are responsible for retention and sharing. Vemoir does not make a recording lawful by itself.

### What if we already use Zoom cloud transcripts?

You can still use this template to normalize decisions and actions. If you only have local Zoom audio, start with the [free Zoom transcription tool](/tools/transcribe-zoom-recording) before you paste text into the template or the browser tool.
