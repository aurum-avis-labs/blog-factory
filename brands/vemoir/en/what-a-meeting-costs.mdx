**What a meeting costs** is the pay of the people in it, for as long as they stay. A 45-minute call with six salaries is six people times 0.75 hours times their rates. Software, the room, and the taxi are extra lines if you care about them. They are not the same number as the wages.

You do not need a transcript to get the wage figure. You need a duration and an hourly rate you are willing to write down. The [meeting cost calculator](/tools/meeting-cost) does the multiplication in the browser and returns one sentence in Swiss francs. Nothing is uploaded.

## What a meeting costs when rates differ

One hourly rate is enough when everyone in the room is priced the same way, which is rare outside a classroom. A client call often mixes a partner, two consultants, and someone from the client whose rate you may only know as a loaded cost. Put each rate on its own row. The duration is shared. The total is the sum of people on that row times rate times hours.

The calculator’s default currency is CHF. That is a formatting choice, not a claim about Swiss salaries. If your rate is in euros or dollars, the arithmetic is identical and the sentence the page prints is still in francs. Rewrite the currency symbol if you file the line outside Switzerland.

Do not hunt for a national average to drop into the form. Your cost is the rate you pay, or the rate you bill. A generic figure from a blog makes the minutes look precise and be wrong.

Loaded cost, if you use one, is salary plus what the employer adds for insurance, workspace, and similar overhead. The calculator does not know your overhead. Type the rate you already use internally. If you only have a salary, say so in the minutes, so a reader does not treat the sentence as a full cost.

## A worked example you can copy

Suppose 50 minutes, with three rows you enter yourself:

- 1 person at CHF 280 an hour
- 2 people at CHF 160 an hour
- 1 person at CHF 95 an hour

Hours are 50 divided by 60, which is 5/6 of an hour. The rows are 280 times 5/6, plus 2 times 160 times 5/6, plus 95 times 5/6. That is about CHF 233, plus CHF 267, plus CHF 79, about **CHF 579** for the 50 minutes. Your own rates will differ. The point of the example is the shape, not a recommended fee.

The calculator prints one sentence rather than a table you have to explain. Recurring weekly, the same room is 52 times that sentence, which is a different conversation from “it was only an hour.” Write the weekly version only if the meeting actually happens every week. Do not annualize a one-off workshop to scare a calendar.

External guests are a judgment call. If the client is not on your payroll, leave them off your cost line, or note their time as their cost, not yours. Putting both sides in one total mixes two companies’ money.

## What people forget to include

**Preparation and the write-up.** A 30-minute standup can hide 20 minutes of status collection. If you want the full cost, add that time as its own duration or add it to the minutes. The calculator will not guess it from the calendar title.

**The people who “only listen.”** They are still not at another task. Include them if their hour is yours. Drop them if they were optional and you want the minutes to show the decision-makers only. Say which you did.

**Partial attendance.** Someone who left after 15 minutes is not a full row at the full duration. The calculator uses one duration for every row. Split the meeting into two passes, or adjust the rate and write the assumption next to the sentence.

**Currency.** Cross-border calls mix CHF, EUR, and USD. Convert before you add, and name the rate date. The page will not do the FX for you.

The meeting’s software subscription is a separate overhead. Do not divide a yearly notetaker invoice by this one call unless finance asked for that allocation. Use the calculator for wages. When the question is the software bill, read the vendor’s own pricing page on the day you renew.

## What the number is not

The page does not say the meeting was a waste. A large number can be a board that had to meet, a hiring panel, or a client who asked for the hour. A small number can still be a meeting that should have been an email. Cost is not a verdict.

It is not a productivity score, not a calendar plugin, and not a reason to cancel a fiduciary or board sitting that the law or the client requires. You typed the inputs. The product is multiplication.

It does not read a transcript, so it cannot count speakers. If you want who held the floor, that is a different tool, and it needs labels that already exist in the text. This calculator never infers them.

It does not store the result. Copy it. If you refresh, enter the numbers again.

## Overhead the calculator will not invent

Travel, the room, and catering are real costs and they are not in the form. Add them in a second sentence if the meeting was off site. A video call has no room hire. It still has the wages.

Opportunity cost is a phrase people use to double-count. If you already included the hourly rate, do not multiply by a second "what they could have billed" unless that rate is different and you label it. Clients notice when the minutes charge the same hour twice.

Recurring meetings need a count of weeks, not a scare number. A weekly hour with the same four people for a quarter is 13 times the single-meeting sentence. Write 13 only if the quarter is booked. A cancelled week is not a cost.

Interns and apprentices are not a zero row unless their time is actually unpaid on your books. A low rate is still a rate. Leaving them off understates the meeting and trains the team to ignore junior time.

## Put the sentence on the minutes

After you run the calculator, copy the line or send it to the [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet). The sheet is a blank record you edit. The cost sentence is one line among decisions and actions, not a replacement for the protocol. A template for the rest of the page is in [meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template).

A practical order:

1. Write who was there and the start and end times.
2. Run the calculator with the rates you are allowed to show. Some firms omit money from the client-facing minutes and keep it in an internal note.
3. Record decisions and owners. A cost line without an owner is trivia.
4. Add action items. The cost of the meeting does not assign the work.

If the minutes are the signed record, a human still edits them. A calculator sentence is not a resolution.

## Frequently asked questions

### What a meeting costs in one formula?

People times hourly rate times hours, summed across groups that do not share a rate. Duration is the same only if they all stayed for the whole meeting.

### Why is the sentence in Swiss francs?

The calculator’s first market is Switzerland. Enter the rate you use. If you need euros or dollars on the page, convert and label the line yourself.

### Does a higher cost mean I should cancel?

No. The tool reports the product of the numbers you entered. It does not judge the agenda.

### Can I pull attendees from a transcript?

No. You type the headcount. The page does not open an audio file.

### Is the result stored on a server?

No. It runs in the browser. Copy the sentence if you need it later.

### How do I handle someone who joined late?

The form uses one duration for all rows. Run a second calculation for the shorter overlap, or note the assumption beside the sentence.

### Should I add the video tool’s subscription?

Only as a separate overhead line. It is not the wage cost of the room.

### Can the sentence go on the minutes sheet?

Yes. Use the handoff on the calculator page. You can still edit the sheet before anyone sees it.
