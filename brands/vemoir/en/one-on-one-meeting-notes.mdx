Good **one-on-one meeting notes** fit on half a page and cover five things: how the team member is doing and how full their plate is, their topics, the manager's topics, where goals stand with a next step, and the agreements with an owner and a date. Everything else is optional. The free [one-on-one template](/tools/minutes-sheet/one-on-one) has exactly these sections and a filled example you can overwrite.

Write the note right after the meeting, send it to the team member, and start the next one-on-one from the open agreements. Keep personal details out of it and out of shared folders. That is the whole method. The sections below explain each part, show a worked example and compare the one-on-one with the annual performance review.

## What to put in one-on-one meeting notes

A one-on-one belongs to the team member. The note should show that in its order: their state and their topics first, the manager's agenda after. Five sections are enough.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>How things are going and workload</h3><p>One or two sentences. Mood, current load, overtime if it is a pattern. This is the line you compare across meetings to spot a slow slide.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>The team member's topics</h3><p>What they brought: a training wish, a blocker, a question about remote days. Write their wording in short, not your interpretation.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>The manager's topics</h3><p>What you needed to raise: a new responsibility, a change in the team, feedback on a piece of work. Keep it factual.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Goals and development</h3><p>Each goal with its status and one next step. Without a next step, a goal just gets repeated meeting after meeting.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Agreements</h3><p>Who does what by when. One owner per line, a real date, no "we will look into it".</p></li>
</ol>

Add the date, both names and the date of the next one-on-one at the top. That header is what makes the note findable three months later.

## How short the notes should be

Half a page is a good upper limit. A one-on-one of 30 minutes rarely produces more than three agreements, and a note that runs to two pages is usually a transcript in disguise. Nobody rereads it, and it records more personal detail than anyone needs.

A useful test: could the team member read the note in one minute and recognize the conversation? If yes, it is long enough. If the note needs a summary, it is too long.

Short does not mean vague. "Talked about workload" is short and useless. "Workload high because of release 2.4, about 6 hours of overtime in two weeks" is short and gives you something to check next time.

## A worked example

Here is the filled example from the [one-on-one template](/tools/minutes-sheet/one-on-one), with Emma Clarke as manager and Daniel Okafor as team member:

```text
One-on-One Meeting Notes
Date: 2026-10-15
Team member: Daniel Okafor
Manager: Emma Clarke
Next one-on-one: Oct 29, 2026

How things are going and workload
In good spirits. Workload is high because of release 2.4.
About 6 hours of overtime in two weeks.

Team member's topics
- Wants training in cloud architecture
- Working from home on Fridays

Manager's topics
- Taking on onboarding for the new colleague starting in November

Goals and growth
Goal: Cloud architecture certification by June 2027
Status: Course selected
Next step: Submit training request

Agreements
- Submit training request | Daniel Okafor | Oct 24
- Confirm budget for the course | Emma Clarke | Oct 31
```

Three details make this note work. The workload line has a number, so the next meeting can ask whether the overtime went down after the release. The goal has a next step that is also an agreement, so it cannot stall quietly. And both agreements have a different owner, which makes clear that the manager owes something too.

Notice what is missing: the Friday remote day has no agreement yet. That is fine. Open topics carry over to the next meeting instead of being forced into a decision.

The template runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded or stored. You can export the note as Word (.docx), PDF or Markdown, and empty sections print as lines to write on if you prefer pen and paper in the meeting itself.

## Share the note with the team member

Send the note to the team member the same day. It takes a minute and avoids the most common problem with one-on-ones: two people leaving with two different memories of what was agreed.

Sharing also changes how you write. A note the other person will read stays factual and fair, because it has to. If you would not want the team member to read a sentence, it probably should not be in the note at all.

Ask for corrections in the reply. If the team member changes something, update the note and keep that version. The agreements then become the first item of the next one-on-one, and the general guide on [action items from meetings](/blog/action-items-from-meetings) applies: an owner, a date and a check at the next meeting.

Prefer to talk without typing? Vemoir records the meeting on your Mac without a bot joining and drafts minutes, decisions and actions afterwards (see [how the record works](/#record)). In a one-on-one, ask the team member before you record, and accept a no.

## One-on-one versus the annual performance review

The one-on-one and the annual performance review are different meetings with different documents. Many companies run both.

| | One-on-one | Annual performance review |
|---|---|---|
| How often | Regular, often every two weeks | Once a year, sometimes with a mid-year check |
| Length | 20 to 45 minutes | One to two hours |
| Purpose | Workload, blockers, development, agreements | Assessment of performance and goals over the year |
| Who sets the agenda | Mainly the team member | Mainly the HR process |
| Document | Short note shared by both | HR form, often filed with HR |

The two feed each other. A year of one-on-one notes is the best preparation for the annual review: goals with their status over time, agreements kept, topics that came back. Nothing in the review should be a surprise if the one-on-ones were honest. The annual form itself follows your HR process, not the one-on-one template.

## How often to meet

Every two weeks is a common rhythm and a good default. Weekly makes sense for someone new in the role or in a demanding project phase. Monthly can work with experienced people who work independently, as long as the meeting does not get canceled whenever it collides with something else.

The cadence matters less than reliability. A one-on-one that moves twice and is then skipped tells the team member where it ranks. Put the next date in the note before you close it, as the template does.

## What not to write down

Notes about a named employee are personal data. Treat them that way: write only what you need for the working relationship, and store the note where only you (and the team member, through the copy you sent) can see it.

If the team member mentions something private that affects work, record only the work consequence you agreed on, for example "reduced load until end of month, review on Nov 12". The reason can stay in the conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should the team member see the one-on-one notes?

Yes. Send them the note after the meeting so both of you have the same record of the agreements. A note you would not show them is a sign that something in it does not belong there.

### Does anyone sign one-on-one notes?

Usually not. A short reply from the team member confirming the agreements is enough. Signatures, where your company uses them, belong to the annual review form and its HR process.

### How long should I keep one-on-one notes?

As long as they are useful for open agreements and goals, typically until the next annual review. Delete old notes with personal details you no longer need, and follow your company's HR policy where it sets a rule.

### What if the team member disagrees with the note?

Correct it together and keep the corrected version. If you still see a point differently, write both views in one neutral sentence rather than silently keeping yours.

### Can I use one-on-one notes in the annual performance review?

Yes, as preparation for both sides. It works best when the team member has seen every note, so the review draws on a record you both already know.

### What if the team member's topics fill the whole meeting?

Then the meeting did its job. Move your own topics to the next one-on-one or a short message, unless one of them cannot wait.
