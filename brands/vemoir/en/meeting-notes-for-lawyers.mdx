**Meeting notes for lawyers** are a record of what was said and what someone must do next. They are not a privileged envelope, a certified transcript, or a statement that a recording was lawful. Whether a conversation is covered by professional secrecy, attorney-client privilege, or a similar rule is a legal question for the lawyer and the jurisdiction. Software does not answer it. What software can do is keep the audio on the Mac and turn a transcript you already trust into notes you edit.

If you already have text, the [meeting notes from a transcript](/tools/meeting-notes-from-transcript) page is the short path. It does not record the call.

## What meeting notes for lawyers need to contain

A usable note answers a future question: who was on the call, when it was, what was decided, what is still unknown, and who does the next thing. Advice, draft language, and a client’s personal story do not all belong in the same excerpt you might forward.

Write these as separate blocks:

- Matter or client name, as your firm already spells it.
- Date, start, and whether the note is a full transcript or a shortened record.
- People present, including anyone who joined late. A transcript will not always catch a mute arrival.
- Decisions, in sentences a colleague can act on.
- Open questions and who owns them.
- What you will not put in email.

The transcript is evidence of the words. The note is the document you are willing to rely on. Edit the note. Do not file the raw transcript as the advice. Numbers, names, and dates in particular are where speech-to-text quietly substitutes a near neighbor. Play that sentence back before it goes into a letter.

This article does not tell you that recording the client was permitted. In Switzerland, professional secrecy and the nLPD are part of the real constraints, and they are not implemented by a checkbox in an app. In other countries the words differ. Ask your own rules, your insurer, and the client. Consent and how long you keep the file are yours.

## Keep the audio off a vendor you did not intend

Two patterns create a processor you did not mean to hire.

**A bot that joins the meeting.** The audio goes to that vendor because that is how the bot works. Some clients and some firms forbid the extra participant outright. [Meeting notes without a bot](/blog/meeting-notes-without-a-bot) walks through that objection.

**A file upload “just to get the text.”** The browser tools on vemoir.ch are built so the file is not uploaded. The model runs in the tab. Limits are 15 minutes and 3 successful runs a week. There are no speaker labels. A hearing or a long client call will not fit. A 10-minute excerpt of instructions might.

For the whole call, a Mac app that records system audio and the microphone locally, then transcribes on the machine, matches the “do not hand the file to a new vendor” constraint. System Audio Recording is the macOS permission for other apps’ sound. It does not record the screen. Cloud text models are off unless you turn them on with your own key, and even then the audio stays on the Mac. Leave that switch off for client work unless you have already decided that vendor is allowed to see text.

None of this is a HIPAA claim, a GDPR certificate, or a privilege claim. “Local” means the default path does not send the recording to Vemoir. It does not mean a regulator has certified the workflow, and it does not mean the note is privileged because it never touched a server. Privilege is about the conversation and the law, not about the brand of the notepad.

Speaker labels are helpful and they are not testimony. Rename a wrong label. Unknown speakers should stay unknown rather than be guessed into a name that could be read as identification.

## From transcript to a note you can file

1. Start from text you are allowed to use. That might be a short browser run, a local recording, or a file the client sent.
2. Open [notes from a transcript](/tools/meeting-notes-from-transcript) for a first structure, or draft the blocks listed above yourself. The page does not invent speakers that were not in the text.
3. Replace every name and figure you did not personally check.
4. Separate legal advice from the narrative. If the note might be forwarded to the client, read it as the client will read it.
5. Put the finished note where the matter file already lives. The transcript tool is not a document management system.
6. Decide what happens to the audio. Keeping it can help you correct the note. Keeping it longer than your retention rule is a different risk. Delete it when your policy says to delete it. The app can drop a recording. It will not know your policy.

A copyable structure for minutes in general is in [meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template). Use the firm’s template when you have one. A generic sheet should not overwrite a precedent your partnership already approved.

Interface language can be English, German, French, Italian, or Dutch. Transcription language follows the speech model. Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages. That is not a promise about legal vocabulary. Add the terms you care about by reading the draft. A glossary in the Mac app can hold preferred spellings. The original transcript remains the evidence either way. This interest article is not a tour of unshipped features, and it does not quote a price.

## Where a firm system is the better file

A practice management or document system your firm already runs is the right place for the finished note. It has matter numbers, permissions, and retention your laptop’s Downloads folder does not. Use transcription to draft. File the result in the system the partnership trusts.

Dictation software is better when the job is a letter you are composing, not a call you are capturing. Do not force a meeting recorder to be a drafting tool.

A shared notetaker the whole firm has contracted, with a data-processing agreement you have read, can be better than a personal laptop when several lawyers must see the same client call and IT forbids local archives. The test is the contract and the client’s instruction, not the convenience of a bot. If the client said no outside tools, neither the firm bot nor a personal app is a clever exception. Take notes by hand.

Opposing counsel’s recording, if any, is not your transcript to “improve.” Do not upload someone else’s recording to find out what it says unless your role and the rules allow that.

Privilege, secrecy, and a clean file path are three different sentences. Keep them apart in any pitch or internal wiki. The product sentence is only the file path.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do meeting notes for lawyers become privileged because they stayed local?

No. This article does not claim privilege, secrecy, or a certified transcript. Local processing is a practical control. The legal character of the conversation is not a product feature.

### Can I record a client without telling them?

This page will not tell you that you can. Follow the rules that apply to your bar and your client. The software does not announce itself inside Zoom or Teams.

### Will the browser tool label who spoke?

No. Speaker labels are not part of the browser tools. A long call with several people needs a workflow that can separate speakers, or your own notes.

### What file length can I transcribe without installing an app?

Up to 15 minutes, three successful runs a week, MP3, M4A, WAV, or MP4, on the free tools. The file is not uploaded.

### Does Vemoir sign a GDPR certificate I can attach to a pitch?

No. Do not write “GDPR-certified” or “HIPAA” about this product. In Switzerland the data-protection statute people mean is the nLPD. Compliance is your process, not a badge.

### Where should the finished note live?

In the matter file your firm already uses. The transcript page is a drafting step.

### Can I ask questions about the call later?

On the Mac app, Ask Vemoir answers from that meeting and can point at the transcript line. Check the line before you rely on it in a letter. The browser tool does not offer that.

### What if the transcript misspells a party name?

Fix it in the note. Do not send the raw line. Names are the usual failure, and they are cheap to check and expensive to get wrong.
