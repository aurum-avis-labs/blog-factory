It is that number times seats, times twelve months, plus whatever usage line the vendor adds for summaries or extra storage. The meeting you just sat in has a separate cost: the people and the hours. ## What a meeting notes subscription cost actually multiplies

Most AI notetakers bill **per user per month**. Add a teammate and the invoice grows, even if that person joins two calls a week. Annual billing is lower per month and is still a renewal. Fireflies documents this shape in its billing article: a seat is created when a teammate is added, and removing someone does not refund the current cycle ([Fireflies billing terms](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/9606045468-fireflies-billing-terms), as of September 2026).

Otter’s pricing page lists Pro at $16.99 per user per month and Business at $30 per user per month, with lower figures when you pay for a year or accept a promotion. Pro includes 1,200 in-app recording minutes and 10 monthly file imports on the page as checked in September 2026 ([Otter pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing)). Business is the row that drops the in-app minute cap and shows a four-hour figure per conversation. Those caps matter more than the sticker price if you import files or run long workshops.

Granola’s pricing page lists Basic at $0 with limited meeting history, Business at $14 per user per month, and Enterprise at $35 per user per month ([Granola pricing](https://www.granola.ai/pricing), as of September 2026). That is still a seat. A four-person Business workspace is $56 a month before tax, or $672 over a year, if the page stays at $14.

Read the vendor page on the day you buy. Promotions, annual toggles, and “first-time subscriber” footnotes move the number you actually pay.

## Seat plans, usage plans, and the free tier that expires

Three patterns show up often enough to name them.

**Seat.** You pay for a person. Everyone who needs the shared library, the admin console, or the CRM push is a line on the invoice. This is the default for Otter, Fireflies, and Granola’s paid tiers.

**Usage on top of the seat.** Fireflies bills the seat and then meters AI credits for features such as AskFred, AI Skills, and custom summaries. The billing article says purchased credits do not carry into the next cycle. A quiet month does not bank the credits you skipped. Treat the credit pack as a second invoice, not as a rounding error.

**Minute pools.** Otter Pro’s 1,200 in-app minutes are a monthly allowance, not an unlimited recorder with a nicer summary. A team that lives in client calls can burn a pool without noticing, then pay to move up a tier. The free plan, where a vendor offers one, is usually a cap on minutes, imports, or history. It is useful for a trial. It is a poor place to keep a year’s archive if the history limit deletes the older notes.

A one-time Mac purchase is a fourth pattern: no seat count and no renewal for the app itself. That price belongs on a product page, not in this explainer. The homepage states it under [pricing](/#pricing).

## A year of seats, worked as arithmetic

Use list prices and your own headcount. Tax, currency, and discounts are yours to add.

Take Fireflies Pro at the annual rate published in the billing article: $10 per user per month, billed annually, or $18 if you pay month to month (as of September 2026). Five people on the annual rate are $50 a month, $600 a year, before AI credit add-ons. The same five people on monthly billing are $90 a month, $1,080 a year. The software did not get better in the second case. The commitment did.

Take Otter Business at the $30 monthly list price from the pricing page. Five users are $150 a month, $1,800 a year, before any annual discount the page is offering that week. If the page’s annual Business row is the one you qualify for, use that row, not a blog’s memory of it.

Take Granola Business at $14. Five users are $70 a month, $840 a year, if you stay on that tier and the page does not change.

None of these figures includes the time an admin spends on seats, the storage you later export, or the cost of the meetings being recorded. They also assume every seat is used. A seat for someone who never turns the product on is still a seat.

## When the subscription is the better buy

A per-user subscription is the right shape when the company wants one shared workspace, calendar auto-join, and an admin who can remove a leaver in one place. A local app on one laptop does not give the sales team a common library. If ten hosts each need the same customer calls in a CRM, the seat is doing a job a single Mac cannot do. [Fireflies](/blog/fireflies-alternative) and [Otter](/blog/otter-ai-alternative) are built for that shared record. Pay for it when that is the job.

The subscription is a poor fit when one person takes notes for their own clients, the audio must stay on that Mac, and nobody else needs a login. Renewing $10 to $30 a seat every month for a workflow you could run on the machine is how a small bill becomes a standing cost. You still have to tell people you are recording. Consent does not come with the plan.

## The cost of the meeting is a different number

Software is a line on an expense report. The meeting is wages. Five people for one hour is five hours of pay, whether or not a bot joined. Mixed rates belong on separate rows: two partners at one hourly figure, three analysts at another, same duration.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a meeting notes subscription cost billed per user?

Usually yes for the products above. Otter, Fireflies, and Granola publish per-user prices on their pricing or billing pages. A “workspace” discount does not erase the seat.

### Does annual billing remove the subscription?

No. Annual billing lowers the monthly equivalent and charges you for the year. You still renew.

### What is a usage charge on top of the seat?

Fireflies sells AI credits for some assistant features. The billing article says unused purchased credits do not roll over. Read which actions spend a credit before you assume the seat includes every summary.

### Do free plans store a year of notes?

Often they limit minutes, imports, or history. Granola’s free Basic plan says meeting history is limited. Check the current cap on the vendor page. Do not put the only copy of a client decision in a tier that drops old notes.

### Should I include the software bill inside the meeting cost?

Keep them separate. The calculator is for wages in the room. The subscription is an overhead cost you can note beside it if finance asks.

### Does the calculator need the audio?

No. You enter duration and rates. It does not read a transcript and it does not record the call.

### Why does a five-person tool cost more than five times “one user”?

It should not, if the price is a clean per-user rate. It does if you also buy credit packs, extra storage, or a higher tier so that one heavy user does not hit a minute cap.

### Where do I check the numbers?

On the vendor’s pricing page, the day you decide: [Otter](https://otter.ai/pricing), [Fireflies billing](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/9606045468-fireflies-billing-terms), [Granola](https://www.granola.ai/pricing). The figures in this article are as of September 2026.
