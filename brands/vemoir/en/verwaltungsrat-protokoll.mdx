A **Verwaltungsrat Protokoll** is the minutes of a Swiss board of directors: what was deliberated and what was resolved. Article 713 paragraph 3 of the Code of Obligations says a protocol of the deliberations and resolutions is to be kept, and that the chair and the minute-taker sign it. The current German text is on [Fedlex, article 713](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/de#art_713). This article is not legal advice. Counsel and the articles of association win over a blog if they disagree.

The working draft can start on a [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet). The signed protocol is a company record. A web form is not the signature.

## What the statute actually asks for

Article 713 paragraph 3 CO, in the version consolidated on Fedlex, requires a protocol of **deliberations and resolutions**, signed by the **chair** and the **minute-taker**. It does not say “a transcript of the call.” A verbatim tape of an hour is not automatically the protocol. The protocol is the document those two people sign.

Paragraph 2 of the same article is why remote boards still need minutes. Resolutions can be taken at a meeting with a place, by electronic means, or in writing, under the conditions the article states. Electronic or written does not delete the protocol duty in paragraph 3. Read the whole article and the company statutes. A blog will not know if your articles change the chair’s casting vote or who may act as minute-taker.

Practice in many companies also records who attended, who was excused, conflicts, and how the vote fell. Commentators often add that the discussion should be detailed enough that a later reader can see the board was informed. That is a reason to write more than the resolution line. It is not a sentence this page is qualified to turn into a legal duty beyond the statute you just opened.

Do not copy a retention period from memory into the minutes. Ask counsel how your company treats these protocols among its records. Getting the signature right matters more than a polished summary.

## A Verwaltungsrat Protokoll you can draft the same day

During the meeting, someone writes. After the meeting, someone cleans the draft while memory still matches the recording or the notes. A usable draft has:

<p><em>Photo by <a href="https://unsplash.com/@prateekkatyal?utm_source=blog_factory&utm_medium=referral" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">prateekkatyal</a> on <a href="https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=blog_factory&utm_medium=referral" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Unsplash</a></em></p>

- Company name, date, start and end, place or the electronic means used.
- Chair and minute-taker named, because those are the people who will sign.
- Present, absent, excused. Guests and the agenda items they attended.
- Each agenda item: short state of the information the board had, the resolution in a single block that can be quoted later, and the result of the vote if it was not unanimous.
- Recusals. A director who stepped out should not look, in the protocol, as if they voted.
- Next meeting, if one was set.
- Signature lines for chair and minute-taker.

The [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) is a blank place to assemble that list. [Meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template) is a more general template. Prefer your counsel’s precedent for the resolution wording, especially for dividends, capital, and appointments. A generic sheet must not invent legal language.

If the meeting was recorded, the recording is a memory aid for the minute-taker. It is not the signed protocol. Transcribe a short stretch in the browser if you need one passage checked: 15 minutes, 3 runs a week, no upload, no speaker labels, via [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording). A full board meeting is longer than that cap. A local recording on the Mac that hosted the call can cover the whole sitting without a bot in the participant list. Tell the board. Guests from outside the company are not a reason to skip the announcement.

Swiss German in the room will not come back as dialect text from the public Whisper models. The protocol is usually written in the company’s language in standard spelling anyway. You are writing a record, not subtitling speech.

## Resolutions versus the discussion that led there

Write the resolution so a banker, a notary, or a new director can read it without the recording. “Approved as discussed” is how later disputes start. Name the document that was in front of the board, the version, and the decision.

The deliberation can be shorter than the transcript and should still show the shape of the discussion: the option rejected, a concern a director asked to record, an expert who attended for one item. Directors sometimes want a dissent noted. If they asked, note it. If you smooth every disagreement into “the board discussed and approved,” you are not recording the deliberation the statute names.

Circular resolutions have their own formalities under the same article. Do not force a circular into the shape of a seated meeting minute, and do not skip a protocol because there was no video call. Follow the article and the statutes.

Conflicts and related-party items need the recusal visible. The transcript will not reliably know that someone left the room unless the minute-taker writes it.

The protocol is signed. An unsigned shared doc in a chat is a draft. Track which version was signed, and store that one with the company records. Editing a draft after signature is a new problem. If a correction is needed, follow whatever process your counsel uses for correcting protocols. Do not silently overwrite the file.

Number the resolutions if your precedent does that, and keep the numbers stable once the draft circulates. A director who comments “I agree with item 4” must still be talking about the same item when you insert a line above it. If you insert, renumber and say that you renumbered.

Attachments the board had in advance (a budget PDF, a contract draft) should be identified by title and date in the protocol, and stored with it or in the data room you already use. “See attached” with no file is a hole. The transcript of the discussion does not contain the spreadsheet.

## Where a signed paper beats a transcript

The signed protocol is the better record whenever someone outside the meeting must rely on it: a bank, a commercial register filing, an auditor, a buyer in due diligence. They are not going to watch the recording. Give them the resolution text and the signatures.

A transcript is the better aid when the minute-taker missed a number and the audio can settle it before signature. After signature, the audio is a working paper with a retention question, not a second protocol that quietly contradicts the signed one. If they differ, fix the situation with counsel before you circulate both.

A cloud notetaker that joined the board call may be forbidden by the directors or by a guest. Board meetings are a common place to refuse extra participants. Local notes or a company-approved recorder match that constraint better than a bot. [Meeting notes without a bot](/blog/meeting-notes-without-a-bot) is the general explanation. The protocol still has to be written and signed by the people article 713 names.

An internal strategy workshop that is not a board meeting does not need this form. Do not label every management call a Verwaltungsrat protocol. The statute is about the board’s deliberations and resolutions, not about a product standup.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Verwaltungsrat Protokoll the same as a transcript?

No. The protocol is the signed record of deliberations and resolutions. A transcript is raw speech and can help you draft.

### Who signs?

Article 713 paragraph 3 CO names the chair and the minute-taker. Read the current Fedlex text and your statutes before you substitute other titles.

### Can the meeting be remote?

Article 713 paragraph 2 allows electronic means in the cases it describes. Remote does not remove the protocol. Confirm the current wording on Fedlex.

### Does this page give legal advice?

No. It points at the statute and at a drafting habit. Your counsel decides disputed points.

### Can I transcribe the whole board meeting in the browser?

Not if it exceeds 15 minutes. The free tool also allows only three successful runs a week and does not label speakers.

### Should the audio be kept next to the signed protocol?

Only under the retention approach your company and counsel set. The signed document is the protocol. The audio is a source.

### What if a director wants a dissent in the minutes?

Write it if they asked for it to be recorded. Do not upgrade a passing remark into a formal dissent they did not request.

### Is a template enough for a capital increase or a dividend?

No. Use counsel’s wording for resolutions that have a formal life outside the company. The minutes sheet is a structure, not a filing.

## Next step

Open the current article on [Fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/de#art_713), draft the resolutions in the [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet), and put the text in front of the chair and the minute-taker for signature. Do not treat the draft as the protocol before they sign.
