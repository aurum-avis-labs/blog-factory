## What Jamie does well

[Jamie](https://www.meetjamie.ai/) is built for people who will not put a notetaker in the participant list. Its own write-up describes a German product that records online and in-person meetings without a bot, writes notes, transcripts, and action items, and keeps storage and processing in the EU, with servers it places in Germany. The same page says audio is processed there and then deleted after a transcript exists, and that your data is not used for third-party AI training ([Jamie LLM info](https://www.meetjamie.ai/blog/llm-info), page dated in 2026, checked September 2026).


That combination is the reason Jamie shows up in European buying conversations next to tl;dv. You get bot-free capture and a written EU story, on a subscription, with notes that live in Jamie’s service so you are not tied to one laptop’s disk. Jamie also publishes compliance language on its own site, including ISO 27001 and GDPR wording. Those are Jamie’s claims about Jamie. They are not a certificate you can paste onto another product, and they do not make your recording lawful.

Plan names and euro amounts belong on [Jamie pricing](https://meetjamie.ai/pricing). Secondary reviews disagree on the exact monthly number. Do not budget from a blog. Confirm whether your plan includes the meeting length and the note allowance you actually use.

Jamie is a cloud assistant that happens to listen on the device. It is not an offline Mac recorder. If the network is down after you press stop, read Jamie’s current help before you assume the transcript will finish on the laptop alone.

## How to choose a Jamie AI alternative

Sort tools by where the file lives after the call. Ignore the calmest homepage.


1. **Must the audio stay on the Mac?** Jamie says audio is deleted after transcription and that notes stay in the EU. A local app keeps the recording on disk unless you export it or turn on a cloud text model yourself.
2. **Do you need another device?** A subscription that syncs notes across computers beats a Mac-only app when you review meetings on a phone or a Windows machine.
3. **Do you need a shared team archive or CRM?** Jamie’s strength is the personal or small-team no-bot path with EU processing. A sales library with clips and seat admin is a different product.

If the meeting is already a file, you do not need Jamie’s live capture. Vemoir’s [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) tool runs in the browser tab. Nothing is uploaded. The cap is 15 minutes, 3 successful runs a week, MP3, M4A, WAV, or MP4, and no speaker labels. Live capture and speaker separation are in the Mac app.

For the next live call on a Mac, you can record from the menu bar without a participant named Jamie.

## Seven Jamie AI alternatives

### Vemoir (Mac app)

**Best for:** keeping audio, transcript, and the generated record on your Mac by default, with no bot and no subscription.

Vemoir is a native Mac app from Aurum Avis Labs GmbH in Oberägeri, Switzerland. You start and stop each recording. Microphone and system audio are separate local tracks, so the app follows Teams, Zoom, Google Meet, Slack huddles, Discord, and any other app that plays the call ([platforms](/#platforms)). Nobody sees an extra participant. You tell people you are recording.

Transcription and speaker separation run on the Mac. Minutes, decisions, actions, Ask Vemoir, and documents run locally unless you explicitly assign text generation to OpenAI or Anthropic with your own key. There is no silent fallback. Audio stays on the device. After models are installed, the workflow can run offline. Internet is for setup and downloads.

Notes you type during the meeting fold into the record. You share only what you choose, through macOS sharing ([the record](/#record)). macOS asks for Microphone and System Audio Recording. Vemoir uses those for audio only and does not record the screen.

Price is a one-time Mac App Store purchase: CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US ([pricing](/#pricing)). Apple silicon, macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, 8 GB of memory, 10 GB free. 16 GB is recommended for long meetings. No Windows, iPhone, iPad, Android, or Intel Mac support.

### tl;dv

**Best for:** a searchable team library of Zoom, Meet, and Teams, with EU hosting described by the vendor, usually with a bot.

tl;dv’s security commitment says no customer data is used to train its AI, that AI hosting can be Europe or the US, and that listed data centers are in Europe ([tl;dv security](https://tldv.io/features/security-commitment/), checked September 2026). If you left a bot product to get Jamie’s no-bot path, tl;dv’s ordinary join flow is a step back on that one point, and a step forward if the company needs one archive. See [tl;dv pricing](https://tldv.io/app/pricing/).

### Granola

**Best for:** a lightweight notepad on computer audio, without a bot, if US cloud notes are acceptable.

Granola says it does not keep meeting audio long term and stores transcripts and notes in encrypted cloud infrastructure ([Granola security](https://www.granola.ai/security), checked September 2026). Its privacy FAQ says anonymized data may be used for Granola’s own model improvements on Free and Business plans, with an opt-out, and that third parties such as OpenAI and Anthropic are not allowed to train on your data ([Granola privacy FAQ](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs)). That training opt-out is a different sentence from Jamie’s “we do not train, and the audio is deleted.”

### Fathom

**Best for:** free-tier recording volume, clips, and CRM, with a documented bot-free path that is narrower than Jamie’s.

Fathom’s 3.0 help article describes bot-free modes on Mac for English-language meetings, and a bot mode for audio and video ([Fathom 3.0](https://help.fathom.video/en/articles/11577345), checked September 2026). Do not treat that article as “any language, any operating system.” Pricing is on [Fathom pricing](https://fathom.video/pricing). The fuller decision guide is [Fathom alternative](/blog/fathom-alternative).

### MacWhisper

**Best for:** a file you already have in Finder.

MacWhisper transcribes locally by default. Cloud providers run only if you enable them ([Keeping transcriptions private](https://docs.macwhisper.com/article/52-keeping-transcriptions-private), updated July 27, 2025). Pro is listed as a one-time €64 license on the site (checked September 2026). Meeting recording exists, including a beta detector that needs Screen Recording permission and is absent from the Mac App Store build ([Record meetings](https://docs.macwhisper.com/article/30-record-meetings)). If the job is the file, MacWhisper wins. If the job is the next live call with minutes and decisions, it is a different shape of product.

### Otter.ai

**Best for:** a visible calendar bot, mobile apps, and shared workspaces.

Otter joins Zoom, Teams, and Meet on subscription plans ([Otter pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing), checked September 2026) and documents how the notetaker appears ([Otter help](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/39339238308503-Recording-Permissions-with-Otter)). Choosing Otter because you dislike Jamie’s cloud still leaves you with a bot and a US-centered product story. Read Otter’s own trust pages before you assume a residency match.

### Fireflies.ai

**Best for:** per-seat conversation intelligence and CRM workflows in a cloud workspace.

Fireflies stores recordings and transcripts in its cloud ([Fireflies pricing](https://fireflies.ai/pricing), checked September 2026). Its privacy policy says it does not use personal information for AI model training and contractually bars vendors from doing so ([Fireflies privacy policy](https://fireflies.ai/privacy-policy)). The terms allow derived usage statistics. It is a team bot product, not a no-bot EU desktop app.

A short exported file can go to [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording). That does not replace Jamie’s live session.

## Where Jamie is still the better choice

Jamie is the better default when you want bot-free capture, a vendor that puts processing in the EU in its own words, and notes that follow you off a single Mac. A one-time Mac app does not sync a library to Windows or to a phone. If you already pay Jamie and the team is happy with the note quality, switching to avoid a subscription only pays off when the audio-on-device requirement is real.

Jamie is also the clearer fit for in-person conversations its own pages say it records, when no meeting app is playing audio on a computer. Vemoir records what the Mac hears through the microphone and system audio. It does not ship a separate in-person room mode, and it does not run on a phone you leave on the table.

If procurement already accepted Jamie’s ISO and EU write-up, and the open question is only “which EU notes app,” do not rip that out for a Mac utility that your Windows colleagues cannot open.

## Honest limits to plan for

“Audio deleted after transcription” means you cannot replay the call later inside that product. If a client dispute needs the recording, a notes-only archive is the wrong artifact. Vemoir lets you choose, in Storage settings, whether future meetings keep their audio. Each meeting keeps the choice that was active when it was created. That is a retention decision you make, not a compliance badge.

Local models need disk and a first download. Long meetings are happier with 16 GB of memory. Vemoir has no team workspace and no CRM push. Ask Vemoir searches meetings on that Mac ([chat](/#chat)). The browser tool will not label speakers, will not run longer than 15 minutes, and allows 3 successful runs a week. Swiss German in those public Whisper models usually comes out as Standard German. The Mac app does not claim dialect output.

None of these products makes recording lawful. You still tell participants. Vemoir does not claim to be compliant, HIPAA-ready, or GDPR-certified. Jamie’s own compliance sentences stay on Jamie’s site.

MacWhisper remains better when the only job is a file in Finder. Do not buy a live meeting app for a folder of M4A interviews unless you also need speaker-aware minutes from a live call.

## A short test on a real call

Take one client call you are allowed to record. Run Jamie the way you do today and note, the next morning, whether you can replay audio, which country the vendor’s page says the notes sit in, and whether a colleague on Windows can open them without you sending a file.

Then record a similar call with Vemoir ([how it works](/#how)). Stop the recording, let the Mac finish, and export only the minutes ([exports](/#exports)). Check the same three questions. If replay matters and the file must stay on the machine, the Mac app matches that constraint. If Windows colleagues must open the same notes without an export, Jamie matches that constraint better.

For a 10-minute file you already have, skip both live apps and use [transcribe a recording](/tools/transcribe-recording). If the language mix is German and English, pick the local model on purpose. Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages. The app interface language is separate ([languages](/#languages)).

After both trials, list the edits you still made by hand. A tool that leaves you fixing names is normal. Drop the one that also puts a bot in the room or uploads audio you promised to keep local.

If you run both tools for a week, say in the invite which one records that day. Two recordings of the same meeting give you two records, and nobody knows which one counts.

Before you cancel, check three practical points. Can you replay the disputed meeting, or only read the note? Can someone on Windows open the text without your email? Does the audio stay on the Mac if you export nothing? Jamie wins the second when notes live in its service. Vemoir wins the first and third when you keep audio in Storage settings. A switch for price alone only pays off when at least two of those three matter more than the app you already use.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a Jamie AI alternative that never uploads the audio?

Vemoir transcribes on the Mac by default. Audio is not sent unless you export it. A cloud text model, if you turn one on, receives text, not the raw audio. Jamie’s own pages describe EU processing and deletion of audio after transcription, which is an upload followed by deletion, not a file that never left the Mac.

### Does Jamie add a bot?

Jamie says it does not. Fathom documents bot-free modes only in a narrower Mac and English setup. Otter and Fireflies join as notetakers. Read the current product page before you promise a client “no extra name,” because vendors change capture paths.

### Where are Jamie’s notes stored?

Jamie’s 2026 write-up says storage and processing stay in the EU, on servers in Germany, and that audio is deleted after transcription. Confirm that on [their page](https://www.meetjamie.ai/blog/llm-info) the week you review the tool. Marketing sentences and the privacy policy should be read together.

### Can I use Jamie offline?

Do not assume it. The published design processes audio on EU servers. Vemoir can run the meeting workflow offline after the models you chose are installed.

### What if I only have a recording file?

Use the [browser tool](/tools/transcribe-recording) for up to 15 minutes and 3 successful runs a week, with no upload and no speaker labels. Longer files belong in the Mac app or in MacWhisper.

### How much does Vemoir cost compared with a subscription?

Vemoir is one Mac App Store payment: CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US. Jamie is a subscription. Compare that to how many months you would pay, and to whether you need Windows or phone access. A cheaper Mac app is not a better team archive.

### Does Vemoir work in German, French, and Italian?

The interface is available in those languages, plus English and Dutch. Transcription language is a separate choice. Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages. That is not a promise of Swiss German dialect text.

### Who should stay on Jamie?

People who want bot-free capture, EU processing in the vendor’s own words, and notes that are not stuck on one Mac. Also anyone whose meetings are in the room, away from a computer, if Jamie’s current in-person path is what they rely on.
