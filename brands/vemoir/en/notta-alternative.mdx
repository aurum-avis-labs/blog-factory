A **Notta alternative** is worth a look when you like automatic transcripts and summaries, and you do not want the audio uploaded for that transcript, or you do not want a bot in the participant list. Notta covers both patterns on its own site: a calendar bot for Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex, and a desktop capture that records microphone and system audio without adding a participant. The desktop path still sends audio to Notta's transcription service. That is the split this comparison hangs on.

Prices and plan names move. Figures below are from Notta's pricing page and product pages, checked September 2026. Open the links before you buy.

## What Notta documents

[Notta](https://www.notta.ai/en) describes itself as an AI meeting assistant that transcribes meetings, interviews, and lectures, then summarizes them. The bot page says you can connect a calendar so a meeting bot joins Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex when the meeting URL is on the invite, and transcribes for you ([Notta Bot](https://www.notta.ai/en/features/notta-bot), checked September 2026).

The same page says Notta Desktop captures microphone and system audio through the operating system, so no "Notta Bot" participant appears. Other attendees do not see an extra person. You still get a transcript with speaker separation and AI notes. The bot is the path when you cannot be on the call. The desktop app is the path when you are on the call, when the platform blocks bots, or when you do not want a join notification.

Notta's recording page says meetings can be recorded on Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, and Webex, including a path that does not ask the host for recording permission because no virtual bot joins. It also says recordings are saved in the cloud, and that people you invite can open the notes ([meeting recording](https://www.notta.ai/en/features/meeting-recording-software), checked September 2026). A later section of that page describes two streams (you, and the voices on the call) routed to Notta's transcription service as separate channels, with echo cancellation and noise handling applied before the audio is uploaded.

The pricing FAQ says customer data is hosted on Amazon Web Services, with backups, and that Notta is SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 certified, with firewalls and access restrictions ([pricing](https://www.notta.ai/en/pricing), checked September 2026). The homepage also uses SOC 2 and GDPR in its security line. Those are Notta's statements. They do not, by themselves, tell you whether a given client contract allows a cloud transcript.

## Bot, desktop, and where the audio goes

Treat "no bot" and "audio stays here" as two different questions.

| Path | Who sees it | Where transcription runs, per Notta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notta Bot | A participant can appear in the meeting | Notta's service, cloud recording |
| Notta Desktop | No extra participant | Audio is uploaded to Notta's transcription service; recordings are stored in the cloud |

If your goal is "nobody sees a notetaker," desktop capture answers that, and so does recording the Mac's own audio with another app. If your goal is "this audio is not uploaded unless I export it," a cloud transcription service does not answer that, even when the capture looks local.

Notta also sells the bot for meetings you do not attend. A menu-bar recorder on your Mac cannot join a call in another city. If that unattended capture is the job, the bot is the product, and a local app is the wrong shape.

## What Notta charges

Checked September 2026, [Notta pricing](https://www.notta.ai/en/pricing) lists:

- **Free:** $0, 120 transcription minutes a month, up to 3 minutes per conversation, 50 file uploads a month, 10 AI summaries a month.
- A paid tier shown at **$9 USD a month billed annually** ($108), with a note that the monthly plan is **$10 USD**. The page associates a single seat, 1,800 transcription minutes a month, up to 5 hours per recording, 100 uploads, and 100 AI summaries with the paid individual band. Confirm which name sits on that price when you open the page. The layout groups numbers tightly.
- A higher band references **$15 USD a month** as a monthly plan, with unlimited transcription, up to 5 hours per recording, and 200 uploads.
- **Enterprise:** custom, starting from 51 seats, with SAML SSO.

The same FAQ says canceling a paid plan does not delete transcript data, but a free account can view only the first 3 minutes of a transcript. Export before you downgrade if you need the rest. Educators and students can apply a Pro discount with an educational email. Payment can be card, or an in-app purchase through Apple or Google.

Minutes do not carry over. A 3-minute cap on the free conversation length is the practical limit for anyone testing a real meeting. A one-hour call does not fit in a 3-minute free conversation.

## How to choose a Notta alternative

Three questions sort the market faster than a feature grid.

1. **Must something join the call when you are absent?** If yes, you are shopping for a bot. Local recorders drop out.
2. **May the audio be uploaded for transcription?** If no, cloud notetakers drop out, including bot-free desktop apps that upload.
3. **What do you need after the call?** A shared team workspace, a phone app, a CRM field, or a local decision log point at different tools.

If you only have a file, and it is 15 minutes or shorter, you do not need a meeting product at all. Vemoir's free [transcribe recording](/tools/transcribe-recording) tool runs in the browser tab. The file is not uploaded. The model downloads once. You get 3 successful runs a week. It does not label speakers, and it does not record a live call. MP3, M4A, WAV, or MP4 only.

## Notta alternatives that fit different jobs

### Vemoir, when the Mac is the system of record

Vemoir is a Mac app from Aurum Avis Labs GmbH in Oberägeri. It records from the menu bar: microphone and system audio as separate local tracks. You start and stop. No bot joins. macOS permission for the call audio is System Audio Recording, an audio-only tap. It does not record the screen.

Transcription and speaker separation run on the Mac. Summaries, actions, decisions, documents, and Ask Vemoir run on the Mac by default. You can turn on your own OpenAI or Anthropic key for text only. Audio does not go with that request. There is no silent fallback if the provider fails.

After the call you get minutes, decisions (wording, person, date), action items, and a follow-up you can copy or send to an Apple Notes folder. Evidence links can open the transcript line. The interface is in English, German, French, Italian, and Dutch. With Parakeet v3, transcription covers 25 European languages. That is standard language coverage, not a dialect mode.

Price in the Mac App Store, as listed on [pricing](/#pricing): CHF 20 in Switzerland, €24.99 in Germany, Austria, France, and Italy, $22.99 in the US. One-time purchase, no per-meeting charge, no seat count. Requires Apple silicon (M1 or newer), macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later, 8 GB of memory, and 10 GB free. 16 GB is recommended for long meetings. Intel Macs are not supported. The store listing is [Vemoir on the Mac App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6794693630).

This is a poor fit if you need Windows, a phone recorder, a bot that attends without you, or a shared cloud workspace for a sales team.

### Otter, when the team already lives in a cloud notetaker

Otter joins Zoom, Meet, and Teams, offers live transcription, and sells per-user plans with minute pools. The [Otter pricing](https://otter.ai/pricing) page, checked September 2026, lists a free Basic plan at 300 transcription minutes a month, and Pro in the neighborhood of $8.33 to $16.99 per user depending on the billing toggle the page shows. Treat the live page as the number. A longer comparison is in [Otter.ai alternative](/blog/otter-ai-alternative), including what Otter publishes about storage and training in [Is Otter.ai safe?](/blog/is-otter-ai-safe).

Pick Otter over a local Mac app when the company already shares one transcript, uses mobile apps, and accepts U.S. cloud processing. Pick a local app when that sharing model is the problem you are trying to leave.

### Granola and Fireflies, for two other cloud shapes

Granola listens on the computer without adding a participant and keeps notes in its cloud. Fireflies is a team conversation product with a bot and per-seat plans. Neither is "audio never leaves." Read [Granola alternative](/blog/granola-alternative) and [Fireflies alternative](/blog/fireflies-alternative) before you treat them as private-by-default. They are reasonable when the team wants shared search more than a local file.

### A file you already have

Notta's paid plans accept uploads, with monthly caps on the tiers above. If the recording already exists and you only need text, a browser tool or a local import avoids a new subscription. On the Mac, Vemoir imports common audio (MP3, M4A, WAV, AIFF, CAF, FLAC) through the same local pipeline as a live recording. A file-only transcriber that never builds minutes can still be the better tool when you want text in Finder and nothing else. Do not buy a meeting system for that job.

### A minutes sheet when you will not record

If the call cannot be recorded, automatic transcription is the wrong product. The [minutes sheet](/tools/minutes-sheet) is a blank in the browser: decisions, actions, open questions. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no model on that page. The copyable wider form is the [meeting minutes template](/blog/meeting-minutes-template).

## Where Notta is still the better choice

Say this plainly. Notta is the better product in several real situations.

**You are not in the meeting.** A bot on the calendar invite can attend Zoom, Meet, Teams, or Webex. Vemoir only hears audio playing on your Mac, or a file you import. If you need coverage while you are in another room, the bot wins.

**The team works on phones and Windows.** Notta's pricing page describes one account for web and mobile, and in-app purchases on Apple and Google. Vemoir is Apple silicon Macs only. A consultant who records on an iPhone between laptops should not force that habit onto a Mac-only app.

**You want a shared workspace.** On Business and Enterprise, Notta's FAQ says members of a team workspace can view and collaborate on each other's content. Vemoir's library is the Mac user. You share by export: copy, a Markdown document, or Apple Notes. There is no org admin console, no SAML, and no seat management.

**You already want the audio in a vendor cloud.** Search across a company's calls, invite a teammate with a link, and keep playback "anywhere" are cloud features. Notta's recording page is explicit that recordings are stored in the cloud. If that is the point, paying Notta for it is coherent. Pretending a local app does the same share model would be false.

**You need a short free test of their bot.** 120 minutes a month is enough to see the join flow, if you accept the 3-minute cap per free conversation. That cap makes the free tier a poor test of a real sales call. It is still a way to see the product without a card.

**Education pricing.** Notta documents a student and educator discount on Pro. A one-time Mac app does not become cheaper because of a `.edu` address.

## Limits to plan for

On Vemoir, live capture is the Mac app, not the website. The browser tools do not record calls, do not label speakers, and stop at 15 minutes and 3 runs a week. They use public Whisper models. Swiss German speech usually comes out as Standard German text. The Mac app does not claim dialect output either. A dialect path is not something to schedule against.

Local models have to be downloaded before you can work offline. Internet is for setup and those downloads. Apple Intelligence, if the Mac has it, can help with cleanup and documents. It does not replace the speech model you selected.

There is no PDF or DOCX export. Copy Markdown or send a note to Apple Notes, then use the client's tool if they require those formats.

Do not read a SOC 2 line, yours or a vendor's, as a promise that a specific meeting is allowed. Consent and retention stay with the people in the call. This article does not say any product is compliant, certified for a sector, or appropriate for privileged conversations.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Notta alternative just another bot?

No. Some alternatives remove the bot and still upload audio. Some keep audio on the device and cannot attend when you are absent. Read both halves.

### Does Notta Desktop keep audio on my computer?

Not according to Notta's own recording page. Desktop capture avoids a visible bot. The page says the streams are sent to Notta's transcription service and that recordings are stored in the cloud.

### Can I try Notta on a full meeting for free?

The free plan, checked September 2026, allows 120 minutes a month but only 3 minutes per conversation. A normal meeting does not fit in that cap. Paid tiers raise the per-recording limit, up to 5 hours on the figures listed.

### Does Vemoir join Zoom or Teams?

No. It records the microphone and the system audio of whatever is playing on the Mac, including Zoom, Teams, Meet, Webex, and other apps. It does not appear as a participant. If the host's audio never reaches your Mac, there is nothing to transcribe.

### What if I only have a file under 15 minutes?

Use [transcribe recording](/tools/transcribe-recording). No install. The file stays in the tab. Past three runs in a week, or past 15 minutes, use the Mac app or another product whose limits fit the file.

### Is the one-time price per seat?

No. The App Store price is for the Mac app, one purchase, every current feature included. It does not cover a team cloud. Notta's team workspace is a different purchase.

### Can I keep Notta for the bot and use a local app for sensitive calls?

Yes. Many people do. Use the bot when the meeting is meant to be shared and you have consent. Use a local recorder when the audio should not be uploaded. Do not run both on the same call unless everyone has agreed to both.

### Does this article rank a winner?

No. Notta wins for unattended bots, mobile, Windows, and a shared cloud workspace. A local Mac app wins when the recording should stay on that Mac unless you export it. The browser tool wins for a short file you already have.

If the next call should be transcribed on the Mac, start with the limits of a short file on [transcribe recording](/tools/transcribe-recording), or read how capture works on [the Mac app](/#capture).
