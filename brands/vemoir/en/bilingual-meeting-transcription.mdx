**Bilingual meeting transcription** is what you search after a call that moved between two languages, often English and German, or French and Italian, sometimes three in the same hour. Models that handle Standard German, English, French, or Italian still write messy text when someone changes language mid-sentence, drops in an English product name, then finishes in French. You get usable notes when one language carries most of the call and the other appears as short loans. You do not get a court-ready, two-column script.

This page is about that mix, not dialect. It does not claim Swiss German dialect output or rank models with accuracy percentages. Local processing on the Mac or in-browser Whisper still fits when the file must not leave the machine. For a short clip, use the [transcribe recording tool](/tools/transcribe-recording): 15 minutes, 3 runs per week, no speaker labels, no live capture.

## What bilingual meeting transcription can reasonably do

Speech models prefer a dominant language per chunk, even when they cover many languages. In a typical Swiss or EU working call you get:

  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Glossary</span><strong>Names and terms</strong><p>Project words help in the Mac app.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Browser</span><strong>15 minutes</strong><p>No speaker labels in the free tab tool.</p></li>
</ul>

- Long stretches of one language clean enough to edit.
- Names, numbers, and terms you will still correct.
- Code-switching in one sentence written as one language, a mash, or a dropped clause.

That is enough for internal minutes: who owed what by when, in whichever language the decision was spoken. It is not enough if you need every French aside preserved as French and every German reply as German for publication or a dispute.

Speaker separation (Mac app only) tells you who talked, not which language they used. The browser does not label speakers.

## Language switching vs two monolingual files

If the agenda is "German until item 3, then English with the US colleagues," you have two meetings glued together. The reliable approach is operational:

1. Record or export one file.
2. Split at the language change (a cut in QuickTime is enough).
3. Transcribe each half expecting one language.
4. Merge the notes yourself. The minutes can be bilingual even when each slice was treated as monolingual.

If people switch every few seconds, splitting will not save you. Accept a noisy transcript and rewrite the decisions by hand, or hire a bilingual notetaker for that hour.

Do not run the same mixed file through three models to vote. Pick one installed model, read the decision passages, type the clean version.

## When a human bilingual transcript is better

Pay a person, or take notes live in both languages, when you will quote people in both languages in a published piece, when a missed phrase in the second language changes a negotiation, when ethics or a client forbids machine processing, or when you need speaker-attributed verbatim you will stand behind.

Local machine transcription fits a private draft of decisions after a working session on the Mac you used for the call. Cloud bots fit when the company already lives in one notetaker and an extra participant is allowed. Those are operations choices, not model magic.

Consent stays with you. A bilingual transcript is personal data. This page does not call the output certified, privileged, or GDPR-certified.

## A Swiss call with an agenda switch

A typical pattern: 25 minutes German with the local team, then 20 minutes English with the US group, one Meet on this Mac. Record once. After the stop, split at the agenda change. Transcribe the German half expecting Standard German, the English half expecting English. You merge the minutes. Decisions may appear in both languages.

Skip running a 45-minute unsplit file through three models and picking the nicest line. A sentence like "we take the Q3 number, yes exactly" will not land cleanly in two languages. Type it.

The browser helps only if you already have two clips under 15 minutes and weekly runs left. It does not add speakers or per-line language tags.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does bilingual meeting transcription mean the app auto-detects every switch?

No. Detection, when a model offers it, is coarse. Mid-sentence switches are the usual failure. Plan one dominant language per file, or split the recording.

### Can I set German for the UI and English for the transcript?

The UI language and the speech model are independent. Choose a speech model that covers the languages actually spoken. There is no accuracy table here.

### Will the browser tool mark which language each sentence is in?

No. You get one text stream, no speaker labels, no language tags per line.

### Does Parakeet v3 make mixed Swiss meetings perfect?

Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages. Coverage does not promise pretty code-switching. Standard languages only; no dialect claim.

### Should I invite a cloud notetaker because it "supports 30 languages"?

A vendor language list does not settle where audio goes or whether a bot may join. If the rule is no extra participant and no upload, a multilingual cloud bot is the wrong tool even when the brochure lists both languages. If the rule is a shared team library, the bot may fit regardless of mix.

### Can I transcribe live in the browser while people switch languages?

No. Live capture is not in the browser.
