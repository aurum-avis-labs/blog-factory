**Steering committee minutes** are the decision record from that meeting. They list who was there and in which role, the project status as a word (green, yellow, or red) for schedule, cost, quality and resources, each request in one sentence with an amount or a date and the decision beside it, the risks and escalations, the actions with one owner and a date, and the next meeting.

Write them so a person who missed the meeting can see what was decided. The free [steering committee minutes template](/tools/minutes-sheet/steering-committee) has these sections, with a filled CRM example you can overwrite. Below is what goes in each part, who sends the minute out, and that example.

## What steering committee minutes are for

A steering meeting can run for an hour and still decide little. The minute is not a retelling of that hour. It is the short page the sponsor, the project lead and the people doing the work will use until the next meeting.

Scope was written down at the kickoff. If someone asks whether the mobile app is in the project, the answer is in the [project kickoff minutes](/blog/project-kickoff-minutes), not on this page. The steering minute picks up the requests that came after that agreement.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Attendees, each with a role</li>
  <li>Status as a word for schedule, cost, quality and resources</li>
  <li>Each request in one sentence, with the decision beside it</li>
  <li>Risks and escalations</li>
  <li>Actions with one owner and one date</li>
  <li>Date, time and place of the next meeting</li>
</ul>

## Names and roles

Write the name and the role, not the name alone. Chair, sponsor, finance, project lead: three months later a reader needs to know who could decide and who was reporting. If someone sent a deputy, write the deputy's name and who they stood in for. If an absence mattered for a decision, one line is enough.

## Status as a word

Four lines cover the meeting: schedule, cost, quality, resources. Green means on track. Yellow means off track, with a fix already in place. Red means the committee has to decide.

Write the color as a word. A black-and-white print, a forwarded email and a screen reader all keep the meaning. The template exports the words Green, Amber and Red for this reason. Amber is the yellow light.

Put one sentence next to the word. "Test system two weeks late, go-live date holds" is a status. "We discussed the test system" is not.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Note</span>
  <p>A red light without a request does not help. Tie every red to a request in the next section, or to an escalation with an owner. If you cannot name the decision you need, the light is not red yet.</p>
</aside>

## The request and the decision

Prepare the requests before the meeting. One sentence, with an amount or a date. "Extra budget of $20,000 for external data cleanup" can be approved or refused in a line. "We should talk about data quality" cannot.

Write the decision in the next column, in the words the chair used: approved, refused, or approved on condition that the go-live date holds. If the committee postponed the request, write until when, and who brings it back.

Skip the debate. If an alternative was rejected for a reason people will ask about again, one sentence under the decision is enough.

## Risks, actions and the next meeting

Risks and escalations are the items that are not a formal request and that the committee still needs to know. One line: the risk, and what was agreed. "Key user availability: line managers free up two days a week starting Dec 1."

Each action has one owner and a date. Two or three that will actually be done are worth more than a list of ten. The line is a verb, one person, one date.

Put the next meeting's date, time and place in the minute before you close the file. The cadence agreed at the kickoff only works if this date is real.

## Who sends the minutes out

The project lead often drafts this minute, because they hold the numbers. The chair usually releases it. Many projects approve the minute at the start of the next meeting: the first item is the previous minute, and corrections are made there.

Write it the same day. By the next morning the room already remembers two versions, and an approved request may need a contract the same week.

A person who missed the meeting should be able to read one page and see the lights, the decisions and their own actions.

## Example from the CRM rollout

This is the filled example from the [steering committee template](/tools/minutes-sheet/steering-committee). The project is the CRM rollout at Brightline Supply, a fictional company. The date is November 4, the first steering meeting the kickoff already put on the calendar.

```text
Steering Committee Minutes
Project: CRM rollout at Brightline Supply
Date: 2026-11-04, 4:00-5:00 PM
Place: Executive meeting room
Chair: Michael Grant | Minutes: Priya Shah

Attendees
- Michael Grant (Chair, Head of Sales)
- Laura Chen (Head of Customer Service)
- Robert Hayes (CFO)
- Priya Shah (Project Manager)

Project status (area / status / comment)
- Schedule / Amber / Test system two weeks late, go-live date holds
- Cost / Green / 62% of budget spent at 60% progress
- Quality / Green / Acceptance tests for the Sales module passed
- Resources / Red / Customer Service key user only 20% available until end of November

Requests and decisions
- Extra budget of $20,000 for external data cleanup / Approved
- Move Customer Service training to January / Approved, on condition that the go-live date holds

Risks and escalations
- Key user availability: line managers free up two days per week starting Dec 1.

Actions (what / owner / due)
- Agree key user time with team leads / Laura Chen / Nov 15
- Award the data cleanup contract / Priya Shah / Nov 11

Next meeting
Dec 2, 2026, 4:00 PM, Executive meeting room
```

Three details make this minute usable. The status is a word, so a black-and-white copy still shows which light you meant. The red resources line has a request next to it (move the training) and an escalation (free up the key user). Each action has one owner.
