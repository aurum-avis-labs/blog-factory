A blank page makes systems work feel abstract, even when the problem is concrete: a business result that returned, a policy that keeps getting worked around, a research question about why a reform fades, or a similar knot in another field. This **Holist-IQ tutorial** is a short path to a first feedback loop map and to one action you can test.

Holist-IQ is a systems thinking platform for that job. You describe a challenge in plain language. The platform helps you map the core dynamic, reveal hidden loops, surface leverage points, and prepare a decision with more foresight than a symptom-by-symptom list. It is a decision-support tool. A picture you never revisit will not do this work.

You do not need to model everything. You need enough structure to see the loops that keep the outcome in place. Plan on about twenty minutes for a first pass. If you want the product's place in the wider toolkit before you start, read the [Holist-IQ guide](/blog/holist-iq).

## What you will have at the end

A small diagram with a readable core, one reinforcing loop and one balancing loop, one leverage point you can touch in two weeks, and one experiment with the assumptions attached to specific links. Use a live problem. A hypothetical hides which link feels wrong.

## Step 1: Choose a measurable outcome

Write one sentence.

- "We want to reduce customer churn in the first 30 days." That is a business outcome.
- "We want to understand why this regulation keeps getting circumvented." That is a policy outcome.
- "We want to trace why this systemic issue keeps resurfacing despite reforms." That is a research or journalism outcome.
- "We want to see why an advocacy win erodes inside a legislative cycle." That is a public-interest outcome.

Add a rough metric if you have one: churn rate, backlog size, cycle time, a complaint count, a defect rate. The metric keeps the map attached to something you could observe. If you have no metric yet, keep the sentence concrete enough that a colleague could tell whether the outcome got better or worse. "Improve alignment" will not survive step 3.

In Holist-IQ you start from that plain-language challenge. Stay with one outcome for this pass. A second outcome is a second map.

## Step 2: List the variables that influence the outcome

Brainstorm drivers and do not debate them yet. For early churn, a workable list is:

- onboarding completion
- time to value
- product complexity
- support response time
- expectation mismatch
- perceived return
- customer confidence

Include what people already argue about, including the awkward items, so the hidden loop has a chance to show up. Aim for a core you can read, and allow the wider list to reach 10 to 15 variables if the side issues are real. Too few and you will draw a straight line and call it a system. Too many on the core and you will not see a loop in twenty minutes. Park extras on a side list instead of deleting them in anger.

Variable names should be nouns you could watch move. "Bad onboarding" is a verdict. "Onboarding completion" is a variable.

## Step 3: Connect variables with cause and effect

For each link, ask: if this increases, does that increase or decrease, at least eventually? Write the sentence before you draw the arrow.

- "Higher product complexity increases support tickets."
- "Longer support response time increases frustration."
- "Higher frustration increases churn."

If the effect shows up later, mark a delay on that link. Delays are where a sensible plan gets cancelled early, because the metric people watch has not moved yet. Hiring is a typical delay. A messaging change that waits on a release is another. Do not leave the delay as the word "eventually."

Also note direction. A link with no direction is a topic association, and Holist-IQ's job is the causal structure, not a mind map of related words. Where you are unsure, keep the sentence and tag it as an assumption. You will collect those in step 7.

## Step 4: Find the first feedback loops

Look for a chain that returns to its start. That return is the difference between a list of causes and a system.

**A reinforcing loop.** Complexity increases, tickets increase, response times worsen, frustration increases, churn increases, pressure to ship more features increases, complexity increases. Name it something the team will repeat, such as "feature pressure loop" or "support spiral."

**A balancing loop.** Tickets increase, hiring increases, capacity increases, response times improve, frustration decreases, tickets stabilize. Name that one too, such as "hiring catch-up loop." Mark the delay on hiring. Without it, the balancing story looks faster than the organization is.

Naming is practical. It lets someone refer to the loop without redrawing it, which is what stakeholder alignment needs in the next meeting. How these loop types behave in operating decisions is covered in [systems thinking in business](/blog/systems-thinking-in-business).

Stop when you have one of each, or when one type is honestly missing. A missing balancing loop is a finding. Say so, and check whether an effect flows back into a variable you left off the page. Holist-IQ helps reveal those hidden loops and the ripple from one link into the rest of the map, so the return path is visible before you pick an action.

## Step 5: Identify one leverage point

Ask where a small change could shift the loop you actually have, and where you have the authority to try it in the next two weeks. Common places to look, as a checklist rather than a promise:

- A delay you can shorten, such as earlier detection of stuck onboarding
- Information people do not share, such as a segment-level completion number
- An incentive that rewards the reinforcing loop, such as shipping features while support load is already high
- Friction on the path you want, such as extra steps, unclear handoffs, or approval with no criteria

Pick one point that sits on a named loop and that you can move in two weeks. An idea that does not touch that loop can wait on the side list.

Holist-IQ is there to surface those points from the structure you mapped. You still choose which one to test.

## Step 6: Turn the map into a testable action

Write one experiment in this form.

- **If** we change X,
- **we expect** Y, a leading indicator, to move within Z time,
- **and we will watch** side effect W.

Example, still a teaching sketch: if we simplify onboarding steps for the top two segments, we expect onboarding completion to rise within 14 days, and we will watch support tickets for confusion about the new wording. Name the loop in the action. After the window, change any link the indicator contradicted.

## Step 7: Write down the assumptions that could be wrong

Pick three to five uncertain links. For each, write what would show the link is wrong.

- "We assume response time drives churn more than product gaps. We would be wrong if response time improves, completion stays flat, and churn does not move."
- "We assume discounting raises churn through lower-fit customers. We would be wrong if those cohorts retain as well as the others once onboarding is the same."
- "We assume complexity is growing faster than support capacity. We would be wrong if tickets per account are flat."

Write what would weaken the link. If you cannot, you are treating it as a fact, and the next decision will not learn from the test.

## Mistakes that waste the twenty minutes

**Modeling the whole organization.** Keep the core small. The side list can grow next time, after one loop has a test.

**Arguing about the truth before the links exist.** Capture the rival story as an alternate link. Test later. A debate with no arrows does not produce a map.

**Skipping delays.** Mark them. They explain most of the surprises in the first review.

**Leaving the map in the session.** Book a short review at the end of the experiment window. Update the links that the indicator contradicted.

**Turning leverage into a backlog.** One point, one owner, one window. Extra actions can wait on the side list.

## Who should wait

Wait if the situation is a one-time choice with no feedback and no chance of recurrence. A loop map will invent a return path you do not have. Wait if you need a controlled financial model or a live operational dashboard as the primary tool today. Holist-IQ does not replace those. Use it when the hard part is seeing structure, ripple effects, and a leverage point before you act. If you are still choosing among approaches, the [systems thinking tool checklist](/blog/systems-thinking-tool-checklist) is the comparison page.
