**Stock-and-flow vs causal loops** is a notation choice with decision consequences. CLDs highlight feedback and polarity; stock-and-flow diagrams highlight accumulation, drains, and numeric rates.

Teams argue about which view is "more rigorous." The practical answer is which view matches the question you must answer this quarter.

## Quick comparison

| Question you need to answer | Start with |
|----------------------------|------------|
| Why do we oscillate or overshoot? | Causal loops |
| How much is sitting in inventory or backlog? | Stock and flow |
| Where is stakeholder disagreement about causes? | Causal loops |
| What inflow/outflow change hits a target date? | Stock and flow |
| Which intervention shifts incentives over time? | Causal loops (often paired with numbers) |

## When causal loops are the better first move

Choose CLDs when:

- The team debates **why** an outcome persists.
- Reinforcing and balancing stories drive the decision.
- You need a shared qualitative model before investing in data plumbing.

Link to [causal loop diagram software](/blog/causal-loop-diagram-software) when the map must live beyond a workshop.

## When stock-and-flow earns the lead

Choose stock-and-flow when:

- A **quantity** must be tracked against physical or financial reality.
- Inflows and outflows set hard constraints this month.
- Simulation or scenario numbers will decide budget.

You can still overlay feedback stories as annotations. Many teams maintain a CLD for narrative and a stock-and-flow sketch for quantities.

## Using both without duplicate maintenance

A lightweight pattern:

1. Build a CLD for the decision story (10 to 15 variables).
2. Identify which variables are **stocks** with measurable units.
3. Attach a small table or linked sheet for those stocks only.
4. Review together in one meeting, not two competing diagrams.

## Mini case: hiring and backlog revisited

CLD view: reinforcing service loop plus balancing hiring loop with delay.

Stock view: backlog as stock, arrivals as inflow, closures as outflow.

Leadership needs the CLD to agree on loop type and delay. Finance needs the stock view to check whether hiring closes the gap by quarter end. One decision, two faithful views.

Evaluate facilitation and maintenance needs via [systems thinking tool checklist](/blog/systems-thinking-tool-checklist).

## Strong next step: label stocks on your current CLD

Take an active map. Circle variables that are quantities. If none exist, ask whether CLD alone matches the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is stock-and-flow only for engineers?

No. Policy inventories, grant pipelines, and workforce backlogs are stocks too.

### Do I need simulation software?

Not always. A CLD plus a spreadsheet for stocks covers many decisions.

### Can Holist-IQ replace stock-and-flow tools?

Holist-IQ focuses on causal feedback for alignment. Pair with spreadsheets or SF tools when quantities dominate.
