A **causal loop mapping workshop** beats a six-hour slide review when a metric keeps returning. Ninety focused minutes can produce a named outcome, three to six variables, two loops, and one testable link.

## Before the room (15 minutes prep)

Send the outcome metric, last three interventions, and ground rules: neutral variable names, no blame, phones away for sketching.

## Minute 0 to 15: Outcome and recurrence story

Facilitator writes the outcome header. Ask: "When did we see this before?" Capture dates and owners. If no recurrence, consider a mind map instead (see [causal loop diagram vs mind map](/blog/causal-loop-diagram-vs-mind-map)).

## Minute 15 to 40: Variable harvest

Silent brainstorm, then cluster nouns. Rename blame labels ("bad communication") into factors ("shared priority clarity").

<Image src={img2} alt="Abstract cluster of nodes suggesting workshop variable harvest" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

## Minute 40 to 65: Links, polarity, loops

Draw only contested links first. Read each aloud. Close loops, mark R/B, note one delay.

## Minute 65 to 80: Pick one leverage test

Choose a link the group can influence within six weeks. Assign owner and leading indicator.

## Minute 80 to 90: Capture and next meeting

Photograph the map, transcribe variable list, schedule follow-up before people leave.

## Facilitation pitfalls

Too many variables, debating polarity before stories, skipping delays, leaving without an owner.

## Mid-step: run a dry run internally

Facilitate a fictional scenario with your team to practice timing.

## Strong next step: digitize the living map

Use [causal loop diagram software](/blog/causal-loop-diagram-software) and the [systems thinking tool checklist](/blog/systems-thinking-tool-checklist). Holist-IQ fits teams moving from sticky notes to maintained maps.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do we need an external facilitator?

Helpful when trust is low; internal owners work when ground rules hold.

### Remote workshops?

Use shared canvas, one speaker at a time, visible timer.
