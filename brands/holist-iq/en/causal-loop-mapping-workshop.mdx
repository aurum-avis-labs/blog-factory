**A causal loop mapping workshop** turns a vague problem into shared loop hypotheses in about ninety minutes. The output is not perfection. It is alignment on structure, polarity, and what to test next.

This agenda fits leadership offsites, policy staff sessions, and product triage rooms. Scale participants down if decision authority is missing from the room.

## Before the session (15 minutes prep)

- Pick **one decision** on the calendar in the next six weeks.
- Invite **six to ten** people with conflicting views, not only allies.
- Share a one-page problem statement: outcome, stakes, constraints.
- Materials: whiteboard or shared map, sticky notes, timer, facilitator.

## Agenda (90 minutes)

| Minutes | Activity | Output |
|--------:|----------|--------|
| 0 to 10 | Outcome variable + success metric | One named variable |
| 10 to 25 | Silent brainstorm of influences | 15 to 25 sticky notes |
| 25 to 45 | Cluster and link (facilitator draws) | First pass map |
| 45 to 60 | Trace two loops, label R/B | Named loops |
| 60 to 75 | Mark delays and contested links | Assumption list |
| 75 to 90 | Choose one test + owner | Next-step card |

## Facilitation rules that keep loops readable

- **One speaker at the whiteboard** to preserve notation.
- **Plain variable names** (Backlog, Trust, Compliance rate).
- **Park detail** in a side list instead of adding twenty nodes.
- **Disagreement becomes dotted links**, not debate without capture.

If the group already uses software, capture photo or export at minute 45 so remote stakeholders can comment asynchronously. [Holist-IQ](/blog/holist-iq) fits teams that want the map to survive after the room empties.

## After the workshop (same week)

1. Publish photo or file with date and attendees.
2. Assign **one loop test** with a metric and review date.
3. Delete or merge nodes that duplicated the same idea.
4. Link to deeper tutorial steps in [Holist-IQ tutorial](/blog/holist-iq-tutorial) for owners who will maintain the map.

Compare long-term tooling needs with [systems thinking tool checklist](/blog/systems-thinking-tool-checklist).

## When not to run this workshop

Skip or postpone if:

- Leadership only wants a rubber stamp, not structure.
- The decision is purely procedural with no feedback story.
- You lack authority to act on any loop you might find.

A CLD workshop without follow-through erodes trust faster than no workshop.

## Strong next step: schedule the ninety minutes

Book the room, send the one-pager, and commit to one testable loop hypothesis by the end.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do we need a systems expert?

You need a neutral facilitator who can enforce notation rules. Domain experts supply links.

### Miro or wall?

Either works. Optimize for readability and a clean photo archive.

### How polarized can the group be?

Polarization is fuel if ground rules keep links hypothetical and testable.

### What if we finish early?

Deepen delay notes or draft a second scenario loop instead of adding clutter.

### Software during the session?

Optional. Many teams sketch first, digitize second. See [causal loop diagram software](/blog/causal-loop-diagram-software) for maintenance criteria.

## Closing next step

**Running a loop mapping workshop** is how teams buy shared structure cheaply. Ninety minutes, two loops, one owner, one dated test.
