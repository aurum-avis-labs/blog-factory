**Unintended consequences** are adaptations, not bad luck. When a rule changes incentives, actors shift behavior along paths planners did not model. **Policy mapping** with causal loops makes those paths discussable before rollout.

This guide is for policy teams, regulators, and program owners who need stakeholder-readable structure without pretending to predict every outcome.

Start from [systems thinking for public policy](/blog/systems-thinking-for-public-policy-designing-better-political-decisions-in-compl) and pair with [leverage points in systems thinking](/blog/leverage-points-systems-thinking) when choosing intervention depth.

## Map actors, not only metrics

List who can adapt: firms, citizens, intermediaries, enforcement staff. Draw links from the policy lever to their constraints, not only to headline KPIs.

## Trace second-order paths explicitly

For each first-order win, ask "who gains time to adapt?" and "where does activity migrate?" Mark delays on enforcement and reporting links.

**Example:** Stricter reporting on large firms shifts contracts to smaller entities with weaker oversight until a later compliance gap appears.

## Run a pre-mortem on the loop map

Ask stakeholders to label links they distrust. Capture alternate polarities as dotted hypotheses instead of arguing facts in hour one.

## Document boundaries and known omissions

State what the map excludes (international spillovers, informal economy) so critics engage with scope, not strawmen.

## When not to publish a loop map externally

Skip public release when security, active investigations, or negotiation leverage requires confidentiality. Internal loop discipline still helps.

## Mid-step: one-page adaptation brief

From your next policy memo, add three adaptation links and one delay. Circulate before the steering group meets.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is this partisan fact checking?

No. It is structural: what feedback and adaptation the headline omits.

### Do we need quantitative simulation?

Not always. Qualitative loops plus targeted monitoring on second-hop variables reduce surprise.

### How is this different from impact assessment templates?

Templates list categories. Loops force directed stories and delays between categories.
