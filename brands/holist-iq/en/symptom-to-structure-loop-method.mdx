Teams arrive with symptoms: rising churn, policy non-compliance, backlog spikes, trust erosion. The symptom-to-structure loop method turns that energy into a small causal map you can debate, test, and revise.

## When to use this method

Use it when fixes keep restoring the old equilibrium within a quarter, stakeholders agree on *what* is wrong but not *why*, or you need a shared model before choosing an intervention.

## Step 1: Anchor on a measurable symptom

Write one sentence with a number, even if rough. If you cannot measure it, narrow the scope until you can.

## Step 2: Elicit variables without debating causality yet

Cluster themes: incentives, capacity, rules, information flows, culture, external shocks. Aim for 10–15 variables.

## Step 3: Link variables with polarity and delays

For each link ask: "If A increases, does B increase or decrease (eventually)?" Mark delays with a `D` on the arrow.

<Image src={img2} alt="Workshop sketch linking variables with signed arrows and delay markers" width={500} quality={80} class="w-full" />

## Step 4: Close loops and name them

Trace chains that return to the starting variable. Name loops in plain language ("Support spiral").

## Step 5: Choose one leverage hypothesis

Pick a link or node where a small shift could change loop dominance. Document assumptions to validate in two weeks.
