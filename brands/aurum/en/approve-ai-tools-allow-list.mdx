## How to approve AI tools at work

To approve AI tools at work, take five steps: find out what the team already uses, pick one or two tools, move work onto business accounts instead of free consumer accounts, check the settings in the suite you already pay for, and announce the list. For most Swiss SMEs the first candidate is already in the subscription: the AI chat in Microsoft 365, or Gemini in Google Workspace. The allow-list is clause 2 of an [AI usage policy](/blog/ai-policy-template-sme). This article is how you fill that clause.

It is written for the managing director or office lead who is, in practice, also the IT department, or who has an external IT partner for admin work. It is not a tool ranking. It also gives no admin-console click paths. Those move with every release, and the vendors' own help pages linked below are where your admin should look.

## Step 1: find out what is already in use

Ask openly, and make clear that honest answers have no consequences. A ten-minute round in the team meeting or a form with three questions is enough: which tool, which account (private free, private paid, company), and for what kind of work.

Expect more than ChatGPT. Translation websites, AI features inside other software, a private Gemini account on the phone. Write each one down with the account type and the kind of data that goes in. If the first honest answer gets a telling-off, the rest of the room stays quiet, and the private accounts stay too.

## Business accounts instead of free accounts

The difference is the contract. With a business account the company has terms with the vendor, data processing commitments and an admin who can add and remove people. With a private free account the employee has terms with the vendor, and the company has none.

What that means for customer data under Swiss data protection law, including the traffic light for which data may go where, is in [ChatGPT and customer data](/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg). Here is what the three vendors most SMEs meet say about their business offers, on their own pages.

### Microsoft 365

Microsoft has renamed its products: Microsoft 365 Copilot is now Microsoft Copilot, and Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat is now Microsoft Copilot Chat. Some licences may still show the old names during the transition ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/enterprise-data-protection)).

Copilot Chat is [included for organisations with a Microsoft 365 subscription](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/faq), and users reach it after signing in with their Entra account, the work account. Microsoft then applies what it calls enterprise data protection: use is covered by the Data Protection Addendum and the Product Terms, with Microsoft acting as processor, and prompts and responses are not used to train the foundation models. A green shield next to the New Chat button shows that it is active ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/privacy-and-protections)). Tell people to look for it.

Two limits to know. Web queries that Copilot sends to Bing fall under the Microsoft Services Agreement, with Microsoft as an independent controller. Admins can switch web search off with the policy "Allow web search in Copilot" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/manage)). And Copilot Chat does not reason over your work data in Microsoft Graph, meaning mailboxes and files. Microsoft places that in the paid Microsoft Copilot licence.

### Google Workspace

With a qualifying Workspace edition, Google's commitment is that your content is not human-reviewed or used for generative AI model training outside your domain without permission, under the Cloud Data Processing Addendum. That covers Gemini in Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive and Meet, and the Gemini app ([Google Workspace Privacy Hub](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/generative-ai/generative-ai-in-google-workspace-privacy-hub)).

The Gemini app is on by default for licensed users. Admins can turn it on or off for everyone, or for an organisational unit or group. Users without a qualifying Workspace edition fall under different terms: their chats may be reviewed by human reviewers and used to improve Google's products ([Google Workspace Help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/generative-ai/gemini-app/turn-the-gemini-app-on-or-off)). Same rule as with Microsoft: the work account is the approved tool, the private Google account is not.

### ChatGPT

OpenAI renamed the ChatGPT Team plan to ChatGPT Business on 29 August 2025. Contracts and terms stayed the same ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12111915-chatgpt-business-rename-faq)). For ChatGPT Business, Enterprise and the API, OpenAI says it does not use business data, inputs or outputs, for training by default, and it supports a data processing addendum ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/business-data/)).

ChatGPT Business makes sense as a second tool for a named group whose work the suite chat does not cover well. It makes less sense as a second account everyone gets "just in case".

## Which tools go on the list

One tool from the suite you already use, for everyone. A second one only for a named group and a named job. Every entry says the account type, the data classes allowed and who uses it.

An example: a property management firm with around 15 people, everything on Microsoft 365. The inventory turned up seven private ChatGPT accounts, two people using a translation website and one person using Gemini on a private Google account. The office lead decides on this list:

| Tool | Account | Allowed data | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Microsoft Copilot Chat | Work account, green shield | Public, internal, drafts of tenant replies | Everyone |
| ChatGPT Business | Company workspace | Public and internal, no personal data | Three people who draft notices and letters |
| Private ChatGPT, Gemini, translation sites | Private | None | Nobody, for work |

Web search gets a decision too. Tenant correspondence is the main use, and the web part runs under different terms, so the IT partner switches web search off for now. The firm reviews that at the next policy review. Health details from tenants stay out of every tool, as clause 3 of the policy says.

## Settings to check, without click paths

Three checks: people sign in with work accounts, the business terms really apply, and access and web search are set on purpose rather than by default.

- **Microsoft 365:** staff open Copilot Chat with the work account and see the green shield. Someone has decided on "Allow web search in Copilot". If only some people should use it, access can be limited to users or groups.
- **Google Workspace:** the Gemini app is on for the organisational units that should have it and off for the rest. Everyone on the list has a qualifying edition.
- **ChatGPT Business:** whoever administers the workspace adds people when they join and removes them when they leave. Work content goes into the workspace, not into a private account.

Where exactly each switch sits changes. Your admin works from the help pages linked above, not from a screenshot in a blog.

## Step 5: announce the list

One page, a short meeting and a name. The list goes into the policy and wherever people actually look, for example a pinned post in the Teams channel.

Say what is off and why: private accounts, because the company has no contract there, not because anyone did something wrong. Give the approved replacement in the same breath as the "no". Name the person who decides on new tools and how fast they answer, a week is reasonable. Put the next review date on the page.

## Where an allow-list stops

An approved tool is not approval for every kind of data. The business account does not make health data, salaries or client dossiers fine to paste. That is the data-class clause, and it stays in force.

The list does not teach anyone to check a draft, and it does not build a workflow between systems. Stop evaluating when the comparison reaches its third meeting. By then you are comparing features, not deciding. Start with the suite tool and at most one more. If your firm handles health data or works in a regulated field, have your data-protection adviser look at the list before you announce it.

## Next step: the team uses the list the same way

Once the list exists, the team has to use it the same way: which approved tool fits which task, what AI does reliably and where it breaks, how to check an output before it leaves. That is the [AI in Business workshop](/services/ai-in-business): half a day or a full day, up to 20 participants, practised on real tasks from your team, on site or hybrid in Switzerland. Your list and your policy are good material for the preparation call.
