## How to approve AI tools at work

To approve AI tools at work, take five steps: find out what the team already uses, pick one or two tools, move work onto business accounts instead of free consumer accounts, check the settings in the suite you already pay for, and announce the list. For most Swiss SMEs the first candidate is already in the subscription: the AI chat in Microsoft 365, or Gemini in Google Workspace. The allow-list is clause 2 of an [AI usage policy](/prozess-check/blog/ai-policy-template-sme). This article is how you fill that clause.

It is written for the managing director or office lead who is, in practice, also the IT department, or who has an external IT partner. It is not a tool ranking and it gives no admin-console click paths. Use the vendors' help pages linked below for switches and policies.

## Step 1: find out what is already in use

Ask openly, and make clear that honest answers have no consequences. A ten-minute round in the team meeting or a form with three questions is enough: which tool, which account (private free, private paid, company), and for what kind of work.

Expect more than ChatGPT. Translation websites, AI features inside other software, a private Gemini account on the phone. Write each one down with the account type and the kind of data that goes in. If the first honest answer gets a telling-off, the rest of the room stays quiet, and the private accounts stay too.

## Business accounts instead of free accounts

The difference is the contract. With a business account the company has terms with the vendor, data processing commitments and an admin who can add and remove people. With a private free account the employee has terms with the vendor, and the company has none.

What that means for customer data under Swiss data protection law, including the traffic light for which data may go where, is in [ChatGPT and customer data](/prozess-check/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg). Here is what the three vendors most SMEs meet say about their business offers, on their own pages.

### Microsoft 365

Microsoft has renamed its products: Microsoft 365 Copilot is now Microsoft Copilot, and Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat is now Microsoft Copilot Chat. Some licences may still show the old names during the transition ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/enterprise-data-protection)).

Copilot Chat is [included for organisations with a Microsoft 365 subscription](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/faq), and users reach it after signing in with their Entra account, the work account. Microsoft then applies what it calls enterprise data protection: use is covered by the Data Protection Addendum and the Product Terms, with Microsoft acting as processor, and prompts and responses are not used to train the foundation models. A green shield next to the New Chat button shows that it is active ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/privacy-and-protections)). Tell people to look for it.

Web queries through Bing run under different terms from the chat itself. Admins can switch web search off with the policy "Allow web search in Copilot" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/manage)). Copilot Chat does not read your mailboxes and files in Microsoft Graph. That requires the paid Microsoft Copilot licence.

### Google Workspace

With a qualifying Workspace edition, Google's commitment is that your content is not human-reviewed or used for generative AI model training outside your domain without permission, under the Cloud Data Processing Addendum. That covers Gemini in Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive and Meet, and the Gemini app ([Google Workspace Privacy Hub](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/generative-ai/generative-ai-in-google-workspace-privacy-hub)).

Admins turn the Gemini app on or off per organisational unit ([Google Workspace Help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/generative-ai/gemini-app/turn-the-gemini-app-on-or-off)). Without a qualifying edition, different terms apply. The work account is the approved tool, the private Google account is not.

### ChatGPT

OpenAI renamed the ChatGPT Team plan to ChatGPT Business on 29 August 2025. Contracts and terms stayed the same ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12111915-chatgpt-business-rename-faq)). For ChatGPT Business, Enterprise and the API, OpenAI says it does not use business data, inputs or outputs, for training by default, and it supports a data processing addendum ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/business-data/)).

ChatGPT Business makes sense as a second tool for a named group whose work the suite chat does not cover well. It makes less sense as a second account everyone gets "just in case".

## Which tools go on the list

One tool from the suite you already use, for everyone. A second one only for a named group and a named job. Every entry says the account type, the data classes allowed and who uses it.

An example: a property management firm with around 15 people, everything on Microsoft 365. The inventory turned up seven private ChatGPT accounts, two people using a translation website and one person using Gemini on a private Google account. The office lead decides on this list:

| Tool | Account | Allowed data | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Microsoft Copilot Chat | Work account, green shield | Public, internal, drafts of tenant replies | Everyone |
| ChatGPT Business | Company workspace | Public and internal, no personal data | Three people who draft notices and letters |
| Private ChatGPT, Gemini, translation sites | Private | None | Nobody, for work |

Web search gets a decision too. The IT partner switches it off for now. Health details from tenants stay out of every tool.

## Settings to check, without click paths

Three checks: work-account sign-in, business terms active, access and web search set on purpose. Switches and policies live in the linked help pages, not in blog screenshots.

## Step 5: announce the list

One page, a short meeting and a name. The list goes into the policy and wherever people actually look, for example a pinned post in the Teams channel.

Say what is off and why: private accounts, because the company has no contract there. Name the approved tool, who decides on new tools, the next review date, and a deadline for replies, a week is usually enough.

## Allow-list or block-list?

An allow-list names what is permitted; everything else stays out until someone requests it. A block-list bans named tools and leaves the rest open. For SMEs with shadow AI, an allow-list is usually faster: less grey area, a clearer business account.

## Who waits and who lists now

Wait if nobody uses AI for work yet and the board or clients are not asking. List now if private ChatGPT accounts already hold client or customer data, or if you pay for M365 or Workspace and nobody knows which chat is approved.

## Where an allow-list stops

An approved tool is not approval for every kind of data. Health data, salaries and client dossiers stay outside the permitted classes. The list does not replace draft checks or build integrations. Stop tool comparisons by the third meeting. If your firm handles health data or regulated fields, have your data-protection adviser review the list before you announce it.
