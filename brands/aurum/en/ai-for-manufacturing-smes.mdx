## AI for a manufacturing SME: the office, not the machines

<p class="blog-lede">AI in manufacturing pays off first in the office of a Swiss SME: where orders, dates, suppliers and batch certificates still pass through the same fields by hand.</p>

In a small manufacturing firm, the recurring work sits between the customer mail and the order in your system. That is order intake from email, the confirmation, the delivery date you tell the customer, the chase when a supplier is late, the quality document that has to travel with the batch, and the quote for a part you do not yet make. It is not a camera on the machine and not a model that reschedules the shop floor. If a large customer already sends orders in the electronic channel your system accepts, leave that channel alone. The work is the rest.

This page is for the owner or the office lead of a Swiss plant with a few dozen people: machining, sheet metal, plastics, assembly, a specialist supplier. Production planning stays with the people who know the machines. The office is where dates, article numbers and PDFs still get copied.

## What the plant office actually repeats

A customer sends an order as a PDF, a spreadsheet or a few lines of text, often against a frame agreement, sometimes as a one-off. Someone matches the article number, the drawing revision and the quantity, and types an order. Then someone sends a confirmation with a date. Later the date moves because material is late, and someone has to tell the customer before the customer asks. A supplier promised bar, sheet or components for Thursday and has gone quiet. With the delivery, the customer wants the certificate for that batch, not a generic PDF from last year.

None of that is a cycle time. It is the same files, with numbers that have to match. A wrong revision on a confirmation is a part you will make and then discuss. A certificate for the wrong batch is a delivery the customer cannot book in.

## Start from the system you already run

Your order system, whatever its name, is where the order, the date and the stock live. If it already imports the big customer's orders and prints the confirmation, do not build a second path for that customer. Use it. The gap is the order that arrives where the import does not: the email from a smaller customer, the call-off in a spreadsheet, the change of quantity in a reply to an old thread.

How to read those emails, match an article number, and leave an unclear line for a person is a job of its own in [capture customer orders from email](/prozess-check/blog/capture-email-orders). This page only says where that job sits. It does not walk the fields again. What your program offers as an interface, you settle against the real file. A brochure that says the system is "open" is not a connector.

## Five office jobs, each with a stop

Order intake applies to customers who do not use your electronic channel. A draft in the system, a person on any line the article number or the revision did not match. EDI, or whatever structured message the large customer already requires, stays the better answer for that customer. Email capture is for the long tail, not a replacement for a channel that works.

The confirmation: once the order is accepted, the confirmation quotes the article, the quantity, the price you agreed and the date that is actually in the system. It does not improve the date to sound helpful. A person sends it, or a rule sends it only when every line matched.

The delivery date, when it moves: customers would rather hear it from you than discover it on the day. That message is a draft from the date in the system, not a new date a model invents. It is frequent enough to be its own sequence. Here it is only named, so it is not confused with taking the order in.

The supplier chase: a missing delivery has an order number, a promised day and a person at the supplier. The mail asks for a new day. It does not replan your workshop. If the new day changes a customer date, that is the delivery-date job, after a person accepts the new day.

Quality papers: the customer asks for the document that belongs to the heat, the batch or the lot on the delivery note. The office looks it up and attaches it. A model can match a batch number on a PDF to the delivery, and a person checks the match when the numbers are dirty. It does not write a certificate the lab did not issue.

Quotes for a part you have not made: the first quote is engineering and a price someone stands behind. The later "did you see our quote" is a different, smaller loop in [quote follow-ups](/prozess-check/blog/quote-follow-ups). Do not fold that chase into a production scheduler.

Which of the five to do first is a count of how often it happens and how expensive a wrong number is. The questions for that choice are in [which process to automate first](/prozess-check/blog/which-process-to-automate-first).

## Partial shipment: delivery note, open quantity, invoice

When only part of the order is ready, a second loop starts that many plants underestimate. Production packs what is finished. The delivery note lists the lines that left. The order still shows an open quantity. The customer expects either a second delivery on a date or an invoice for what arrived. The office has to keep the same numbers in three places: order, delivery note, invoice.

AI rarely helps here with "intelligence". It helps with matching: lines on the delivery note against the open quantity, a short mail to the customer when the partial shipment diverges from the confirmation, and a nudge to planning about which remainder date should sit in the system after a person sets it. Do not automate a partial-shipment mail while the open quantity in the system is still a guess. Fix the quantity, then the draft. That applies everywhere: the office sends what production and quality have already decided. Nothing on this page releases a batch, changes a drawing, or moves a job on the machine.

## Who should wait

Wait if article numbers and drawing revisions are not stable. Matching mail to a moving catalogue creates confident wrong orders. Wait if one person already keeps the small-customer orders without errors you feel, and the large customers are on the electronic channel. Wait on anything sold as intelligence for the spindle, the press or the line. That is a different purchase, with a different person in the room, and it does not empty the order mailbox.

A plant that ships the same repeat order to three customers on a portal, with no email and no certificates, may have nothing here to build. The test is the pile on the office desk, not the noise of the hall.
