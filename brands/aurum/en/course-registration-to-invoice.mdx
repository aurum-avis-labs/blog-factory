## From course registration to the invoice

<p class="blog-lede">A course registration becomes an invoice when the form splits participant and payer, the seat count is checked before confirmation, the QR-bill goes to whoever pays, and attendance triggers the certificate.</p>

The course software stays the place for the catalogue. This chain is the work beside it: form, waiting list, bill, reminder, certificate. Which office jobs are worth looking at is in [AI for course providers](/prozess-check/blog/ai-for-course-providers). This page walks the path from the form to the bill, for a small provider or private school that still copies names between a form, a spreadsheet and the accounting program.

## Split the participant from the payer on the form

The form fails when it has one "name" and one "email". On a company-funded course the person in the room and the company on the invoice are different. On a children's course the child and the parent are different. Ask for both in separate fields: who attends (name, course code, date), and who pays (name or company, billing address, email, and a reference such as a cost centre if they need one on the bill).

If the payer's email is empty, do not invent it from the participant's address. Hold the registration and ask. A bill to the wrong company is a harder repair than a one-day delay.

Do not collect health details, a school report or a copy of an identity document "just in case". If a course genuinely needs a medical note, that field stays in the system you already use for pupil files, not in a general web form that also feeds the mail-out. When you are unsure, ask your data-protection adviser. This is a working rule, not legal advice.

## Check the seat before you confirm

The place check is a number you already keep: seats on this course, minus confirmed registrations that are not cancelled. The form does not decide the number. It reads it.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Match the course</h3><p>Course code and date must exist. An unknown code stops, with a short note to the office. No nearest-match guess.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Count free seats</h3><p>Confirmed and not cancelled. A waiting-list name is not a seat.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Confirm or wait</h3><p>A free seat gets a confirmation: course, time, place, what to bring, and that the invoice follows to the payer. A full course gets a waiting-list position and nothing that sounds like a booking.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Release a seat only by your rule</h3><p>If someone cancels, the next waiting-list line is offered, not silently enrolled. The person confirms before you take the seat.</p></li>
</ol>

Write the cancellation rule in one sentence the mail can quote. "Unpaid seven days before the start, the seat is released" is a rule you chose. The flow only applies it. It does not invent a legal claim about whether the course fee is still owed. That sentence is yours, or your adviser's.

## The QR-bill goes to the payer

In Switzerland the written invoice is a QR-bill. [SIX](https://www.six-group.com/en/products-services/banking-services/payment-standardization/standards/qr-bill.html) states that it has been in circulation since June 2020 and replaced the old payment slips on 1 October 2022. It can be printed or sent as a digital file. The Swiss QR code carries the payment data, and the same data appear in plain text for checking.

On the address inside that code, the same SIX page (read on 30 September 2026) says two things. A notice at the top says the structured address, type S, remains scheduled for November 2026. A later section says that with guidelines version 2.3, in force on 22 November 2025, only the structured address is permitted. We will not pick one date when the page states both. Read the current page, and ask the vendor of the program that creates your bills which address form that version emits. Street and town stored as separate fields can feed either form. A single free-text line cannot be split later without someone checking it. A free generator is a workaround. SIX lists such tools and does not warrant them. A school that invoices every week should let the debtor program create the QR-bill.

What that program offers as an interface, you settle when you look at the real file. We have not built a connector to a named accounting package as a standing product. The aim is smaller: the registration already holds the payer, the amount, the course reference and the address in the structured fields the QR-bill needs, so nobody retypes them into the invoice.

The reference on the bill should let the office match the payment to the course and the participant without opening the email thread. The course code plus the participant's name is usually enough. Put the company's purchase reference in the field they asked for, not instead of yours.

## Reminder, attendance, certificate

A reminder is a second mail to the payer, on a date you set, quoting the invoice and the consequence you already wrote (the seat is held, or it is released). It is not a debt-collection step. If the payment arrives, the reminder stops. If the amount is disputed, a person takes it. Do not let a model rewrite the fee in the reminder.

On the day, the teacher or the office marks who attended. That mark is the source for the certificate, not the original registration. Someone who paid and did not attend does not get a certificate because the invoice was paid. Someone who attended and whose employer pays later does get it, if that is your rule. Write the rule down so the template does not guess.

The certificate is a document from a template: name, course, dates, and a line you approved. How templates stay a fixed path is in [generate documents from templates](/prozess-check/blog/generate-documents-from-templates). Send it to the participant, and send a copy to the payer only if they asked for proof of attendance.

If the form only collects enquiries and there is no course seat count yet, start smaller: acknowledge receipt and file the row in the CRM. This page starts where the course already has a seat limit and the QR-bill is part of daily work.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: an evening language course, 14 seats, a fixed fee of CHF 890. By Wednesday, 11 seats are confirmed. Three registrations arrive. Two name the participant as the payer. The third names an employer with a cost centre. The place check confirms all three, because 11 plus 3 is still within 14. Confirmations go to the participants. Two QR-bills go to the participants, one to the employer with the cost centre in the reference. Address fields are separate so the billing program can follow the current SIX page.

The next morning a fourteenth registration arrives, and a fifteenth. The fourteenth is confirmed. The fifteenth is told they are first on the waiting list, with the course date and time so the wait is clear. Four days before the start, one confirmed participant cancels. The office offers the seat to the waiting-list person and waits for a yes before the invoice is created.

One participant has not paid the first QR-bill. On the day before the course, the reminder goes out with the sentence from the handbook: seat held until the start, then released. They pay that afternoon. The reminder stops. After the last evening the teacher marks 13 present. Thirteen certificates go out from the template. The person who cancelled does not get one. Anyone who paid but did not attend does not get one either, under the rule the office wrote down earlier. Nothing in this paragraph is a client.

## When you should not build this

Skip it when the course system already takes enrolment, payment and the certificate, and your staff use it. Skip it when you run a handful of courses a year and one person holds the list without errors you care about. Skip the model when every fee is negotiated. A fixed course price can be a field. A negotiated price is a draft a person confirms before the QR-bill exists.

If the only pain is "emails from the website form sit in the inbox", that is a narrower job than a full course chain. Do not buy a waiting list for a missing acknowledgement.

Keep personal data out of the marketing form when the same form feeds the billing address. A «newsletter yes» field next to the cost centre mixes purposes. Split registration and marketing into two forms, or two clearly separate steps, if your data-protection adviser asks. That is again a working rule, not legal advice.
