## Answer group and event enquiries in hospitality

You answer a group or event enquiry in a fixed order: take the request apart, check whether the date is actually free, send an offer built from your packages, follow up once, then confirm only when the deposit matches. Judgement, price and yes or no stay with the house. Copying from last year's file, and chasing when nobody replies, are not decisions.

This is for the owner or the office of a Swiss restaurant or small hotel that gets several event mails a week. Where that job sits among the other office work is in [AI in restaurants and hotels](/prozess-check/blog/ai-in-hospitality). This page is only the offer path.

## What has to be in the enquiry before you write

An offer is only as good as the fields you can fill. Before anyone writes prices, the enquiry needs a date, a start time, a party size, and whether they want a table in the restaurant, a private room, or rooms plus dinner. Often missing are the menu wish, drinks, a projector or microphone, and who receives the invoice.

If those are missing, the first reply is a short list of questions, not an offer. Sending last year's package to a mail that only says "dinner for about 20 in June" produces a second round of mail, and a date you have half-held.

Many of these mails land in a shared address. Sorting that inbox is its own job, described in [sorting a shared inbox](/prozess-check/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). Once a mail is marked as a group enquiry, this sequence starts.

## Check availability before the offer goes out

The offer promises a place. That promise has to match the book you already use.

In a restaurant, check the private room and the cover count on the main floor. A party of 18 in the room can still be wrong if the floor is already full and the same kitchen serves both. In a small hotel, check room types and arrival dates as well. A channel that syncs ordinary rooms does not answer "twelve rooms and dinner" on its own.

A workflow can read the date and party size from the mail and place a draft hold, visible as a draft, not a confirmed booking. If the book has no connection you use, the draft is a line in the list the office checks each morning. A person releases or keeps it. The workflow does not tell a guest the date is free.

## Build the offer from the package, not from the last file

The usual method is to open the last similar offer and hope the old company name is gone from the footer. That is how the wrong price and room survive into March.

The reliable method is in [creating documents from templates](/prozess-check/blog/generate-documents-from-templates): one template per document type, named fields, one data source, a PDF, a person checking before send. For events the fields are party size, date, start and end, room or table, menu name, price per person, drinks package, room hire if you charge it, deposit, reply date, and the cancellation line you already use. Dietary notes stay free text that a person reads.

Prices live in the package list, not in prose. If Thursday dinner is a different package from Saturday, those are two rows. The workflow fills the row that matches the date. It does not invent a discount. A person can still change the price; the change is on the offer before it goes out.

Supplier ordering starts after confirmation, in the [KitchenCrew case](/prozess-check/blog/kitchencrew-supplier-ordering-case). Do not fold the kitchen order into the guest offer.

## Follow up, confirm, and match the deposit

Group offers go quiet. The reply date on the offer is the trigger. How follow-up differs from a first offer is in [following up on quotes](/prozess-check/blog/quote-follow-ups). For events: while you wait, the date is only half-held. If the reply date passes and you still need the room, release it rather than send a third reminder. A workflow can draft the reminder and list expired holds. A person sends the reminder and decides the release.

Confirmation is the deposit, not a friendly "yes" in the mail. Amount and payment reference are on the offer. When the bank line arrives, someone matches the reference to the enquiry and marks the booking confirmed. A workflow can match when the reference is in the payment text, and flag a payment that is short or matches nothing. It should not mark confirmed on a partial payment unless your rule says so.

Only then does the kitchen or housekeeping receive the sheet: date, time, party size, menu, allergies as written, room setup. Before the deposit, the sheet is a draft. After it, it is an order the service can trust.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a restaurant with one private room and a floor of ordinary tables. An enquiry arrives for 18 people on a Thursday in March, from 18:30, two menu choices, a drinks package, and a projector. The mail does not name an invoice address.

The workflow pulls date, time and party size, and stops for the invoice address. The book shows the room free. The floor already has a normal Thursday load; a person judges that acceptable because the room has its own service station. The draft offer uses the house Thursday package: menu B, drinks package, room hire as a separate line, deposit, reply within ten days. None of these figures is a market price; they show the fields. A person checks the package is still current, writes one sentence about the projector, and sends the PDF from the office mailbox.

Ten days later, with no reply, a reminder draft is waiting. There is still no reply, so the person releases the room. A different enquiry in the same week pays the deposit with the reference on the offer. The workflow matches the bank line, marks that booking confirmed, and puts the menu sheet in the kitchen folder. The person still decides whether a projector means an earlier setup, and writes that on the sheet.

Keep reply dates and deposit amounts in the package list, not only in the last offer. Then office and service see the same numbers when three enquiries run in parallel in March.

## Who decides what in the house

One person owns group enquiries, another owns the book, a third sends PDFs. Without names on paper, the flow stops when someone is on holiday. A workflow may prepare drafts; it does not replace the person who releases a tight Thursday or writes a discount for a regular guest.

## When to leave it alone

Leave it if you send a few event offers a year. Opening last year's file is faster than maintaining packages. Leave it if every enquiry is priced from scratch with no stable package. Leave it if the book itself is unreliable. Automating an offer on a book nobody trusts only sends confident mail about dates you do not have.

A hotel that already produces function sheets in its property system should use that sheet. The gap is often the mail that never becomes a sheet, and the deposit that never gets marked.
