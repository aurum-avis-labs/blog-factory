## Answer group and event enquiries in hospitality

You answer a group or event enquiry in a fixed order: take the request apart, check whether the date is actually free, send an offer built from your packages, follow up once, then confirm only when the deposit matches. The judgement, the price and the yes or no stay with the house. The copying from last year's file, and the chase when nobody replies, do not.

This is for the owner or the office of a Swiss restaurant or small hotel that gets several event mails a week: a company dinner, a family celebration, a room block with a meal. Where that job sits among the other office work is in [AI in restaurants and hotels](/blog/ai-in-hospitality). This page is only the offer path.

## What has to be in the enquiry before you write

An offer is only as good as the fields you can fill. Before anyone writes prices, the enquiry needs a date, a start time, a party size, and whether they want a table in the restaurant, a private room, or rooms plus dinner. Useful, and often missing, are the menu wish, drinks, a projector or a microphone, and who the invoice goes to.

If those are missing, the first reply is a short list of questions, not an offer. Sending last year's package to a mail that only says "dinner for about 20 in June" produces a second round of mail, and a date you have half-held for a party that was never defined.

Many of these mails land in a shared address, mixed with reservations, supplier notes and invoices. Sorting that inbox is its own job, described in [sorting a shared inbox](/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). Once a mail is marked as a group enquiry, this sequence starts.

</ol>

## Check availability before the offer goes out

The offer promises a place. That promise has to match the book you already use, not a second spreadsheet.

In a restaurant, check the private room and the cover count on the main floor. A party of 18 in the room can still be wrong if the floor is already full and the same kitchen has to serve both. In a small hotel, check room types and arrival dates as well as the restaurant. A channel that already syncs ordinary rooms does not answer "twelve rooms and dinner". Someone still looks.

A workflow can read the date and the party size from the mail and place a draft hold, visible as a draft, not as a confirmed booking. If the book has no connection you can use, the draft is a line in the same list the office already checks each morning. A person releases or keeps it. The workflow does not tell a guest the date is free.

## Build the offer from the package, not from the last file

The usual method is to open the last similar offer, save it under a new name, and hope the old company name is gone from the footer. That is how the wrong price and the wrong room survive into March.

The reliable method is the one in [creating documents from templates](/blog/generate-documents-from-templates): one template per document type, named fields, one source for the data, a PDF, a person checking before it is sent. For events the fields are not "customer, project, amount". They are party size, date, start and end, room or table, menu name, price per person, drinks package, room hire if you charge it, deposit amount, reply date, and the cancellation line you already use. Dietary notes stay a free-text field that a person reads. They are not a checkbox the template invents.

Prices live in the package list, not in the prose. If Thursday dinner is a different package from Saturday, those are two rows. The workflow fills the row that matches the date. It does not invent a discount. A person can still change the price for a guest you want, and that change is written on the offer before it goes out.

Supplier ordering is a different job. It starts after the event is confirmed, and it is covered in the [KitchenCrew case](/blog/kitchencrew-supplier-ordering-case). Do not fold the kitchen order into the guest offer.

## Follow up, confirm, and match the deposit

Group offers go quiet. The reply date on the offer is the trigger, not a feeling at the end of the week. How a follow-up differs from a first offer, and when to stop, is in [following up on quotes](/blog/quote-follow-ups). For events one extra rule matters: while you wait, the date is only half-held. If the reply date passes and you still need the room for someone else, the next step is a release, not a third reminder. A workflow can draft the reminder and, separately, list holds that have expired. A person sends the reminder and decides the release.

Confirmation is the deposit, not a friendly "yes" in the mail. The amount and the payment reference are on the offer. When the bank line arrives, someone matches the reference to the enquiry and marks the booking confirmed. A workflow can do that match when the reference is in the payment text, and it can flag a payment that is short or that matches nothing. It should not mark a booking confirmed on a partial payment unless your own rule says so.

Only then does the kitchen, or housekeeping, receive the sheet: date, time, party size, menu, allergies as written by the guest, room setup. Before the deposit, that sheet is a draft. After it, it is an order the service can trust.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a restaurant with one private room and a floor of ordinary tables. An enquiry arrives for 18 people on a Thursday in March, from 18:30, two menu choices, a drinks package, and a projector. The mail does not say who receives the invoice.

The workflow pulls date, time and party size, and stops for the invoice address. The book shows the room free. The floor already has a normal Thursday load, which a person judges as acceptable because the room has its own service station. The draft offer uses the Thursday package the house actually sells in this story: menu B, a drinks package, room hire as a separate line, a deposit, and a reply within ten days. None of these figures is a market price. They exist only so the fields are visible. A person checks that the Thursday package is still current, writes one sentence about the projector, and sends the PDF from the office mailbox.

Ten days later, with no reply, a reminder draft is waiting. There is still no reply after the reminder, so the person releases the room. A different enquiry in the same week pays the deposit with the reference on the offer. The workflow matches the bank line, marks that second booking confirmed, and puts the menu sheet in the kitchen folder. The person still decides whether a projector means an earlier setup, and writes that on the sheet.

## When to leave it alone

Leave it alone if you send a few event offers a year. Opening last year's file is faster than maintaining packages. Leave it alone if every enquiry is a negotiation with no stable package: a wedding that is priced from scratch each time. And leave it alone if the book itself is unreliable. Automating an offer on top of a book nobody trusts only sends confident mail about dates you do not have.

A hotel that already produces function sheets inside its property system should use that sheet. The gap, if there is one, is the mail that never becomes a sheet, and the deposit that never gets marked.
