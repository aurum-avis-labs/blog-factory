## Is security awareness training mandatory in Switzerland?

Security awareness training is not spelled out as a standalone legal duty in Switzerland, but the revised Federal Act on Data Protection requires appropriate technical and organisational measures for data security, and training is one of the most common ways SMEs show they take human risk seriously. Insurers, customers and standards such as ISO/IEC 27001 often ask whether you train staff, even when the law stays silent on the word «awareness».

This article is for owners and office leads in German-speaking Switzerland with roughly 20 to 250 employees. It is practical orientation, not legal advice. Which topics belong in a session is covered in [security awareness training topics](/security/blog/security-awareness-training-topics).

## What the FADP actually requires

[Art. 8 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de) obliges controllers to ensure data security with measures suited to the processing. The ordinance adds detail on documentation and breach handling. Neither text lists «annual phishing course» as a fixed sentence.

In practice, regulators and advisers treat staff behaviour as part of organisational measures: who may send client data, how payments are verified, how incidents are reported. Ignoring behaviour while buying firewalls alone is hard to defend after a preventable fraud.

Criminal fines under the FADP can reach CHF 250'000 against natural persons and require intent ([EDÖB overview](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/strafbestimmungen)). That is not a reason to scare staff, but it explains why management asks for documented steps.

## Who asks besides the law

Cyber insurance applications and renewals often include questions about training frequency and content. We have not verified a universal insurer checklist here; read your own form and the insurer’s published guidance.

Large customers, especially in the EU supply chain, may require training evidence in contracts or supplier audits. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 control A 6.3 expects an ongoing awareness programme with records. A one-hour session does not certify you, but it can be part of the evidence.

EU customers may pass NIS2-style obligations through contracts even though NIS2 does not apply directly in Switzerland. Article 20 NIS2 expects training for management bodies and regular offers for staff. That is contractual pressure, not Swiss statute. If a contract includes a training clause, read that clause, not a general leaflet.

## What is not a general SME duty

The [BACS cyberattack reporting duty from 1 April 2025](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/25-mp-de) applies to operators of critical infrastructure, not to most SMEs. Do not tell every workshop that they must report to BACS within 24 hours.

## How often should you train?

The law does not set a frequency. Auditors and insurers usually expect at least annual refreshers and training at onboarding. After a real incident (phishing click, changed IBAN, CEO fraud), an extra session makes sense even if the calendar already shows «training done».

## E-learning or live training?

Both can count as evidence. E-learning scales for basics and logged completion. Live sessions fit questions from your own inbox, payment checks and reporting paths. Many SMEs combine: live once a year, a short module at hire. What matters is relevance to your risks, not generic examples only.

## What counts as proof?

Keep it simple: calendar invite or sign-in sheet, slide titles or one-page summary, facilitator name, language used. Store ISO/IEC 27001:2022 control A 6.3 mapping separately from attendance. The session does not certify the company.

## Security awareness and privacy training

Security awareness covers fraud, phishing and authentication. Privacy training covers everyday handling of personal data under the FADP and GDPR. Many firms book both; audiences overlap but the questions differ. Privacy content does not automatically belong in every phishing slot.

## Who can wait briefly

A five-person startup with no client payments and no personal data beyond payroll may prioritise access control first. Once you handle client mail, invoices and HR files at scale, «we are too small to train» stops working.

Companies with a full-time security team and learning platform are outside this article’s audience.

## Culture beats checkbox

Mandatory on paper but skipped in practice helps nobody. The certificate matters less if management never says why the training exists or never sits in the room.

If someone clicks a real link, training did not fail once; your response path still must work. See [clicked a phishing link at work](/security/blog/clicked-a-phishing-link-at-work).

## Working with your IT partner

Most SMEs deliver training with an external facilitator while IT owns mail rules and MFA. Agree who sends reminders, who collects attendance, and who answers report mail after the session. Without ownership, annual training becomes an HR calendar entry that IT never hears about.

## Swiss context reminders

Fraud dominates public reports to BACS; payment and CEO-style requests belong in every annual slot even if last year was «only» phishing links. The cyberattack reporting duty for critical infrastructure does not apply to most SMEs; do not confuse staff with BACS 24-hour rules unless you know you are in scope.
