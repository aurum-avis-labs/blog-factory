## MVP brief template

An **MVP brief template** is a short document that states which decision the first release should enable, who it is for, the one path users must complete, how you will know it worked, and what you are not building yet. It is not a pitch deck, a feature wish list, or a substitute for discovery. Partners use it to cut scope before anyone writes production code. If you cannot fill the success line without guessing, you are not ready to commission a build; you are still choosing the experiment.

This is for founders and innovation leads in Switzerland who already have a direction and need a partner, an agency, or an internal team to align on the same learning goal. What an MVP is in the first place is in [what is an MVP](/blog/what-is-an-mvp). When a fake door is enough and when behaviour needs a deployed product is in [fake door test vs real MVP](/blog/fake-door-test-vs-mvp).

## MVP brief template: the sections that matter

Copy the headings into a doc. One page is enough if each line is specific.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li><strong>Decision.</strong> One sentence: pricing, segment fit, investor demo, internal stop, or something you can argue with in a meeting.</li>
  <li><strong>User and segment.</strong> Who must complete the path without you sitting beside them. Not «everyone with a phone».</li>
  <li><strong>Core path.</strong> The smallest journey that tests the riskiest assumption. Login, one job, one outcome.</li>
  <li><strong>Success measure.</strong> A number or observable rule in the first four to eight weeks after launch, with a date.</li>
  <li><strong>Out of scope.</strong> Features, integrations, languages, and roles you refuse to fund in v1.</li>
  <li><strong>Constraints.</strong> Must-use tools, data residency, compliance you already know about, and who owns the repository.</li>
  <li><strong>Open risks.</strong> Payments, identity, a second language, or an API you have not spoken to yet.</li>
</ul>

A brief that only says «build the platform» invites a larger quote. A brief that says «eight of fifteen invited users finish onboarding without a call» invites cuts.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>A brief does not protect the idea. Ideas as such cannot be protected; execution and brand can ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)). It does not prove market size, unit economics, or that your first channel scales. It only makes the learning goal legible before money and calendar time move.</p>
</aside>

## What the brief does not prove

Filling every box feels productive. It is still a plan, not evidence.

The brief does not show that strangers want the product. That may need a fake door or paid traffic first. It does not show that a vibe-coded prototype is safe to scale; export and review are a different step, as in moving a Lovable app to production code. It does not replace a discovery conversation when integrations, payments, or regulated data are in play.

Treat the success measure as falsifiable. «Users love it» is not a measure. «At least six of ten pilot accounts upload one file in week four» is.

    <ul>
      <li>Founders who can name the user and the one path, even if details shift in discovery</li>
      <li>Teams that already ran a demand test and need behaviour measured next</li>
      <li>SME leads who must show the board a test with a defined stop rule</li>
    </ul>

    <ul>
      <li>Anyone whose segment or promise changes every week</li>
      <li>Teams that only need a landing page and analytics, not a product loop</li>
      <li>Buyers who want a fixed full product scope before any user sees v1</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

## A made-up example: one page before the first call

A made-up example: a founder in Bern, not a client. The product matches students with short household jobs. The decision line reads: «In eight weeks, decide whether at least five unrelated students complete a job posting and receive one application without us manually introducing them.» The user is a student with a Swiss mobile number. The core path is sign up, post one job with a category and budget band, receive an in-app message from an applicant, mark the job filled. Success is five postings that reached «filled» with no founder in the chat. Out of scope lists payments, ratings, insurance, contracts, KYC, and a native app store release. Constraints note Azure in EU, German and English copy only in v1, and that the Git organisation must sit with the startup from day one. Open risks flag identity for minors and whether job text needs moderation.

That page took an afternoon. The first partner call spent forty minutes on the open risks and the measure, not on whether to add a social feed.

## Discovery still belongs after the brief

A good partner will challenge the path, suggest a narrower cut, or ask for a paid discovery week before a fixed price. That is not a failure of your template. It is the point where assumptions meet someone who has shipped before.

Ask how scope changes when user feedback arrives, who approves the change, and what you keep if the engagement ends. Employee-written code in a Swiss firm often belongs to the employer under copyright rules; with an external studio you still want assignment and repository access in writing ([software ownership overview](https://www.rentschpartner.ch/ict-law/schutz-von-software)). The brief should not pretend those topics are settled when they are not.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>How long should an MVP brief be?</h3>
  <p>One to three pages if each section has a concrete line. Longer documents usually hide an unfixed decision or a scope list that should be in the out-of-scope section instead.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Can I use the same brief for a fake door and a full MVP?</h3>
  <p>Reuse the decision and user lines. Change the core path and the success measure. A fake door brief ends at clicks or signups; an MVP brief must describe behaviour inside a deployed product.</p>
</div>

## When a discovery conversation on the contact page fits

Use the brief as the attachment for a structured first conversation on the [contact page](/contact) when the decision, the measure, and the out-of-scope list are written, and you need a partner to stress-test them before build. We use that document in discovery to see whether the next step is a scoped validation engagement, a narrower experiment, or more discovery on your side.

**Do not book** if you only want a price without a learning goal, if you are still choosing the segment, or if you expect a full product quote from a one-line idea. In those cases, finish the brief or run a lighter test first. **Do book** when the brief is honest about risks and you are ready to argue about what v1 must prove in the first two months.
