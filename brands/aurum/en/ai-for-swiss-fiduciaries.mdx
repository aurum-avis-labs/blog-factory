## AI for Swiss fiduciary firms: the work around the software

For a small Swiss fiduciary firm, AI is worth it in the workflows around the accounting software, not inside it. bexio, Abacus and similar programs already take receipts, match bank lines and give you access to your clients' books. What still eats the week is the path to that point: receipts that clients send in every possible shape, the bank statement you ask for three times, the same client questions by mail, and the checklist you rebuild every period. Those four are the candidates. The booking decision, the close and the advice stay with you.

This is for the owner or partner of a Treuhand firm with a handful of people and anywhere from a few dozen to a few hundred clients. It is not a case for replacing your accounting software, and it is not a claim that software can do fiduciary work.

## What the accounting software already covers

Start with what you already pay for, because a lot of it is there. bexio describes on its [accounting software page](https://www.bexio.com/en-CH/accounting-software) that receipts can be uploaded to the account or photographed with the bexio Go app, that credits and debits from e-banking are reconciled automatically with recorded debtors and creditors, and that clients can give their fiduciary direct online access. Abacus lists in its [accounts payable flyer](https://www.abacus.ch/fileadmin/ablage/02_dokumente/01_flyer/de/flyer_abacus_kreditorenbuchhaltung_de.pdf) automatic supplier and receipt capture with DeepBox, approval of documents with one click in MyAbacus, and payment formats such as pain.001.

If a client already photographs receipts into the app and the bank feed runs, there is nothing to build on that stretch. A second reader on documents the software already reads costs money and adds one more place for errors to hide.

The gap sits before and beside it: everything that happens before a document reaches the software, and everything that runs between you and the client while you wait for it.

## Four workflows worth a look

Four workflows repeat in almost every fiduciary office, follow rules, and start from data that is already digital. That is the profile that pays off.

### Receipt intake from clients

Clients send receipts by mail, as a phone photo, as a zip file at quarter end, forwarded from their own inbox without a word. Someone opens each mail, works out which client and which period it belongs to, saves the attachment in the right folder and notes that it arrived. That is sorting by rules: sender address, client number, document date. A workflow can do the sorting and put the doubtful cases in a separate queue.

### Requests for missing documents

Before the VAT return or the year-end, you know what you expect from each client: twelve bank statements, the credit card statements, the payroll list, the lease. The comparison between "expected" and "received" is a list. Writing the third reminder is not advisory work. A workflow can compare, prepare a reminder draft per client that sounds like your office, and track the status until the file is complete. A person still sends it.

### Client communication

Many client mails ask the same things. Has my tax return been filed, what is still missing, when is the next VAT deadline, can you send me last year's accounts. A workflow can assign such mails to the client, draft an answer from the client file and put it in front of the responsible person. It does not answer tax questions on its own.

### Recurring checklists

The VAT return for each period, the year-end documents list, the tax return every year. The checklist stays the same, only the client and the dates change. Preparing it per client, pre-filled with what is already known, is a job for a template and a rule, not for someone copying last year's file.

## Why a chat account alone does not change the week

A chat assistant helps with the sentence, not with the sorting. Swiss AI offers for fiduciaries are emerging. According to [Organisator](https://www.organisator.ch/en/management/2025-12-01/ki-fuer-2000-schweizer-treuhandbueros/), TREUHAND|SUISSE and Connect AI launched TreuhandGPT, an AI platform for more than 2,000 fiduciary offices, operated on Swiss infrastructure, with five specialised agents including tax and translation. That answers a question many firms had: where does client data go when someone drafts a letter with AI.

What such a tool does not do by itself is watch your client mailbox, file an attachment in client 214's folder for the third quarter, and tell you on Friday which six clients still owe a bank statement. That is a workflow between your mailbox, your file storage and your task list. The chat and the workflow are different jobs, and many firms need both.

## What stays with you

Judgement stays with a person: the booking when account, period or input VAT is unclear, the year-end, the tax return, the conversation with the client about their numbers. A workflow prepares, sorts and reminds. It does not decide.

Some work is not worth automating even though it repeats. A client who sends three receipts a year. A mandate where every document needs a phone call before it makes sense. A step one partner does once a year. The four questions in [which process to automate first](/blog/which-process-to-automate-first) apply here as well: it repeats, it hurts, it has a clear end, and the data already exists.

## Client data and confidentiality

Client documents contain personal data, often salaries and bank details. Under [Art. 9 of the Federal Act on Data Protection](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en#art_9), processing may be handed to a processor only if the data is processed as you would be allowed to process it yourself, and no statutory or contractual duty of confidentiality prohibits the transfer. A processor may bring in a third party only with prior approval. If your firm itself processes data on behalf of your clients, a new service is exactly such a third party. Many engagement letters also contain a confidentiality clause. Check both before client documents run through a new service, and ask your data-protection adviser when in doubt. This is practical guidance, not legal advice.

In practice, the workflow runs in an environment you control, for example your own Microsoft 365. Microsoft lists Switzerland as a [data residency geography](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/enterprise/m365-dr-overview) for Exchange Online, SharePoint and Teams. Client documents do not belong in free chat accounts.

## Where to start

Pick the one of the four workflows that costs your office the most hours in a normal month. Count for four weeks: how many client mails with documents, how many reminders, how long each one takes. If the numbers are small, stop there. If one stands out, you have your case.
