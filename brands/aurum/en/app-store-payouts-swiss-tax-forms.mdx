## App Store payouts and tax forms for Swiss companies

App Store payouts and tax forms for Swiss companies start in the store consoles, not in your bookkeeping software. Apple and Google pay the legal entity on the developer account after you sign the paid-apps agreement, add a bank account they accept, and submit the US tax paperwork they require from every merchant, including Swiss firms. The forms address US withholding and reporting on money Apple or Google treats as US-source income. They do not replace Swiss income tax, VAT registration, or the conversation with your fiduciary about how to book the first franc that lands.

This is for founders and innovation leads who already ship or plan to ship on the stores and need a calm map of admin work, not for teams still debating whether the product belongs in a browser first. The submission path itself is in [publish an app on the App Store](/blog/publish-an-app-on-the-app-store). Whether the binary will survive [guideline 4.2](/blog/app-store-guideline-4-2) is a separate gate. Production quality before you chase revenue is set out in [why we build production-quality MVPs](/blog/why-we-build-production-quality-mvps).

## What the US forms are for

Apple and Google act as withholding agents under US rules on payments to non-US persons. W-8BEN-E is for entities: it documents non-US status and any treaty claim on income they pay you. Apple routes non-US developers through questions in Connect; some corrections need Apple support after submission.

Google’s payments centre states that without a valid tax form it may apply backup withholding at 24% or chapter 3 withholding at 30% on applicable payments, and that a treaty may reduce the rate when you submit a valid form with a treaty claim that matches the income type.

## W-8BEN-E in practice (experience, not tax advice)

Founders often complete the electronic questionnaire in App Store Connect or the Google payments profile, declare Switzerland as tax residence, enter the Swiss UID or other identifier requested, and tick treaty benefits only when their adviser says the income type matches. Apple’s vendor guidance states that a valid treaty exemption on W-8BEN-E requires a US or foreign tax identification number and that the form generally expires three years after signature.

That workflow is operational experience, not tax advice. Treaty articles, beneficial ownership, and income characterisation belong with your adviser. If you are unsure about a checkbox, stop before submit: Apple notes that some fields cannot be changed later inside Connect.

## Swiss side: VAT and income tax

Google Play’s VAT help lists Switzerland among countries where you are responsible for VAT on paid app and in-app purchases by customers in Switzerland, and states that Google may apply Swiss VAT on its service fees to Swiss developers in some circumstances. Apple’s country annex in Connect focuses on other jurisdictions; Swiss accounts still file the US forms and use monthly finance reports for local VAT.

App turnover is ordinary business income for most Swiss companies. VAT registration is mandatory from CHF 100'000 turnover in many cases. An accepted W-8BEN-E does not replace MWST filing. Your fiduciary needs every proceeds report, not only the net wire.

## What store payouts do not prove

An arriving payout shows that someone paid Apple or Google, that the platform processed refunds and commissions, and that your bank details worked. It does not prove product-market fit, retention, or that strangers will find the app without paid acquisition. It does not prove your MWST filings are complete or that US and Swiss numbers reconcile without manual adjustments. First revenue is a finance milestone, not validation on its own.

## A made-up example: first wire in March

A made-up example: a Zürich GmbH with a niche B2B tool ships on iOS and Android. The account holder signs the Paid Apps Agreement, adds a CHF business account, and completes Connect’s tax questions for a Swiss entity; Google’s payments profile gets a parallel W-8 the same week. February sales are modest. In March, Apple’s finance report shows gross charges, commission, and a small foreign VAT line; the net wire matches the report. Play’s export adds a reminder that Swiss VAT on domestic sales is the developer’s job. Both PDFs go to the fiduciary, who books gross turnover and fees and asks whether MWST registration is still voluntary. Nobody treats the wire as proof that ads will scale.
