## Sharing files that contain personal data: what to check first

Sharing files that contain personal data is everyday work in Swiss SMEs: customer lists in Excel, payroll exports, project folders on SharePoint, or a USB stick for an auditor. The Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP) does not forbid spreadsheets; it expects you to limit data, control access, and choose channels that match sensitivity ([Art. 8 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). E-mail attachments are covered separately in [sending personal data by email](/security/blog/sending-personal-data-by-email). This page is for owners, HR, and finance in firms of roughly 20 to 250 people who need practical rules for links, sticks, and shared drives.

Payment controls sit in a different article; see [four-eyes principle for payments](/security/blog/four-eyes-principle-for-payments) if finance asked you to review transfers, not file shares.

## Excel and exports: what goes wrong

Finance exports «all customers» for a campaign. HR sends a full payroll sheet to a manager who only needed one team. Sales adds e-mail addresses to a file that lives on a personal laptop sync folder. Each copy multiplies retention and breach risk. Before you share, ask: who must see named rows, for how long, and can the recipient download or forward?

Password-protected Excel is better than nothing, but passwords travel in chat or sit on sticky notes. Prefer company storage with login, group permissions, and expiry on guest access. Where the product allows it, use view-only for external readers instead of download.

## Cloud links and «anyone with the link»

Link sharing is fast and dangerous when defaults are loose. A link forwarded in WhatsApp or pasted into a ticket system can outlive the project. Set links to specific people where the product allows it, add expiry dates for externals, and turn off anonymous download for health or salary data.

If a partner insists on their own portal, check contract and jurisdiction. Swiss hosting is not always mandatory, but you must document transfers ([Art. 16 ff. FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)) and inform people where required.

  <p>Version history and recycle bins keep «deleted» rows for weeks. A share mistake may still be recoverable from backups; incident response should include IT, not only the person who clicked Share.</p>

## USB sticks and physical media

USB sticks are convenient for installers and auditors. For personal data, use encrypted sticks issued by IT, register who received which device, and collect sticks after the meeting. Never leave candidate CVs or patient lists on a stick in a drawer.

Printed lists from an export belong with clean desk habits: do not leave them on desks overnight or bin them without shredding. A dedicated blog post on desk and file disposal follows this one in the same series.

## Auditors, trustees, and advisers

Externals often ask for a folder «for the review». Create a separate share with read access, an expiry date, and logging instead of duplicating the live customer folder. The trustee usually does not need every historic marketing export. After the job: revoke guest access and ask whether anyone saved a local copy.

## A made-up example: the marketing export

A made-up example: a services firm with 62 employees in Bern. Marketing asks IT for «the customer Excel» for a mail campaign. IT exports 4'200 rows with phone numbers and last purchase dates to a desktop file and uploads it to a link «anyone in the company can edit». An intern copies the link into a private notebook app. Three months later the notebook vendor has a breach. The firm documents the incident, tightens export approval, and moves campaigns to a role-limited list with no download for juniors. HR adds a ten-minute slot in onboarding: exports need a ticket and a named approver.

The gap was habit, not malice.

## Contracts with processors

When an agency edits your export on their laptop, they usually act as a processor. Your file share should not replace a data processing agreement where [Art. 9 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de) requires one. Note the agency in your records and limit what they download.

## Who can keep it light

A ten-person firm with one shared drive and no USB culture may only need: no public links, no full exports without the owner, and shred printed lists.

A 200-person firm with sales, support, and external agencies needs written export rules, logging, and a short incident path when a link leaks.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Export only approved columns to a named recipient</li>
  <li>No «anyone with the link» on HR or payroll folders</li>
  <li>Put guest expiry dates in the calendar</li>
  <li>After offboarding: review and remove that person’s shares</li>
</ul>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>May we use WeTransfer for CVs?</h3>
  <p>Only if contract, retention, and access match your hiring process and you informed candidates. Many firms prefer the applicant portal or encrypted company storage instead of consumer transfer sites.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do we have to document every shared file?</h3>
  <p>The FADP does not require a form per row, but you should be able to show who had access and when. A short ticket or an e-mail naming the recipient, purpose and expiry is enough in many SMEs. Without a trail, a later erasure request becomes difficult.</p>
</div>

## Sync folders on private devices

When sales stores customer data in a personal sync folder, you lose control over backups, sharing, and deletion. Ban personal data outside approved shares in writing and spot-check at offboarding. IT can add DLP rules that flag exports to known consumer clouds.

## Excel passwords and separate channels

A spreadsheet password protects against reading on a lost laptop, not against forwarding the file together with the password. Separate the channels: sensitive lists sit in company storage, and the password travels by a second channel (a call, not the same chat thread). For very sensitive data, stop using Excel as the transport and use a portal or signed access with logging instead.

## When a link leaks

A forwarded link can be a data security breach. Document internally whether a high risk to affected people is likely, and treat FDPIC notification separately from an e-mail mistake. What matters is content and audience, not the word «Excel».

## Before you hit send

If you only need one column, do not export the whole table. Check the recipient before the link leaves the firm. An open link with no expiry is a copy you cannot take back.
