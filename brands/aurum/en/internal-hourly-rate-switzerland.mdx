## Calculate the internal hourly cost of an employee in Switzerland

The internal hourly cost is what the company pays to have that employee available for a year, divided by the hours they are actually there to work. For 2026 the employer's share of AHV, IV and income compensation (EO) is 5.3 percent of gross pay. The employer's share of unemployment insurance (ALV) is 1.1 percent, only up to CHF 148'200 in that job. Pension, accident insurance and family allowances are not one Swiss percentage. You take those from this person's invoices. The result is a cost, not the rate you bill a customer.

[How to calculate the benefit of an automation](/blog/calculate-automation-benefit) uses this hour as an input and does not derive it. What a later build costs is in [what automation costs in Switzerland](/blog/what-automation-costs-switzerland). The percentages are the 2026 leaflets, read on 30 September 2026. Replace them in a later year. Reference age, cross-border work and private-household settlement follow other rules from the compensation office.

## What you add on top of the annual gross

Start from the annual gross, including a 13th monthly salary if you pay one. Add only money the company pays on top. Amounts already deducted from the wage are not a second cost.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Annual gross</h3><p>Salary, a 13th month if you pay it, and regular allowances that count as AHV wages. A bonus you expect again belongs here. A one-off you will not repeat does not.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>AHV, IV, EO and ALV, the employer half</h3><p>These percentages are the same across Switzerland. Use the next section, not a round surcharge.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Pension, accident, family allowances, admin, daily sickness</h3><p>These are invoices. Copy this year's employer amounts. There is no federal rate to type in.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Hours the person is there to work</h3><p>Contracted hours minus holiday, public holidays on a working day, and other paid absences.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Divide</h3><p>Annual employer cost divided by those hours is the internal hourly cost.</p></li>
</ol>

The same three lines can sit behind a calculator later. This page does not link to one, because that page does not exist yet.

- Annual employer cost = annual gross + employer AHV/IV/EO + employer ALV + employer pension + occupational accident premium + family-allowance contribution + compensation-office admin fee + the employer share of daily sickness cover
- Productive hours = weekly hours times (52 minus holiday weeks), minus (public holidays on working days + other absence days) times (weekly hours divided by 5)
- Internal hourly cost = annual employer cost divided by productive hours

That hour line assumes five working days. Another pattern needs the hours from the contract.

## Which 2026 rates are the same for every employer

The employer pays 5.3 percent AHV/IV/EO: 4.35 percent AHV, 0.7 percent IV and 0.25 percent EO. The employee pays the same 5.3 percent, which you deduct. Only the employer half goes into this cost. [Leaflet 2.01](https://www.ahv-iv.ch/p/2.01.d), status 1 January 2026, states the 10.6 percent total and the split.

ALV is 2.2 percent in total, so 1.1 percent for the employer, on pay up to CHF 148'200 in that employment. Since 1 January 2023 nothing further is due above the ceiling. [Leaflet 2.08](https://www.ahv-iv.ch/p/2.08.d), status 1 January 2025, was still the sheet on ahv-iv.ch when we checked, and leaflet 2.01 points to it. AHV, IV and EO continue on the full wage. The ceiling applies to each employment on its own. Compensation offices also charge an admin fee, borne by the employer. Leaflet 2.01 says so and publishes no national rate, so you copy the Ausgleichskasse invoice.

  <p>There is no single Swiss percentage for the pension fund, accident insurance or family allowances. The Federal Social Insurance Office says those current-year rates cannot be stated, because they depend on the fund, the insurer and the canton. An average from an earlier year would be the wrong number in your sheet.</p>
</aside>

Pension is compulsory when the annual wage with that employer is above CHF 22'680. The [amounts valid from 1 January 2026](https://www.bsv.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/sAgdISSXenMT/d_Betr%C3%A4ge%202026.pdf) keep the thresholds: entry CHF 22'680, coordination deduction CHF 26'460, upper wage limit CHF 90'720, minimum coordinated wage CHF 3'780. Obligatory old-age credits on the coordinated wage are 7 percent at 25–34, 10 percent at 35–44, 15 percent at 45–54 and 18 percent at 55–65, on the [BSV old-age page](https://www.bsv.admin.ch/de/altersvorsorge). The employer pays at least half. The invoice is often higher, because the plan can go further and disability and death are billed too. Risk cover starts after age 17, old-age savings at 25. Use this year's employer column.

Occupational accident premiums are owed by the employer. Non-occupational accident insurance is paid by the employee, unless the contract puts it on the company. The [BSV contribution overview](https://www.bsv.admin.ch/de/beitraege-im-ueberblick) states that split. Family allowances follow the Familienausgleichskasse for the canton of the employer's seat. There is no Swiss rate. Add a daily sickness share, or the non-occupational accident premium, only if the company actually pays it.

## How many hours you divide by

Divide by hours the person is available to work, not by 52 times the weekly hours. Holiday, public holidays and other absences are time you pay and do not get as work.

The legal minimum is four weeks a year, five until the 20th birthday. A contract or collective agreement can give more. That is the [SECO holiday FAQ](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/faq-ferien) on article 329a of the Code of Obligations. [kmu.admin.ch](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/de/gutes-management-der-ferienplanung) states the same minimum. Public holidays are cantonal. Count those that fall on a working day. There is no federal total.

Other absence is last year's pattern for the role, or a normal year if last year was unusual: sickness, accident, military or civil protection you still pay. Do not also apply a billing ratio. That prices an hour sold to a client. For internal work, the person counts when they are there.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: an office role at CHF 85'000 gross, 13th month included, five weeks of holiday, a 42-hour week, eight public holidays on working days and five other absence days. The pension, accident, family-allowance, admin and daily sickness lines are invented. Replace them. The wage is invented too.

Only the percentages and thresholds come from the 2026 leaflets. CHF 85'000 is under the upper limit of CHF 90'720, so the coordinated wage is CHF 85'000 minus CHF 26'460, which is CHF 58'540, above the minimum of CHF 3'780. At age 40 the minimum old-age credit is 10 percent, CHF 5'854, and the employer must cover at least half, CHF 2'927. The example invoice of CHF 6'200 sits above that floor, which is what a statement usually does once risk cover and a fuller plan are included.

| Line | Source | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual gross | Invented wage | CHF 85'000 |
| AHV/IV/EO, employer 5.3 percent | Leaflet 2.01 | CHF 4'505 |
| ALV, employer 1.1 percent | Leaflet 2.08, under the ceiling | CHF 935 |
| Pension, employer share | Invented invoice | CHF 6'200 |
| Occupational accident | Invented premium | CHF 510 |
| Family allowances at 1.8 percent | Invented fund rate | CHF 1'530 |
| Compensation-office admin | Invented fee | CHF 255 |
| Daily sickness, employer share | Invented premium | CHF 680 |
| Annual employer cost | Sum of the lines | CHF 99'615 |

Productive hours are 42 times (52 minus 5), minus (8 plus 5) times (42 divided by 5): 1'864.8 hours. CHF 99'615 divided by 1'864.8 is CHF 53.42 per productive hour. That is not a Swiss standard. Change the holiday, the absences or the pension invoice and the hour moves. Pay above CHF 148'200 changes only the ALV line: 1.1 percent stops at the ceiling, while 5.3 percent does not.

## When this figure is the wrong one

Part-time uses that person's own gross and weekly hours. Scaling a full-time cost by workload, then dividing by full-time hours, counts the charges twice or not at all. Dividends and the self-employed scale stay out. If an owner is also on the payroll, use the salary alone.

The benefit article uses a short stand-in: gross plus about 15 percent, divided by 1'800 hours. That shows the order of size. When payback sits close to the limit you set, replace the stand-in. A pension invoice far from 15 percent of gross, or a year with a lot of absence, is when the shortcut misleads.
