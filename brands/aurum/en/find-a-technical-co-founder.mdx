## Find a technical co-founder

You **find a technical co-founder** by writing down what they would own in the first six months, searching people who already understand your industry before you post a generic "CTO wanted", and running a small paid or unpaid trial before you discuss equity. In Switzerland that usually means your supplier network, former colleagues, alumni groups, and trade events, not only startup pitch nights. A co-founder is a long contract. Treat the search like hiring a board member who will also ship code.

This piece is for owners and innovation leads in a Swiss SME who want to turn know-how into an app, a portal, or a small SaaS and who believe they need a technical partner inside the cap table. It is not a guide to agency tenders or to hiring a dev shop for a fixed specification. If your first question is budget, start with [MVP cost in Switzerland](/blog/mvp-cost-switzerland) so you know what "build" can mean before you offer shares.

## What "technical co-founder" should mean on your one-pager

Most stalled searches fail on scope, not on talent. "Build the app" is not a job description. Split the work you imagine into product decisions, architecture, hands-on development, DevOps, security, and customer-facing demos. Mark what must live inside the company and what a contractor could do for twelve weeks.

A technical co-founder typically owns the technical direction and a large share of implementation while you own domain access, sales, and regulatory relationships. If you only need someone to execute a fixed spec you already wrote, you are looking for a lead developer or a partner firm, not a co-founder. If you need someone to tell you whether the idea should be an app at all, you are still in discovery.

## Where Swiss SMEs actually find technical co-founders

Cold posts on social media reach many people and filter few. For B2B know-how, warmer paths often work better:

- Former colleagues or integrators who have seen your processes
- Alumni networks from ETH, HSLU, HEIG-VD, or applied universities, approached with a concrete problem, not a pitch deck
- Industry associations and supplier events where engineers already speak your language
- Second-degree introductions from your accountant, lawyer, or key customer, with permission to use their name in the first line

Startup meetups in Zurich, Basel, or Lausanne can help when you want a consumer or platform play. For a niche B2B workflow, one credible intro from a plant manager often beats fifty LinkedIn messages.

## Equity, salary, and what you should not promise

Swiss founders often mix cash and equity. The split is a conversation, not a formula this article can print. What you can prepare: runway for at least six months of part-time work, clarity on whether the person keeps a day job, and written vesting so a short trial does not turn into a permanent cap-table surprise.

Ideas alone do not create legal protection. The Swiss Institute of Intellectual Property states that [ideas as such cannot be protected](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden). Non-disclosure agreements help with discipline; they do not replace a relationship where both sides can walk away. If you hire someone as an employee instead, remember that [software created by employees belongs to the employer](https://www.rentschpartner.ch/ict-law/schutz-von-software) unless your contract says otherwise.

Grants such as [Innosuisse Initial Coaching](https://www.innosuisse.admin.ch/de/initial-coaching-fur-start-up) can pay for external coaching while you search. They do not replace a technical partner and they do not fund unlimited dev hours.

## What finding a co-founder does not prove

A willing engineer is not proof that strangers will pay, that your channel works, or that the workflow survives without your personal introduction. Co-founder enthusiasm can keep you building the wrong product for imaginary buyers. Pair the search with customer evidence: interviews, a paid pilot, or a landing page test with a costly call to action.
