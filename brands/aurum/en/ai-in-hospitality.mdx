## AI in restaurants and hotels: the short answer

<p class="blog-lede">AI in a restaurant or small hotel earns its place in the office beside the till and the booking book, not on the pass during service.</p>

If you want to use AI in a Swiss restaurant, café or small hotel, the candidates usually appear after lunch: supplier orders, group emails, reservations outside the book, supplier invoices, hours on paper. The till, reservation software and hotel channels already cover the guest bill, the online table and channel availability. The menu, the price and the promise to a guest stay with you.

This is for owners and the person who runs the office, often alone after service.

## What the till, the book and the hotel system already do

Start with the systems you already pay for.

[Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/pos/restaurant/inventory/) describes inventory that deducts stock when a dish is sold, warns when stock is low and can trigger recurring supplier orders. [resmio](https://www.resmio.com/reservierungen/) states that guests can reserve online, including on your website and on Google, and that those bookings land in the reservation software. Its [table plan](https://www.resmio.com/planung/tischplan/) shows free, reserved and occupied tables. [Mews](https://www.mews.com/en/products/mews-channel-manager) states that its channel manager connects to more than 400 channels and syncs rates and availability in real time.

If your till already counts stock, your book already takes online tables and your hotel system already updates the channels, there is nothing to rebuild on that stretch. A second program that retypes what those systems already store only adds conflicting records. What your software can connect to is a question for the check.

The gap sits beside those systems: the message, the paper and the phone call nobody has entered yet.

## Five office jobs that keep returning

Five jobs show up in almost every small hospitality business. They follow rules, start from something written down and land on the same person.

### Supplier orders

A kitchen buys from several suppliers, each on a different path: web shop, email, phone, form. The list starts on the wall, at the pass or in a message after service. Someone splits it, turns "two crates" into the supplier's unit and types it again, once per supplier. Which article you need stays a kitchen decision. How one product turns a single list into drafts per supplier is in the [KitchenCrew case](/prozess-check/blog/kitchencrew-supplier-ordering-case). This page does not retell that case.

### Group and event enquiries

A company asks for dinner for 24, or a family asks for rooms and a meal. Someone checks whether the room or corner is free, looks up last year's package and writes an offer with menu, price per person, deposit and reply date, then chases quiet enquiries. Whether you want that group on a Saturday, and at what price, stays with you. Copying the old offer is not judgement.

### Reservations outside the book

Online bookings sit in the system if you use resmio or similar. The leak is everything else: a regular calls and a server writes on the pad; a guest emails the website address to move a booking and the mail stays in a personal inbox. By evening the book and pad disagree. A workflow can match mail or phone note to date and party size and put a draft in the book for a person to confirm. It should not confirm a table the book already shows as full.

### Supplier invoices and deposits

The guest bill is the till's job. The office job is the butcher's PDF, the drinks invoice that does not match the delivery note, and the deposit on the bank account never marked on the booking. Matching a clear invoice to an open order is sorting. Disputing a quantity or deciding whether a deposit is forfeited after a late cancellation is not.

### Hours for payroll

Service writes start and end on paper or in a chat, including a split shift and a one-evening replacement. Once a month someone retypes into the payroll file. Whether a shift counts as overtime stays with the person who runs payroll. A workflow can collect sheets into one list per person and flag a day with no entry. It does not decide the wage.

## What a small hotel adds

A restaurant's book is tables and covers. A small hotel adds arrivals, room moves and messages from booking sites. If a channel manager such as the one Mews describes already syncs availability, you do not need a second tool to stop double booking on those channels. You still need a person or a careful workflow for requests that are not a standard room: twelve rooms plus dinner, an extra bed, a late arrival written only in the guest's mail. Housekeeping hears about it when someone copies that mail onto the list.

## Why a chat account does not clear the afternoon

A chat assistant helps with a sentence: a polite answer to a group, a translation for a guest. It does not watch the inbox, check the book, file the supplier invoice or turn Friday's paper hours into a list. Those are workflows between the mailbox, the booking tool and the folder where you keep offers. Guest names, telephone numbers and invoices do not belong in a free chat account. Where customer data may go is in [ChatGPT and customer data under the Swiss DPA](/prozess-check/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg).

## What stays with the host

Judgement stays with a person: menu and price, whether you accept a group on the busiest Saturday, a promise about an allergy, a complaint at the table. A workflow prepares, sorts and reminds. It does not speak for the house.

Some of the five jobs are not worth automating even though they repeat. A restaurant that orders from one supplier in that supplier's shop once a week. A hotel with a handful of group enquiries a year. The four questions in [which process to automate first](/prozess-check/blog/which-process-to-automate-first) still apply: it repeats, it hurts, it has a clear end, and the data already exists.

## Where to start

Pick the job that costs the most hours in a normal fortnight. Count supplier orders, group mails, stray reservations, invoices and hour sheets. If one number is small, leave it. If one stands out, that is the case.
