## Role-based awareness training

Role-based security awareness training for finance, HR and reception in one Swiss SME means a shared core on phishing, reporting and MFA, then examples each function recognises in daily work. Finance sees changed IBANs and CEO payment mail, HR sees hiring scams and payroll phishing, reception sees fake couriers, tailgating and calls «from IT support». You do not need three copied courses if moderation and timing rotate the roles visibly.

This fits firms with about 20 to 250 staff planning one live session. A general topic list is in [security awareness training topics](/security/blog/security-awareness-training-topics).

## Shared core before roles split

Everyone stays for phishing recognition, the report path (mailbox, Teams button or ticket), password and MFA rules, and what not to put in private WhatsApp for work. Without this block, finance and reception often end up with different report addresses.

Staff who only raise purchase orders without outbound payments may leave after the core if four-eyes approval is written down. Anyone who approves or pays invoices stays for the finance module.

## Finance: stop payments before money leaves

CEO fraud and business email compromise with a changed IBAN are among the cases [BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/business-email-compromise-de) highlights for finance: call back on a number you already trust, never the number in the suspicious mail.

In the module you practise: read the mail, apply the callback rule, report internally, no payment before release. A four-eyes sketch on a flip chart is enough when it matches the ERP rule.

## HR: applications, payroll and candidate data

HR mailboxes see fake LinkedIn approaches, «new payroll software» and malware in attachments. No payslips to unchecked addresses, no passwords by mail.

When applicant and employee data are central, [data protection for recruitment agencies](/security/blog/data-protection-for-recruitment-agencies) extends the HR block without replacing a full privacy training day.

## Reception: physical and on the phone

Tailgating and fake couriers are not «IT only». USB sticks from the lobby do not go into office ports. «Support» calls follow your written rule for callback or transfer, not gut feeling.

## Timing in one session

<ol class="blog-flow">
  <li><strong>Core</strong><p>Phishing, report path, MFA, sixty to ninety minutes for everyone.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Roles</strong><p>Examples and questions per role, about twenty minutes, moderation switches visibly.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Close</strong><p>One report address, one IBAN rule, attendance sheet with role groups.</p></li>
</ol>

Remote works when each function speaks. For quiet participants, name one speaker per role for the chat window.

If finance dominates airtime, reception hears too little. The facilitator names the active role aloud and asks one question from a different function after each block.

## Brief modules for other functions

Not every firm has three classic roles. Warehouse and logistics staff who see supplier mail need five minutes on fake delivery notes and payment changes, without a second course. IT technicians stay for the core, with one sentence on remote support and password handover.

## A made-up example: one room, three tables

A made-up example: 40-person services firm. The trainer keeps everyone for reporting and MFA, then three flip charts with one anonymised near-miss each: IBAN in finance, applicant file in HR, support call at reception. Attendance sheet with columns for finance, HR, reception and other staff.

## Simulations and e-learning

[Phishing simulations allowed in Switzerland](/security/blog/phishing-simulations-allowed-switzerland) covers advance notice and labour-law limits. A simulation straight after the live session without notice undermines trust.

If you onboard with video, combine the live session with [e-learning vs live security awareness](/security/blog/e-learning-vs-live-security-awareness). E-learning does not replace role discussion in the room.

Tell staff before any simulation. Do not use results for hidden performance scoring. Art. 26 ArGV 3 and Art. 328b OR frame monitoring; this is orientation, not legal advice.

## After the session

A follow-up conversation a few weeks later: did reception report vishing? Does the IBAN rule still match the ERP? One page per role with three bullets at finance and reception beats an intranet post nobody opens.

Four weeks later you can count reports from finance and reception separately. Rising reports or stable rules in conversation suggest the role split worked.

## Mistakes to avoid

Three identical slides with the word «finance», «HR» or «reception» swapped, but no shared core. Two different report addresses in the same hour. A simulation on the same day without notice.

Penetration test training, a full privacy day and payment awareness are different formats. This article bundles finance, HR and reception risks in one live session.

## Prep with the provider

Send anonymised patterns from finance, HR and reception mailboxes before the session, no customer names. IT confirms which report button is visible in Outlook or Teams. The trainer picks one scenario per role.

In mixed teams, role examples can sit on flip charts in German and English. What matters is that finance hears the IBAN rule in the language it uses when calling suppliers.

## Records and annual planning

Note role groups on the attendance sheet, not only «all staff». One PDF with three bullets per role at finance and reception, not only on the intranet. One folder per year: invitation, list, topics, follow-up notes.

Slot the date in your [annual awareness training plan](/security/blog/annual-awareness-training-plan) so finance, HR and reception can attend together.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do we need three separate dates?</h3>
  <p>No, if core and role modules fit one session. Two dates only for very large headcount or when payment modules must be separate.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Can leadership only watch?</h3>
  <p>At least one leader should ask a payment question or report an example. Otherwise security looks like «for everyone else».</p>
</div>
