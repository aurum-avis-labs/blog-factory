## AI for architecture and engineering offices

AI for a small architecture or engineering office pays off on the office work around the project, not on the drawing. Hours land on the wrong phase, or not at all, until the fee invoice is late. Minutes of a meeting become a task list nobody entered. Plans go out, and six months later nobody can say which revision the client has. Tender papers are copied from the last job and cleaned by hand. Those are the jobs. Producing a plan, a model or a rendering is a different trade, and it is not this one.

This is for owners and project leads of Swiss offices with a handful of people, working to a contract that names phases and a fee. The CAD system stays. The question is what still happens in mail, in a spreadsheet, and on paper beside it.

## What the contract already defines

[SIA 102 (2020)](https://shop.sia.ch/normenwerk/architekt/102_2020_d/D/Product) is the SIA order for the services and fees of architects, valid from 1 January 2020. [SIA 103 (2020)](https://shop.sia.ch/normenwerk/ingenieur/103_2020_d/D/Product) is the order for civil engineers, valid from the same date. Your contract says whether you are on those orders, on a lump sum, or on time spent. A workflow does not choose the fee method. It uses the method you already signed: hours against a phase, a share of a phase, or a monthly bill of what was agreed.

If the hours are not on the project, the order cannot help you. The gap is the record, not a missing paragraph in the standard.

## Five jobs around the project file

Five jobs repeat on almost every mandate. Each has a clear end: an invoice, a protocol filed, a transmittal, a tender set. None of them is a sketch.

### Hours before the fee invoice

People book time on Friday, or they do not. Some hours sit on "internal" because the phase code was wrong. The fee invoice, whether it is a monthly time bill or the close of a phase, waits for that list. A workflow can compare who was on the project this week with who booked time, flag a phase that has no hours, and prepare the lines for the invoice. It should not move hours from one project to another to make a budget look calm. The steps from booked hours to the fee invoice are their own piece of work. This article only marks the gap.

### The letter that goes with the invoice

The figures may come from the time system. The letter, the phase name as the client uses it, and the attachments the contract asks for are still assembled by hand. That is a template with fields, which is the subject of [creating documents from templates](/prozess-check/blog/generate-documents-from-templates). For this office the fields are project, phase, period, amount, and what the contract says must be enclosed. A person signs. The workflow does not send the invoice on its own.

### Minutes and the task list

A site or a design meeting produces decisions and tasks: who changes which plan, by when, for whom. The recording, if you have one, is not the file. Someone still types the decisions into the project list. A draft from the notes, in your headings, with tasks named to people who are actually on the project, is useful. A summary that invents a decision the room did not take is not. The person who was in the room checks the draft before it is filed and sent.

### Who has which plan

Issuing plans is a list: document, revision, date, recipient, and whether it was a replacement. Six months later a contractor prices an old revision, and the office searches mail for the transmittal. If your drawing tool already logs the issue and the recipients, use that log. If the issue still leaves as a zip file in an email, a workflow can file the message against the project, read the revision from the filename only when the name follows a rule you set, and list who received it. It should not guess a revision from a file called "final".

### Tender papers

A submission set is last year's folder, plus the new site, plus the pieces that changed. The copying is dull. The error is a paragraph from the wrong building. A workflow can start from your template and drop in the project fields. The quantities, the exclusions and the price stay with the person who will sign the tender. Do not ask a model to invent a specification line that is not in your template or your model.

## What stays with the project lead

The design, the advice to the client, the fee you will stand behind, and any change of scope: those stay with a person. A workflow may assemble and remind. It should not promise a delivery date the project schedule does not show, and it should not close a phase because the hours happen to match a budget.

[Which process to automate first](/prozess-check/blog/which-process-to-automate-first) applies here as elsewhere: it repeats, it hurts, it ends in a document someone else relies on, and the data is already in the time system, the mail or the drawing log. An office with two projects a year, billed as one lump sum with no time record, will not repay a route for hours. An office that wants a picture of a facade is in the wrong article.

## Where to start

Count one month: hours chased before an invoice, letters built from the last invoice, protocols typed, transmittals you could not find, tender folders copied. If one of those dominates, that is the case. If the drawing tool already covers it, leave it there.
