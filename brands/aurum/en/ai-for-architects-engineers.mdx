## AI for architecture and engineering offices

<p class="blog-lede">AI pays off in a small architecture or engineering office on the office work around the project: hours, invoices, minutes, plan issue and tender papers. The drawing itself stays in the CAD tool.</p>

Hours land on the wrong phase, or not at all, until the fee invoice is late. Minutes become a task list nobody entered. Plans go out, and six months later nobody knows which revision the client holds. Tender sets are copied from the last job and cleaned by hand. Producing a plan, a model or a rendering is a different trade.

This is for owners and project leads of Swiss offices with a contract that names phases and a fee. The CAD system stays. The question is what still happens in mail, in a spreadsheet and on paper.

Many offices already use transcription or summaries in one-off cases. The bottleneck is rarely the model. The bottleneck is the rule: which data may go where, who checks the draft, and where the result lands in the project folder.

## What the contract already defines

[SIA 102 (2020)](https://shop.sia.ch/normenwerk/architekt/102_2020_d/D/Product) is the SIA order for architects' services and fees, valid from 1 January 2020. [SIA 103 (2020)](https://shop.sia.ch/normenwerk/ingenieur/103_2020_d/D/Product) is the order for civil engineers from the same date. Your contract says whether you work on those orders, on a lump sum or on time spent. A workflow does not choose the fee method. It uses the method you already signed.

If the hours are not on the project, the order cannot help you. The gap is the record.

## Five jobs around the project file

Five jobs repeat on almost every mandate. Each ends in a document: an invoice, filed minutes, an issue log, a tender set.

### Hours before the fee invoice

People book time on Friday, or they do not. Some hours sit on "internal" because the phase code was wrong. The fee invoice waits for that list. A workflow can flag who was on the project this week with no time booked and prepare lines for the invoice. It should not move hours between projects to calm a budget.

Travel time and meetings at the client's site need the same codes as office work. If the contract treats travel separately, that belongs in the invoice template, not in a hidden spreadsheet column.

### The letter that goes with the invoice

Figures may come from the time system. The letter, the phase name as the client uses it, and the attachments the contract asks for are still assembled by hand. That is a template with fields, in [creating documents from templates](/prozess-check/blog/generate-documents-from-templates). Fields here: project, phase, period, amount, enclosure list. A person signs.

### Minutes and the task list

A meeting produces decisions and tasks: who changes which plan, by when. The recording is not the file. Someone still types decisions into the project list. A draft from notes in your headings is useful when the person who was in the room checks it before filing. A summary that invents a decision is not.

Number tasks the way your office later finds them in time recording. "Update sheet B-02" in the minutes and "internal" on the time sheet are two worlds. If you record meetings, check your privacy notice and the client contract on whether recording and an AI draft are allowed. Personal data from the room does not belong in a free account without approval.

### Who has which plan

Issuing plans is a list: document, revision, date, recipient, replacement yes or no. If your drawing tool already logs issue and recipients, use that log. If issue still leaves as a zip in email, a workflow can file the message to the project and read the revision from the filename when the name follows a rule you set. It should not guess a revision from a file called "final".

### Tender papers

A submission set is last year's folder plus the new site plus the pieces that changed. The error is a paragraph from the wrong building. A workflow can drop project fields into your template. Quantities, exclusions and price stay with the person who will sign the tender.

Checklists from earlier projects help when they still match the current standard and contract. A stale fire-protection line from the last dossier costs more than the copying.

### Authority mail and deadlines

Permits, follow-up requests and municipal deadlines land in mail and on desks. Missed dates often surface only when the client asks. A workflow can log receipt date, authority and deadline and remind you before expiry. It does not replace a technical review of the file.

## Recording extras before you invoice

The client emails "could you also …". Someone books hours before the phase or scope is confirmed in writing. Months later the office argues over the fee or forgets the work on the invoice.

A useful workflow files each such email to the project, suggests a phase or extra-services line, and reminds you to obtain written confirmation before the hours pile up. It does not set a price and it does not open a new phase without the project lead.

## What stays with the project lead

Design, advice to the client, the fee you will stand behind, and any change of scope stay with a person. A workflow may assemble and remind. Delivery dates the schedule does not show, or closing a phase because hours happen to match a budget: those stay human decisions.

[Which process to automate first](/prozess-check/blog/which-process-to-automate-first) applies here: it repeats, it hurts, it ends in a document someone else relies on, and the data already sit in the time system, mail or the drawing log. An office with two projects a year on a lump sum with no time record will not repay a hours route. An office that wants a facade picture is in the wrong article.

## Where to start

Count one month: hours chased before an invoice, minutes typed, transmittals you could not find, tender folders copied, extra-work emails with no written sign-off, missed authority deadlines. If one of those dominates, that is the case. If the drawing tool already covers it, leave it there.

Offices with external specialists receive their minutes and plans as well. Then a clear folder per project pays off before any AI step mixes the wrong files.
