Almost every office that "does not have an AI strategy yet" already has the symptom: someone has a private ChatGPT account. Customer copy lands in the prompt because the draft is faster that way. There is no allow-list. Leadership thinks the topic is still outside the door. It is already at the desk.

The [AXA SME labour-market study](https://www.deepcloud.swiss/newsroom/artikel/ki-in-schweizer-kmu-wir-sind-bereit/), cited by DeepCloud, points the same way: conscious AI use in SMEs from 22 percent (2024) to 34 percent (2025). Translation and correspondence are the most common jobs. Internally the tool is often still not approved. That is not our survey. It explains why shadow AI appears: the benefit is there, the approval is not.

## What happens if you only ban it

A ban without a replacement is an invitation to use a phone. The prompt leaves the company laptop for a private account. Then you have no contract, no log, and no way to steer the data class.

The same is true of "we will just buy Copilot for everyone" with no rule for which file may go where. A licence is not a guideline.

What Swiss data protection means for customer data in ChatGPT is in [ChatGPT and customer data](/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg). This piece is the organisational hole in front of that: nobody said which tool counts.

## The minimum, without policy theatre

Three things, on one page:

1. Allowed tools. A company chat, Copilot inside the existing Microsoft 365, nothing else for personal data.
2. Data classes. Public, internal with no people, personal data. Red stays out, or only in the contracted channel with a check.
3. Human review. Anything that goes to a customer is read by a person. The model may draft. It may not send.

Not a 40-page document. One page the managing office signs, and 30 minutes with the team. If you want that in a guided day: the [AI in Business workshop](/services/ai-in-business).
