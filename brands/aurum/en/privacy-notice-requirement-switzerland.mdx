## Privacy notice requirement Switzerland: when you must inform people

Privacy notice requirement Switzerland means that anyone who collects personal data must inform the data subject in an understandable form before or at the latest when collection happens ([Art. 19 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). That covers customers, applicants, and employees, not only marketing lists. A website privacy page does not replace workplace information in the handbook when purposes differ. This text is for owners, HR, and office staff in firms of about 20 to 250 people without a full-time data protection officer. Orientation, not legal advice. On erasure: [right to erasure under the FADP](/security/blog/right-to-erasure-under-the-fadp).

## What the notice must contain

At minimum you need: identity and contact of the controller, processing purposes, categories of personal data, recipients or categories of recipients, any transfer abroad, retention period or criteria, and the person’s rights (access, rectification, erasure, objection, complaint to the FDPIC). A footer link titled "Privacy" that omits a block is still incomplete.

## Where to publish it

Typical places: footer link to a full page, checkout, contact form, job ad, application confirmation, onboarding pack. Short text at the form plus a link to the long page is common. Tiny print alone rarely works if someone cannot find their rights.

If you [send personal data by email](/security/blog/sending-personal-data-by-email), the notice should say why and how long you keep it. Applications need their own purposes and retention; see [how long to keep job applications](/security/blog/how-long-to-keep-job-applications-switzerland). Shared files with customer data belong in recipient categories or processor references; see [sharing files that contain personal data](/security/blog/sharing-files-that-contain-personal-data).

## Why a downloaded template is not enough

Many German templates mix GDPR deadlines, processor contracts, and cookie rules that sit differently in Switzerland. A sold "template" is not a legal product that matches your purposes automatically. You still map purposes, systems, and recipients yourself. Borderline cases (profiling, special categories, new US tools) need counsel, not copy-paste.

## Separate customers and employees

Customer interests: contract, support, invoicing. Employees: pay, working time, appraisal, IT monitoring. One website page for both confuses people and is often wrong. HR text in the handbook, customer text in the footer. Candidates need recruiting purposes and rejection retention.

## Updates and evidence

Put a date or version on the page. When you add a CRM, newsletter tool, or AI scoring, update text before go-live. A PDF snapshot helps many SMEs when revision or a customer audit asks. A processing register is exempt for many firms under 250 staff; an internal system list still helps you write the notice.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a 45-person services firm in central Switzerland runs a website, HubSpot, and an application form. Leadership finds the 2019 handbook silent on IT purposes and the careers page still cites "GDPR 2018". In one week HR drafts employee information, marketing updates the customer page (purposes, Swiss hosting, recipient categories, rights, FDPIC contact), and recruiting gets one sentence on keeping rejected applications. No new software, only text and a calendar reminder.

## Imprint, cookies, and the privacy notice

An imprint names the legal entity and contact for official mail. The privacy notice explains processing of personal data. Cookie banners often cover technology and consent; they do not replace information for contract or application data. A German cookie banner alone often leaves CRM and HR still undocumented.

## Special categories and cameras

If you process health, criminal, or religion data, or add video surveillance, you usually need more than boilerplate. Early counsel helps. Even without cameras: if you photograph visitors or keep guest books, say so in the notice or at the entrance.

## Who owns the task in practice

Owners or leadership decide content. Marketing or IT publishes on the website. HR maintains employee text. External advisers can review but cannot replace your knowledge of systems. Without an owner, the footer link ages while the CRM already runs a new US tool.

## Do you need a lawyer?

For typical SMEs with customer and HR data, internal work plus a one-off review is often enough. Counsel fits disputes, investigations, high-risk new processing, or when a corporate customer demands a specific clause. The notice does not replace processor contracts.

## Processors in the notice

Name categories of recipients (hosting, newsletter tool, fiduciary, payroll SaaS) without publishing every contract. Link to contact for detail questions. When you add a new US vendor, update text and date before marketing goes live.

## Who can start with a short notice

Very small sites with only a contact form and few fields may start with transparent short text plus mail contact, as long as all Art. 19 points are reachable. Once you add a shop, applications, or CRM, a dedicated page pays off. EU customers may trigger extra GDPR duties separately.
