<p class="blog-lede">PoC vs prototype vs MVP is not three names for the same weekend in Cursor. A proof of concept asks whether the idea can work technically. A prototype asks whether people understand the flow and tolerate the friction. An MVP asks whether strangers behave in a way that survives a second visit. If your Lovable export already has login and a dashboard, you may still be at prototype stage if every record is fake and nobody outside your chat group has used it.</p>

This is for founders who already shipped something with Lovable, Bolt, v0, Replit, Cursor, or Claude Code and now hear "nice MVP" from a friend, an investor, or their own pitch deck. The labels change what you measure next and what you should not claim. For the wider definition of an MVP as a learning artefact, see [what is an MVP](/blog/what-is-an-mvp). This page is only the ladder: PoC, prototype, MVP, and where each stops.

## PoC vs prototype vs MVP: what each stage is for

The same repository can sit in different stages depending on what you did with it last week. Stage is defined by the **question you are trying to answer**, not by how many screens Figma or the AI generated.

At PoC stage the question is narrow and technical: can data move from A to B, or does the model return usable output? The audience is often you and a technical sparring partner, and success is a yes or no on feasibility, sometimes on a throwaway branch you never merge. At prototype stage you ask whether people get the job and where they stall. The audience is interviews, a design partner, or a friendly pilot. Success is revised flows and a list of confusions; payments, compliance, and scale can stay out on purpose. At MVP stage you ask whether anyone outside your network repeats the core action. The audience is strangers or a segment you reach with honest acquisition. Success is measurable behaviour: return visits, completion of one loop, or a cost per lead you can compare week to week.

A vibe-coded app can jump straight to prototype because the tool removed the old cost of screens. That does not skip the questions. You can have a beautiful prototype that never answered whether the API rate limit kills the use case (that was PoC work you postponed). You can have a PoC that looks like a product because the template shipped auth on day one.

## What each stage proves, and what it does not

A PoC can show that the risky path works on your stack and that latency or cost per call sit in a workable range. It does not show demand, clear UX for non-builders, or safe operations at load. A prototype can show that the problem framing lands in conversation and that someone finishes a guided task; it does not show habit, retention, or unit economics. An MVP can show that strangers complete the core path without you present and that drop-off is visible in analytics; it does not show product-market fit, a durable acquisition channel, or margin if billing stayed out of scope.

## Who should stop where

If the open risk is whether the tech works, stay in PoC until one path is demonstrably feasible. Paid ads on a PoC rarely make sense unless the ad only promises a research call. If the risk is whether anyone understands the job, a clickable prototype is enough for structured interviews; see [when a clickable prototype is enough](/blog/clickable-prototype-when-enough) before you commit to a full build. If the risk is whether behaviour repeats, you need an MVP-shaped build: deployed, instrumented, narrow scope, real users. Production quality matters for measurement, not for impressing investors with feature count.

Founders who already vibe-coded should be honest about which risk is still open. Many teams are at prototype with MVP branding because auth and a database were free from the template.

## Naming the next step without rebuilding twice

Before you add another feature, write one sentence: "The riskiest open question is …" If the answer is technical, time-box a PoC branch. If it is UX, run five interviews on the current build without shipping new modules. If it is behaviour, cut scope to one loop, deploy, and turn on analytics before you polish secondary screens.

[Prioritize MVP features](/blog/prioritize-mvp-features) helps once you accept you are in MVP stage. Until then, prioritising features can be procrastination in a nicer font.

## When the product validation package fits

The [product validation package](/services/mvp-validation) is for teams that have passed "does the screen make sense?" and need a Go, Pivot, or Stop recommendation from real usage: deployed web app, measurement, paid acquisition where it fits the hypothesis, and up to three pivot iterations on the core loop. It is not a PoC workshop and not a Figma-only prototype engagement.
