## Is a data protection adviser required in Switzerland?

Is a data protection adviser required in Switzerland for a typical SME? No: unlike Germany, Swiss law does not impose a general mandatory data protection officer on every company of a certain size. The [FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en) expects the controller to ensure compliance and to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures ([Art. 8 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en)). Someone must answer for privacy, but that can be an owner, an HR lead or an external consultant without the title "DSB".

This note is for management in firms with 20 to 250 staff comparing Swiss rules with German expectations. It is orientation, not legal advice. Criminal fines and personal liability belong in [FADP fines and personal liability](/security/blog/fadp-fines-and-personal-liability), not here.

## When a typical SME does not have to appoint a "DSB"

A trades firm, a fiduciary or a wholesaler serving mainly Swiss clients, without a large health or monitoring project, usually has no duty to appoint a data protection officer in the German sense. Headcount alone does not trigger a DPO in Switzerland. An ISO audit does not replace that rule either.

If you stay in this band, you still name internally who receives breach reports, who maintains the privacy notice and who checks processor agreements before leadership signs. That is Art. 8 organisation, not a certificate job.

## When you should still name someone clearly

Customers, suppliers and auditors expect a reachable contact in the privacy notice. Without one, requests land in a shared inbox and delays look like neglect.

Typical triggers for more than "the MD does it on the side":

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>First processing of sensitive personal data at meaningful scale</li>
  <li>EU framework contract or group policy with a GDPR annex</li>
  <li>Several processors in third countries and messy shared drives</li>
  <li>Repeated access requests or a documented breach</li>
</ul>

Sensitive data raises the risk picture. What the statute means is in [sensitive personal data under the FADP](/security/blog/sensitive-personal-data-under-the-fadp).

## What a "data protection adviser" is not

<div class="blog-pair">
  <div>
    <h3>It is</h3>
    <p>A sparring partner for measures, contracts, records and training needs.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>It is not</h3>
    <p>A substitute for leadership, not a CISO, not a four-eyes payment process, and not a shield against fines.</p>
  </div>
</div>

External advice does not transfer the controller's duties. It delivers drafts and priorities; your team implements and trains staff. A DPO title in an email signature does not satisfy Art. 8 on its own.

Payment approvals and fraud control are separate. See [four-eyes principle for payments](/security/blog/four-eyes-principle-for-payments).

## External advisers and internal champions

Many SMEs book an accountant, a lawyer or a privacy boutique for a few days per year. That is enough if recommendations are acted on and staff know whom to call after a wrong send.

An internal champion (often HR or the office lead) plus two external coaching days can work for 40 staff. Document the role on the org chart and note holiday cover. Auditors want an accountable person, not a "DSB CH" certificate.

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>Slow growth, few special categories</h3><p>Leadership plus an annual external half-day often suffices; focus on agreements and the privacy notice.</p></li>
  <li><h3>First health or HR analytics project</h3><p>External hours before go-live, internal owner for day-to-day requests.</p></li>
  <li><h3>EU sales with contract pressure</h3><p>Advice on scope and a named EU contact, separate from Swiss day-to-day work.</p></li>
</ul>

## GDPR-driven appointments

If [the GDPR applies](/security/blog/does-the-gdpr-apply-to-swiss-companies) to parts of your business, EU customers may contractually require a European representative or DPO functions. That is contract and GDPR pressure, not automatic Swiss statute.

Some firms therefore appoint someone with DPO experience even when Swiss law does not require a DPO for everyone. Read contracts and group policies before you advertise titles externally.

## Public buyers and sector law

Cantonal or municipal buyers sometimes require named contacts in tenders. That is contract law. Separately, check sector and cantonal rules (health, finance). This text does not replace sector-specific advice.

There is no general duty like Germany's mandatory data protection officer for an ordinary SME. Still name someone who receives breach reports, and book external hours when contracts or data types clearly raise the risk.
