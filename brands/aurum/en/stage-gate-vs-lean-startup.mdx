## Stage-gate vs lean startup

**Stage-gate vs lean startup** is not a winner-takes-all choice. Stage-gate is a phased innovation process with explicit Go, Kill, or Hold decisions between stages, built for portfolios and capital allocation. The lean startup is a learning loop: ship the smallest test that can falsify an assumption, measure behaviour, then pivot or persevere. If you already shipped something in Lovable, Bolt, Cursor, or Claude Code, you used lean tactics whether you named them or not. The open question is whether your next audience, a board, a sponsor, or a corporate innovation committee, expects gate language, learning metrics, or both.

This is for founders and product leads in Switzerland who must explain progress without pretending a prototype is a business case. Validating inside a company has its own politics: [validate a product idea inside a company](/blog/validate-a-product-idea-inside-a-company). Winning budget is a separate conversation. Choosing who builds the next version is in [getting an MVP built for you](/blog/getting-an-mvp-built-for-you).

## What stage-gate optimises for

Stage-gate, in Robert Cooper’s formulation, spreads risk across discovery, scoping, business case, development, testing, and launch. Each gate is meant to stop weak projects before they absorb a full budget. That suits organisations with many ideas, formal governance, and auditors who ask where the money went. The process does not promise truth about customers on its own. A polished gate deck can pass while the underlying assumption, that a segment will pay or that a channel works, stays untested.

<ul class="blog-facts">
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Strength</span><strong>Portfolio control</strong><p>Leadership can compare projects on the same rubric.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Strength</span><strong>Audit trail</strong><p>Decisions and criteria are visible after the fact.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Risk</span><strong>Slow feedback</strong><p>Heavy business cases before behaviour can protect careers, not customers.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Risk</span><strong>False certainty</strong><p>Forecasts and spreadsheets are not experiments.</p></li>
</ul>

## What lean startup optimises for

The lean startup treats the early product as an instrument for learning under uncertainty. Eric Ries’s build-measure-learn loop is the familiar shape: define the riskiest assumption, build the minimum that can test it in the field, measure cohort behaviour, then decide. That matches a weekend prototype, a concierge workflow, or a deployed web app with analytics, which is how many vibe-coded builds start. It does not remove the need for discipline. Without a written assumption and a falsifier, you only collect anecdotes.

<ol class="blog-flow">
  <li><strong>Assumption</strong><p>One sentence you could be wrong about.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Minimum test</strong><p>Deployed artifact or manual stand-in, not a deck.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Measurement</strong><p>Events and cohorts tied to the test window.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Decision</strong><p>Pivot, persevere, or stop with what the data did not prove.</p></li>
</ol>

## Stage-gate vs lean startup in one table

| Question | Stage-gate emphasis | Lean startup emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary audience | Sponsors allocating capital across projects | Team learning what customers do |
| Success at gate zero | Clear problem and fit with strategy | Evidence that someone has the job to be done |
| Mid-process proof | Business case and plan | Behaviour in a bounded experiment |
| Failure mode | Zombie projects that pass gates on narrative | Endless pivots without a falsifier |
| Fits when | Many ideas, compliance, shared templates | Unknown market, first real build |

Neither row replaces judgment. Ideas as such are not protectable in Switzerland; execution and brand are ([IGE: can ideas be protected?](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)).

## A hybrid most Swiss innovation leads actually use

Corporate innovation often keeps stage-gate headings for the board pack and runs lean experiments inside each stage. Discovery might be twenty interviews and a smoke test, not a fifty-page brief. The business case stage might require a live cohort and cost per lead, not only a five-year model. If you arrive with a vibe-coded app, treat it as evidence for an early gate, not as proof of scale. Session recordings and funnel drop-off belong in the same slide as the gate checklist.

  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Renaming your process «lean stage-gate» does not create learning. A gate without falsifiable criteria is theatre. A pivot without measurement is opinion.</p>
</aside>

## What neither process proves

Passing a gate does not show product-market fit, defensible unit economics, or that you can hire a sales team. Completing three build-measure-learn cycles does not satisfy procurement or grant reporting on its own. Stage-gate and lean startup are governance and learning frames, not substitutes for statutory accounts or legal review when personal data, payments, or regulated industries are in scope.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is an MVP a stage-gate deliverable?</h3>
  <p>Often yes at an early gate, if the MVP is defined as a test of one assumption with metrics, not as a feature-complete product. A repackaged website is a weak deliverable for either framework.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Does vibe coding replace stage-gate?</h3>
  <p>No. Fast tools shorten the build part of build-measure-learn. They do not answer whether your sponsor will fund the next stage or whether the experiment was designed honestly.</p>
</div>
