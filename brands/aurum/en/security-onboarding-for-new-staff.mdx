## Security onboarding checklist: before the first client contact

Security onboarding for new staff in a Swiss SME covers accounts, reporting path, password rules, and payment verification before they touch client mail or invoices. Offboarding is a different checklist. Use this list in HR and IT handoffs for firms with about 20 to 250 employees. The full topic list for the annual session is in [security awareness training topics](/security/blog/security-awareness-training-topics).

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Accounts only after signed contract; no shared passwords</li>
  <li>Show internal phishing report address or button; have them use it once</li>
  <li>MFA enrollment on day one if policy exists</li>
  <li>Payment verification rule if the role touches invoices or approvals</li>
  <li>Clean desk and visitor badge rules if reception or site visits apply</li>
  <li>Date for next firm-wide awareness session visible in the calendar</li>
</ul>

## Who does what on day one

IT creates accounts, sets MFA, and shows where to reset passwords. HR hands over the reporting path in writing, not only in a corridor chat. The line manager explains role risks: no payment without release, no client data on a private phone without a documented exception. Do not stop at «we will train later» when the person reads client mail on Tuesday.

  <li data-step="1"><h3>Morning day one</h3><p>Contract, account, MFA, show the report button in Outlook or Teams.</p></li>

</ol>

## A made-up example: first week in finance

A made-up example: new finance clerk, week one. HR walks the reporting address, IT sets MFA, the team lead shows the four-eyes table and phone verify for IBAN changes. No half-day workshop on Monday; instead a calendar invite for the company live session in six weeks. Payments above a set threshold wait for a second person even when «only this week» pressure appears.

## Does onboarding replace annual training?

No. Onboarding is the minimum at entry; annual live or refresh still covers new fraud patterns. Run phishing simulations only after staff are informed; see [phishing simulations allowed in Switzerland](/security/blog/phishing-simulations-allowed-switzerland). ## Part-time, interns, and remote starts

Part-time staff on afternoon shifts need the same checklist in a shorter slot. Interns with mail accounts count as staff, not guests without rules. Remote hires get the list on a video call with screen share to the report button; then a one-page summary in writing. Anyone without mail on day one still gets visitor and password rules on site.

Temporary workers with mail: shortened but complete reporting and password rules. Access ends on the last day; plan offboarding separately.

## Questions new hires actually ask

May I forward suspicious mail to my private address to show someone? No, use the official path. May I WhatsApp a client file to a colleague? Only if policy allows; onboarding repeats the written rule. Skip MFA on my phone? Only with a documented exception from leadership, and that should be rare.

## Gaps to watch and how to control them

Shared passwords for shop or shared inboxes stay unchanged. The report address lives only in an IT wiki. Finance pays «temporarily» without four-eyes. Gaps in month one become permanent. Once per quarter, sample whether HR actually triggers the «day 1 security» task in your system.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Have them repeat the reporting path, not only listen</li>
  <li>Show where MFA is configured</li>
  <li>Clarify group chat rules for client data</li>
  <li>Make the firm-wide training date visible in the calendar</li>
</ul>

## HR system template and records

Store the checklist as a «day 1 security» task due on the start date. Repeat it on internal moves into finance or reception, not only external hires. File the onboarding note in personnel records with the same retention as other HR papers, not scattered in chats.

Give line managers half a page: reporting path, who calls IT, that new hires in probation must not release client payments. That reduces pressure on people still learning the role.

## IT partner and internal moves

External IT often provisions accounts. Contractually require documentation of reporting path and MFA, not only a password handover. HR owns the checklist.

Move from production to finance: run the full payment checklist again. Move into HR: repeat hiring and payroll phishing themes. When managers ignore rules, new hires notice immediately.

## Day one vs week one in detail

Day one closes reporting path and MFA before finance or HR file access. That is sequence, not an extra form. Part-time staff on their first visit day still tick the checklist; content can move to the first full day with mail, not «sometime later».

Devices and BYOD: when private phones receive mail, mention password and lost device in the checklist without copying a full BYOD article. Point to the internal mobile policy. Language: English hires may need a bilingual one-pager in firms that otherwise run in German.

Onboarding does not replace privacy training or the full security topic list. It stops new people working for weeks without rules. Simulation or e-learning duties only after the article on allowed simulations; inform first, do not surprise.

## Security culture from day one

When leadership mentions in the welcome that reports are welcome, the threshold to speak up drops. Same rules for everyone, including managers. Finance and HR file access only after the person has seen the reporting path.

After the firm-wide session, send a one-page reminder with report address and IBAN rule within a week. Store e-learning certificates for new hires in the same folder as live attendance lists so insurers see one trail.

Name one owner for training records and one for the report mailbox. HR schedules dates, IT checks examples match your mail system, finance confirms payment stories. Without names on paper, the programme stops when someone is on holiday.
