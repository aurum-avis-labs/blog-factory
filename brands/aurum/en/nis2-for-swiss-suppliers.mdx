## NIS2 for Swiss suppliers

NIS2 for Swiss suppliers does not apply as Swiss law inside your firm. EU customers and large buyers still pass security and training duties through contracts, supplier questionnaires, and audits. Your SME with about 20 to 250 staff may need proof of awareness, incident reporting, and access control even though you are not a «NIS2 operator» in Switzerland.

This article is for leadership, sales, and whoever fills supplier forms. Orientation, not legal advice. Whether your company is in scope of NIS2 in the EU depends on activity, size, and role in the supply chain; check that with counsel and the directive text, not from this page alone.

## What EU customers typically ask

Questionnaires mix NIS2 language with ISO 27001, GDPR, and house templates. Common topics:

- Proof of security awareness for staff and sometimes for management
- Incident reporting paths and deadlines (often stricter wording than Swiss FADP)
- Access control, MFA, offboarding, backups, and sub-processors
- Use of AI tools and subprocessors

Read each line as a contract question: «What does this customer actually require?» and «What can we evidence with processes we already run?» Not every line means your firm is NIS2-regulated in the EU.

## Check scope before you sign blindly

Some questionnaires imply every supplier must be «NIS2 compliant». Review with counsel: role in the supply chain, whether you provide an «essential» or «important» service, whether the customer classifies you as a critical subcontractor, and whether the contract is Swiss law only. You can meet contractual security requirements without claiming your firm is EU NIS2-regulated.

Flag unclear deadlines («report within 4 hours») and ask whether they apply to your service tier. A contract clarification beats an empty checkbox before the audit.

## Art. 20 NIS2 and training (orientation)

Article 20 of the EU NIS2 directive mentions training for management bodies and regular similar training for staff. Check exact wording on EUR-Lex before quoting it in bids or contract replies. For Swiss suppliers the practical question is usually: «What evidence does this customer accept?»

A live session with attendance, topics, and follow-up often fits better than anonymous e-learning with no date. [Is security awareness training mandatory in Switzerland](/security/blog/is-security-awareness-training-mandatory-switzerland) explains the Swiss baseline; NIS2 here is the questionnaire trigger, not Swiss statute.

## What you can send when asked

Training dates, topics, attendance, and a named sponsor in leadership. Link or screenshot of the internal reporting path. Short description of offboarding and MFA without overclaiming certification. If an incident affected customer data, internal documentation matters, not marketing copy; see [data breach through an AI tool](/security/blog/data-breach-through-an-ai-tool) for AI risk in questionnaires.

## Keep records where audits look

Store PDFs or scans in one folder per year: invitation, attendee list, topic summary, follow-up notes, and copied answers to recurring questionnaires. When leadership changes, the folder survives turnover better than chat threads.

Tie training to real processes: the same IBAN checks finance uses in ERP, the same report mailbox IT monitors. When processes change after training, send a one-page update rather than waiting a year.

## Labour and privacy limits on evidence

Inform staff before phishing simulations. Do not use awareness metrics for hidden individual performance scoring. Art. 26 ArGV 3 and Art. 328b OR frame monitoring; orientation, not legal advice ([FDPIC](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/technische-mittel-zur-uberwachung-am-arbeitsplatz)).

## Who prepares what internally

One owner for training records, one for the report mailbox and incident log. HR schedules dates, IT checks examples match your mail system, finance confirms payment stories in slides. Without names on paper, the programme stops when someone is on holiday.

Within a week after a session, send a one-page reminder with the report address and IBAN rule. Schedule follow-up questions before people forget. Register e-learning completions in the same folder as live attendance.

## When not to promise deadlines

If a questionnaire starts the discussion, plan [security awareness training](/security/security-awareness) before you promise delivery dates you cannot staff. A realistic date with evidence beats an empty PDF template.
