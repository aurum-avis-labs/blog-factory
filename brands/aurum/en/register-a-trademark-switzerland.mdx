<p class="blog-lede">To register a trademark in Switzerland you file a sign with the Swiss Federal Institute of Intellectual Property (IGE), pay the official fee for at least one Nice class, and receive national protection for ten years if the application succeeds. The published filing fee for one class is CHF 450; renewal is CHF 550. Recheck the current figures on the IGE FAQ before you budget.</p>

## Register a trademark in Switzerland

You register when the product name or logo is stable enough to defend in Switzerland and you are willing to pay for a national right, not when you still rename the app every sprint. A trademark protects a distinguishing sign for goods or services in the classes you list. It does not protect the business idea itself ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)).

This is for founders in Switzerland who are past the napkin stage and need a straight answer on cost, sequence, and limits before they spend on ads or store listings. Privacy paperwork for an MVP sits elsewhere: [privacy requirements for a Swiss MVP](/blog/privacy-requirements-swiss-mvp) and [App Store privacy labels](/blog/app-store-privacy-labels). How Aurum scopes software work in Switzerland is in [startup software development in Switzerland](/blog/startup-software-development-switzerland). Nothing here is legal advice on whether your sign is distinctive or whether a competitor will oppose.

## What you are actually buying

A Swiss trademark gives you the exclusive right to use the registered sign for the goods and services in your application, subject to earlier rights and successful registration. It is separate from a company name in the commercial register, a domain, an App Store listing name, and copyright in your code. Those layers can align, but they are not automatic. Filing is worth it when the public name is fixed for the next launch cycle and you will run paid campaigns or print it on packaging. While positioning and the name still change weekly, or only an internal codename exists, you can wait.

## Steps from search to filing

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Freeze the sign you will file</h3><p>Word mark, figurative mark, or combination. Filing a sketch you will redesign next month wastes the fee.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Search the register</h3><p>Use IGE tools and common sense on similar spellings in your sector. A clear hit is a signal to rename or get advice, not a reason to file quietly and hope.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Pick Nice classes honestly</h3><p>List the goods and services you sell or plan to sell. Too narrow leaves gaps; too broad invites objections and extra class fees on the IGE schedule.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>File online and pay the official fee</h3><p>Keep the filing receipt and the exact representation you submitted. Recheck <a href="https://www.ige.ch/de/etwas-schuetzen/marken/faq">IGE trademark FAQ</a> for the fee total before payment.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Plan for examination and publication</h3><p>IGE examines formal requirements and absolute grounds. Third parties may oppose after publication. A granted mark is not instant on the day you click submit.</p></li>
</ol>

## A made-up example: one class before a soft launch

A made-up example: a solo founder, not a client. The B2B tool name has been the same for four months. The founder searches IGE, sees no identical word mark in class 42 for the planned SaaS scope, and files a word mark in that class only. Budget line: CHF 450 plus an hour with an adviser to sanity-check the class wording. The product is still a web MVP with ten design partners; no revenue. The filing does not change the roadmap. It only means that if the launch sticks, the public name has a Swiss registration in train. The founder keeps building and measuring activation while IGE processes the file.

## What Swiss registration does not prove

You can hold a trademark and still fail review under App Store rules, lose a domain dispute abroad, or discover that a similar sign was already in use in commerce. Swiss protection does not replace contracts with contractors on who owns the logo files. Employee-built UI assets belong to the employer in typical Swiss employment law, but external designers keep copyright until assignment; trademark filing does not fix a missing assignment.

If the open question is whether anyone will pay, spend the next month on interviews and a measurable MVP path before you optimise IP spend. If the open question is whether the name is safe enough to print on invoices and ad copy in Switzerland, search and filing belong on the checklist.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is the commercial register name enough?</h3>
  <p>No. A GmbH or AG name identifies the legal entity. It is not a substitute for a trademark on the product brand customers see.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do we need a lawyer?</h3>
  <p>Many founders file simple word marks themselves. Complex figurative marks, crowded classes, or an opposition are where specialised advice pays off.</p>
</div>
