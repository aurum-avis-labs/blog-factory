## AI for recruitment and staffing agencies

<p class="blog-lede">AI for a small recruitment or staffing firm pays off on the office work around the people: filing dossiers, drafting client profiles, shortlists, chasing timesheets. Who gets the assignment stays with a consultant.</p>

Dossiers arrive by email. A client wants a short profile the same afternoon. Hours from several client sites come back on paper or in a spreadsheet, and the same numbers have to feed payroll and the client invoice. Those are the jobs worth looking at. A licence does not move with the software either: placement and hiring-out of staff remain regulated in Switzerland.

This is for owners and office leads of private employment agencies and temporary-staffing firms. It is not a guide for a company hiring its own staff. Sorting your own applications is a different job, with different limits. A separate post in the series covers that topic a little later.

## Placement and hiring-out are two licences

A chat tool does not replace the licence. Under [Article 2 of the Employment Services Act (AVG)](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/392_392_392/de), anyone who regularly and for a fee brings jobseekers and employers together in Switzerland needs a cantonal operating licence. Placement to or from abroad also needs a licence from SECO. [Article 12](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/392_392_392/de) covers the other activity: employers who commercially lend workers to client companies. Hiring-out from abroad into Switzerland is not permitted. [SECO](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/private-arbeitsvermittlung-und-personalverleih) states that the joint register currently lists more than 7,400 licensed businesses. The Act is there to protect jobseekers and hired-out workers.

Rewriting profiles does not retire the licence. If a new service would start placing or lending people, the permit comes first. This is a practical reading, not legal advice.

## What your software already does

Start with the programme you already pay for. If it already holds the candidate, the client order, the assignment and the invoice, and approved hours become an invoice without retyping, leave that stretch alone.

The gap sits beside it. CVs still arrive as attachments or as files from a job board that nobody has opened. The client profile is a document copied from last time. The consultant knows the pool by memory. For firms that lend staff, Friday's timesheets from several sites are a second book. A second matching product on a database you already trust adds a fee and another place for a wrong name to hide.

## Four jobs around the dossier

Four jobs repeat, follow rules, and start from material you already have. The judgement in the middle stays human.

### Incoming dossiers

A candidate writes in, a consultant forwards a CV, a client sends someone they met. Someone opens the message, finds the name, the trade, the availability and the notice period, and files it on the right record. A workflow can pre-fill those fields and park anything unclear. It should not invent a qualification that is not in the file.

### A profile the client can read

Clients often want a short profile before a name: trade, years, languages, availability, and the assignments that matter. Consultants rebuild that page by hand for every client. A workflow can assemble it from fields you already keep, leave out what the client does not need yet, and stop at a draft. Sending the whole dossier because it was faster is the habit to drop. What you may pass on is a legal limit, covered below.

### A shortlist, not a decision

A client order arrives as an email: a role, a place, a start date, a rate. The useful help is a short list of people already in your pool whose stored skills and availability fit that text, each with the reason taken from the record. The consultant drops names, adds someone the list missed, and speaks to the client. A model that writes to the client on its own, or ranks people on a score you cannot explain, is doing the work you are paid for. Keep the shortlist as a draft.

This is not an employer sorting applicants to its own vacancy. Here a pool you already hold is offered against a client order.

### Timesheets and the client invoice

If you lend staff, the week has a second loop. Hours come back from the worker or the client site, the client signs, and payroll and the client invoice must use the same figures. Deviations go to a person. If the client company is covered by a universally binding collective employment contract, [Article 20 AVG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/392_392_392/de) requires you to follow that contract's wage and working-time rules. The steps from timesheet to pay and invoice are a separate piece of work. A follow-up post in the series covers that loop. Here we only mark it, so it is not confused with a trades daywork sheet.

## What stays with a person

The fit, the rate, the words to the client and the rejection stay with a person. A workflow may prepare. It does not decide, and it does not send.

Public ads have a line you must not lose. [Article 18](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/392_392_392/de) requires a hiring-out firm to give its name and exact address, and to state clearly that the worker is employed for hiring-out. A draft that reads like a direct job with the client is wrong even if the prose is smooth.

Leave it when the volume is small: a few mandates a month, or a role you fill twice a year by phone. [Which process to automate first](/prozess-check/blog/which-process-to-automate-first) asks the same four questions here: it repeats, it hurts, it has a clear end, and the data is already to hand.

## Candidate data has a statutory limit

For placement, [Article 7 paragraph 3 AVG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/392_392_392/de) allows data on jobseekers and vacancies only as far and as long as the placement requires, and it must stay confidential. For hiring-out, [Article 18 paragraph 3](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/392_392_392/de) allows employee data to be passed to the client company only as far and as long as the assignment requires. Anything further needs the person's express consent.

Anonymised statistics for the licensing authority (Article 7 paragraph 2 and Article 18 paragraph 2) are not the profile you send a client. A nameless profile can still be right, because the client needs the trade and the availability, not the full file.

A free chat account is a poor place for CVs, permit details and rates. Why those files do not belong in an open chat tool is set out in [ChatGPT and customer data under the Swiss FADP](/prozess-check/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg). Run the workflow where you already keep the dossiers. Ask your adviser before a new service sees them. This page is not that advice.

## Where to start

Pick the job that eats the most hours in a normal month. Count for two weeks: dossiers typed in by hand, profiles rewritten for clients, timesheets chased, invoices built a second time from the same hours. If the numbers are small, stop there. If one stands out, that is the case.
