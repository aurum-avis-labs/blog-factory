## Screening job applications with AI: what is allowed

<p class="blog-lede">Screening job applications with AI, in a Swiss SME, means the same fields for every dossier, an acknowledgement that a letter arrived, and proposed interview times after a person has chosen whom to invite.</p>

It is not a licence to rank people, to reject them without a human decision, or to analyse a voice or a video for a score. Whether a particular tool crosses that line in your process is a question for your adviser. This page is the office pattern, not a legal opinion.

The reader is an owner or an office lead who hires occasionally, without a personnel department. A staffing agency's matching work is a different business. Which tools the firm may use at all belongs on the [allow list](/prozess-check/blog/approve-ai-tools-allow-list), and the house rules in an [AI policy for an SME](/prozess-check/blog/ai-policy-template-sme). A CV does not go into a free chat account. That limit is in [ChatGPT and customer data under the Swiss FADP](/prozess-check/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg). A CV is personal data of the same kind of sensitivity.

## What the employer may collect

The Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner states that hiring data follow the same rules as a conventional process. The employer collects what is needed to judge whether the person can do that job ([EDÖB](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/datenbearbeitung-durch-den-arbeitgeber)). Questions about origin, union membership, religion or politics are only acceptable when the organisation's purpose makes them necessary. Health questions are tightly limited. Information from a former employer needs the candidate's consent first. The file is for HR or the direct manager, not for a general inbox ([EDÖB, phases of employment](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/verschiedene-phasen-des-arbeitsverhaeltnisses)).

A practical test before any model sees a file: would you ask this in the first conversation? If the answer is no, it does not go into the prompt, the table, or a "nice to have" column. A model will happily extract a photo guess, a gap explained as illness, or a line about family. Delete those fields from the export. Do not store them "in case".

The Act also applies to AI because it is written in a technology-neutral way ([EDÖB](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/update-geltendes-datenschutzgesetz-ist-auf-ki-direkt-anwendbar)). Tell applicants, in the job notice or the acknowledgement, that a tool may sort documents into fields and that a person decides. That is the plain version of being recognisable. Your adviser writes the notice if you process sensitive data or use a scoring product.

## What a person still decides

The interview invitation, the rejection, and the reason you give the applicant stay with a person. A model may propose fields and draft the acknowledgement. It does not choose who is invited, and it does not send a rejection because a number sits under a threshold.

Article 21 of the Federal Act on Data Protection covers a decision based exclusively on automated processing that has a legal consequence for the person or significantly affects them. The controller informs the person. On request, the person may state their view and may demand that a natural person reviews the decision. There are narrow exceptions, including explicit consent and some contractual cases where what the person asked for is granted ([Art. 21 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)).

A table that a manager reads is not, by itself, that decision. A flow that sends rejections because a score is under a threshold can be. Do not build the second one in order to "save the first look".

Bias is not fixed by a sentence in a prompt. The EDÖB's point is proportionality and whether you should be building a profile at all. If the tool's selling line is "personality from the voice", that is the section of the EDÖB page about video and voice analysis. It is the wrong product for a small firm hiring an administrator.

## Acknowledgement, interview, rejection

The office tasks that do repeat are dull and safe to standardise.

The acknowledgement says the application arrived, names the role, and says when the person will hear. A draft from a template is fine. A person sends it from the company address, not from a private chat.

Interview times are scheduling. Offer two or three slots after the shortlist exists. The model may hold the slots. It does not choose the names.

The rejection is short, from a person, and does not quote a score. If you invite some people and not others, you can say the role is filled or that you are moving ahead with other applications. You do not owe a paragraph generated to sound kind. You do owe not to invent a reason the model dreamed up.

## How long the file stays

After the process, the EDÖB says documents of people you did not hire are returned, and copies are destroyed, unless there is a justification. With the person's consent, you may keep them until a date you set in advance, if you expect to need them again. An overriding interest is sometimes arguable if you inform the person ([EDÖB](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/verschiedene-phasen-des-arbeitsverhaeltnisses)).

A separate FAQ says that, if you do not hire, you may keep the application data for up to three months after you tell the person, so you can defend a claim of discriminatory refusal under the Gender Equality Act. A short extra period may be justified if a court serves a claim late ([EDÖB FAQ](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/faq-datenschutz)). Those two statements sit side by side. Do not turn them into a private archive "for a year, to be safe". Ask the adviser which rule you are relying on, write the date on the folder, and delete when it passes. A talent pool is consent plus an end date, not a silent copy of every CV.

## A made-up example: forty applications, one role

A made-up example: a firm of 25 people hiring one office administrator. Forty applications arrive in two weeks. The office creates one row per person: name, contact, languages, earliest start, file link. A model proposes the fields. The lead corrects seven rows where the model took the recruiter's address, and deletes a column for "career gap reason". She reads matching letters and invites six. The rest receive a short rejection she approved. Nobody is scored. Unused dossiers are deleted on the date the adviser set.

## Who should not add a model

Do not add one if you hire one person a year and read ten letters. Reading is faster than cleaning an export.

Do not add one if the only available tool is a consumer chat account, or a product that asks for video interviews you do not need. Put approved tools on the allow list first.

Do not use screening as a way to avoid writing a clear job notice. If the notice is vague, the table will be vague, and a person will still have to read everything.
