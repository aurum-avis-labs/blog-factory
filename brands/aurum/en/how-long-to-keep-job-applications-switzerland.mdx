## How long to keep job applications in Switzerland

How long to keep job applications in Switzerland depends on whether you hired the person, rejected them, or asked to keep the file for a later role. The FADP requires you to delete or anonymise personal data when the purpose is fulfilled, unless a law allows longer storage ([Art. 6 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). There is no single federal rule that says "six months for every CV" in all cases. You set defensible periods in a retention list and follow them. This article is for HR and management in a company of about 20 to 250 employees who store applications in e-mail, folders, or an ATS without a dedicated privacy team.

For how Swiss law compares to the EU GDPR on retention culture, see [Swiss FADP vs GDPR](/security/blog/swiss-fadp-vs-gdpr). E-mail habits are covered under [sending personal data by email](/security/blog/sending-personal-data-by-email). Shared files with personal data need the same care: only the people who need them, and not on a private drive.

## How long after rejection: practice, not a fixed month in statute

For rejected candidates, the processing purpose (assessing this vacancy) ends when you communicate the decision, subject to a short waiting period for feedback or a concrete dispute. A common SME practice is to delete application data within **three to six months** unless the person agreed to a talent pool. That is operational practice under the FADP, not a fixed number of months in federal law. Keeping files longer than you need weakens your position if someone asks for erasure or if the FDPIC questions proportionality.

<aside class="blog-callout">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Note</span>
  <p>"We might need the CV for a lawsuit" is rarely a reason to keep every rejected file for years. If a concrete dispute exists, secure those documents with legal advice; do not use it as a default for all applicants.</p>
</aside>

Unsolicited applications without a matching vacancy follow the same logic: without pool consent, delete once you have clearly declined or the person stops following up.

## After you hire someone

When the candidate becomes an employee, application documents (CV, certificates, interview notes, checks you were allowed to run) typically belong in the personnel file or HR system. Retention then follows employment law, accounting, and social insurance rules, not the "application" purpose anymore. Sensitive items such as health information from before the contract may need separation or earlier deletion if they are not required for the job relationship ([Art. 328b CO](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/de) limits employee data to what is job-related). This is orientation, not legal advice.

Do not leave duplicate copies in the recruiting inbox or on a manager’s laptop after onboarding. How long items stay in an active personnel dossier is a separate topic; this article covers only the application phase.

## Talent pool and open applications

If you want to contact candidates about other roles, ask separately: purpose, duration, and how to opt out. A pre-ticked box on the application form is weak evidence of consent. Store pool members in one place with an expiry date and review annually. Keep consent records separate from the CV so a withdrawal only requires deleting the pool entry.

## Access, correction and erasure requests

Applicants have rights to information and erasure where no statutory retention blocks deletion. If someone asks for erasure, you must find every copy: mailbox, ATS, drive, paper, and the backup window IT keeps. Saying "we deleted the folder" while a line manager still has PDFs is not enough.

## Copies managers forget

Line managers sometimes export "top three" CVs to a desktop or forward them to personal mail for convenience. Those copies follow the same retention as HR’s folder. Onboarding should say: recruiting data stays in HR systems; local saves are deleted when the vacancy closes unless the person joined the team.

If you use an applicant tracking system hosted abroad, retention and deletion must still match Swiss purpose rules; the hosting location is a separate transfer question for your privacy notice.

## E-mail, portals and backups

Many applications arrive as attachments or through a portal. Portal contract end dates and exports into shared mailboxes belong on the same deletion list as HR folders. If IT keeps backups for seven days or seven weeks, HR needs to know whether rejected files can still reappear there. A short alignment meeting with IT saves incomplete answers to erasure requests later.

## Paper and scanning

If you still accept paper applications, you need a shredder or a contracted disposal service and the same periods as for digital files. Scanning into an archive does not replace destroying the paper original when the period expires.

## What this is not

Not guidance on automated screening or AI ranking of CVs. Not the full content list for an active personnel dossier. Not a template for background checks under cantonal law.

## A made-up example: inbox full of PDFs

A made-up example: a trading company with 55 staff recruits twice a year. HR keeps PDFs in a shared mailbox and on a drive folder named "Bewerbungen 2024". After twelve months, 90 files remain, including people who were rejected in January. A candidate requests deletion. HR must find every copy, including the line manager who saved favourites on a desktop. The company adopts a rule: rejected files deleted after four months, pool only with written consent, hired files moved to the payroll archive within two weeks of start date. IT adds a recurring reminder to empty the recruiting mailbox.

The gap was copies, not missing law.
