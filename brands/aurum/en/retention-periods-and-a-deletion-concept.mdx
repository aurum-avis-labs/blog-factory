## Retention periods and a deletion concept for customer data

Retention periods for customer data tell you how long names, contracts, and support history may stay in CRM, mail, and shared folders. The [Swiss FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de) requires that processing does not outlast the purpose ([Art. 6 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)) and that people are informed when you collect data ([Art. 19 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). A deletion concept is your internal map: which customer data sits where, for how long, who deletes it, and when you review the rules. This text is for firms with about 20 to 250 staff and no full-time data protection officer. It is orientation, not legal advice. Newsletter consent has its own logic, see [newsletter consent in Switzerland](/security/blog/newsletter-consent-in-switzerland).

## What this article does not cover

Retention in the [personnel file and job applications](/security/blog/how-long-to-keep-job-applications-switzerland) is a separate article. This deletion concept does not override accounting retention your auditor sets under Swiss company law. It does not replace a legal hold during litigation or an investigation. Borderline cases belong with counsel.

## Build the retention table

A workable table has six columns: data type, legal or business basis, retention period, system, responsible person, deletion method. Start with ten rows, not an empty enterprise project.

| Data type | Basis | Period (orientation) | System | Owner | Deletion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Invoice with customer address | Accounting, contract | Often ten years per auditor | Accounting | Finance | Archive rule, no mail dump |
| Active contract | Contract | Until end plus short proof window | CRM | Sales | Mark inactive, then delete |
| Support ticket | Contract, legitimate interest | e.g. 24 months after close | Ticketing | Support | Check export, then delete |
| Newsletter contact | Consent | Until withdrawal plus short proof | Marketing tool | Marketing | Scrub list, see newsletter article |
| Lead without order | Consent or interest | e.g. 12 months without contact | CRM | Sales | Delete or anonymise |

The years in the table are orientation. Your accountant names binding periods for books. Marketing often needs a shorter period than finance for the same person because the purpose differs.

<aside class="blog-callout">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Note</span>
  <p>The period in your privacy notice and in the table must match. If the notice says «until withdrawal», the CRM must not quietly keep records for five more years.</p>
</aside>

## Where customer data hides

Without a storage map you delete the CRM and leave copies in mail, Excel, and cloud links. List at least: main CRM, shared mailboxes, accounting, file shares, tools used for [sending personal data by email](/security/blog/sending-personal-data-by-email) and [sharing files with personal data](/security/blog/sharing-files-that-contain-personal-data). Every export to a spreadsheet is a new location with its own row.

Old phones, trade-show USB sticks, and «just for a moment» screenshots count too. IT can tell you which systems still sync when you disable an account. The business owner decides whether the content is relevant and belongs in the table.

## Deletion versus statutory retention

Accounting may keep invoices longer than marketing keeps contacts. That is fine when the table separates purposes. It becomes messy when the same person sits in five tools with different periods and nobody knows which system is authoritative. Mark one system as the master for customer records and reference only IDs or contract links elsewhere.

During a dispute or investigation, routine deletion pauses for the affected records. Note in the memo who triggered the pause and when the normal rhythm resumes. That does not replace counsel in a specific case.

## Deletion concept in five steps

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Name owners</h3><p>One business owner and one IT contact per data type. They approve deletions and report at the quarterly meeting.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Write periods down</h3><p>A memo with the table, date, and management sign-off. Not a twenty-page policy nobody reads.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Plan technical deletion</h3><p>CRM status, ticket archive, marketing list. IT supplies reports; the business line confirms.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Include backups</h3><p>Note whether old backups can restore deleted rows and how long rotation runs.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Schedule review</h3><p>Calendar entry for yearly checks and when you add CRM modules or acquire another company.</p></li>
</ol>

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a 40-person services firm in Aargau finds leads still in the CRM after three years although the privacy notice says twelve months without an order. The admin extends the table, sets a quarterly report for «inactive 12 months», and trains reception not to copy customer data into private note apps. No new software, only clear roles.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Table covering invoices, contracts, leads, newsletter, support</li>
  <li>Storage locations including mail and exports listed</li>
  <li>Next review date in the calendar</li>
  <li>Privacy notice checked against the same periods</li>
</ul>

## Document deletion runs

A quarterly run does not need a twenty-page log. Often enough: date, system, count of deleted or anonymised records, name of the approver, short note on exceptions («three contracts under review, not deleted»). That shows deletion is operational, not wishful thinking.

If IT reports that a backup still holds deleted rows, note the rotation period. Many SMEs accept a documented residual window when live systems are clean and the privacy notice explains it.

## When to involve counsel

New processing of sensitive data, a dispute with a customer over erasure, or a client audit with stricter deletion clauses: get advice before the next bulk delete. For everyday hygiene the internal table plus training is often enough.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do we need a lawyer for every row?</h3>
  <p>Not for normal SME hygiene. Engage counsel for investigations, disputes, or when a customer contract demands shorter deletion than your table allows.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>What about «delete everything» requests?</h3>
  <p>The right to erasure is a separate topic. Your retention table shows what you delete routinely. Individual requests need their own process and may allow exceptions for accounting.</p>
