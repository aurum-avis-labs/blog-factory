## AI phone assistant for a small business

<p class="blog-lede">An AI phone assistant for a small business is either notes after a person answers, or a voicebot that picks up. For most Swiss offices of 5 to 80 people, the note is the job that fits.</p>

In the first pattern, a person answers, writes what the caller needs, and that note is routed to the right colleague. In the second, a voicebot answers the caller. The bot brings recording rules, a duty to say it is a machine, and a habit of promising things the firm did not mean. This is a practical comparison, not legal advice. Before you record a live line, ask your adviser whether your calls even fall under the narrow exception below.

Written messages are the cousins of this page. Sorting a shared mailbox is in [sort emails in a shared inbox](/prozess-check/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). A bot on the website, and when a page of answers is enough, is in [website chatbot for a small business](/prozess-check/blog/website-chatbot-sme). The phone adds a voice, and a voice that is stored is a recording.

## What changes if you keep the voice

A note that a person types is personal data if it identifies the caller, and you still say why you keep it. It is not, by itself, a recording of the conversation. The moment you keep the audio, or a transcript taken from the audio, you are in the rules the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner sets out for recordings.

Recording a non-public conversation without the consent of everyone involved can be an offence, whether you are on the call or not (Art. 179bis and Art. 179ter of the Criminal Code, as the EDÖB states them). Consent has to explain the recording and its purpose, and it has to come before you press record, so the person can refuse ([EDÖB](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/aufzeichnung-von-gespraechen)). A sentence in the privacy policy that nobody hears is not that consent.

The Criminal Code treats two telephone situations as not punishable: calls with emergency, rescue and security services, and business calls whose content is orders, assignments, reservations and similar transactions (Art. 179quinquies para. 1). The EDÖB reads the second case narrowly: mass business under time pressure, such as an order line or a reservation line, where stopping to warn every caller in several languages was judged too heavy. A complaint or a longer contract negotiation is not in that case. For those, you inform people first if you record.

Even where the exception applies, the recording may be used only as evidence. The EDÖB says you may not use it for marketing, for training, or to check how staff sell, and you may not evaluate it beyond proof, unless you informed people beforehand. Passing the recording to a third party remains punishable. A transcript that an AI writes so you can "see the themes this month" is an evaluation. It needs the prior information.

The Data Protection Act applies beside the Criminal Code. People can ask what you hold. Do not record in order to feed a report.

## When a voicebot is even a candidate

A bot that speaks is a language model in a conversation. The EDÖB says that where such a model talks or writes directly to a person, the person has a right to know they are dealing with a machine, and whether what they say is used to improve the model or for other purposes ([EDÖB](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/update-geltendes-datenschutzgesetz-ist-auf-ki-direkt-anwendbar)). The first sentence of the call says it is an automatic assistant, what it can do, and how to reach a person. If the vendor trains on your audio, that fact is part of the sentence, or you switch the training off.

At collection you also name who you are and the purpose ([Art. 19 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). A bot that asks for a name, a callback number and the reason for the call is collecting personal data. The spoken line is the information. A link the caller cannot open while driving is not.

The calls that might belong on a bot are the ones that are the same every time and that the exception might cover: opening hours, a simple reservation, a standard order. Your adviser confirms that your line really is that, before you rely on Art. 179quinquies. If a third of the calls are complaints, delays or "I want to change the contract", the line is not an order phone. Those calls need a person, and if you record them you need prior information.

## Errors and the handover

A note can be incomplete. The fix is a required field: who called, what they need, by when, and who in the firm owns it. The note then enters the same queue as an email. Someone sees it the same day.

A bot can be wrong in a way a note is not. It can offer a time you cannot keep, quote a price from an old page, or tell a caller the matter is closed. Limit it to the answers you have written. Anything else, including anger, a legal threat, or a question it has not seen, goes to a person while the caller is still on the line during opening hours, and to a callback message outside them. The callback is a note again. Do not let the bot end the call with "someone will be in touch" and no record.

| Question | Notes after a person | Voicebot that answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who speaks to the caller | Someone from the firm | A machine, named as such at the start |
| What is stored | A written note | Often the voice or a transcript, which is a recording |
| Legal starting point | Purpose and access for the note | Consent, or the narrow order-and-reservation exception, plus the machine disclosure |
| Good for | Mixed and awkward calls | Repeated orders or reservations, if the exception holds |
| Main failure | The note is never written | A promise, or a recording used for a report |
| Handover | The note has an owner | A live person, or a callback that becomes a note |

The note path is office work you may already be close to: a form or a mailbox, an owner, a reminder if the note sits. That is the same shape as other inbound work. It does not require a telephone platform. If the volume is a few calls a day and the same person always writes the note, leave it as a habit. If notes are missing and callers ring again, the missing note is the recurring task.

## Who should skip the bot

Skip the bot if your calls are quotes, complaints, care, or anything where a wrong sentence costs a customer. Skip it if you wanted the recording "for quality" or "to see what people ask". That purpose is exactly what the EDÖB says needs prior information.

Skip the note automation if you are three people in one room and the book by the phone is reliable. Do not do both at once on day one. A bot plus a pile of notes is two systems and no owner.

## A made-up example: forty calls, three complaints

A made-up example: a service firm of 14 people, not a client. About forty calls a week. Most are "when are you here" and "which day is the technician". A few are new work. About three are complaints. Today the person at the phone writes a line on a pad. Some lines never reach the colleague who drives.

They keep the person on the phone. After each call that needs a follow-up, a short form is filled: caller, need, day, owner. The form lands in the same queue as the shared mailbox. Complaints are not recorded. The pad remains for the three hours when the form is down, and those lines are copied the same day. They looked at a bot that would answer and transcribe everything into a weekly theme report. They left it. The report would be an evaluation of recordings, including the complaints, which is outside the exception the EDÖB describes, and the bot would still have to hand the complaint to a person. The note does that without the audio.

No reduction in calls is claimed. The change is that the colleague who drives sees the request without hearing the call again.
