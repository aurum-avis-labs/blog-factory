## Reporting validation results to a board

**Reporting validation results to a board** means translating a messy experiment into a short read a non-builder can challenge: what you tested, for how long, with whose money, against which decision rule, and what you will not claim yet. The board does not need every funnel screenshot. It needs to see whether the riskiest assumption moved, whether the sample was honest, and whether the next cheque buys learning or vanity.

This is for founders and innovation leads in Swiss firms who already ran a smoke test, a fake door, or a first MVP slice and must justify the next tranche to owners, a family council, or an internal steering group. If you are still choosing between a landing page and a product build, start with [fake door test vs real MVP](/blog/fake-door-test-vs-mvp). If the question is how to close a validation phase, see [Go, Pivot, or Stop](/blog/go-pivot-or-stop-decision). What usage can and cannot say about fit sits in [product-market fit: how to find it](/blog/product-market-fit-how-to-find-it).

## Reporting validation results to a board: what belongs on one page

Boards compress fast. One page, seven blocks, each with a number or a clear "not measured":

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li><strong>Hypothesis in one sentence.</strong> "Strangers in segment X will complete job Y without us in the room." Not a vision statement. If you cannot name the segment, you are not ready to report KPIs.</li>
  <li><strong>Instrument.</strong> Fake door, concierge test, deployed MVP, or pilot with one partner. Say which, and link the method to what it can prove, as in [fake door test vs real MVP](/blog/fake-door-test-vs-mvp).</li>
  <li><strong>Period and spend.</strong> Start date, end date, total acquisition spend, and whether traffic was paid, organic, or warm intros only.</li>
  <li><strong>Primary KPI and guardrails.</strong> One main metric you agreed before spend (activated accounts, completed jobs, paid pilots, repeat sessions). One or two guardrails (bounce on onboarding, support tickets, credit burn) so a spike in signups does not hide a broken loop.</li>
  <li><strong>Decision rule written in advance.</strong> "If fewer than N activated users in 21 days, we pause ads and run ten interviews." Without a rule, every board meeting becomes storytelling.</li>
  <li><strong>Explicit non-claims.</strong> List what the test cannot show: market size, moat, margin at scale, legal readiness. Ideas as such cannot be protected; only execution and brand can be ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)).</li>
  <li><strong>Recommendation.</strong> Go, Pivot, or Stop, with the next costed step. If you are not ready to recommend, say "extend measurement" and name the single missing input.</li>
</ul>

## What honest KPIs look like in practice

Internal builds teach the difference between attention and behaviour. On Holist-IQ, a modest Google campaign produced roughly 5'300 impressions, 299 clicks, about 5.7 percent CTR, near CHF 0.65 average cost per click, and about CHF 195 spend. On the product, eleven people signed up, five had sessions longer than four minutes, three returned, two hit the free credit cap, and nobody bought credits. That is a useful board read: acquisition worked well enough to learn; monetisation and depth did not. Reporting only signups would have flattered the story.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>One campaign on one product is not a benchmark you should paste into next year’s budget. Do not present internal figures as targets. The point is to show how primary KPI, guardrails, and non-claims fit on one page.</p>
</aside>

## A made-up example: a fifteen-minute slot in June

A made-up example: a B2B founder in Zurich reports to three family shareholders after a four-week validation sprint on a staff scheduling web app. Hypothesis: care-home managers in canton Zurich will publish one week of shifts without a sales call. Instrument: deployed MVP with email invite, no payments. Spend: CHF 1'200 on LinkedIn, period 3 May to 31 May. Primary KPI: eight managers with one published week. Guardrail: fewer than 30 percent of signups stall on the invite step. Decision rule agreed in April: if fewer than five activated managers by day 28, stop ads and book eight interviews.

The slide shows 412 landing sessions, 34 signups, 6 activated managers, 41 percent stuck on invite because SMS verification failed on older phones. Recommendation: Pivot onboarding (two-week fix), pause scale spend, do not claim willingness to pay. Non-claims: no proof of Swiss-wide TAM, no proof that CHF 49 per month pricing holds. Next ask: CHF 15'000 for a focused pilot with two design partners, not a full sales hire.

The meeting ends with a recorded Stop on scaling ads, not with applause for "34 signups".

## What boards often ask, and how to answer without bluffing

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is this product-market fit?</h3>
  <p>Usually no, not from one validation window. Say what you measured (behaviour in a narrow segment) and point to what fit would require: repeat use, channel economics, and often revenue. [Product-market fit: how to find it](/blog/product-market-fit-how-to-find-it) describes the gap.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Why did we spend if results are mixed?</h3>
  <p>Because the alternative was building for a year on assumptions. Frame spend as tuition with a receipt: you now know invite friction blocks activation, not that "the market said no".</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Can we 10x the budget next month?</h3>
  <p>Only if the decision rule for scale was met and the guardrails stayed green. Otherwise you are buying a louder version of the same ambiguity.</p>
</div>
