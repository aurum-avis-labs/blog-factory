## CCTV at work in Switzerland

CCTV at work in Switzerland is permitted when purpose, scope, and information align. Cameras at entrances, warehouse zones, or tills are common; continuous desk filming is rarely proportionate. The Federal Act on Data Protection expects appropriate measures ([Art. 8 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)) and fair information where collection occurs ([Art. 19 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). [Art. 26 of the Labour Ordinance](https://www.seco.admin.ch/dam/seco/de/dokumente/Arbeit/Arbeitsbedingungen/Arbeitsgesetz%20und%20Verordnungen/Wegleitungen/Wegleitungen%203/ArGV3_art26.pdf.download.pdf/ArGV3_art26_de.pdf) forbids monitoring systems meant mainly to control behaviour. The [FDPIC](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/technische-mittel-zur-uberwachung-am-arbeitsplatz) expects advance notice and proportionality. Orientation for owners and office staff in firms of about 20 to 250 people, not legal advice. Phone fraud in the office is a separate risk, covered in [vishing calls at work](/security/blog/vishing-calls-at-work).

## What workplace video is not

Covert cameras without notice are not a default for SMEs. «Productivity analytics» via screen video looks more like behaviour monitoring than a security camera. BYOD and WhatsApp customer chat are privacy topics, not substitutes for a camera policy.

## Signage, information, and involvement

Tell staff before activation: locations, retention, who may view clips. Minutes from a staff meeting or a one-page note is enough evidence for many firms. People should know how to ask questions. Art. 328b of the Swiss Code of Obligations limits employee data to the employment relationship; video counts.

## Night staff, cleaners, and contractors

Cameras that capture cleaning firms or night watch need the same notice as for permanent staff. Third-party contracts should state whether footage stays on your premises and when it is deleted. Subcontractors with body-worn cameras are a separate contract topic; your policy should state what is allowed on your site.

## Data subject rights and access requests

Staff may request information about their personal data ([Art. 25 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). For video that often means: which clips exist, purpose, and recipients. Keep processes simple: who handles requests, deadlines, no unnecessary copies. Disputes about «was I filmed?» are easier with minutes and signage.

## Cloud vendors and storage location

Many SMEs use NVR or cloud dashboards. Check vendor contract and privacy notice: storage location, subprocessors, who has technical access. Record it in your processing inventory if you keep one. A cheap package without retention rules becomes costly when someone requests access.

## Retention and access to footage

Delete on schedule unless an incident needs a hold. Document who exports for police or insurers. Access only for roles that need it, not for managers «out of curiosity». If a vendor stores cloud footage outside Switzerland, note it in your record under [Art. 12 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de).

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a 40-person services firm in Aargau wants a loading-bay camera after a warehouse break-in. Leadership notes purpose «theft and access», 14 days storage, access for facilities and management. Staff receive a one-page note before mounting. No desk view. The company name is invented; the approach is typical for SMEs without a privacy office.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Inventory existing cameras (including old DVR units)</li>
  <li>Align with rules on logs and screenshots</li>
  <li>Extend the privacy notice for video if you publish one</li>
  <li>Review yearly whether purpose and field of view still fit</li>
</ul>

## Audio, sound, and video-only cameras

This article covers image cameras. Adding sound tightens the balance: staff must understand whether conversations are captured. If you are unsure audio is needed, start without it and document the decision. Covert audio recording at work is not an everyday SME tool.

## When to skip cameras

An office with no theft history and few visitors may need access control and lighting rather than CCTV. Filming «everywhere» to «measure culture» quickly conflicts with Art. 26 of the Labour Ordinance. Talk with HR and affected staff once before mounting hardware.

## Audit, insurance, and customer questions

Auditors and insurers sometimes ask about «CCTV». Answer concretely: which sites, which retention, who has access. The same discipline you use for other retention rules in the firm helps, even when video is only a small part of your data.
