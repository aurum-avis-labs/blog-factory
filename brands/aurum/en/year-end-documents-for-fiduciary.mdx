## The year-end pack your fiduciary asks for, collected once

After 31 December, assemble one dated pack for the fiduciary: bank statements that include the balance-sheet date, the stock list, open customer and supplier balances, payroll, contracts that changed, and the card receipts that are still missing. Each line has a status, received or missing. The fiduciary prepares the accounts. You stop the January thread that asks for the same PDF three times.

This page is the client side. How a fiduciary chases missing receipts across mandates is in [the client inbox and missing receipts](/prozess-check/blog/fiduciary-client-inbox-missing-receipts). Here the reader is the firm that must send the file.

The checklist below is what offices usually ask for. It is not a statute that names every document. If you keep accounts under the Code of Obligations, books, accounting records, the annual report and the audit report must be kept for ten years from the end of the financial year. The federal SME portal states that. Books may be on paper or electronic if they still match the transactions and can be read again. Printed, signed copies of the annual report and of the audit report stay on paper.

## What usually goes in the folder

Agree the list with your fiduciary once, in autumn, not on 6 January. Then the same file names return every year. A typical small firm is asked for the following. If a line does not apply, mark it not applicable.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Bank and postal statements that include 31 December, plus a note of cash on that day</li>
  <li>The stock list: quantities, differences, locations, without a homemade valuation</li>
  <li>Open customer invoices and open supplier invoices, dated to the cut-off</li>
  <li>The payroll journal or the annual payroll file your fiduciary names</li>
  <li>Loans, leases and other contracts that started, changed or ended in the year</li>
  <li>Credit and debit card statements for the financial year, plus receipts for postings that are still open</li>
  <li>A short note of anything unusual: a large write-off, a dispute, a private booking</li>
</ul>

The stock list is a count, not a price opinion. How to build it is in [prepare the year-end stocktake](/prozess-check/blog/year-end-stocktake). Loans and leases matter because a payment in the bank file is not the remaining debt. The contract shows the fiduciary what is still owed.

Card lines without a receipt are the common follow-up in January. Drop the statement in as soon as the provider closes the month, and attach missing receipts to the line, not in a second mail.

Private expenses on the business account, and the reverse, belong in the short note. A quiet line in the statement otherwise survives into March.

## One folder, one status, no second thread

Create the year folder where you already share files with the fiduciary, using the names from the list. During December, save each file as soon as it exists: the leasing PDF when it is signed, not when someone remembers it in January. On the first working day after the cut-off, export open items and the bank file into that same folder.

Each line has a status: received, missing, or not applicable, plus the person who owns the missing piece. A workflow can remind that person once, naming the file. A second reminder is a person, because the first may have gone to the wrong inbox.

Do not keep a parallel zip on a desktop. The shared folder is the pack. If you mail a copy, send the link once and name the lines that are still open. Twelve messages, one per document, is how the fiduciary's queue fills up.

## What you do not prepare

You do not draft the balance sheet, pick the inventory value, or decide the tax return in this folder. Those stay with the fiduciary. Writing off open invoices to make the list look tidy is off limits too. A customer who will pay in January is still open on 31 December. An early write-off creates a correction later.

  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>The pack is evidence for the close. It is not the annual accounts, and it is not a tax filing. Ten-year retention and electronic storage follow the Code of Obligations and the ordinance on business books. This page is not legal advice.</p>
</aside>

On electronic copies, the ordinance allows a carrier that cannot be altered. A carrier that can be changed is allowed only if a technical method shows the information stayed intact, the time of storage can be proved, and the method and its logs are written down. A digital signature is one example, not the only one. If you are unsure whether the shared drive meets that, ask the fiduciary before you throw away the paper. The portal's page on [electronic retention](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/en/electronic-bookkeeping) is the readable summary. The ordinance itself is on Fedlex.

Keep the signed annual report and the signed audit report on paper anyway. The ten-year period runs from the end of the financial year, so a file from the 2026 close is not something to delete in 2027.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a firm with one bank, a small parts shelf and two equipment leases. On 5 January the shared folder holds the December bank PDF, the open customer and supplier lists from the first working day, the annual payroll file and the stock list with the counter's name. The December card statement is there; three receipts are still missing and marked with an owner. One lease is missing. The workflow reminds the signatory by contract name. She drops the PDF in. The office reports: folder complete, except a supplier invoice noted in the short note. The fiduciary does not have to ask for the bank again.

## Who should skip a workflow

Skip it if the fiduciary already gives you a checklist inside their portal and you upload fewer than ten files, once, with no chasing. Skip it if the "folder" would be a new place nobody opens. Skip it if the books are months behind. Catch the receipts up first.

The recurring task worth a workflow is the chase: which lines are still open on day five, and who has the file.
