## The year-end pack your fiduciary asks for, collected once

After 31 December, collect one dated pack for the fiduciary: bank statements that include the balance-sheet date, the stock list, open customer and supplier balances, the payroll file, and the contracts and leases that changed. Put each item in one folder with one status, received or missing. The fiduciary prepares the accounts. You stop the January mail that asks for the same PDF three times.

This is the client's side of the close. The other side, a fiduciary chasing missing receipts across many mandates, is described in [the client inbox and missing receipts](/blog/fiduciary-client-inbox-missing-receipts). Here the reader is the firm that has to send the file.

The list below is what offices are usually asked for. It is not a statute that names each document. What the law does require, if you keep accounts under the Code of Obligations, is that books, accounting records, the annual report and the audit report be kept for ten years from the end of the financial year. The federal SME portal states that, and it states that books may be on paper or electronic if they still match the transactions and can be read again. A printed, signed copy of the annual report and of the audit report stays on paper.

## What usually goes in the folder

Agree the list with your fiduciary once, in autumn, not on 6 January. Then the same names come back every year. A typical small firm is asked for the following. If one line does not apply, mark it not applicable so nobody asks again.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Bank and postal statements that include 31 December, plus a note of cash on that day</li>
  <li>The stock list: quantities, differences, locations, without a homemade valuation</li>
  <li>Open customer invoices and open supplier invoices, dated to the cut-off</li>
  <li>The payroll journal or the annual payroll file your fiduciary names</li>
  <li>Loans, leases and other contracts that started, changed or ended in the year</li>
  <li>A short note of anything unusual: a large write-off, a dispute, a private booking</li>
</ul>

The stock list is a count, not a price opinion. How to build that list is in [prepare the year-end stocktake](/blog/year-end-stocktake). Loans and leases matter because a payment in the bank file is not the same as the remaining debt. The contract tells the fiduciary what is left.

Private expenses paid from the business account, and the reverse, belong in the short note. A quiet line in the bank statement is how those survive into March.

## One folder, one status, no second thread

Create the year folder in the place you already share with the fiduciary, with the names from the list. During December, drop a file in as soon as it exists: the leasing PDF when it is signed, not when someone remembers it in January. On the first working day after the cut-off, export the open items and the bank file into that same folder.

Each line has a status: received, missing, or not applicable, plus the person who owns the missing piece. A workflow can remind that person once, with the name of the file, not with "please send everything again". A second reminder is a person, because the first one may have gone to the wrong inbox.

Do not keep a parallel zip on a desktop. The shared folder is the pack. If you mail a copy, mail the link once, and say which three lines are still open. Twelve messages, one per document, is how the fiduciary's queue fills up.

## What you do not prepare

You do not draft the balance sheet, pick the inventory value, or decide the tax return in this folder. Those stay with the fiduciary. You also do not "clean" open invoices so the list looks short. A customer who will pay in January is still open on 31 December. Writing it off to make the pack tidy creates a correction later.

  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>The pack is evidence for the close. It is not the annual accounts, and it is not a tax filing. Ten-year retention and electronic storage follow the Code of Obligations and the ordinance on business books. This page is not legal advice.</p>
</aside>

On electronic copies, the ordinance allows a carrier that cannot be altered. A carrier that can be changed is allowed only if a technical method shows the information stayed intact, the time of storage can be proved, and the method and its logs are written down. A digital signature is named as one example of that method, not as the only one. If you are unsure whether the shared drive meets that, ask the fiduciary before you throw away the paper. The portal's page on [electronic retention](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/en/electronic-bookkeeping) is the readable summary. The ordinance itself is on Fedlex.

Keep the signed annual report and the signed audit report on paper anyway. The ten-year period runs from the end of the financial year, so a file from the 2026 close is not something to delete in 2027.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a firm with one bank, a small parts shelf and two equipment leases. On 5 January the shared folder already holds the December bank PDF, because someone downloaded it on the second of the month and checked that 31 December appears. It holds the open customer list and the open supplier list, exported the same morning, and the annual payroll file from the payroll program. The stock list is there as quantities and differences, with the counter's name. One lease is missing. The workflow sends one reminder to the person who signed it, naming the contract, not "year-end documents". She drops the PDF in the folder. The office then sends one message: folder complete, nothing disputed except a supplier invoice marked in the short note. The fiduciary does not have to ask for the bank again.

## Who should skip a workflow

Skip it if the fiduciary already gives you a checklist inside their portal and you upload fewer than ten files, once, with no chasing. The portal is the workflow. Skip it if the "folder" would be a new place nobody opens, beside the mail you will send anyway. Skip it if the books are behind by months. A tidy year-end pack on top of an empty ledger does not help. Catch the receipts up first.

The recurring task worth a workflow is the chase: which of the six lines is still open on day five, and who has the file.

## When a Prozess-Check fits

A [Prozess-Check](/prozess-check) fits when that chase is the work every January: the same three files missing, the same person asked twice, the stock list retyped from a count sheet. You bring that one recurring task. The meeting is 60 minutes on Microsoft Teams. You leave with a concrete case, whether it pays off, a price range and a rough duration. The hour does not commit you to a project.
