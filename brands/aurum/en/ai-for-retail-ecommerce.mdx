## AI for small retailers and online shops

AI for a small retailer or online shop pays off on the office work beside the shop system, not on a new slogan and not on replacing the shop. Customers ask where an order is. Returns sit in the inbox until someone looks at the order and the terms. Product facts exist in one language and get pasted into a second. A supplier order starts as an email when stock runs low. A card payout arrives as one amount for many orders. Those are the jobs. Writing advertising copy is a different search, and it is not this one.

This is for owners and office leads of Swiss shops and webshops with a small team, in a store, online, or both. The shop software stays. The question is which weekly tasks it does not finish.

## What the shop system already does

Start with the system you already pay for. On Shopify, the help pages are specific. After you add and publish a language, checkout and email notifications can show the translated content, and the checkout follows the language the customer used to browse. If a translation of a notification exists, the customer is sent that notification in the language of the order. If a translation is missing, the store falls back to the primary language. In customer-facing notifications, the product and variant name can be translated to the checkout language when it differs from the store default. From the order itself you can write to the customer, or resend the order confirmation to the customer or to yourself. That is [Shopify's localization guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/localization-and-translation) and the page on [managing an order](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/managing-order-details).

If the shop already sends the status mail, holds the stock you trust and creates the invoice, leave that stretch. Do not put a second system on top of it. What another product does is not stated here unless we have read that vendor's own page. In the Check we look at the interfaces your program actually offers.

## Questions, returns, languages, suppliers, payouts

Five jobs repeat in a small shop. Each starts from an order or a product record you already have. None of them is a caption.

### The question the status mail missed

"Where is my parcel?", "Can I collect it?", "You sent the wrong size." The order page already holds the answer to the first kind of question, if the shipment was recorded there. A workflow can recognise that question, draft a reply from the order, and leave it for someone to send. It should not promise a date that is not on the order. Sorting a mixed inbox in general is covered in [sorting a shared inbox](/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). Here the reply has to come from this order, not from a template that ignores it.

How to classify "where is my order", a return label and a refund is a separate piece of work. This article only names it, so it is not confused with a general complaint about a faulty product.

### Returns follow your terms, not a European habit

In Switzerland there is no statutory right to return or exchange goods you have bought, in a shop or online. [ch.ch](https://www.ch.ch/en/safety-and-justice/returning-or-exchanging-goods) says each shop sets its own rules, and that some shops voluntarily allow a change of mind, often within 14 days or more. Door-to-door sales and contracts agreed on the telephone can be cancelled within 14 days if the goods are worth more than CHF 100. A workflow can check an order against the rule you wrote down, draft the reply, and prepare the refund or the refusal. It should not invent a right the terms do not give, and it should not tell a customer the opposite of your terms because a model has seen other countries. What to do with the damaged item and the supplier is a person's decision. This is a practical reading, not legal advice.

### Product facts in a second language

A Swiss shop often needs German and French, sometimes Italian or English. The shop can show a translated title once you have entered it. The slow part is the rest: the material, the size, the delivery note, the sentence about care, kept the same in every language when the product changes. [Multilingual customer messages](/blog/multilingual-customer-communication) covers replies to customers. Here the gap is the product record. A draft from the language you already trust, into the fields of the shop, with a person checking the facts, is the useful version. A slogan that sounds better in one language than it is true in another is the version to refuse.

### Supplier orders

When a line runs low, someone writes the wholesaler, often from memory or from a spreadsheet next to the shop. The delivery comes back as a note that does not match the lines you ordered. If the shop already creates the purchase order and matches the delivery, leave that stretch. If the order still leaves as an email and returns as a PDF, you are typing the same lines twice. That pattern, across industries, is [stopping double entry](/blog/stop-double-data-entry). For a shop, the fields are article, quantity, supplier and the delivery you expected.

### The payout and the orders

Card and wallet payouts often arrive as one amount for many orders, minus fees, plus a refund from last week. The shop shows orders one by one. Someone matches the list. A workflow can line up order numbers that appear in both places and park the lines that do not. It should not mark a payout as reconciled because the totals were close. The leftover, a fee you do not recognise, a refund without an order, stays with a person. What your provider's report actually contains is a question for your file, not a feature we state here.

## What stays with a person

A damaged delivery, a customer who was promised something the shop never offered, a discount, and a date you are not sure of: those stay with someone who can look at the order and stand behind the reply. A workflow may draft. It should not send the reply that creates a claim, and it should not change a price.

[Which process to automate first](/blog/which-process-to-automate-first) still applies: it repeats, it hurts, it has a clear end, and the data is already in the shop or the inbox. A shop with a handful of orders a week will not repay a new route. Neither will a task that is really "write our next campaign".

## Where to start

Count one ordinary week: status questions you answered by opening the order, returns you judged against your terms, product texts you copied into another language, supplier mails, and the payout you matched by hand. If one of those dominates, that is the case. If none of them takes long, stop.
