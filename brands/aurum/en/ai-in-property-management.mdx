## AI in property management: where it pays off and where it does not

In a Swiss property management firm, AI is worth it wherever text arrives and has to be put into a fixed shape. That mostly means five workflows: tenant requests and damage reports, contractor orders, the paperwork around a tenant changeover, reporting to owners, and acknowledging rental applications. Rent, accounting and the service charge statement are already handled by your property management software. The manual work sits in between: between the inbox, the phone and that software.

This is for the managing director or head of property management at a small or mid-size Swiss firm. You run property management software such as Rimo R5, ImmoTop2 or GARAIO REM, and your property managers still spend a good part of the day reading, searching and retyping. This is not about replacing the software. It is about the work that still runs by hand next to it.

## Where the time goes in day-to-day property management

The time rarely goes into the difficult cases. It goes into the many ordinary ones.

An email lands in info@: "The water in our shower isn't draining." No address, no flat number, just a sender. Someone looks the tenant up in the software, finds the building, checks who is responsible, looks for the plumber who usually works there, writes the order, replies to the tenant and files a note. Ten minutes if everything fits. Then the next report arrives, this time by phone at reception, on a slip of paper.

On top of that come notices to confirm, handover inspections, key handovers, questions about the service charge statement, and the quarterly report for an owner who wants to know which repairs are still open. Each task is small. Together they fill the day, and they pile up as soon as someone is on holiday.

## Why a portal or a chatbot on its own rarely fixes it

The usual answers solve part of the problem and leave the rest.

Some property management systems now offer something for this themselves. GARAIO REM describes its [tenant services](https://www.garaio-rem.ch/de/mieterservices) (German page) as request and work-order management, run in partnership with imofix.io, with master data and responsibilities synchronised automatically. For Rimo R5, eXtenso lists [EasyTicket](https://www.extenso.ch/), through which tenants report damage and requests digitally. Check first what your system already does and whether you actually use it. If a portal covers most of your reports, there is nothing to build.

The gap remains wherever tenants do not use the portal. Some call. Others send an email, because they know the firm's address and not the login. The law requires tenants to report defects they are not obliged to remedy themselves ([Art. 257g CO](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/en#art_257_g)). It does not prescribe a channel. So you almost always have several inputs, and someone merges them by hand.

A chatbot or a ChatGPT subscription helps with wording. It does not match a report to the building, the flat and the person responsible, because it does not know your master data. And a tenant's email with name, address and phone number does not belong in a private account.

More staff solves it too, at a price. The new person does the same manual work.

## Five workflows that are often worth it

Whether a workflow is worth it depends on how often it happens and whether it follows fixed rules. In most firms, these are the five.

| Workflow | What can run automatically | What stays with you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tenant requests and damage reports | Read email, phone note and form; recognise building, flat, category and urgency; find the person responsible | Deciding what to do, every emergency |
| Contractor orders | Draft order with address, flat, contact, description and photos | Awarding the job, cost ceiling, sending |
| Tenant changeover | Confirm the notice, propose inspection and handover dates, watch deadlines, prepare letters | The inspection on site, judging the defects |
| Owner reporting | Compile figures from the export, list open repairs and vacancies, draft a commentary | Interpretation, sign-off |
| Rental applications | Acknowledge receipt, check the file is complete, set a deletion date | Choosing the tenant |

The first workflow is almost always the most frequent. How to capture and route those reports step by step is the subject of a separate article on tenant requests and damage reports.

For the changeover, look at what you already have. GARAIO REM offers a [digital handover protocol](https://www.garaio-rem.ch/de/garaio-rem-erweitern/digitales-abnahmeprotokoll-dap), and Rimo R5 has RimoSign. The inspection itself is then already digital. What often still runs by hand are the steps before and after it: coordinating dates, informing tenants, passing defects on to contractors, releasing the rent deposit.

## Rental application files: careful and practical

Rental applications contain personal data, up to a debt-collection extract. AI should prepare here, not select.

The FDPIC has published a [factsheet on application forms for rented flats](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/dam/en/sd-web/F7ojQw99LBH-/Merkblatt%20zu%20Anmeldeformularen%20f%C3%BCr%20Mietwohnungen%20DE_20250715.pdf) (in German), last updated in July 2025. Several points in it affect any automation directly. The form may ask about pending debt-collection proceedings and loss certificates from the last five years, but not about marital status, religion or nationality, except for the type and duration of a residence permit. You may ask all applicants for a copy of the debt-collection register extract. Once the flat is let, the forms and attachments of the other applicants have to be destroyed, unless there is a specific reason, such as a waiting list the applicant has agreed to.

In practice that means: application files only go through accounts your firm has a contract for, never through a private chat. The automation knows the deletion date and reminds you of it. If you are unsure what you may keep and for how long, ask your data protection adviser. This section is guidance for the office, not legal advice.

## Who should wait

Not every firm should have something built now.

Wait if your master data is unreliable. Routing compares every report with tenants, flats and responsibilities in your software. If email addresses are missing, or responsibilities exist only in one person's head, even the best automation routes reports to the wrong place. Then tidying up comes first.

Also wait if only a few reports arrive per day and one person handles them easily on the side, or if your portal already covers nearly everything. And if nobody owns the workflow, you would be automating the mess.

## Where to start

Take one workflow, not five. In most firms that is tenant requests and damage reports, because they arrive every day.

For one normal week, count: how many reports, through which channel, how many minutes until each one is assigned, how often you had to ask back. Those four numbers show whether a build pays off.
