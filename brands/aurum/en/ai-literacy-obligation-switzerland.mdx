When Swiss firms search for an **AI training obligation**, they usually ask: must we train staff on AI, and is a workshop enough? In EU law that duty is **AI literacy** ([Art. 4 AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/4/)). In Switzerland the FADP applies in parallel. This article maps both. It is not legal advice.

## What Art. 4 requires

Art. 4 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 requires providers and deployers of AI systems to take measures so that staff and other persons operating AI on their behalf have a sufficient level of AI literacy. Technical knowledge, experience, education and the use context are taken into account. The duty does **not** require guaranteeing a specific literacy level for every individual.

According to the [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai), prohibitions and AI-literacy obligations have applied since **2 February 2025**. Later GPAI and high-risk timelines are staggered; as of October 2026, re-check the Commission page because Omnibus changes can shift dates.

## Does it apply to an SME only in Switzerland?

If you neither offer in the EU, have an establishment there, nor operate a system treated as high-risk there, Art. 4 is rarely the first compliance lever. Start with the revised FADP, cloud contracts and an allow-list. See [EU AI Act and Swiss SMEs](/prozess-check/blog/eu-ai-act-swiss-smes).

Once you have EU customers, EU staff or a system with effects in the EU, AI literacy becomes part of deployer duties. “We mentioned ChatGPT once” is not enough.

Regardless of the AI Act, Swiss FADP still applies: whoever processes personal data with AI remains responsible ([FDPIC](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/ki-und-datenschutz)).

## What “training obligation” means in practice

AI literacy is useful when everyone who uses AI at work can afterwards:

1. Name allowed tools and forbidden data types.
2. Pick the right tool for the task.
3. Give enough context and check the output.
4. Stop and escalate when something does not fit.

Same bar as [AI training for employees](/prozess-check/blog/ai-training-for-employees). A tool demo without rules does not replace it.

## A four-week mini programme for Swiss SMEs

| Week | Content |
|------|---------|
| 1 | Allow-list and red/amber/green rules. Template: [AI policy](/prozess-check/blog/ai-policy-template-sme) |
| 2 | Half-day workshop on real tasks, group size that can practise |
| 3 | Owner for seats, offboarding, incident reporting |
| 4 | Sample three real cases; written go/stop |

If you have EU exposure, also keep evidence that measures were taken (agenda, attendance, rule version). Art. 4 does not require a certificate; documented measures help when asked.

## FAQ

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is there a Swiss FADP “AI training duty”?</h3>
  <p>The FADP does not spell out training word-for-word. Appropriate organisational measures (Art. 8) often include recurring awareness in practice. Art. 4 is EU law and applies with EU nexus.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is an online ChatGPT course enough?</h3>
  <p>Rarely alone. Without an allow-list, customer-data examples and an escalation path, the operational link Art. 4 and the FDPIC expect is missing.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Who must be trained?</h3>
  <p>Everyone who operates or steers AI for the company: not only IT. Sales, fiduciary, HR and assistants often first.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Workshop or Process Check?</h3>
  <p>Workshop when the team needs shared rules and practice. Process Check when one workflow should be automated. They are not the same purchase.</p>
</div>
