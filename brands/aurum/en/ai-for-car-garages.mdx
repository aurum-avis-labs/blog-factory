## AI for car garages: the office side

AI for a car garage belongs in the office around the workshop system, not in the diagnosis and not on the ramp. The workshop software already holds the job card, the calendar, the courtesy car and the invoice. What still takes the afternoon is the work that arrives by phone and email before a job exists, and the paper that leaves with the customer: a service request in the tyre season, a repair quote written after the inspection, a parts order at the wholesaler, a reminder before the vehicle test, and the invoice that still has to be checked against the job. Those are the candidates. The diagnosis, the price and the safety call stay with the workshop.

This is for the owner or the service desk of a Swiss independent garage, a handful of people, one or two advisors at the counter. It is not a page about diagnostic testers.

## What the workshop software already does

Start with the system you already run, because a second job card helps nobody.

[WERBAS//KSR](https://werbas-ksr.com/net-planung/) describes a workshop calendar for customer appointments and for service and wheel scheduling. Appointments can be planned before a job exists and later turned into job time. The same planning module lists courtesy cars you can lend during the service and can generate a prefilled courtesy-car agreement. On the [order side](https://werbas-ksr.com/net-auftragsabwicklung/), the product describes quotes, jobs, delivery notes and invoices, including a split between the customer and an insurer for an accident, a warranty or a goodwill case.

If your advisors already book the ramp in that calendar, already lend the courtesy car from the same screen, and already turn a finished job into an invoice, do not rebuild those steps. Other workshop programs offer their own versions of the same jobs. What yours can connect to is a question for the check. This page does not claim an integration Aurum has built. The gap is what never gets entered: the call, the email, the note on the key tag, the wholesaler cart someone filled on a phone.

## The office work that sits beside the job

### Service and tyre requests

In a quiet week the counter can take a call and book a slot. In March and October the same counter has a queue of tyre changes, plus the ordinary services, plus the cars that will not start. Requests also arrive by email and by the form on the website, often after the desk has gone home. Someone copies name, registration number, tyre size and a wished date into the calendar. That copy is the work. Whether the ramp can take another car on a Saturday is a workshop decision. The calendar is not the problem. The path into the calendar is.

### Repair quotes after the inspection

The customer hears a price in the yard, or gets a call once the car is on the lift and the brake discs are worse than the service suggested. The advisor writes a quote: parts, labour, what can wait. The workshop software can hold that quote once someone types it. The delay is the typing, and the chase when the customer does not reply and the car is still occupying a bay or a parking space. A workflow can turn the advisor's notes into a quote draft in the fields the software already has, and it can list quotes that have had no answer. It does not decide that a repair is necessary.

### Parts at the wholesaler

The mechanic finds the part in a catalogue or a wholesaler shop, orders it, and the desk types the same part number onto the job so the invoice is right. Two entries, one part. When the delivery note arrives it is checked against the job by eye. A workflow can take a clear order confirmation or delivery note and propose the lines on the job. A wrong part number, a surcharge, or a part that was for a different car stays with a person.

### The vehicle test reminder

The official test is not something the garage invents. For passenger cars, the [Canton of Lucerne road traffic office](https://strassenverkehrsamt.lu.ch/strassenverkehr/fahrzeug/fahrzeugpruefungen/pruefungsintervalle) states the interval in Art. 33 of the ordinance on technical requirements for road vehicles (VTS): first five years, but no later than six, after first registration, then after three years, then every two years. Vans follow a different rhythm on the same page, first after four years. The [Canton of Aargau](https://www.ag.ch/de/smartserviceportal/dienstleistungen?dl=periodische-oder-freiwillige-fahrzeugpruefung-bc817f0e-7aa6-4ba8-9ef7-80d4bb067c5f_de) states that the licensing authority sends the invitation for the periodic test. The legal text is [Art. 33 VTS](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1995/4425_4425_4425/de#art_33).

A garage reminder is a service on top of that invitation, for customers whose cars you already know. It can be a draft mail a few weeks before the date you have stored, offering a check or a slot. It is not the official summons, and it is not legal advice about whether a car is due. If you do not store the first-registration date, you cannot compute the interval, and you should not pretend to.

### The invoice after pickup

The job is done, the keys are at the desk, the customer is in the waiting room or already on the way. The invoice should match the work that was approved, not the work someone hoped to sell. Where the software already builds the invoice from the job, leave it. The remaining work is the exception: a goodwill deduction, a warranty claim, an insurer that pays only part. Those exceptions are the advisor's decision. A workflow can highlight a job whose invoice total moved after the quote the customer accepted.

## How this differs from a trades business

A trades business loses time between the site and the office: daywork sheets, photos, material bought on the way. That pattern is in [AI for trades businesses](/blog/ai-for-trades-businesses). A garage is the other way round. The vehicle comes to you. The customer is on the phone or at the counter. There is no daywork sheet in a van at the end of the week. The waste is the second typing between the call, the wholesaler and the job card, and the reminder the official test letter does not replace if you want the customer back in your workshop.

## What stays with the workshop

Judgement stays with a person: whether a car is safe to leave, what the repair should cost, whether a noise is worth opening, how you speak to a customer whose bill is higher than the quote. A workflow prepares a draft and a list. It does not sign the job card.

Skip it when the desk already lives inside the workshop system and the phone queue is short. Skip a test-reminder project if you do not keep registration dates. The four questions in [which process to automate first](/blog/which-process-to-automate-first) apply here too: it repeats, it hurts, it has a clear end, and the data is already written down.

## Where to start

Count two peak weeks and two quiet ones: calls and mails that become appointments, quotes typed from notes, parts typed twice, test reminders you meant to send. The largest of those is the case. If it is small, stop.
