## Terms of use for an MVP

**Terms of use for an MVP** are the contract between you and people who sign in, post content, or pay through your product. They say what the service is, what users may not do, how accounts end, and how you may change the offer while you are still learning. They sit beside the privacy notice, not inside it. You need a reachable URL before real users arrive and before many app store reviews, even when the product is a thin first release.

This is for founders in Switzerland who are shipping a first web or mobile MVP and do not yet have in-house counsel on speed dial. Privacy duties for a Swiss MVP are a different article: [privacy requirements for a Swiss MVP](/blog/privacy-requirements-swiss-mvp). App Store privacy labels are covered in [app store privacy labels](/blog/app-store-privacy-labels). How you commission software locally is in [startup software development in Switzerland](/blog/startup-software-development-switzerland). Nothing here is a template you can paste and call done. It is a checklist of decisions to take to someone who drafts contracts for your sector.

## Terms of use for an MVP: decisions before you draft

Copy these headings into a working doc. Fill them with facts, not marketing. Your adviser turns them into enforceable wording.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Name the parties and the service</h3><p>Legal entity, contact address, and what the product actually does today. For an MVP, say that features may change, break, or disappear while you test.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Accounts and eligibility</h3><p>Minimum age, one account per person, how you verify email or phone, and what happens to data when an account closes.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Acceptable use</h3><p>What users may upload or do, what is forbidden (abuse, scraping, impersonation, illegal content), and how you may remove material or suspend access.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Fees, trials, and refunds</h3><p>If you charge, state currency, renewal, and how someone cancels. If everything is free in v1, say so and say whether that may change.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Intellectual property</h3><p>Who owns the software, your brand, and user-generated content. Users usually grant you a licence to host and display what they post. That is separate from who owns the codebase you paid to build ([software ownership overview](https://www.rentschpartner.ch/ict-law/schutz-von-software)).</p></li>
  <li data-step="6"><h3>Liability and warranties</h3><p>An MVP is not sold as enterprise-grade. Limits and disclaimers belong here, but B2C and B2B rules differ. This step is for counsel, not for guessing from a blog post.</p></li>
  <li data-step="7"><h3>Changes, law, and contact</h3><p>How you notify users when terms change, which law and courts apply, and how to reach you with legal notices.</p></li>
</ol>

## What publishing terms does not prove

Publishing terms does not show product-market fit, safe architecture, or that your processing is lawful. It does not protect an idea as such ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)). It does not replace store policies: Apple still expects more than a repackaged website ([guideline 4.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), and Google Play may require a closed test before a wide release for some new accounts ([Play testing rules](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)).

  <p>This article is product and launch planning, not legal advice. Marketplaces, payments, health data, children, or EU consumers each change what must be in the contract and what you cannot waive. Stop and brief counsel before you invite paying strangers.</p>

## Who should wait on formal terms

      <li>Founders with sign-up, user content, or in-app messaging going to people outside friends and family</li>
      <li>Teams submitting to an app store where a terms URL is part of the listing</li>
      <li>B2B pilots where the customer asks for a document before connecting their directory</li>

      <li>A single landing page with analytics but no login</li>
      <li>Internal demos behind one shared password with no external users</li>
    </ul>

## A made-up example: beta sign-up in Basel

A made-up example: a founder in Basel, not a client. The MVP matches freelancers with half-day admin help for SMEs. Terms are linked in the footer and again at account creation. The service section states that matching is manual in v1, that listings may be removed without notice, and that the app may change weekly during beta. Acceptable use forbids hate speech, fake profiles, and scraping. User posts grant a non-exclusive licence to display the text inside the app. Fees say the beta is free and that paid tiers may appear later with email notice. Liability is left to the lawyer; the founder only supplies the facts that the product is experimental and not an employment agency. When a user deletes an account, the terms point to the privacy notice for retention of invoices. The App Store listing uses the same URL as the web app. The founder does not claim the document made the product compliant. It made expectations visible before strangers arrived.

## Practical placement before launch

Host terms on a stable path on your own domain, not only inside a PDF in email. Link them from the footer, the sign-up screen, and anywhere users pay. Keep a dated version or a change log once counsel approves updates. If you use a click-wrap checkbox, store what the user saw, not only a boolean in the database.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do I need separate terms for web and mobile?</h3>
  <p>One document is enough if the service is the same. Mention that the app is distributed through Apple or Google and that store rules also apply. Add a sentence if mobile permissions differ from the browser.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Can I reuse another startup's terms?</h3>
  <p>Another company's terms describe their risk, entity, and product. Copying them misstates who contracts with the user and often breaks on liability and consumer rules. Use them as a conversation starter with your adviser, not as a file you upload unchanged.</p>
</div>
