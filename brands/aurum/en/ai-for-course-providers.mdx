## AI for course providers and private schools

<p class="blog-lede">For course providers and private schools, AI pays off in the office around the calendar: registration, waiting lists, invoices, certificates and repeated messages. Teaching, assessment and advice stay with the people who run the course.</p>

If your course software already takes the booking and the payment in one place, do not build that path again.

This page is for owners and office leads of small course providers and private schools in Switzerland: language schools, continuing education, music, sport, tutoring, a school with a few dozen to a few hundred pupils. It is not a guide to AI in lessons, and it does not replace your school's data-protection adviser when a child's file is involved.

## Where the course office loses the day

The day is not lost on the difficult class. It is lost on the sixth registration for a course that has two seats left.

A form arrives, or an email, or a message from a parent. Someone checks the course, the date, the language and the number of free places. If there is a seat, they confirm. If not, they put the person on a list that lives in a spreadsheet, or in their head. Then they work out who pays. Often it is not the person who will sit in the room. An employer pays for a member of staff. A parent pays for a child. The invoice address, the purchase-order number and the participant's name are three different lines, and they have to land on the same bill.

After the course someone asks for the certificate, the attendance, or a copy of the invoice for the HR department. The same questions arrive in German, French or English, especially at a language school. None of this is teaching. All of it waits when the person who "knows the list" is away.

## What your course software may already do

Start with the program you already pay for. Many providers have a system for the catalogue, the rooms and the enrolments. If participants book there, pay there and receive their confirmation from there, that stretch is done. A second tool that retypes the same enrolment is a new place for mistakes.

The gap is what happens beside that program: email that skips the form, employer billing with a cost centre, a waiting list the software does not run, certificates finished in Word, reminders the night before a trip. Ask the desk which of those they still do outside the program. That list is the candidate list. Do not assume a connector exists because a brochure says "integration".

## Five jobs worth a closer look

Registration and the seat. A form should check the course code, the date and the number of places before anyone confirms. A full course becomes a waiting-list entry with a clear position, not a polite "we will see". The confirmation names the course, the time, the place and what to bring. It does not invent a discount.

The payer. The participant and the invoice address are separate fields. If they differ, the bill goes to the address that will pay, and the participant's name stays on the line that says who attended. A missing cost-centre number is a question, not a guess.

Payment reminders before the course, not three months later. The school's own rule decides whether an unpaid seat is released. The automation only applies the rule you wrote down.

The certificate or confirmation of attendance, from a template, after a person has marked who was there. A document from a template is a fixed path. How that pattern works in general is in [generate documents from templates](/prozess-check/blog/generate-documents-from-templates).

Messages that are the same for a whole group: room change, what to bring, the material, the certificate is ready. One approved text, sent to the people on that course. Individual advice stays a personal mail.

Which of these to do first is the same question as for any office: frequency, time, and a rule you can state. The way to pick one is in [which process to automate first](/prozess-check/blog/which-process-to-automate-first).

## Language, parents and pupil data

A language school, or a course that parents book, often answers the same fact in more than one language. The stable sentences (time, place, price, what is included) belong in a short glossary so the wording does not drift. How to keep that glossary, and where a person still checks, is in [multilingual customer communication](/prozess-check/blog/multilingual-customer-communication).

Pupil and parent data are personal data. Health information, a report, or a conversation about a child does not belong in a free chat account. The traffic light for that is in [ChatGPT and customer data](/prozess-check/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg). Administration of a course, without the file, is a different bucket from the file itself. If you are unsure where a field sits, ask your adviser before it leaves the school system.

  <p>Do not automate the decision about whether a pupil belongs on a course, a grade, or a disciplinary step. Those stay with the school. The office can confirm a seat, send a bill and file a certificate after a person has decided.</p>

## What to leave alone

Leave the teaching alone. A tool that drafts a worksheet is a different purchase, and it is not this page. Leave the course software alone if the desk already lives inside it. Leave a chatbot on the public site for later if the real load is the inbox and the list. And leave any flow that would read a pupil dossier in order to "save time". The time saving is in the repeated mail, not in the judgement.

If you only run two courses a year, the spreadsheet may still be honest. Automation pays when the same sequence happens every week and a mistake means a double booking or an invoice to the wrong company.
