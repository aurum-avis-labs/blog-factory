## AI for real estate agents in Switzerland

<p class="blog-lede">AI in a brokerage pays off where one listing produces many near-identical enquiries and the team still answers each one by hand.</p>

In a small Swiss estate agency the same jobs repeat: reply with approved documents, viewing slot, reminder, follow-up after the visit, short report to the instructing owner. Who gets the property stays with a person. Managing a building you already let, with tenants and damage reports, is a different office, covered in [AI in property management](/prozess-check/blog/ai-in-property-management).

This page is for the person who runs a small firm: a handful of staff, several portals, a full mailbox after a flat or house goes live. It is not a portal review, and it does not tell you which buyer to prefer.

## What fills the day once the listing is live

A new listing often creates twenty messages with the same four questions: still available, viewing, extra costs, plans. Some come through the portal contact form. ImmoScout24's help for seekers points to that form; the portal deletes personal application dossiers on its side. You may still get mail, a notification, or a call from someone who did not use the form.

Each reply is small. Together they take the morning, and the good prospects wait behind the copies. Meanwhile the owner who instructed you asks what happened this week: how many enquiries, how many viewings, whether anyone is serious. That report is not a tenant damage list. If you also manage buildings, keep the two reports apart. Mixing them is how a seller hears about a leaking shower in a block they do not own.

## What the portal requires of the listing

Before automation, the [ImmoScout24 insertion rules](https://www.immoscout24.ch/c/de/ueber-uns/insertionsregeln) of SMG Swiss Marketplace Group apply. The published facts must be true and must refer to that object: price, location, size, the contact person, condition, availability, images and plans. The price field is the effective gross rent or the final sale price, or the label that the price is on request. A listing without a concrete price is placed last in the results. A valid phone number or email must be on the listing. Images must not show an email, a postal address, a phone number or a licence plate, and a face only with that person's consent. A third party may publish only with the owner's or the manager's consent. Each new listing is checked before it goes live. Deleting and republishing the same object by automated means, to get around a cap on how many listings you may run, is named there as abuse.

Those rules matter for the mail you send afterwards. The price, the availability and the address in the reply have to match the listing. A fluent paragraph that "improves" the size or says "still available" when it is not, is a false listing in another channel. Any automated reply should quote the fields you already published, not rewrite them.

## Five recurring jobs

**Reply with the pack.** Once the facts are approved, the tenth enquiry does not need a new text. It needs the plans, the cost sheet you are allowed to send, and the sentence about when viewings are. A person writes that pack once per object. The flow attaches it. It does not draft a new description.

**Viewings.** A group viewing on Thursday at 17:00 is a calendar, not a negotiation in every thread. Offer the published slots. A request outside them goes to the person who has the key. A reminder the day before cuts the empty slots. Confirm who is expected, so you are not holding a viewing for a name you cannot match to a message.

**Follow-up after the visit.** The same day or the next morning: are they still interested, is there a question the pack did not answer, what is the next step you actually offer. One approved text, a place for their reply, and a stop if they say no. Chasing someone who has said no is how the mailbox gets noisy again.

**Owner report.** A short note: enquiries, viewings held, viewings missed, questions you could not answer from the pack, and anything that needs the owner's decision (a price, a date, access). Numbers from the list you keep, a sentence a person edits. Not a valuation, and not a recommendation of a named buyer.

**Odd cases.** A question about building rights, a neighbour, financing, or "will you take my flat in part exchange" is not the pack. It goes to the broker. Sorting a shared inbox so the standard mails and the odd ones land in different piles is the pattern in [sort emails in a shared inbox](/prozess-check/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). The listing is the reason the mail exists. The sort is the same idea.

Which job hurts most is a count. How to pick the first is in [which process to automate first](/prozess-check/blog/which-process-to-automate-first).

## What to leave alone

Leave the portal's own tools alone if your team already answers inside them and the pile is small. A second inbox that copies every notification creates two places to miss a reply. Leave valuation models and "matching" products for a separate decision. They are not the morning's repeated mail. Leave any flow that publishes or republishes listings on its own. The insertion rules treat automated republishing to dodge a limit as abuse, and a wrong price in an automated update is your listing, not the tool's.

If you sell two properties a year, the personal reply is the product. Automation pays when one listing produces enough copies that the broker types the same four answers instead of being at the viewing.
