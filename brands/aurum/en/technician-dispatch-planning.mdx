## Dispatch planning for field technicians

"Automate dispatch planning" in a small firm rarely means new software on day one. It means turning a request into a proposed visit that fits skill, area and parts, confirming a window with the customer, and when the day moves, sending a message someone checks before it goes out. Recurring contract maintenance with reminders weeks ahead is the contract calendar in [schedule recurring maintenance](/prozess-check/blog/maintenance-service-reminders). This page is the running day: confirmed visits, breakdowns, moves. Report and invoice afterwards in [from daywork report to invoice](/prozess-check/blog/daywork-report-to-invoice). Do not build a second invoice path here.

  <li><strong>Fit</strong><p>Skill, area, part on the van or in the workshop.</p></li>

## What the request needs before assignment

Many firms collect requests by phone, form and mail from the technician on site. The flow needs one intake that sets the fields before the board is visible. Otherwise the dispatcher types the same data twice.

"Heating, Müller, sometime" cannot be planned. Before a name goes on the board, the office has fields, not a paragraph to reread:

- Site and who opens the door
- Visit type: maintenance, breakdown, quote, return for a missing part
- Skill in your words (gas, controls, brand you are not certified for)
- Part if the symptom usually needs one: van, workshop, order
- How fixed the time is (production stop vs. filter that can wait a day)

The phone call can fill the fields. The board should not depend on memory of the call.

If the part is not on the van or in the workshop, the proposal does not promise a repair today. It proposes a look, or a day when the part can be there. Promising the fix and arriving without the part wastes both mornings.

## Proposal, then confirmation

Match the request to someone with the skill and a sensible patch, without wrecking the rest of the day. For a handful of technicians with known areas, that is a visible rule: north, city, lakeside, plus skills per person. The board suggests. The dispatcher confirms. A sheet does not calculate the best drive across the canton. It stops the office sending the only skilled person to the far site while a nearer visit waits.

The customer gets a window, not a fantasy minute. "Between 10 and 12" is honest if the day can hold it. A single clock time only when you mean it. The appointment is confirmed when they agree, as on the contract calendar. A breakdown may be "this afternoon, we will call from the road". That is still a message you can file.

Parts and skill stay on the row for the dispatcher. The customer sees window, site and intent.

Breakdowns that sound "immediate" still need fields. "Immediate" without a site sends the wrong person. The dispatcher decides whether "immediate" displaces a planned maintenance visit. That choice stays human. The flow only proposes who fits geographically and by skill.

## When the day moves

Breakdown at nine, wrong part, visit runs long: the board shows the new order, and customers whose window changed hear it before they set off.

The flow can draft the message from the new window: who, where, new range, call again if it moves further. A person sends when the time is still a guess. A drafted "14:00" the dispatcher has not accepted will be wrong in the customer's diary.

Keep a short log: original window, new window, who was told when. The argument later is whether anyone said the time had moved. A call with no line does not count.

Do not reshuffle the whole week here. Today is the board. Next week stays on the contract calendar unless someone pulls a visit forward.

If technicians set status themselves ("on the way", "done"), you need a rule for who may do that and when the office steps in. Otherwise "done" appears on the phone while the customer still waits, or the reverse. The flow may suggest status; the dispatcher or technician confirms. The board proposes a fit and drafts the update. No repair without the part, no new time without dispatcher acceptance.

## When field-service software is the better buy

Buy or use trade software when the small board is the bottleneck.

A simple measure of "worth it": how often a window moves each week, and how many "where are you" calls arrive with no log line. If that is rare, a template plus one board is enough. If it is daily, a clean draft path pays off even without a new ERP.

You do not need a new product when five technicians cover patches on a notepad, the pain is "the customer did not know we moved", and a message plus one board fixes that. You also do not need one when you already pay for a service module and the office still plans on paper. Plan in the module you have.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: four technicians, heating and controls, one dispatcher. Monday starts from confirmed maintenance. At 8:40 a breakdown: boiler in the same town as the morning's second visit, skill matches the technician already heading there. Common board on the van sheet. Window after that visit; customer agrees to "late morning". At 11:00 a different board on site. No repair today. Message: looked, part ordered, window proposed for Wednesday. Two customers whose windows slipped an hour get a draft after a glance at the board; the dispatcher sends once the new order is firm. The log reads: originally 10–12, new 11–13, text to both at 10:45. The morning report goes to the invoice path the firm already uses, without inventing a second billing logic here. Firm and times are invented.

## What to leave alone

Leave a planning flow when the dispatcher is also the only technician and the "board" is the van diary. A delay template may still help. Leave contract law in the contract: the board only places person and time. Automating match against an empty skill list assigns whoever is free.

Contract maintenance and same-day breakdowns share technicians, not the same priority rule. The contract calendar may propose. The day board may propose. Which breakdown moves a maintenance slot is for the dispatcher with both lists in view. One undifferentiated "AI plan" mixes contract and breakdown and produces calls nobody can explain.

## Short questions

### Does this replace GPS tracking?

No. Live map position is a product feature. Here, a confirmed window, a message when the slot moves, and a report afterwards are often enough.
