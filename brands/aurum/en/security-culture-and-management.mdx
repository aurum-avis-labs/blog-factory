## Security culture and management responsibility

Security culture in a Swiss SME means staff report suspicious mail because leadership does the same. Finance verifies changed IBAN details because owners and partners follow the same steps. Training is a management duty, not a hidden IT video. Tools help, but behaviour follows what leaders reward, visibly join, and do not exempt themselves from.

This article is for owners and board members in firms with about 20 to 250 staff. Measuring click rates is a different page. Whether training is legally required is in [mandatory training in Switzerland](/security/blog/is-security-awareness-training-mandatory-switzerland). When you introduce systems that touch many personal data records, plan the screen in [when a DPIA is required](/security/blog/when-a-dpia-is-required-in-switzerland) even though culture is not the same topic as privacy law.

## Management visibility

Attend at least part of the live awareness session. Before audit season, say in one sentence why reporting matters more than silence. When [someone clicks a real phishing link](/security/blog/clicked-a-phishing-link-at-work), run the documented process, not a public shaming. If leaders dismiss reports ("nothing happened"), others stop reporting within days.

## What weak culture costs in practice

CEO fraud and invoice change scams exploit urgency and authority. [BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/business-email-compromise-de) publishes rising reports on fraudulent payment instructions; those figures are external statistics, not your own measurement. Without a reporting culture, the second mail often reaches the same clerk who already paid the first, because nobody heard the rule leaders break themselves.

## HR, finance, and one reporting line

HR owns onboarding messages; finance owns IBAN rules; IT owns technical checks. Contradictory messages destroy culture at the first CEO fraud mail. Write down who reads the report mailbox, who responds within which timeframe, and who may stop payments. Without names on paper, the programme stops when someone is on holiday.

## EU contracts and supplier questionnaires

Swiss firms without a direct EU NIS2 statute still receive questions on training evidence, reporting paths, and leadership sponsorship. Answer with dates, topics, attendance, and one leadership sentence in the minutes. Do not overclaim certification. Art. 8 FADP requires appropriate technical and organisational measures; this is orientation, not legal advice.

## Records for the board, insurer, and auditor

Store PDFs or scans in one folder per year: invitation, attendee list, topic summary, follow-up notes, and copied insurer answers. When the board asks "is this enough", show topics across years and one metric you actually measure, such as reports per quarter. When the insurer asks for evidence, attach the same folder instead of rewriting each year. Do not promise results you do not measure.

## Tie training to real processes

Mention the same IBAN verification steps in training that finance uses in ERP. Mention the same report mailbox IT monitors. When processes change after training, send a one-page update rather than waiting a year. Inform staff before simulations; do not use awareness metrics for hidden performance scoring. Art. 26 ArGV 3 and Art. 328b OR frame monitoring; link SECO and FDPIC guidance when HR asks.

## Reports that show culture

Near-misses are valuable when leadership treats them seriously. Someone who reports CEO fraud mail before payment deserves the same recognition as someone who stops a misdirected file. If only "real" damage counts, others learn to ignore smaller signals. In the annual review, note two or three anonymised examples that went well, not only click metrics.

## Onboarding and role changes

New hires hear how to report mail in the first week, before they memorise the Wi-Fi password. When someone moves into finance or reception, repeat IBAN rules in writing. Anyone coming from a sector where reporting was "snitching" needs an explicit leadership sentence that reporting is welcome.

## Who should not prioritise this yet

A one-person firm without payment approvals needs less formality than a team with several approval tiers. Groups with their own CISO run different programmes. This text does not replace advice on simulations or dismissal.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Does one session satisfy ISO A 6.3 forever?</h3>
  <p>No. Auditors expect an ongoing programme with refresh and onboarding coverage. Keep dates and topics across years.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Must finance attend the same session as the warehouse?</h3>
  <p>Core reporting and MFA content should reach everyone. Payment modules can go deeper for anyone who can approve money movement.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>May I forward suspicious mail to my private address?</h3>
  <p>No. Use the official path. Private forwarding moves data and undermines reporting culture.</p>
</div>

When leadership treats reports seriously, fraud totals do not automatically fall. You gain time because IT and finance can intervene earlier.
