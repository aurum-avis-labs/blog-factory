<p class="blog-lede">You get budget approved for an MVP when finance and the board see a bounded experiment, not an open-ended build: one hypothesis, a fixed horizon, named deliverables, stop rules, and a plain list of what the spend will not prove.</p>

## Get budget approved for an MVP

Innovation leads are often blocked because the request looks like IT without a finish line. A validation sprint asks what you would need to see in twelve weeks to continue, pivot, or stop.

This is for innovation managers and corporate venture staff in Swiss firms who must fund a first market test without pretending the outcome is certain. Partner choice sits in [getting an MVP built for you](/blog/getting-an-mvp-built-for-you). Security and vendor gates are in internal approvals for an external MVP partner. Venture-building context is in corporate venture building partner.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>State one falsifiable hypothesis</h3><p>One sentence: which audience, which behaviour, by when. Not "digitise the service" but "operations managers in logistics SMEs will connect a data source and export a report within seven days of signup during a paid test."</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Cap time, scope, and spend</h3><p>Name the weeks, what ships in each block, and the maximum cash (and any equity or mixed terms) on the table. Open budgets read as product development, not learning.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Define Go, Pivot, and Stop before build</h3><p>Write what you will do if the numbers are strong, weak, or ambiguous. The board should know a Stop is a valid outcome that saves the next tranche.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Attach ownership and exit artefacts</h3><p>Who owns code, data, and designs at the end. What the company keeps if the experiment stops. Ideas as such are not protectable; execution and contracts are ([IGE guide on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)).</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Line up the gates you cannot skip</h3><p>Data protection, vendor review, and brand risk are parallel tracks, not surprises in week eight. Budget approval is not the same as security sign-off.</p></li>
</ol>

## What budget for an MVP does not prove

Twelve weeks can show whether strangers complete a core action and whether acquisition cost is plausible. It does not prove a five-year revenue line, regulatory clearance, or global support capacity. Your memo should state who receives deliverables and where IP lands ([Art. 17 URG](https://www.rentschpartner.ch/ict-law/schutz-von-software) for employee-created software).

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Approval to spend is not approval to process personal data, to bypass procurement, or to publish in app stores without review. Budget and compliance move on separate calendars.</p>
</aside>

## A made-up example: one page for the investment committee

A made-up example: an innovation lead in a Swiss industrial group, not a client. The division wants self-serve reporting for mid-market customers. One-page memo: twenty percent of trial accounts connect a CSV and export one chart within ten days during a paid LinkedIn test in DACH; twelve-week cap with mixed cash and equity; web app and landing page by week six; weekly funnel table. Stop if fewer than five percent connect data after four weeks of paid traffic. Pivot with one UI pass if connection is strong but export is weak. Go proposes a seed squad with the same metrics. Code stays with the company, NDA signed, IT security before week one. Finance approves because the worst case is a documented Stop.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Why not an internal hack week?</h3>
  <p>Hack weeks produce demos. Approvers fund measured acquisition when they need a decision. Name that decision in the memo.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>What if the test fails?</h3>
  <p>Stop with shared data and retained IP is a successful experiment. Hiding that outcome forces sponsors to dress the story up for leadership.</p>
</div>
