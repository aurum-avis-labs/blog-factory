## Home office security rules for staff: daily habits

Home office security rules for staff mean locking the screen when you leave the desk, not sharing the work laptop with family, maintaining the home router, and reporting odd login prompts the same way you would at the office. Swiss SMEs with hybrid teams often assume «they know IT»; a one-page checklist beats vague «be careful» mail. This is not a BYOD policy and not a hardware procurement guide. Paper personnel files at home belong under [personnel file contents and retention](/security/blog/personnel-file-contents-and-retention).

Report incidents like at the office: [internal incident reporting](/security/blog/internal-incident-reporting). Remote finance still needs [four-eyes payment controls](/security/blog/four-eyes-principle-for-payments). For changed payment instructions, see [invoice fraud with a changed IBAN](/security/blog/invoice-fraud-changed-iban).

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Lock the screen or close the laptop when making coffee, at the door, or in a shared kitchen</li>
  <li>Use company VPN for admin tasks and sensitive file shares when your firm provides one</li>
  <li>Keep work files in approved cloud storage, not an unsynced desktop folder</li>
  <li>Separate guest Wi-Fi from the work device where the router allows it</li>
  <li>Report lost devices and unexpected MFA prompts to the same hotline at once</li>
  <li>Do not release payments from public Wi-Fi without full controls and VPN</li>
</ul>

## Wi-Fi, router, and shared flats

Change default router passwords and install firmware updates when the ISP offers them. In shared households, guests on the same Wi-Fi can reach poorly secured printers or NAS boxes. Do not leave the work laptop on guest Wi-Fi without VPN.

A made-up example: a designer in a flat share disables network discovery on the work laptop, uses VPN only for file shares, and streams on a personal tablet. The router has a separate guest network; visitors get only that password.

## Screens, video calls, and shoulder surfing

Video may show whiteboards with customer names or salary notes. Use blur or a neutral wall for external calls. Do not read MFA codes aloud when someone is in the room. Shred printed payslips if HR posts them home. Angle the screen so passers-by cannot read it.

Customer data on the phone follows the same identity checks as in the office: [verifying identity on the phone](/security/blog/verifying-identity-on-the-phone).

## What these home office rules are not

They are not employee monitoring guidance under Swiss labour rules. They are not a «personal PC only» decision: that is BYOD with different risks and needs its own review. They do not replace backup and patch management on office servers. They assume the firm already defined access and storage locations.

  <span class="blog-kicker">Note</span>
  <p>Art. 8 FADP expects appropriate measures for home processing too. Document which systems may hold personal data and train staff on reporting loss or phishing.</p>
</aside>

## Devices, updates, and smart home

Prefer company laptops with automatic updates. Allow personal PCs only with written risk acceptance by leadership. Enable full-disk encryption; recovery keys sit with IT, not in plain text in a kitchen drawer.

IoT devices, children’s tablets, and smart speakers on the same network can open paths inward. Mute or move speakers during confidential calls. Secure home printers on Wi-Fi or avoid printing sensitive HR papers.

Do not plug unknown USB sticks into the work laptop; hand found sticks to IT. Use the company password manager, not notes on the monitor. IT support only via agreed remote tools; do not give passwords over the phone.

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>Children on the family PC</h3><p>Separate Windows account, no company profile on the same login. Games and downloads stay on the personal account.</p></li>
  <li><h3>Client visit at home</h3><p>Guest Wi-Fi for visitors, work device over VPN or a separate network, no customer files on the kitchen table overnight.</p></li>
  <li><h3>Exception «just this once on personal»</h3><p>Document in writing with an expiry date; exceptions without dates become the rule.</p></li>
</ul>

## Travel, theft, and public Wi-Fi

Never leave devices unattended in cars, trains, or co-working spaces; use a cable lock where theft is common. On theft, call police and IT immediately; remote wipe only works if configured beforehand.

Hotel and café Wi-Fi needs VPN for admin. Avoid banking or payment release without the same controls as at the office. Screenshots of error messages help IT when staff know how to capture them without passwords.

## Publishing and refreshing the checklist

HR and IT co-sign the one-pager, publish it on the intranet, and repeat it in onboarding. After a phishing wave, resending the MFA and reporting section is often enough. Quarterly three bullet points on the intranet: lock screen, VPN, reporting path.

Leadership should follow the same rules visibly, for example a locked screen on video calls without open customer data in the background.

Line managers remind in the first weeks after rollout until lock screen and reporting are automatic. Onboard remote staff on day one, not after the first incident. If someone works abroad long term, review access and storage location with counsel; this checklist assumes work in Switzerland or hybrid.

Paper at home follows the same clean-desk logic as the office: no customer files on the table overnight, lock HR papers. That overlaps the personnel file article when payslips are posted home.

## Security awareness for office and home

A live security awareness hour can walk through office and home scenarios (phishing, MFA, reporting). See [security awareness training](/security/security-awareness).

## Mirror office rules at home

Lock screen, reporting path, no foreign USB sticks: the same points as at head office, plus Wi-Fi and household extras. If MFA is mandatory in the office, it applies at home too. Payment release from home follows the four-eyes article, not a «faster from the sofa» exception.

Train new staff on the first remote day, not only at the yearly mandatory session. A screenshot of the reporting hotline on the intranet helps when the number is not memorised.

## Insurer questionnaires and customer audits

Insurers sometimes ask about home working and device hygiene. This checklist is not a copy-paste answer for every form; it helps you stay consistent internally. Do not claim controls you do not run. If the questionnaire covers remote access to production systems, IT belongs in the room, not only HR.

After an incident, check whether staff understood the rule or only signed it. A live session then helps more than a longer PDF. Tie that to the incident reporting article so nobody calls a different number «because I was home».

The rule stays short: work mail on the work laptop, no customer files on a private computer, and the same reporting path as at the head office.
