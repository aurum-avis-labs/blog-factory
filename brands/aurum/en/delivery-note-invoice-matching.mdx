## Match the delivery note to the order and the invoice

<p class="blog-lede">Three-way matching means each line on the purchase order, the goods receipt and the supplier invoice must agree before payment is released.</p>

Match supplier invoices to the purchase order and the delivery note before anyone approves payment. Quantity and price inside a tolerance you have written down can move to approval. A partial delivery with no open balance, a price outside the order and a missing receipt go to a named person. Paying the PDF because the supplier looks familiar is how you pay for goods that never reached the shelf.

This page is **purchasing**, not customer orders from a mailbox. That route is in [capturing orders from email](/prozess-check/blog/capture-email-orders). If accounting already captures the invoice but nobody compares it to the order, see [invoice capture](/prozess-check/blog/invoice-capture-what-software-misses). Releasing the bill after a successful match is in [invoice approval](/prozess-check/blog/invoice-approval-workflow). If you are the carrier and invoice from your own proof of delivery, you need a different workflow.

No product menu is described here. Whether your system already matches, you see in that system. If staff use the built-in match, stay there. The usual gap is the paper or PDF delivery note that never hits the order line.

## Three documents, one line

A line fits when the same article, unit and quantity appear on the order and on the receipt, and the invoice does not ask for more than was received, at a price the order allows. **Two-way match** (order against invoice) is all you have when nobody records a receipt. Then the invoice can mirror the order while the pallet is still in transit. **Three-way match** adds the delivery note or goods receipt.

  <li><strong>Receipt</strong><p>What was counted on arrival day, tied to that order.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Invoice</strong><p>What the supplier asks to be paid. Compared with both, not with memory.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Exception</strong><p>Outside tolerance, a person decides. Inside it, the line can go to approval.</p></li>

Post the receipt when goods arrive, not when the invoice lands three weeks later. By then the pallet is open and nobody counts it back. Several delivery notes against one order line add up until the open quantity is zero or someone cancels the remainder. If the delivery note is a PDF in a mailbox, the workflow's job is to read article, quantity and order number into the receipt. Unreadable fields stay blank and go to a person. A guessed quantity that "looks like" the order will match perfectly and still be wrong. A workflow that reads only the invoice, with no receipt, stays two-way match and the risk of paying without goods.

## Tolerances and partial deliveries

Write quantity and price tolerance down before you automate. Keep it small enough that you would not call purchasing for it. A few rappen of rounding is not a project. A price that moved without a new order is. There is no universal legal percentage for "close enough". If a contract states one, use it. Otherwise the person who may accept a difference sets the number.

If you received 80 of 100, the order stays open. An invoice for 80 can match. An invoice for 100 against a receipt of 80 does not get rounded up. It is an exception: release the 80, ask about the 20, or reject the invoice. Closing the order because the invoice says complete hides the shortage until stocktaking.

Freight, minimum-order fees or extra lines match only if the order or contract allows them. Otherwise they are an exception with the invoice attached, not a quiet booking to "other costs".

## Who decides the exception

Inside tolerance, the matched invoice enters the approval you already use. Outside it, someone in purchasing or the person who ordered sees quantities ordered, received and invoiced, plus the prices. They accept, accept in part, or send the invoice back. The workflow records that decision next to the invoice. A complaint to the supplier goes out only after that person sends it. The same exception on the same article deserves a note on the supplier, not a wider tolerance.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: an order for 100 boxes at CHF 4.20. Delivery note Tuesday: 80 boxes, posted that afternoon. Invoice the following Monday: 100 boxes at CHF 4.35. Both gaps sit outside tolerance. Purchasing pays nothing automatically, accepts 80 at the order price, asks for a corrected invoice and leaves 20 open. Approval sees only the agreed 80 after correction.

## Common questions

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Does every SME need three-way matching?</h3>
  <p>No. It is usually not a legal requirement. It pays off when you regularly buy goods in, write purchase orders, and could still pay invoices with no receipt recorded.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>What if the delivery note only exists as a scan in a mailbox?</h3>
  <p>Someone must put the quantity into the receipt on arrival day, by hand or with a workflow that reads article, quantity and order number. Without a receipt you only have two-way match and the risk of paying without goods.</p>
</div>
