## Smishing: parcel and delivery text scams

Smishing is phishing by SMS. A smishing parcel SMS tells you a package is held until you pay a small fee, customs charge or open a tracking link. On a work phone the message may reference a recent order your company placed, which makes it feel plausible. [BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/hjb-25-2-de) noted in the second half of 2025 that SMS blasters increasingly bypass carrier filters. «It came by text» is not proof of legitimacy.

Do not tap the link. Open the carrier or shop app you used for the real order, or ask purchasing. Report the SMS internally if your policy covers company mobiles. This article is not about QR stickers (quishing).

## What a fake parcel SMS looks like

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>If the text names Swiss Post or a courier</h3><p>Correct Swiss spelling and a Swiss dialling code are not enough. Check tracking numbers only in the app where you ordered.</p></li>
  <li><h3>If it arrived on a shared logistics phone</h3><p>Tell the team lead so purchasing is not fooled by copycat messages the same afternoon.</p></li>
  <li><h3>Small payment via link</h3><p>Post and major couriers rarely ask for customs or cash-on-delivery through a strange short link in SMS.</p></li>
</ul>

  <strong>Filters</strong>
  <p>BACS reported SMS bypass techniques in the second half of 2025. External observation, not your carrier contract.</p>
</div>

## What smishing is not

A real pickup code from a colleague on Teams. Two-factor codes you requested yourself (never share those). Marketing SMS you opted into. A genuine status mail in the shop account opened in the browser, without using the SMS link.

General phishing signs: [how to spot phishing emails](/security/blog/how-to-spot-phishing-emails).

## What to do instead of clicking

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Ignore the link</h3><p>No payment and no login through the SMS.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Verify the shipment</h3><p>Use the order portal or the Post or courier app you already trust.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Report internally</h3><p>Screenshot without tapping the link to IT or the security mailbox if policy requires it.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Delete the thread</h3><p>Remove it after reporting so nobody taps later by mistake.</p></li>
</ol>

<aside class="blog-callout">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Note</span>
  <p>Blocking all foreign SMS is rarely practical for firms that trade abroad. Training plus a reporting path beats a blanket block.</p>
</aside>

## Work phones and mixed use

Staff who combine personal and business SMS on one device blur boundaries. State in acceptable use whether parcel texts on a company phone must be reported. Purchasing can list open shipments so logistics is not guessing. Fraudsters also use messengers; privacy on chat tools is a neighbour topic in [WhatsApp for business and data protection](/security/blog/whatsapp-for-business-and-data-protection), not parcel logistics.

## Reporting path without a CISO

Most firms between twenty and two hundred fifty employees rely on an external IT partner. Write the partner into the path: they need the SMS text or a screenshot without a tap, not only a verbal description. Voluntary reports to [BACS](https://www.bacs.admin.ch) are possible when you suspect fraud; they do not replace internal documentation.

If purchasing uses a central account, one reported SMS often saves the whole warehouse from clicking the same afternoon.

## If someone already clicked

Block the card, change passwords for the affected account, inform IT immediately. No blame culture: reports are welcome. Email phishing detail is in [how to spot phishing emails](/security/blog/how-to-spot-phishing-emails); AI-generated text in [spotting AI-generated phishing](/security/blog/spotting-ai-generated-phishing).

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Must every parcel SMS be reported?</h3>
  <p>No. Report suspicious SMS internally per policy. Externally only when you confirm fraud and BACS asks for it.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Does this cover two-factor SMS?</h3>
  <p>Codes you requested yourself are different. Still never forward codes; fraudsters combine smishing with phone calls.</p>
</div>

## Swiss Post and couriers in brief

Swiss Post and major couriers notify you in apps or customer accounts, not through unknown short links. Import customs run through official portals; an SMS asking for «CHF 1.50» on a strange domain rarely matches the real process. If logistics uses several carriers, a short intranet note «this is what our real alert looks like» beats a generic warning.
