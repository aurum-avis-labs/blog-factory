## Transferring personal data to the United States from Switzerland

Transferring personal data to the United States happens whenever a Swiss company uses US-hosted software, US support staff can read mailboxes, or a backup lands in an American data centre. Under the [Swiss FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de), disclosure abroad is only permitted if the Federal Council has decided adequate protection exists, appropriate safeguards apply, or a narrow legal exception applies ([Art. 16 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). For many SMEs the practical question is whether the vendor participates in the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and whether your contract matches reality. Statutory cyber reporting to BACS usually does not apply to a standard office, see [cyberattack reporting obligation Switzerland](/security/blog/cyberattack-reporting-obligation-switzerland).

This article is for owners and finance leads in Swiss firms with about 20 to 250 employees who run Microsoft 365, US CRM tools, or marketing platforms. It is orientation, not legal advice. Certification under the framework is a property of the US recipient, not something you claim about your own company.

## When data actually leaves Switzerland

A Swiss tenant in a cloud product may still be processed in the US if the provider's architecture, support, or disaster recovery routes traffic there. Employee names in Slack, customer mail in Google Workspace, applicant CVs in a US ATS, and newsletter lists in Mailchimp are all transfers if the recipient organisation or its subprocessors are subject to US law. "We only have an office in Zurich" does not stop transfer when the contract says processing may occur in the United States.

Domestic hosting in Switzerland with a US parent company can still involve group-wide access from abroad. Read the processing agreement and subprocessor list, not only the marketing map on the website.

## Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework in plain terms

The Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework is a mechanism the Swiss authorities recognise for certain US organisations that self-certify and commit to defined principles. It applies to certified US companies as recipients, not to your Swiss firm as sender. Before you tell a customer "our supplier is covered", check the current certification list published by the US Department of Commerce and any FDPIC guidance. A vendor's blog post is not proof.

If the recipient is not certified, you need another recognised safeguard such as standard contractual clauses adapted for Switzerland, or you must assess whether an exception in law applies. That assessment belongs with counsel for sensitive health, legal, or employee data.

## What this is not

It is not the same as signing a data processing agreement under Art. 9 FADP. You need both where a US processor handles data on your behalf. It is not GDPR "adequacy" for the EU; Swiss rules are separate even when contract text looks similar. It does not remove your duty to secure accounts: shared passwords and missing MFA still cause incidents regardless of transfer paperwork.

## Who can worry less for now

A firm that processes personal data only on servers physically operated in Switzerland, with no US subprocessors in the chain, has a simpler map. That is rare once mail and collaboration are cloud-based. Micro-businesses with only paper files and a local accountant may have few transfers until they adopt SaaS tools.

Freelancer tools on US servers for one-off projects can still be a transfer even when the main contract says "Switzerland only". List those exceptions in the inventory instead of running them in the shadows.

The opposite applies to health data, legal mandates, or large-scale employee analytics to US tools: if you plan such transfers, or your customer contract forbids US processing, stop and take counsel.

## GDPR customers and contracts

If you process people in the EU, customers often expect GDPR-style clauses as well as Swiss rules. That does not remove your own FADP assessment for US transfers. Two contract frameworks can apply in parallel; the technical reality in your tools should match both where agreed.

## What to do first

Inventory which tools hold personal data and where the vendor says processing occurs. For each US-facing service, note processing agreement, subprocessors, and whether the recipient appears on the framework list if they claim it. Ask your IT partner how admin support is delivered. Fill in customer questionnaires honestly: "we rely on vendor X's certification" only after you verified it.

After a near-miss on mail fraud, technical hygiene matters as much as transfer law.
