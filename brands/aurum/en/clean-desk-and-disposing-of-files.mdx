## Clean desk and disposing of files: rules that work the same day

Clean desk and disposing of files properly protect personal data that still lives on paper in Swiss offices: contracts on printers, CVs in meeting rooms, salary notes on sticky pads. The FADP expects appropriate organisational measures ([Art. 8 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)), which includes not leaving identifiable data where visitors or cleaners can read it. When retention ends, paper needs the same care as server folders ([Art. 6 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). Digital sharing is covered in [sharing files that contain personal data](/security/blog/sharing-files-that-contain-personal-data). This checklist is for owners and office managers in firms of about 20 to 250 people.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Clear desk before external meetings in your own rooms</li>
  <li>Lock cabinets that hold HR or customer files overnight</li>
  <li>Collect print jobs promptly; disable open trays where possible</li>
  <li>Use cross-cut shredding or a bonded disposal service for personal data</li>
  <li>Do not put intact dossiers in public recycling</li>
</ul>

## What clean desk means in your firm

Clean desk does not mean sterile desks with nothing on them. It means no unattended personal data in shared or public view: no applicant folders in the cafeteria, no patient lists at reception after hours, no payroll printouts in the kitchen. Remote work adds home risk; company policy can ask staff to store paper in a lockable place at home when they must print.

<aside class="blog-callout">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Note</span>
  <p>Post-it notes with passwords or IBANs are personal data and security risks. Treat them like documents: destroy when done, do not photograph into private phones.</p>
</aside>

## Disposing of files when retention ends

When retention for applications or customer files ends, deletion must include paper, not only folders on the server. Shredders should meet cross-cut standards for everyday HR and finance; particle cut helps when dossiers are thick or audit asks for it. Large archives may need a disposal firm with a handover receipt. Keep a simple log: date, category (e.g. "applications 2023"), method (shred or vendor), initials.

## Printers, scanners, and shared devices

Shared printers are a weak point. Use secure release where available, route HR jobs to a restricted device, and train staff to collect jobs before leaving the room. Scanners that e-mail to a group inbox need the same access rules as cloud folders. Fax trays, if you still have them, belong in the same control as the printer.

## Buy and maintain shredders

A broken shredder pushes paper into drawers. Check oil capacity, bin volume, and whether staples jam the machine. One device per floor beats a central bin nobody reaches. Label the shred bin clearly: confidential paper only, not general waste.

## A made-up example: the open interview stack

A made-up example: a 45-person agency in Basel leaves printed CVs in a glass meeting room over lunch. A candidate for another role reads names on the top sheet. Complaint follows. The agency introduces lockable interview trays, shreds rejects within the week, and adds a five-line clean-desk rule to visitor briefings. No new software was required.

## Visitors, cleaners, and night staff

Brief visitors that they may not photograph whiteboards or desks. Cleaners and security should not need access to unlocked HR cabinets; if keys are shared, log who holds them. Night cleaning in open offices is a common moment for exposed CVs; either secure rooms or adopt end-of-day desk checks for teams that handle applications.

## Home office paper

When staff print contracts at home, define whether company rules require a lockable drawer and destruction through the same shredder standard when the project ends. Company-owned printers shipped to homes should return or be wiped when employment ends. Private printers without a shredder are a risk: bring paper to the office for destruction or use a confidential disposal service.

## Applications and customer files

Paper applications follow the same deadlines as digital folders. Customer files at reception belong in locked storage, not on the counter during a break. When clients send documents by post, name who receives them and when they move into the cabinet.

## Labels and archive boxes

Mark archive boxes with year and category, not only "old stuff". When boxes return from storage, confirm they match the destruction date in your retention table before anyone opens them for curiosity. The same label on the digital folder helps HR and facilities align.

## Short training for reception and HR

Reception sees deliveries and visitors daily; HR sees applications. Both need the same short rule on visible names and shredding. A shared 15-minute slot is often enough when the checklist hangs at the printer or reception desk and is refreshed yearly.

## End-of-week routine

Friday ten minutes: clear meeting rooms, collect USB sticks from the loan pool, empty the printer tray. Reception adds a tick box to the closing checklist. When external guests arrive on Monday, the Friday pass often beats a new policy document.

## Questionnaires and audits

Customer questionnaires and insurer forms sometimes ask about clean desk or file destruction. You do not need to quote every ISO clause. Describe what you actually do: locked cabinets, HR shredding, annual disposal tied to your retention table. If an audit finds paper that was not on the digital deletion list, the story looks inconsistent. The same date for digital and paper is the simplest proof.

## Data protection training

A live session covers paper and digital habits together so HR and reception share one story, in one to two hours. See [data protection awareness training](/security/datenschutz).

Paper with personal data belongs in the shred bin, not in the recycling container next to the printer.
