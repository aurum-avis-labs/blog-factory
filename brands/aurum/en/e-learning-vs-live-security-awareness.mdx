## E-learning vs live security awareness training

**E-learning vs live security awareness training** is not a purity contest. Swiss SMEs with 20 to 250 staff usually need a live session at least once so finance and reception can ask about real payment rules. After that, e-learning or short refreshers work when headcount grows or people join mid-year. E-learning scales and leaves a completion log. Live training surfaces questions you did not know to put in slides.

This comparison is for owners, HR and the person coordinating IT who must choose a format before an audit or insurance renewal. Phishing simulations are a separate decision ([allowed in Switzerland](/security/blog/phishing-simulations-allowed-switzerland)). Which topics belong in either format is in [security awareness training topics](/security/blog/security-awareness-training-topics).

## What each format does well

**E-learning modules** deliver the same baseline to many people, on their own schedule, with a timestamp when they finish. That helps distributed teams, night shifts, and proof that "everyone clicked through" before year-end.

**Live training** (remote or in the room) lets people ask "what do we do when the CEO mail arrives on Friday afternoon?" and forces alignment on the internal reporting path. That matters after you define [internal incident reporting](/security/blog/internal-incident-reporting) on paper but nobody trusts it yet.

## Side-by-side for a typical SME

| Question | E-learning | Live session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proof of completion | Strong: system log | Strong if you keep sign-in sheet or calendar invite list |
| Payment fraud and CEO requests | Generic scenarios | Can use your IBAN and approval rules |
| Reporting culture | States the address | Can practise where to click in Outlook or Teams |
| Cost at 30–50 staff | Often lower per head if platform already paid | Fixed session cost, travel included for on-site providers |
| Language mix | Easy to offer DE and EN modules | Aurum runs live in German or English in one session block |
| Refresh after an incident | Quick to redeploy one module | Better for leadership Q and A |

Neither row wins every column. Many firms do live first, e-learning for newcomers and annual refresh.

## When e-learning alone is weak

If your last incident was invoice fraud or a clicked link in finance, generic modules rarely cover your four-eyes process. If staff distrust HR or management, clicking through videos does not fix reporting fear.

Auditors often accept e-learning logs, but ISO/IEC 27001:2022 control A 6.3 asks for an ongoing programme with evidence of effectiveness, not only attendance. Interviews still reveal whether people know whom to call.

## When live alone is weak

If you hire every month, scheduling a full room each time delays onboarding. If half the team works abroad on client sites, a single on-site day misses them unless you repeat remotely.

Live without follow-up fades. A short module three months later can repeat passwords and reporting without another half-day off.

## Combining without checkbox training

A sensible sequence: live session with role examples, document topics and attendees, assign one e-learning refresh six months later, measure reporting, repeat live every one to two years or after a serious incident.

Do not assign modules nobody reads and call the year done. Management should say why training exists, same as for live.

## Cost shape without platform lock-in

E-learning pricing is often per seat per year. Live pricing is often per session by headcount band. Compare total cost over three years including staff time, not licence price alone. Two hours in a room plus one hour of prep is visible cost. Thirty minutes clicking modules while multitasking is hidden cost with little benefit.

Below fifteen staff with one shared calendar, a live remote session plus a written one-page reminder may beat a three-year LMS contract. If nobody owns follow-up, fix ownership before buying seats.

## Vendor questions worth asking

For e-learning: where data is hosted, whether content mentions Swiss payment fraud, how exports look for auditors, whether you can add your reporting address on the completion page. For live: what happens in prep, session length, whether follow-up is included, whether mapping to ISO A 6.3 is documented on request. Confirm languages and on-site travel for Switzerland before you compare quotes.

## Limits of both formats

Neither replaces MFA, backups or payment controls. Neither makes you "compliant" as a label. They support organisational measures under the FADP and customer contracts. Simulations are not e-learning. If you run them, inform staff first and do not use individual clicks in HR reviews.

Remote live with chat open is still live, not e-learning. On-site in Switzerland suits firms that want reception and finance in one room for payment exercises. Ask any provider whether travel is included for on-site delivery in Switzerland. Aurum includes travel for on-site sessions in Switzerland as stated on the service page checked 2026-09-30.

Mixed German and English teams can split questions in one bilingual facilitator session or run two shorter blocks. For e-learning with language toggles, verify content is equivalent, not a shorter English stub.

E-learning dashboards show who finished. They do not show whether finance will phone before paying a new IBAN. Pair completion data with internal report counts and short interviews after incidents.

Hosted platforms process employee names and completion data. Check where data is stored and whether sub-processors fit your client contracts. Live training with a Swiss provider may keep fewer artefacts in a cloud gradebook, but you still keep your own attendance list.

Revisit the mix after a fraud attempt, after doubling headcount, or when audit feedback says people know policy but not practice. Do not switch format only because a salesperson offered a discount on seats.

## Proof for audits and insurers

Keep it simple: calendar invite or sign-in sheet, slide title or one-page summary, facilitator name, language. Store ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A 6.3 mapping separately from attendance. The session does not certify the company.

Most SMEs use an external facilitator while IT owns mail rules and MFA. Agree who reminds people, who collects attendance, and who answers the reporting mailbox after the session.
