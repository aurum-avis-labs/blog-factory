## Building a SaaS inside an SME

A Swiss SME often already has the raw material: repeat questions from customers, a spreadsheet that three people maintain, or a process your team does better than anyone else in the region. Turning that into subscription software is tempting because the know-how is real. The failure mode is just as real: you ship a portal that only your existing clients tolerate, call it a product, and discover that strangers will not sign up or pay without the relationship you already have.

This is for owners and commercial leads in established firms who want a market test without betting the whole company on a twelve-month IT programme. ## Separate the product from the day job

The core business pays the bills. A SaaS experiment needs protected time, a named owner, and a budget line that does not disappear the first busy month. If every feature request from existing customers lands in the same backlog as the "new product", you will build a customised client tool and label it scalable.

Write one sentence for what the SaaS must prove. Examples: strangers complete onboarding without a sales call, or at least five firms outside your client list pay for month two. If you cannot name that sentence, you are still exploring the idea, not running a product test.

## Ownership, contractors, and what you cannot lock in

Ideas as such cannot be protected. What you can control is who owns the code, the data, and the brand when the experiment ends.

Software written by your employees in the course of employment belongs to the employer under Swiss copyright practice for employee works ([Art. 17 URG](https://www.rentschpartner.ch/ict-law/schutz-von-software), summarised by Rentsch Partner). That helps inside the firm, but it does not replace clear contracts when freelancers or agencies write the first version. External authors keep copyright until you have a written assignment. Before you show the prototype to customers or investors, check repository access, cloud accounts, and assignment clauses, not only who paid the invoice.

A separate GmbH is sometimes used to ring-fence liability and cap table clarity. Formation requires at least CHF 20'000 share capital, fully paid in ([kmu.admin.ch on the GmbH](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/de/gmbh-haftung-stammkapital-gruendung)). It is a governance choice, not proof that the product works. Many teams run the first behavioural test under the existing company and only spin out when repeat revenue or external investment forces cleaner boundaries.

## What a first build should and should not prove

A pilot can show whether the packaged job resonates outside your network, where people stall in onboarding, and whether anyone returns without a reminder from your sales desk. It cannot show a durable acquisition channel, margin at scale, or that the SME can operate two businesses without strain.

Internal ventures follow the same rule. A restaurant-supply pilot built on an existing platform may learn most from conversations and events in the region, not from ads alone. Warm interest from people who already trust the brand is useful signal. It is not the same as proving that cold traffic converts.

## Governance without a corporate programme

You do not need a full stage-gate programme on day one. You do need a stop rule. If after a defined period nobody outside the client list completes the core path, pause before adding modules. If IT security or procurement must approve every tool, involve them early with a narrow data map, not a slide deck that promises "AI everywhere".

[Stage-gate vs lean startup](/blog/stage-gate-vs-lean-startup) compares two rhythms for larger organisations. A twenty-person firm can run a lighter version: one hypothesis, one build slice, one readout to the partners or the board.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is our internal tool already a SaaS?

Not if only your staff and captive clients use it, if you customise every account by hand, or if switching off the tool would not upset anyone who found you on the open market. SaaS implies a repeatable offer strangers can adopt with limited hand-holding.

### Should we build inside the SME or found a new company first?

Many teams validate under the existing legal entity to reduce cost and friction, then separate when revenue, liability, or investors require it. The wrong order is founding a company before anyone outside your network has used the product.
