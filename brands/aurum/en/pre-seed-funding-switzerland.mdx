<p class="blog-lede">Pre-seed funding in Switzerland is the first outside capital that lets you test a product in the market before a formal seed round. It usually comes from your own savings, friends and family, business angels, or non-dilutive programmes, not from a typical venture fund term sheet.</p>

## Pre-seed funding in Switzerland

Pre-seed sits after the idea and before institutional seed. In Switzerland there is no single legal label and no published standard cheque size for that stage. Angels and syndicates write their own tickets. Public programmes cap support in francs and ask for milestones, not a slide about total addressable market alone.

This page is for founders in Switzerland with an idea or early prototype, often without a technical co-founder, who wonder whether to raise now, from whom, and what evidence to gather first. It is not a pitch template. For how validation data changes a later round, see [why validated MVPs raise more money](/blog/validated-mvp-fundraising). For grants and coaching in one map, see [Swiss startup funding programmes](/blog/swiss-startup-funding-programmes) and [Innosuisse start-up coaching](/blog/innosuisse-start-up-coaching).

## What "pre-seed" means here

Investors use the word loosely. Some mean the first tranche to ship software and run ads. Others mean the round before a seed fund leads at a priced round. What matters for you is the question each source asks: can this team learn fast enough with the money, and what will they measure?

Incorporation is a separate decision. A GmbH needs at least CHF 20'000 share capital, fully paid in at formation ([kmu.admin.ch](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/de/gmbh-haftung-stammkapital-gruendung)). Many founders validate in a simpler structure first and incorporate when contracts or investors require it. That choice is for your adviser and your bank, not for this article.

## What angels and early backers look for

Business angels in Switzerland often enter through networks, alumni groups, or a warm introduction. They rarely fund a concept with no customer contact. A credible pre-seed conversation usually includes:

- A named problem and who feels it weekly, in their words where possible
- A founder team that can ship or supervise shipping, or a plan to hire that skill
- Something measurable already moving: interviews logged, waitlist with context, pilot users, or paid tests with real budgets
- Honesty about what you do not know yet, including distribution

They are buying time to reduce demand risk, not proof that you will be a unicorn. A polished deck without behaviour data is a common reason conversations stop after the first meeting.

## Public programmes are not a substitute for angels

Non-dilutive support can pay for coaching, prototypes, or travel to programmes without giving up equity on day one. [Innosuisse Initial Coaching](https://www.innosuisse.admin.ch/de/initial-coaching-fur-start-up) offers a voucher of up to CHF 10'000 for coaching over up to 12 months for start-ups up to five years old (ten in life sciences) with an innovative, science-based idea. It is coaching money, not a licence to skip customer discovery.

[Venture Kick](https://www.venturekick.ch/) runs a competitive track with up to CHF 150'000 across stages plus mentoring. The split between stages is published on their site; check it before you plan a budget. Winning is selective. Losing does not mean the idea is dead. It often means the story or the team fit for that jury was not ready yet.

## What pre-seed money does not prove

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Closing a pre-seed round or winning a grant does not show product-market fit, repeatable acquisition, or that strangers will pay without your network. It shows someone believed your next experiment was worth funding.</p>
</aside>

Grants reward eligibility and jury fit. Angel money rewards trust in the team and the narrative. Neither replaces cohorts of users you did not recruit yourself. If the only metric is "we raised", the next round will still ask for retention, activation, or revenue.

## How this connects to an MVP investors can underwrite

Angels and seed funds increasingly treat technology risk as manageable and demand risk as open. That shift is why a deployed product with analytics matters more than another mockup. The argument is developed in [why validated MVPs raise more money](/blog/validated-mvp-fundraising); this page does not repeat that case.

If your pre-seed plan is "build for a year, then fundraise", you are likely underwriting the wrong risk. If the plan is "ship something narrow, measure drop-off and return visits, then raise with those charts", you are aligned with how Swiss conversations tend to go.
