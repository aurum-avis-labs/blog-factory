## After the Process Check: what happens next

After the Process Check you receive a short written summary. Then nothing happens until you decide. If you want the workflow built, the next step is a fixed-price package: scope and price in writing, access, examples and one contact person from your side, a tested build, and then a monthly running fee that you can cancel. The hour itself commits you to nothing.

This article is for owners and for office and ops leads in a Swiss SME who have had the hour, or are about to book it, and want to see the path from case to running workflow. What happens inside the 60 minutes is described in [what the Process Check is](/prozess-check/blog/what-the-process-check-is). This article starts where that one stops.

## What the written summary contains

The summary is the case on paper, short enough to forward. Four questions sit at its centre: where to start, whether it pays off, what implementation would cost, and roughly how long it would take.

"Where to start" names the task and the exact step someone does by hand today. "Whether it pays off" rests on what you said in the hour: how often the task comes up, how long each run takes, who needs the result. The cost is a price range with a rough duration. A fixed price comes later.

The business partner who was not on the call, the board, the person who does the task every week: each of them should understand what is proposed without asking you. If someone raises a question that the summary does not answer, note it. It belongs in the next step, before a price is fixed.

## Your decision after reading

The hour was free. That is all you owe.

Four paths are common:

- **Have it built by us.** The fixed price is for the build. Nothing comes off it for the Check, because the Check is free.
- **Build it in-house or with someone else.** The summary describes your task and where automation pays off. Your IT partner or a colleague can work from it.
- **Wait.** The task is real, but this quarter is full, or the owner is about to change roles. The case does not expire.
- **Stop.** The numbers showed the task is too rare or too short to be worth a build.

If the hour found nothing worth automating, that is a result. If a case exists and you decide not to build, you simply stop there.

## From price range to fixed price

The fixed price comes after the Check, in writing, for the scope you agree. You are not billed by the hour, and testing is always included. The package sizes below are guide prices from the offer page. The number that counts is the written fixed price.

| Package | What it covers | Effort | Guide price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | One clearly bounded workflow with few systems | 2–3 days | CHF 3'200–4'800 |
| Medium | Several steps or systems, for example a form, a check, an entry and a notification | 7 days | about CHF 14'000 |
| Large | A complex workflow, or several connected ones, with documentation and handover to your team | 10 days | about CHF 20'000 |
| Max | Several linked workflows with many interfaces | from 12 days | from CHF 25'000 |

Effort is counted in working days. It describes the size of the job and promises no delivery date.

The number of systems the workflow touches matters most. One export into one sheet is a different size from a form, a check against a second system, an entry and an email to someone. If the summary names one system, expect the lower bands. If it names four, do not budget two days.

Before you sign, check that the written scope states four things: where the workflow starts, where it ends, which systems it touches and what stays manual. If one is missing, ask. A fixed price on a vague scope only moves the argument to the end of the project.

## What you provide for the build

Three things. The build waits until they are in place.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Access to the applications the workflow touches, at the level the workflow needs</li>
  <li>Test data, or real examples with names and amounts blacked out or made up</li>
  <li>One contact person who knows the task and has time to answer questions</li>
</ul>

Without access, nothing can be tested. What your software offers, whether an API, an export or a shared mailbox, is one of the things the Check clarifies. The build uses what was found there.

Ten real invoices, reports or orders from last month show the exceptions that nobody mentions in a meeting: the supplier who sends two PDFs, the export with a missing column. With names and amounts blacked out, they are enough to develop and test against. Real data flows only once the workflow runs live in your systems.

The contact person is whoever does the task today, which is not necessarily the manager who booked the hour. They know which field is always empty and which customer is the exception. During the build they answer questions, and at the end they check the results.

## Tests and handover

Every package includes tests. Have the contact person run the workflow on examples they know well and compare the output with what they would have done by hand. A difference found now is cheaper to fix than one found in the second week of live use.

In the Large package, documentation and handover to your team are part of the scope. That matters when your own IT or a colleague is to operate or change the workflow later. If you want to run a smaller workflow yourself, say so before the fixed price is set, so that it ends up in the written scope.

## Running costs: hosting, AI usage, cancellation

A workflow that runs needs a place to run and someone who looks after it. Robert Schlittler, co-founder, develops the automations and runs them afterwards.

Hosting and running cost CHF 50–150 a month, depending on the solution. AI service costs come on top, billed by usage, and you pay them. You can cancel the running costs at any time.

Budget that line like a software subscription. A workflow that runs every day and sends each document to an AI service uses more than one that fills a table once a week. Clarify which of the two yours is when the fixed price is discussed, before the first invoice arrives.

## Who should not go further after the Check

Some cases should stop at the summary, and that is a useful result.

- **The task has no owner.** Without someone to check the results, the build has to guess.
- **It is rare.** Four times a year seldom earns back a fixed price.
- **The input changes shape every week.** Moving columns or redesigned forms: stabilise the input first.
- **The software has no interface and no usable export.** Then the gap is the software.
- **You hope the build will decide which process matters.** That decision comes before the build.
- **You want a new product or an MVP.** That is a different offer.
