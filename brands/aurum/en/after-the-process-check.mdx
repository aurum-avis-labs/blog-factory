## After the Process Check: what happens next

After the Process Check you get a short written summary, and then nothing happens until you decide. If you want the workflow built, the next step is a fixed-price package: scope and price confirmed in writing, access, examples and one contact person from your side, a tested build, and then a monthly running fee you can cancel. The hour itself commits you to nothing.

This page is for owners, office and ops leads in a Swiss SME who have had the hour, or are about to book it, and want to see the path from case to running workflow. What happens inside the 60 minutes is in [what the Process Check is](/blog/what-the-process-check-is). This article starts where that one stops.

## What the written summary contains

The summary is the case on paper, short enough to forward. It answers four questions: where to start, whether it pays off, what implementation would cost, and roughly how long it would take.

"Where to start" names the task and the exact step someone does by hand today. "Whether it pays off" rests on what you said in the hour: how often the task comes up, how long it takes each time, and who needs the result. The cost is a price range and a rough duration. It is not yet a fixed price.

It is written to be passed on internally. The partner who was not on the call, the board, the person who does the task every week: each of them should understand what is proposed without asking you. If someone raises a question the summary does not answer, write it down. It belongs in the next step, before a price is fixed.

## The decision: build, wait or stop

The hour commits you to nothing. You paid CHF 99 for it, and that is all you owe.

After reading the summary you have four honest options:

- **Have it built by us.** The CHF 99 is then credited against the fixed price.
- **Build it in-house or with someone else.** The summary describes your task and where it pays off. Your IT partner or a colleague can work from it.
- **Wait.** The task is real, but this quarter is full, or the person who owns it is about to change roles. The case does not expire.
- **Stop.** The numbers showed the task is too rare or too short to be worth a build.

If the hour found nothing worth automating, you get the full fee back through Stripe. That applies only if the appointment took place. A case that exists, but that you decide not to build, does not trigger a refund. The refund is tied to a missing case, not to your decision.

## From price range to fixed price

The fixed price comes after the Check, in writing, for the scope you agree. You are not billed by the hour, and testing is always included. The package sizes below are guide prices from the offer page. The written fixed price is the number that counts.

| Package | What it covers | Effort | Guide price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | One clearly bounded workflow with few systems | 2–3 days | CHF 3'200–4'800 |
| Medium | Several steps or systems, for example a form, a check, an entry in the right system and a notification | 7 days | about CHF 14'000 |
| Large | A complex workflow, or several connected ones, with documentation and handover to your team | 10 days | about CHF 20'000 |
| Max | Several linked workflows with many interfaces | from 12 days | from CHF 25'000 |

Effort is counted in working days. It is the size of the job, not a delivery date.

Which size fits follows from the case, and the number of systems the workflow touches matters most. A workflow that reads one export and fills one sheet is a different job from one that reads a form, checks it against a second system, writes an entry and tells someone. If the summary names one system, expect the lower bands. If it names four, do not budget two days.

Before you sign, check that the written scope states four things: where the workflow starts, where it ends, which systems it touches, and what stays manual. If one of them is missing, ask. A fixed price on a vague scope only moves the argument to the end of the project.

## What you provide for the build

Three things, and the build waits until they are there.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Access to the programs the workflow touches, at the level the workflow needs</li>
  <li>Test data, or real examples with names and amounts blacked out or made up</li>
  <li>One contact person who knows the task and has time to answer questions</li>
</ul>

Access comes first, because nothing can be tested without it. What your program offers, an API, an export or a shared mailbox, is one of the things the Check clarifies. The build uses what was found there.

Examples matter more than descriptions. Ten real invoices, reports or orders from last month show the exceptions nobody mentions in a meeting: the supplier who sends two PDFs, the export with a column missing. With names and amounts blacked out, they are enough to develop and test against. Real data only flows once the workflow runs live in your systems.

The contact person is the one who does the task today, not necessarily the manager who booked the hour. They know which field is always empty and which customer is the exception. During the build they answer questions. At the end they check the results.

## Tests and handover

Every package includes tests. Use them as your acceptance step. Have the contact person run the workflow on examples they know well and compare the output with what they would have done by hand. A difference found now is cheaper to fix than one found in the second week of live use.

Handover depends on who runs the workflow afterwards. In the Large package, documentation and handover to your team are part of the scope. That matters when your own IT or a colleague should be able to operate or change the workflow later. If you want to run a smaller workflow yourself, say so before the fixed price is set, so that it ends up in the written scope.

## Once it runs: hosting, AI costs, cancellation

A workflow that runs needs a place to run and someone who looks after it. Robert Schlittler, co-founder, develops the automations himself and runs them afterwards.

Hosting and running cost CHF 50–150 a month, depending on the solution. AI service costs come on top, by usage, and you pay them. You can cancel the running costs at any time.

Budget that line like a software subscription. A workflow that runs every day and sends each document to an AI service uses more than one that runs once a week on a table. Ask which of the two yours is when the fixed price is discussed, not after the first invoice.

## Who should not go further after the Check

Some cases should stop at the summary. That is a useful result, not a failed hour.

- **The task has no owner.** If nobody checks results or answers questions during the build, the build has to guess.
- **It is rare.** A task that comes up four times a year seldom earns back a fixed price, however annoying it is.
- **The input changes shape every week.** Columns that move, forms that are redesigned every month: make the input stable first.
- **The program has no interface and no usable export.** Then the gap is the program, not the workflow.
- **You hope the build will decide which process matters.** It will not. That decision comes before the build.
- **You want a new product or an MVP.** That is a different offer, not this path.
