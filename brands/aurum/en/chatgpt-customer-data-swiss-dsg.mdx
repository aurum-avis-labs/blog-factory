The question in the managing office is usually the same: may people paste client or customer data into ChatGPT? The short answer: the Swiss Data Protection Act still applies. There is no extra AI statute that replaces it. This article is not legal advice. It is the traffic light a small Swiss office needs before someone pastes the next contract.

The [FDPIC](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/en/ai-and-data-protection) is clear: the DPA is technology-neutral. Whoever processes personal data with an AI tool remains responsible. A foreign vendor does not change that.

## Free, Plus, and the company account

On ChatGPT Free and Plus, OpenAI may use content to train models unless the user opts out. Customer files, named quotes, sick notes or payroll lists do not belong there.

Business, Team and Enterprise plans plus the API: OpenAI says organisational data is not used for training by default, and a data processing addendum exists. That does not green-light especially sensitive data. It is the difference between "a private account on a phone" and "the firm has a contract and an allow-list".

The company stays responsible. A subscription is not an internal rule.

## Traffic light: three baskets

**Green, public.** Press copy, Wikipedia-grade facts, figures already on the website. A chat is usually fine here, as long as nobody mixes in personal data.

**Amber, internal, no people.** An internal process note with no names. An anonymised sample invoice. Still: not the private Free chat if the file is a trade secret.

**Red, personal data.** Name, address, AHV number, salary, mandate file, health data, a job application. Not in Free/Plus. Even in a business chat only if purpose, contract and a human check are clear. Especially sensitive data does not belong in a language model "just this once".

If the basket is unclear: treat it as red.

## A rule beats a ban

A blanket ban sounds clean. In practice the chat moves to a private phone. That is [shadow AI at work](/blog/shadow-ai-in-the-office).

The minimum is dull and often enough: which tools sit on the allow-list, which data class may go where, and that a human checks before anything goes to a customer. Not a 40-page policy theatre.
