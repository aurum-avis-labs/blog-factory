## What the Process Check is

The Process Check is a free hour online about **one** recurring workflow in your business. You pick a slot. Confirmation arrives by email with a Microsoft Teams link. You leave with a written automation case and an estimate of which fixed-price package (Small through Max) would fit a build, should you want one later. It is not an intro call, a workshop or a tool pitch.

## What you leave with after 60 minutes

One hour, online, one workflow. The Process Check looks at work your team still does by hand on a regular basis. Invoices moving from the inbox into the accounting tool. A weekly report glued together from CRM, a spreadsheet and a PDF. Quotes where the follow-up sits with one person. Excel as the link between two systems that do not talk to each other.

You leave with a case. The case is that work in writing: what exactly it is, who does it today, how often it comes up, where it starts and where it stops. Alongside it comes an estimate of which fixed-price package would fit that scope, if you later want it built.

After the call you get a short written summary that you can pass on internally. Nobody else has to have been on the call.

Sometimes the result is that this workflow is not worth automating. You hear that during the hour. It does not turn into a project proposal just so that something «with AI» can be said to have happened.

The same offer, in shorter form, is on the [Process Check page](/prozess-check).

### How the hour runs

You show us on screen, or simply tell us, how the work runs today: where the data comes from, who does what and how often. We mark where automation can take over and what stays manual. Before we hang up, you hear a price range for a build. It also goes into the written summary.

The hour works on your actual work, so there is no intro call with a slide at the end.

### What to bring

Bring a task that repeats, or one concrete workflow: a report, an export, a handful of invoices. The hour works best if the person who does that work today is on the call. Management often knows how much time is lost, but the clicks are known by whoever makes them every week.

You do not have to show customer data. Describing the process is enough. An example with blacked-out fields or made-up numbers helps when the workflow otherwise stays too abstract. You do not need to send anything in advance.

### Why only one workflow

Put five topics on the table in one hour and you leave with five half-finished sentences. The check stays with one workflow: a clear start, a clear end, a rhythm you can name. One-off work, or work that is reinvented every time, is a poor fit, and you do not need 60 minutes with us for that.

## Who the Process Check is for

Owners, ops, finance or office leads in a Swiss SME, often with 5 to 80 people and little or no in-house IT. You decide yourself, or with one other person. The check fits when manual work eats time every week and you want to know **which single workflow** is worth doing first, before a licence, a workshop or a project.

Workflows that often fit: five exports glued into Excel every Monday. Invoices from the inbox into the ERP, the same steps each time. A weekly report from three sources. Workflows that fit badly: calming down a difficult customer (a conversation, different every time), or «we want to be more digital» with no repeating task.

If ChatGPT is already open in a tab but the Monday report is still assembled by hand, you are closer to the check than to a tool demo.

## Who should skip the check

You do not need a Process Check if you cannot name a recurring task yet. You also do not need it when the goal is team training on rules and approved tools. That is the [AI in business workshop](/services/ai-in-business), a different format from an hour on one workflow.

Engineering teams that want agents in the repository fit the [agentic coding workshop](/services/agentic-coding) better. Several linked workflows, or AI inside your own product without a clear first step, fit [AI implementation consulting](/services/ai-implementation-consulting). For **one** office workflow, the Process Check stays the small entry point.

If you only want a rough cost picture without a concrete workflow, a general cost article may be enough. The check pays off when you want to walk through one recurring process and leave with a written case.

## What the check costs

The Process Check is free.

The hour commits you to nothing. A later build has its own fixed price. If the hour produces a case and you still do not want a build, that is fine.

After the check, fixed-price packages are available for the build, should you want one. Guide prices, and what running costs look like once the workflow is live, are on the [offer page](/prozess-check), because the check first settles whether any scope fits, and which one.

## What the Process Check is not

The check is not a workshop. A workshop is for teams that need shared rules and approved tools. You come to the check with a workflow, not for a training day.

It is not tool training either. There is no subscription to sell in the hour and no prompt pack.

The time does not go into a studio pitch. There is no slide deck about us and no product sale on the call.

If you want to validate a product idea or cut an MVP, the Process Check is the wrong format. It is not built for that.

After the hour you do not have to have anything built. The build is optional.
