## What you leave with after 60 minutes

One hour, online, one workflow. The Process Check is about work your team still does by hand on a regular basis. Invoices moving from the inbox into the accounting tool. A weekly report glued together from CRM, a spreadsheet and a PDF. Quotes where follow-up sits with one person. Excel as the glue between two systems that do not talk to each other.

You leave with a case. The case is the work in writing: what exactly, who does it today, how often it comes up, where it starts, where it stops. Not a list of tools to buy. Plus an estimate of which fixed-price package would fit that scope, if you later want it built.

After the call you get a short written summary. You can pass that on internally. Nobody else has to have been on the call.

Sometimes the result is that this workflow is not worth automating. You hear that during the hour. It does not turn into a project proposal just so something "with AI" can be said to have happened.

The same offer, in shorter form, is on the [Process Check page](/prozess-check).

### How the hour runs

You show me on screen, or just tell me, how the work runs today: where the data comes from, who does what, how often. We mark where automation can take over and what stays manual. You hear a price range for a build before we hang up. After the call you get it in a short written summary.

The hour is paid because it sits on your actual work. It is not an intro call with a slide at the end.

### What to bring

Work that repeats, or one concrete workflow. A report, an export, a handful of invoices. The hour works best if the person who does that work today is on the call. Management often knows the time lost. The clicks are known by the person who does them every week.

You do not have to show customer data. Describing the process is enough. An example with blacked-out fields or made-up numbers helps when the workflow otherwise stays too abstract. You do not need to send anything in advance.

### Why only one workflow

If you put five topics on the table in the hour, you leave with five half-sentences. The check stays with one workflow: a clear start, a clear end, a rhythm you can name. One-off work, or work reinvented every time, is a poor fit. You do not need 60 minutes with us for that.

## What the check costs

The launch price is CHF 49 for the first 10 calls. After that the Process Check is CHF 99.

If you later book a build package, the check fee is credited in full toward that package. The check is then not an extra invoice on top. It becomes part of the package price.

Money back only if both are true: the call took place, and the hour did not produce a tangible automation case. If the call did not happen, the refund does not apply.

If the hour does produce a case and you still do not want a build, that is fine. The refund is tied to a missing case, not to a decision against implementation. The amount you paid then stays the check price until you book a package and the credit applies.

After the check there are fixed-price packages for the build, if you want one. Guide prices and what running costs look like once it is live are on the [offer page](/prozess-check). No price table in this article: the check first settles whether any scope fits, and which one.

## What the Process Check is not

The check is not a workshop. A workshop is for teams that need shared rules and approved tools. You are here because of a workflow, not because of a training day.

It is not tool training. There is no subscription to sell in the hour, and no prompt pack.

The time does not go into a studio pitch. No slide deck about us, no product sale on the call, no "buy this tool".

If you want to validate a product idea or cut an MVP, the Process Check is the wrong format. It is not built for that.

After the hour you do not have to have anything built. The build is optional.

## Who it is for

Owners, ops, finance or office leads in a Swiss SME. Often between 5 and 80 people, little or no in-house IT. You decide yourself, or with one other person.

The check fits when manual work eats time every week and you want to know which workflow is worth doing first. Before a licence, a workshop or a project.

A workflow that often fits: every Monday, five exports glued into Excel. One that fits badly: calming down the difficult customer. The first has frequency, a boundary and data that already exists. The second is a conversation, different every time.

The check is not for "we need a product idea". Not for marketing teams that want AI copy and images. Not for a company with an AI department and an RFP.

If ChatGPT is already open in a tab but the Monday report is still assembled by hand, you are closer to the check than to a tool demo.
