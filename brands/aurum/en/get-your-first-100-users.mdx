<p class="blog-lede">You get your first 100 users by naming what "user" means for this test, sending cold traffic to one honest action on a live product or page, and counting only people who are not in your address book unless you label them as warm leads.</p>

## Get your first 100 users

"One hundred" is a milestone founders use because it is larger than friends and family, not because the market awards a prize at that number. For a Swiss founder without a technical co-founder, the work is the same whether the target is forty or one hundred: define the behaviour you need to see, pick one primary channel you can explain in a sentence, run until your decision rule fires, then read what the list does not prove. The wider menu of tests sits in [how to validate a startup idea](/blog/how-to-validate-a-startup-idea). Here the focus is acquisition mechanics for early strangers, beta invites, and the trap of counting inboxes that never open the product.

Honest beta testers and design partners are adjacent problems. When the bottleneck is feedback quality, recruit honest beta testers deliberately. When you sell to firms and need a named champion, look for design partners for a B2B MVP.

## Get your first 100 users: a sequence that stays measurable

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Write the one-sentence hypothesis</h3><p>Who benefits, what changes for them, and which action falsifies polite encouragement from your network.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Choose the counted action</h3><p>Signup alone is weak if nobody returns. Prefer an action that costs time: first session, completed onboarding, or a booked call tied to the product story.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Ship something real enough to use</h3><p>A landing page with a fake button teaches copy, not habit. A thin live path, a concierge delivery, or a waitlist with follow-up you will send is closer to the truth.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Instrument before traffic</h3><p>Analytics and session recordings from day one show where strangers stop, not only the total at the bottom of a spreadsheet.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Run one primary channel at a time</h3><p>Paid search, paid social, a community post, or events in one city. Note creative, audience, and spend per run so you can compare attempts, not blend them into a story.</p></li>
  <li data-step="6"><h3>Apply the rule and segment the list</h3><p>Split warm introductions from cold completions. Interview the cold minority. Silence after signup is data.</p></li>
</ol>

## What one hundred shows and what it does not

<ul class="blog-facts">
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Shows</span><strong>Message reach</strong><p>Some strangers understood the offer enough to complete your scored step under real distraction.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Shows</span><strong>Early segments</strong><p>Role or context fields cluster so you know whom to call next.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Does not show</span><strong>Payment</strong><p>Unless money moved, price and urgency stay untested.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Does not show</span><strong>Retention</strong><p>A list size does not prove weekly use or marketplace balance on both sides.</p></li>
</ul>

Internal builds rarely march to exactly one hundred on the first try. Holist-IQ recorded eleven signups with a handful of longer sessions and a few returns, not revenue. do4me saw seventeen students and four job posters, mostly from people the team already knew, which did not prove marketplace liquidity. KitchenCrew found warmer response in Zurich conversations and events than from ads alone; clicks without booked pilot calls still left the demand question open. Those numbers are case notes, not targets you should copy.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Chasing one hundred signups from broad ads while the product is empty teaches distribution before value. Stop or pivot when cold completions stay near zero after an honest page and fair spend, or when every row in the sheet is someone you messaged yourself.</p>
</aside>

## Switzerland: language, trust, and channels you can defend

Swiss founders often split German, French, and English audiences. One landing page in one language with ads aimed at one region beats a single mixed campaign you cannot interpret. B2B teams frequently start on LinkedIn because job titles are visible; product-led tests may lean on Google search intent. Local meetups and partner introductions still matter, especially when ads produce clicks but not conversations. Ideas as such are not protectable; what you ship and how you brand it is ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)). This article does not quote CPC or conversion benchmarks: your niche and creative dominate, and invented averages would mislead.

If you retarget visitors or run cross-site tracking, handle information and consent the way Swiss practice expects for profiling and third-party use. Put the notice on the page before ads start. That is not legal advice.

## A made-up example: eighty-seven scored accounts in five weeks

A made-up example: a founder in Bern targets team leads in firms with roughly twenty to eighty staff. The hypothesis is that status updates still live in chat threads, and strangers will finish a five-minute onboarding in a web app if the page names audit trails, not "AI for teams". One English page for one campaign. The counted action is "first project created", not email captured. Analytics and recordings run before the first franc on LinkedIn.

The decision rule: continue if at least thirty cold accounts finish onboarding within five weeks and eight agree to a twenty-minute call from the automated follow-up. After five weeks: eighty-seven accounts, but fifty-two came from the founder's alumni chat. Cold completions: twenty-nine. Recordings show drop-off on the invite-colleagues step. They hide that step for two weeks, add a single optional field, and reach thirty-four cold completions with eleven calls booked. Still no pricing test. Next step: paid pilot with five firms, not another headline. The number eighty-seven would have flattered a slide; the segmented count drove the decision.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do you need exactly one hundred?</h3>
  <p>No. The useful part is the rule and the stranger share. Forty cold completions with clear segments can justify interviews; one hundred warm signups cannot.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">

</div>
