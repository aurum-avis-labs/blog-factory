## Handle customer complaints and warranty claims

<p class="blog-lede">You handle a customer complaint by recording it the day it arrives, classifying it, and notifying the person who has to answer. The remedy stays with a person on the contract.</p>

A warranty claim here means the goods or the work are defective. The office can prepare the file, the dates and the supplier notice. This page is not legal advice. The contract, a lawyer, or your trade adviser decide what you owe.

Sorting the email into "complaint" in the first place is the shared-inbox job, in [sort and route a shared inbox](/prozess-check/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). An online shop's "where is my order" and return labels are narrower, in [returns and customer enquiries in an online shop](/prozess-check/blog/returns-customer-enquiries-shop). This page is the defect, the warranty and the supplier, for firms that sell goods or carry out work.

## What to capture on the day it arrives

A complaint that lives only in a mailbox will miss a date. Put these fields in one record, filled from the email and the attachments, with a person checking what the reading got wrong:

- Who complained, and the order, delivery note or invoice number.
- The date you received the message, not the date someone got around to it.
- The date of delivery or of acceptance of the work, if the message or your records show it.
- What is wrong, in the customer's words, plus your own short classification.
- Photos and, where you use them, a serial or batch number.
- What the customer is asking for: repair, replacement, a credit, or a visit.

Unreadable photos and a missing order number trigger a reply that asks for them, the same day. The record still exists, with the gap visible. The mailbox already holds the photos. Do not paste them into a free chat tool to summarise the case.

## Which clock applies, and where the portal and the statute differ

Two clocks are easy to mix up. One is how fast you must tell the other side about the defect. The other is how long a claim can still be brought. Missing the first can mean the goods are treated as accepted, even if the second has years left.

For a sale of goods, [Article 201 of the Code of Obligations](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/de#art_201) says the buyer must examine the thing as soon as it is feasible in the ordinary course of business and notify the seller immediately of defects. If the buyer does not, the thing is deemed approved, except for defects a customary examination would not show. Those must be notified immediately after they are discovered. Since 1 January 2026, a defect in a thing that was built into an immovable work as intended, and that makes the work defective, must be notified within 60 days. Hidden defects of that kind: within 60 days of discovery. An agreement on a shorter period is void.

[Article 210](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/de#art_210) is the other clock. Claims for defects prescribe two years after delivery to the buyer, even if the defect is found later, unless the seller took on a longer period. The period is five years where a defect in a thing built into an immovable work makes that work defective. An agreement that shortens the period is void only when three conditions are all true: the shortening goes below two years, or below one year for used goods; the goods are for the buyer's personal or family use; and the seller is acting in business. Between businesses, a shorter period in the contract is not automatically void. [Article 197](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/de#art_197) sets the seller's liability for defects that remove or seriously reduce the value or the fitness for the use that was assumed, even if the seller did not know.

For work you carry out, the work-contract rules apply. [Article 367](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/de#art_367) requires the customer to examine the work and notify defects. For an immovable work the notice period is 60 days, and a shorter agreement is void. The same 60 days covers defects in a movable work that was built into the immovable work. [Article 371](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/de#art_371) sets two years from acceptance for defect claims, and five years in the immovable and built-in cases. That five-year period cannot be cut to the customer's disadvantage.

The federal portal [ch.ch](https://www.ch.ch/en/safety-and-justice/returning-or-exchanging-goods) tells consumers that new products have a two-year warranty, used products one year, and that sellers may not shorten those periods but may exclude the warranty. In the statute, the one-year figure is the floor in Article 210 paragraph 4 for a shortening on used consumer goods, and the ban on shortening needs all three conditions. A letter to a business customer that quotes the portal as the whole rule can be wrong. Have the contract read before a deadline or a refusal goes out. A contractual guarantee can be longer than the statute. Use the word the contract uses.

## A change of mind is not a defect

[ch.ch](https://www.ch.ch/en/safety-and-justice/returning-or-exchanging-goods) states that there is no statutory right to return or exchange goods bought in a shop or online. Each seller sets their own rule. Door-to-door sales and telephone contracts can be cancelled within 14 days if the goods are worth more than CHF 100. That is a withdrawal, not a defect.

Classify "I no longer want it" apart from "it does not work". The first follows the return rule you published, or a refusal if you published none. The second follows the defect path above. If the shop path, labels and "where is my order", is most of your mail, use the shop page. Do not run both through one template that promises a refund.

## Tell the supplier, and tell the customer only the status

Your customer claimed against you. You may need to claim against your supplier, under your purchase or your subcontract. That notice is a second record: the same photos, your purchase reference, the date you told the supplier. Do not wait until you have agreed a remedy with the customer if the supplier must be told promptly. The office can draft that notice the day the complaint is classified as a defect. A person sends it.

The customer update is a status, not an offer. "We have the photos, the order number, and we have told the supplier" is a status. "We will replace it" is a remedy. The draft may contain the status. The remedy stays out of the automatic mail until a person has chosen it and it matches what you are actually prepared to do: repair, replacement, a price reduction, or a refusal with the reason. A wrong promise is more expensive than a day's silence. The incoming date still has to be logged that day.

## Repeated defects are a purchasing fact

Once complaints sit in one list, count them by article, supplier and, if you have it, batch. Three complaints on the same article in a month are a reason for purchasing to look at the next order. The Monday list is for a person. The flow does not email the supplier about quality on its own.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a trade wholesaler receives an email with two photos and an order number. The flow finds the order, files the photos, and sets the type to "defect" because the customer describes a cracked housing, not a change of mind. The arrival date is today. The delivery date on the order is inside the two-year period in Article 210, which the record shows as a fact, not as a legal conclusion. A draft notice to the wholesaler's own supplier contains the order reference, the photos and the description. A draft to the customer says the message was received and the supplier was informed. The service lead reads both, sends them, and does not promise a replacement. The same article already has two open complaints. It appears on Monday's list. The firm, the article and the counts are invented.

## What to leave alone

Leave a full case system alone if you receive a few complaints a month and one person already logs them the same day. A shared sheet with the fields above is enough. Do not automate the legal conclusion. A date in a field is useful. A sentence that says "your claim has expired" is not something a flow should send. If your contracts change the statutory periods, those contracts have to be in the record before any draft mentions a deadline. If they are not, the draft mentions no deadline at all.
