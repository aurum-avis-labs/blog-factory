<p class="blog-lede">Swiss hosting for an MVP means choosing where primary customer data is stored and processed, not only where the URL is served from. For many vibe-coded stacks that means the database region at project creation, a separate question from the global CDN in front of your React build. Switzerland can be a real option on backends such as Supabase; many default exports still land in US or EU regions unless you change it on day one.</p>

This is for founders who already shipped a first version with Lovable, Bolt, v0, Replit, Cursor, or Claude Code and now hear "where is the data?" from a Swiss buyer, a fiduciary, or their own privacy notice. It is not legal advice, not a vendor scorecard, and not a promise that one checkbox replaces access control. For why deployment quality matters to validation, see [why we build production-quality MVPs](/blog/why-we-build-production-quality-mvps).

## Swiss hosting for an MVP

Hosting splits into layers. Confusing them is how teams answer the wrong question in a diligence call.

<ul class="blog-facts">
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Static app</span><strong>Often global</strong><p>Built files on a CDN may be cached worldwide. That is performance, not where your database rows live.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">API and database</span><strong>Region-bound</strong><p>Postgres, auth metadata, and file buckets follow the cloud region you picked for the project.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Logs and analytics</span><strong>Third parties</strong><p>Session tools, error trackers, and ad pixels may sit in another country even when the app is "Swiss".</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Tool default</span><strong>Check the console</strong><p>Exports from AI builders often create a Supabase or Firebase project for you. Open settings before real users arrive.</p></li>
</ul>

For an MVP you are usually optimising for speed and learning. Swiss hosting still matters when you store names, messages, health hints, payroll fields, or anything a customer would list in a data-processing questionnaire. It matters less for a fake door with no accounts, provided you are honest about that scope.

## Supabase and the Zurich region

If your MVP uses Supabase, region is explicit at creation. Supabase documents **Central Europe (Zurich), eu-central-2** as a specific region, and states that the region you choose is where primary project data is stored ([Supabase regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)). The same page warns that broader "Europe" groupings can include cities outside a jurisdiction you had in mind, and that **region selection is a data-location control, not proof of compliance**.

Practical implications for a vibe-coded app:

- Create the Supabase project in Zurich before you connect production traffic, if Swiss storage is a requirement. Treating region as "we will fix it later" often means migration work you did not budget for.
- Row level security, bucket policies, and service-role keys still decide who can read data. A Swiss region with open policies is not a security story.
- Backups, replicas, and support access may involve subprocessors listed in Supabase’s terms. Read those lists for customer-facing answers, not only the map pin.

Firebase and other Google-backed stacks follow **your** project settings in the console; misstating them is worse than saying you will confirm in the project.

## Azure, managed previews, and full production deploys

Some founders move past BaaS and deploy a NestJS or Node API with a managed Postgres instance on a hyperscaler. Aurum’s validation build uses **Azure** with React and NestJS for that layer, as described in the service offer, without tying every MVP test to a single template.

If you are still on the tool’s hosted preview URL, ask the vendor where that environment runs and whether you can export to your own cloud account. A Swiss postcode on the invoice does not tell you where bytes rest.

Self-hosting on a Swiss VPS is possible for a technical co-founder, but it shifts patching, backups, and TLS to you. For a two-week learning MVP that is rarely the fastest path unless compliance already forbids shared multi-tenant BaaS.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Choosing Switzerland for the database does not make the MVP FADP-complete, GDPR-proof, or acceptable to every enterprise security questionnaire. Purpose, retention, contracts, subprocessors, and access control still need a human owner. Hosting answers "where", not "whether you may".</p>
</aside>

## What Swiss hosting does not prove

A clear region choice helps you tell the truth in a privacy notice and in sales calls. It does **not** prove strangers will pay, that your auth model is safe, or that the MVP will survive production load. It does **not** replace analytics, session recording, or a Go, Pivot, or Stop read after paid traffic.

Edge caching in Frankfurt or Virginia can still serve your JavaScript quickly while rows stay in Zurich. Conversely, a `.ch` domain on a US-only database is not "Swiss hosting" because the brand is local. Prioritise a Swiss region as soon as you store personal data from Swiss users, a buyer requires residency in a contract, or you plan ads and a public waitlist. For a fake door with no login and synthetic data, the migration can wait.

Deferral is not permission to copy customer inboxes into a US sandbox without disclosure. It is permission to validate demand before you pay migration cost.

## Questions buyers actually ask

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is the app "hosted in Switzerland" if we use Supabase Zurich?</h3>
  <p>Primary Supabase data for that project are stored in the Zurich region you selected. Your front end, analytics, email provider, and support tools may still process data elsewhere. Answer with a short list of systems and regions, not a single yes.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Can we move region after launch?</h3>
  <p>Assume it is expensive until your dashboard says otherwise. Plan region at creation when residency is non-negotiable.</p>
</div>
