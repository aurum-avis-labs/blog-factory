<p class="blog-lede">A pitch deck with validation data is a slide set that puts evidence from a deliberate test ahead of vision slides: the hypothesis, how you reached people, what they did, what it cost, and the limit of what you can claim. It is for a Swiss SME that must show a board, a bank, or a programme that the idea was tested, not only described.</p>

This page is for an owner or innovation lead who already has a product story and hears "show us traction". It is not a template for a student competition or a crypto token. If you are still choosing what to build, start with validation work before you polish animations. If you are comparing what an MVP should cost before you fund a test, see [MVP cost in Switzerland](/blog/mvp-cost-switzerland).

## What a pitch deck with validation data means on the slides

The phrase does not mean pasting every dashboard into an appendix. It means one narrative thread: you named a risk, you ran a bounded experiment, you report numbers you can defend, and you say what would change your mind.

Typical order for a Swiss B2B or Mittelstand audience:

1. Problem and segment in one slide, with the customer you actually spoke to or acquired.
2. Hypothesis: the behaviour or willingness to pay you tried to observe.
3. Method: live product, landing page, concierge pilot, or paid channel, with dates.
4. Results: signups, sessions, retention, cost per lead, or paid pilots, with the sample size visible.
5. Interpretation: go, pivot, or stop, plus what the data cannot prove.
6. Ask: money, pilot partners, or time, tied to the next test.

Programmes such as [Innosuisse start-up coaching](/blog/innosuisse-start-up-coaching) or the wider [Swiss startup funding landscape](/blog/swiss-startup-funding-programmes) often want to see that you understand risk, not that you already have revenue.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Write the hypothesis on one line</h3><p>State what had to be true for the venture to deserve the next cheque or internal budget. If you cannot write it without adjectives, you are not ready to claim validation.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Collect only metrics you can source</h3><p>Export from analytics, ads, or CRM. Note the date range and whether testers were warm introductions.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Pair every chart with a limit</h3><p>Sample size, channel bias, and whether users paid or only clicked.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Align the ask with the evidence</h3><p>A seed round needs a different slide than an Innosuisse coaching voucher or a division budget.</p></li>
</ol>

## What validation data can and cannot prove

Validation data can show that a narrow group behaved in a measurable way under specific conditions. It can support a pivot decision or a request for a larger build budget. It cannot replace a financial model, a legal opinion, or proof that strangers will pay at scale.

Ideas as such cannot be protected under Swiss law; the deck should show what you learned while executing, not secrecy around the concept ([IGE overview](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)). A strong chart from friends and family is still a weak market read. Say so on the slide. Total addressable market as a substitute for behaviour, vanity logos without a defined pilot outcome, projections drawn before the first completed test, and claims that one workshop equals product-market fit belong later in the deck or not at all.

## A made-up example: pitching an internal portal

A made-up example: a 40-person logistics firm in Bern, not a client. The innovation lead tested whether branch managers would complete a supplier checklist in a web app instead of email. Over four weeks the team shipped a thin portal, ran LinkedIn ads to facility managers in Switzerland, and logged completions in analytics.

The deck opens with the email pain, then the hypothesis ("managers finish the checklist without a chase from head office"). The results slide shows 38 signups, 9 completed checklists, average session length under two minutes, and CHF 420 spent on ads with cost per completed form above what finance would accept for a manual process. The interpretation slide says "interest, not habit" and proposes a second test with two real suppliers integrated. The ask is CHF 25'000 internal budget and six weeks, not a seed round.

The board gets a decision, not a hero video.

## Who should keep the deck vision-only for now

Skip the validation narrative if you have no test yet and the meeting is only exploratory. Some angel conversations are relationship-first; forcing charts early can backfire. If your regulated domain blocks real users until licences are in place, say that on a "constraints" slide instead of implying traffic you never had.

If the audience is a corporate IT team that only wants extra developers, validation slides will not change their procurement model. Send a technical scope document instead.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Validation data in a deck supports honesty about uncertainty. It does not guarantee Innosuisse approval, bank debt, or a venture round. Funders still judge team, market, and terms.</p>
</aside>

## How this differs from a deck after a full validation sprint

A twelve-week validation engagement can end with a live product, paid acquisition data, iteration notes, and a deck built around that evidence, including a documented go, pivot, or stop read. That is a different depth from a four-week internal test. The structure is the same: hypothesis first, limits visible, ask matched to the next experiment.

Early internal products sometimes produce numbers like a handful of signups and a clear "paid interest did not appear" story. That is still validation data when it is sourced and dated. It is not failure if the slide says stop before a seven-figure build.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do I need revenue in the pitch deck?</h3>
  <p>No. You need a metric that matches your stage: completed tasks, pilot renewals, cost per lead, or letters of intent. Revenue helps when it is real, not when it is a forecast.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>How many slides should validation take?</h3>
  <p>Often three to five in the main flow, plus one limit slide. More charts rarely convince; clearer method and sample size do.</p>
</div>
