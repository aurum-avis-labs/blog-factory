## Does AI have to run in Switzerland?

No. The Swiss Data Protection Act (DSG, also called the nDSG or FADP) does not require an AI system to run in Switzerland. It regulates when personal data may be disclosed abroad. For ordinary office work, a provider in a country on the Federal Council's list is often enough. A contract, a professional-secrecy duty, or a customer's instruction can still make Swiss or European hosting the practical choice. This page is a working guide for owners and office leads. It is not legal advice.

The question usually arrives as a slogan: "our data must stay in Switzerland." Sometimes that slogan is in a customer contract. Sometimes it is a feeling after a board meeting. The statute asks a narrower question: are these personal data, and if they leave Switzerland, which of the permitted paths applies?

## What "abroad" means in a normal office

Disclosure abroad is not only a file you email to a supplier in another country. It is also the moment a cloud service processes a name, an address, or a message on infrastructure outside Switzerland. The [Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC)](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/en/cross-border-transfer-of-personal-data) states that a transfer is allowed only if the conditions are met, and that the controller stays responsible.

A private free chat account is a different problem, covered in [ChatGPT and customer data](/prozess-check/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg). This page is about location for a contracted tool. The EU AI Act is a different statute again; for a firm that only sells in Switzerland, it is rarely the first filter, as in [the EU AI Act and Swiss SMEs](/prozess-check/blog/eu-ai-act-swiss-smes).

## Three paths the Act actually names

[Art. 16 and Art. 17 DSG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en) describe three routes. You do not pick the one that sounds easiest. You check which one is true for this vendor and this file.

<ul class="blog-facts">
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Country list</span><strong>Art. 16 para. 1</strong><p>The Federal Council has found adequate protection. Transfer to that state is then permitted.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Safeguards</span><strong>Art. 16 para. 2</strong><p>No such decision. A treaty, notified clauses, standard clauses, or binding corporate rules must cover the gap.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Exceptions</span><strong>Art. 17</strong><p>A short list, including express consent. Not a general permission to paste a dossier into any tool.</p></li>
</ul>

The FDPIC states that, where protection is adequate, private companies may transfer personal data to that state without a further mechanism under Art. 16 para. 2. Where it is not, the FDPIC has recognised the EU standard contractual clauses. The data subject must be informed of disclosure abroad (Art. 19 para. 4 DSG). Countries and safeguards belong in the register of processing activities (Art. 12 DSG). Which path applies to your files is a question for your data-protection adviser.

## The country list, and the United States line

The list is Annex 1 of the [Data Protection Ordinance](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/568/en). Germany, Austria, Belgium, France and Italy are on it, among others. A provider that processes only in one of those states sits on the Art. 16 para. 1 path for that processing. You still need a processor contract, and you still need to know which subprocessors see the file.

The United States line is narrower. Since 15 September 2024, adequate protection for the US applies to personal data processed by organisations certified under the Swiss-US Data Privacy Framework. A US company that is not certified is not covered by that line. Ask for the certification, or treat the transfer as one that still needs Art. 16 para. 2 or an Art. 17 exception.

## When the server location is still the practical question

A customer contract, a mandate, or an internal rule may say the data stay in Switzerland or in the EU. If you signed it, the country list does not rewrite it.

Professional secrecy can block a transfer the DSG would otherwise allow. In a medical practice, health data and the duty in the Criminal Code sit on top of the hosting question, as in [AI in medical practices](/prozess-check/blog/ai-in-medical-practices). Banks, lawyers and other professions have their own duties.

If the board wants Swiss or EU hosting, put that in the processor agreement and in the [allow-list](/prozess-check/blog/approve-ai-tools-allow-list), so staff do not open a second tool that ignores it.

## A mailbox in Switzerland is not the model

Microsoft defines data residency as the place where customer data is stored at rest. On its [overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/enterprise/m365-dr-overview), Switzerland is a local-region geography; Exchange, SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams and Microsoft 365 Copilot are among the core services named there. The Data Location card shows geography for your tenant: storage at rest for those services, not a promise that every model inference happens in Switzerland. Read the card, then the Copilot terms for the plan you bought.

OpenAI's API documentation describes a project option labelled [Europe (EEA + Switzerland)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data), on eu.api.openai.com, for eligible customers. Customer content is stored at rest in the selected region where the endpoint needs to keep it. System data may still be processed outside it, and the option names no Swiss data centre. Consumer ChatGPT is a different product.

If a vendor page does not state a location, leave the location out of the decision. A sales sentence is not the trust page. Regional API endpoints and "data residency" often cover only storage at rest. Ask the vendor whether model requests use the same region when that matters for your file.

Four checks before you approve a tool:

- Which country processes this workflow, and which stores it at rest?
- Is that state on Annex 1, or is the US firm certified under the Swiss-US framework?
- If neither, which Art. 16 para. 2 safeguard or Art. 17 exception applies?
- Who are the subprocessors, and does our customer contract forbid this path?

## Who should frame the question differently

Free chat accounts without a contract, shadow tools in the browser, and files with especially sensitive personal data need different answers than «is the server in the EU». Internal notes without names often carry less pressure than customer dossiers in a new AI mailbox.

## What to settle with the team

Location is one line on the allow-list, next to which files may enter the tool. The people who draft mail need the same sentence as the person who signs the contract.

Note in the register of processing activities which states each tool uses, and which Art. 16 or 17 path applies. Without that line, the allow-list becomes a list of slogans.
