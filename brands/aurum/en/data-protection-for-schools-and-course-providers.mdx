## Data protection for schools and course providers

Data protection for schools and course providers in Switzerland puts minors' data first: enrolment forms, emergency contacts, learning notes for teachers, photos on the website, and class lists on the whiteboard. Private language schools, driving instructors, vocational course providers, and supplementary schools face the same [FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de) logic as larger institutions, often with fewer staff per channel.

For school directors and operations leads with teams of a few dozen adults serving children and teenagers. Orientation, not legal advice and not a substitute for cantonal education law.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Parent or guardian contact rules; who may pick up whom, documented</li>
  <li>Photo consent with event scope; no names on public galleries without thought</li>
  <li>LMS and video tools listed with [processor agreements](/security/blog/data-processing-agreement-under-the-fadp)</li>
  <li>Class chats not on random consumer apps without leadership sign-off</li>
  <li>School laptops encrypted; no pupil lists on personal phones</li>
  <li>Deletion when pupils leave; alumni mail is a separate purpose</li>
</ul>

## Cases that fit schools, not generic offices

A teacher posts a class photo on private Instagram with school tags visible. A course admin exports minors' phone numbers for "SMS reminders" and shares the file with an intern. A phishing mail to the bursar redirects fee payments. A substitute receives a full health note though they only teach sport for one hour. A coding camp stores passport copies in a US signup form without review. Each case involves children or adolescents.

## What it is not

Not university research ethics. Not a generic SME checklist with a swapped noun. Not aimed at adult-only courses without minors, though photos and payment data still matter there.

## Parents, guardians, and phone calls

Only authorised guardians receive grades or behaviour notes. Verify identity on the phone before reading marks aloud; callback rules help even against rare deepfake calls. Split channels: parent app versus staff-only. Emergency contacts do not belong in open group chats with all parents.

## Substitutes, coaches, and trips

A one-day football coach must not download the full pupil list. Time-bound accounts and object permissions are better than sharing a PDF roster. School trips: passenger lists on school devices, delete after the trip, no group SMS without opt-out.

## Exams, rankings, and recordings

Ranked lists on doors identify minors to peers and visitors. Internal spreadsheets emailed to all staff are disclosures too. Recorded video lessons may capture faces and home backgrounds: consent, retention, no marketing reuse without clear agreement.

## DPIA and new apps

Camp apps with location or biometric attendance may need a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) before parents install anything. Hosting providers need written processor terms.

## Incidents and notification

A lost tablet with the class register triggers the same breach thinking as corporate mail: document, contain, advise. High risk to minors may require FDPIC notification; this is orientation, not legal advice. Photos of children on the website often raise urgency.

## School health data and trusted staff

Health data for allergies or medication should be visible only to people who need it for care. A substitute for one lesson does not need the same depth as the class teacher or school nurse. Split fields in the LMS instead of one long free-text note for everyone.

## Fees and phishing

Phishing aimed at the bursar or parent payment portals is common in private schools. Callback rules for changed bank details apply as in any SME; pair that with short training for admin and reception. Parents usually spot wrong debits quickly; give them a clear channel for questions.

## Course providers without a campus

Tutoring and holiday courses without a building often have less IT but the same minor lists. Do not park participant data in personal cloud folders "until we have an LMS". Keep parent payment data separate from marketing newsletters. When you partner with schools, agree in the contract who owns the shared list.

## Training by role

Seasonal staff and unpaid helpers need short orientation on phones, photos, and phishing. [Role-based security awareness](/security/blog/role-based-security-awareness-training) separates finance, HR, and reception; for schools, leadership, admin, and teachers are sensible audiences. Pair it with a [security and privacy overview](/security) before the autumn term or camp season.

## Website gallery each term

Review the public gallery each semester: remove pupils who left and honour withdrawals promptly. Parents trust schools with data; a visible mistake hurts more than a delayed honest fix.

## Guest pupils and partnerships

For guest pupils from other cantons, ask the home school which data you really need instead of copying everything. Sports clubs training on site need clear limits on health data the coach may see. Mixed minor and adult classes: separate enrolment data, do not photograph a shared sign-in sheet at the door.

## Keep minor and adult marketing apart

Adult course marketing must not reuse pupil mailing lists. Separate brands and domains reduce mix-ups. Tutoring firms without a campus still avoid minor lists in personal cloud folders.

## Internal roles

Small IT teams should not load one person with both security and classroom wiring if the budget allows splitting. Parent evening sign-in sheets: collect or destroy at the end, do not leave them in the staff room.

Boards often ask about cyber insurance: honest answers mention training and access control, not unchecked security claims. Paper sign-in sheets at reception are personal data: lock them away.

When you partner with external course providers, agree in the contract who is controller for the shared participant list. A named data contact on the staff page helps parents without inventing a formal DPO. Breaches involving photos of minors often lower the bar for notification; keep counsel contact ready without spreading alarm.

Adult-only courses can drop minor-specific items but still document photo and payment data. A driving school with teenagers keeps the full checklist. If cantonal rules require extra registers, ask the cantonal office, not only this article.

## Frequently asked questions

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>May we publish class photos on the website?</h3>
  <p>Only with appropriate consent and easy withdrawal. Blur or skip identifiable pupils when in doubt.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Can coaches use personal phones for attendance?</h3>
  <p>Discourage storing pupil lists on personal devices. School-owned apps with access control are safer.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Parent asks what data you hold?</h3>
  <p>Answer within statutory timeframes. Search LMS, paper, mail, and photo archives. Counsel helps on refusals.</p>
</div>
