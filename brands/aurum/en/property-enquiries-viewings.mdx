## Handle property enquiries and viewings

When a listing goes live on Monday and the inbox is full by Tuesday, you are not looking for an "AI tool for agents". You need a chain: the same questions get the same approved reply, viewings run through published slots, and after the visit you know who is still interested. Price changes, legal questions and preferred applicants stay with the broker. What belongs in a brokerage office versus property management is in [AI for real estate agents](/prozess-check/blog/ai-for-real-estate-agents). This page is the sequence for the listing that just drew a rush of mail.

It fits a small agency where one person runs viewings instead of typing all morning. You already have price, address, availability, plans and what you may send before the first reply goes out.

Treat seekers' personal data like any other client mail: only what you need, no dossiers in free chat tools, access limited to people with a task. The chain stores reference, name, contact and status (asked, invited, attended, interested yes/no). It does not store a credit score or an "AI assessment".

## One listing, one pack

Before the first reply, a person builds the pack for that object only: plans, cost sheet, availability sentence, viewing times. Price and availability match the listing. The [ImmoScout24 insertion rules](https://www.immoscout24.ch/c/de/ueber-uns/insertionsregeln) require true published facts, including price and availability. A reply that "clarifies" into a different number is effectively a second listing.

The pack is a file or short sentences to attach, not a prompt that re-describes the flat. If a fact changes, a person updates pack and listing together. The flow never updates one alone.

Name the object for the inbox: listing reference, not "the nice flat in town". Twenty threads with no reference are twenty objects to software.

## Standard reply, then a person

The first message is usually one of four questions: available?, viewing?, extra costs?, plans?. If the mail is only those, reply with pack plus slots. No compliment, no financing guess, no "you seem like a good fit".

If the message asks outside the pack, it goes to the broker with the pack attached. How a shared inbox splits routine from exception is in [sort emails in a shared inbox](/prozess-check/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). A form on your site is a second door, in [send website form enquiries into your CRM](/prozess-check/blog/web-form-to-crm). Both lead to the same pack.

Do not send the pack on a wrong reference. Ask in one line. A wrong pack is worse than a slow reply.

ChatGPT can suggest sentences. The facts come from the listing and the owner, and a person approves the pack. The model does not replace insertion duties.

Portal notifications and mail to info@ are often only the start: "There is a new enquiry." The full text may sit in the portal or a second mail. Your chain must know where the complete message lives before step 2 runs. Otherwise you reply to a subject with no body.

## Slots, reminder, empty viewing

Publish two or three slots, not an open "whenever suits you". Group viewing: one slot with a limit. Individual viewing: one person per slot. Full slots leave the reply. No overbooking "just in case". If you offer individual viewings, keep slot length realistic: travel, keys, questions in the stairwell. A slot that is 15 minutes on paper and 40 on site moves the rest of the day without the inbox noticing.

The day before, one reminder: time, meeting point, what to bring, how to cancel. People who do not reply stay on the list unless your written rule says otherwise. Cancellations free the place in enquiry order, not in "promising" order.

On the day: key and name list. Without the list you reconstruct badly the next morning. The broker runs the conversation on site; the list says who had a slot. Someone who turns up unannounced is an exception with its own rule, not a standard place.

Use your portal's booking if you pay for it. Copying every confirmation into a second calendar is how two lists disagree. We have not verified booking on a given agency account. Check the account you actually use.

## One question after the visit

One question, once, to people on the list: still interested?, missing fact from the pack? Reply: yes, no, or a question for the broker. Yes means a shorter call list, not selection. No closes the thread without a second nudge.

If five people ask the same missing fact, add it to the pack once after a person checks you may say it. Do not rank visitors from those answers.

What you collect belongs in the owner report: enquiry count, viewing attendance, still interested, open special questions. That is not an AI score of visitors, but ordered numbers from the chain. The chain does not score visitors, recommend a buyer, or change the price. It records who asked, who came, and who is still interested.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a 3.5-room flat, listed Monday. By Tuesday evening, 16 messages. Eleven ask only for availability, plans and a viewing. Three ask about a small dog (not in the pack). Two give no reference.

The eleven get the pack and two slots: Thursday 18:00 (8 places), Saturday 10:00 (8 places). Eight take Thursday, two Saturday, one cannot make either: waits for the broker. Dog questions to the broker with pack. No reference: one line which listing, no wrong plans.

Wednesday evening, reminder to the eight Thursday names with the meeting point and what to bring if you ask for ID. One cancels; the freed place goes to the person who asked first outside the slots. Thursday, nine names on the list, not sixteen threads. Friday, one question to those nine: four yes, three no, two silent. The broker calls the four. The owner hears: 16 enquiries, 9 at the viewing, 4 still interested, one open pet question after checking the rules. People and counts are invented.

## When not to build it

Skip it at three messages and known senders: the personal reply is the work. Skip a second calendar when the portal holds the slots. Skip full automation when every object is negotiated and no stable pack exists. Skip anything that publishes or bumps listings on its own. That is not enquiry handling.

If you later connect the chain to a CRM, decide which field holds the listing reference before you build automations. Without it, viewings attach to the wrong object and reporting cannot be fixed cleanly.

## Do you need a CRM first?

No, but you need a reference per object and a home for the pack. A CRM often comes later, when several brokers see the same inputs and owner reports should come from the system. The chain can run on inbox plus folder as long as the rules stay the same.

## The language of the reply

Reply in the language of the enquiry when you can tell it safely. Do not mix languages in one mail because the model can do both. A wrong language reads less professional than a slow reply in the right one. If you get enquiries in several languages, keep one pack per language with the same facts, not an automatic translation of the availability.
