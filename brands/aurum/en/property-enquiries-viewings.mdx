## Handle property enquiries and viewings

You handle a rush of enquiries on one listing by answering from a pack you have already approved, offering the viewing slots you published, sending one reminder, and asking one question after the visit. Everything else, a price change, a legal question, a person you want to prefer, stays with the broker. The wider picture of what belongs in a brokerage office, and what belongs in property management, is in [AI for real estate agents](/blog/ai-for-real-estate-agents). This page is the sequence for the listing that just went live.

It is for a small agency where one object produces more messages than one person can type in a morning, and where the broker would rather be at the viewing than in the inbox. It assumes you already have the facts: price, address, availability, plans, and what you are allowed to send.

## One listing, one pack, written once

Before the first reply, a person builds the pack for that object and no other. Plans, the cost sheet you are willing to send, the sentence on availability, and the viewing times. The price and the availability are the ones on the listing. The [ImmoScout24 insertion rules](https://www.immoscout24.ch/c/de/ueber-uns/insertionsregeln) require published object facts to be true, including price and availability. A reply that "clarifies" them into a different number is a second listing you did not mean to publish.

The pack is a file or a short set of sentences the office can attach. It is not a prompt that asks a model to describe the flat again. If a fact changes, a person updates the pack and the listing together. The flow never updates one of them alone.

Name the object in a way your inbox can match: the listing reference, not "the nice flat in town". Twenty threads with no reference are twenty objects to a machine and one headache to you.

## The standard reply, then a person for the rest

The first message on a new listing is usually one of four questions: is it available, when can I see it, what are the extra costs, can you send the plans. If the message is only those, the reply is the pack plus the slots. It does not add a compliment, a guess about financing, or a promise that the person is "a good fit".

If the message asks for something the pack does not contain, it does not get the standard reply alone. It goes to the broker, with the pack already attached so they are not starting from an empty mail. How a shared inbox separates the routine from the exception, with a draft instead of an automatic send, is in [sort emails in a shared inbox](/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). A form on your own website, as opposed to a portal notification, is a different input, covered in [send website form enquiries into your CRM](/blog/web-form-to-crm). Treat them as two doors into the same pack, not as two processes.

Do not send the pack to someone who asked about a different object, and do not send it when you cannot tell which listing they mean. Ask which reference, in one line. A wrong pack is worse than a slow reply.

## Slots, reminders, and the empty viewing

Publish two or three slots, not an open "whenever you like". A group viewing is a slot with a limit you choose, so the stairwell is not a crowd you did not plan. An individual viewing is a slot of one. The flow offers only what is still open. When a slot is full, it disappears from the reply. It does not get overbooked "just in case".

The day before, one reminder: time, meeting point, what to bring if you ask for anything, and how to cancel. People who do not reply to the reminder stay on the list. You do not drop them for silence unless that is your written rule. People who cancel free the place for the next person who asked, in the order they asked, not in the order a model finds "promising".

On the day, someone has the key and the list of names. The list is the point of the whole chain. A viewing without names is a conversation you will reconstruct badly the next morning.

If the portal account you pay for already books viewings and sends the reminder, use that. Copying every confirmation into a second calendar is how the two lists disagree. We have not verified a booking function on a given portal's agency account, so check the account you actually use before you build a second one.

## One question after the visit

The follow-up is one question, sent once, to the people who were on the list and not merely in a thread: are you still interested, and is there a fact the pack did not answer. The reply is a yes, a no, or a question for the broker. A yes does not mean they are selected. It means the broker has a smaller list to call. A no closes the thread. Do not follow a no with a second nudge.

Feedback you collect is for the owner report and for the pack: if five people ask the same missing fact, add it to the pack once, after a person checks that you are allowed to say it. Do not let those five answers become a ranking of the visitors.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a 3.5-room flat, listed on a Monday morning. By Tuesday evening there are 16 messages. Eleven ask only for availability, the plans and a viewing. Three ask whether the building allows a small dog, which is not in the pack. Two give no address and no reference, only "the flat I saw".

The eleven receive the pack and two slots: Thursday at 18:00, eight places, and Saturday at 10:00, eight places. Eight take Thursday, two take Saturday, one says neither time works. That one waits for the broker, because the key is not offered at random hours. The three dog questions go to the broker with the pack attached. The two without a reference get one line asking which listing. They are not sent plans for the wrong flat.

Wednesday evening, the eight Thursday names get a reminder with the meeting point at the front door. One cancels. The person who could not make either slot is offered that freed place, because they asked before anyone else outside the slots, and they accept. Thursday, nine people are on the list in the broker's hand, not sixteen threads. Friday morning, one question goes to those nine. Four say they are still interested, three say no, two do not reply. The broker calls the four. The owner hears: 16 enquiries, 9 at the viewing, 4 still interested, and one open question about pets that the broker will answer only after checking the regulations. None of these people is a client of ours. The counts are here so the rules are visible.

## When not to build it

Skip it when a listing draws three messages and you know each sender. The personal reply is then the work, not the overhead. Skip a second calendar when the portal already holds the slots and your team lives in it. Skip an automatic send when every object is a negotiated sale and there is no pack stable enough to attach. And skip any flow that would publish the listing or bump it back to the top of a portal on its own. That is not enquiry handling, and the insertion rules treat automated republishing to dodge a cap as abuse.

## When a Prozess-Check fits

The [Prozess-Check](/prozess-check) fits when this is the recurring task: the morning after a listing, the same questions, the same pack, the same slots. It is 60 minutes on Microsoft Teams, for CHF 99. You describe one recent listing and where the messages arrive. You do not bring seekers' dossiers.

You leave with where to start, whether it pays off, a price range and a rough duration, and a short written summary. The hour commits you to nothing. If it finds nothing worth doing, the fee comes back, provided the appointment took place.
