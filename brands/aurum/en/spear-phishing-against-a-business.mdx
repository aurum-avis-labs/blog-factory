## Spear phishing against a business

Spear phishing against a business is e-mail aimed at one company, often one role, using correct names, suppliers, or project titles to push a payment, a password reset, or a file download. It is not a million-address spam blast. Attackers read your website, LinkedIn, and the Swiss trade register before they write. BACS voluntary reports counted 971 CEO-style fraud cases in 2025, up from 719 in 2024. AI tools polish the wording; the structure is still «urgent», «confidential», and «use this new account».

This article is for owners, finance, and assistants in a Swiss SME with twenty to two hundred fifty staff. Generic signs sit in [how to spot phishing e-mails](/security/blog/how-to-spot-phishing-emails). When spear mail asks someone to e-mail personal data, the handling overlaps with [sharing files that contain personal data](/security/blog/sharing-files-that-contain-personal-data).

## How spear phishing differs from mass phishing

Mass phishing sends the same story to many domains. Spear mail is written for your firm. The sender may spoof a director, a supplier you pay monthly, or HR during hiring season. The hook is plausible because parts of it are true. That is why «check spelling» alone fails: the text can be flawless.

Business e-mail compromise is the family name when the goal is a payment or a credentials handover. Some threads start as spear mail and continue by SMS. That is a different channel, not a different motive.

## Typical storylines in Switzerland

A message carries a director's name but replies through free webmail. A supplier «updates banking details» the day before a real invoice arrives. HR receives «payroll portal» links while you advertise roles. IT gets a password reset that matches a migration you announced internally, except the link domain is wrong.

## What spear phishing is not

Not penetration testing you ordered. Not a marketing newsletter from a trade fair lead list. Not the same as smishing, though the story may continue by text message.

## Who should focus here

Finance, executive assistants, and project leads who receive external mail about money. Warehouse staff may see fewer targeted mails but should still report carrier clones. IT needs a sample with full headers, not a screenshot of the body alone.

## Finance and assistant workflows

Spear phishing often lands when someone is alone at the desk. Pair holiday cover with payment holds: no new beneficiary without a second signature. Assistants should have a plain reply ready: «I will confirm with my manager on the published number.» Attackers hang up when the script is boring.

When the story is a changed IBAN, treat it as invoice fraud until proven otherwise. The step-by-step for finance sits in [invoice fraud with a changed IBAN](/security/blog/invoice-fraud-changed-iban).

## LinkedIn and register data

BACS notes that criminals research executives on LinkedIn and in the Swiss trade register. Your public org chart helps sales and also helps fraud. That does not mean hiding leadership. It means payment controls that do not trust e-mail authority alone.

## When mail comes from a real colleague mailbox

If spear mail is sent from a compromised account, filters may not flag it. IT resets passwords, checks forwarding rules, and finance pauses outbound payments. Report it as an incident, not only as user error.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Save the message with headers for IT</li>
  <li>Do not pay from the mail thread alone</li>
  <li>Tell staff where to forward samples</li>
</ul>

## A made-up example

A property manager with twenty-five staff receives mail on Monday to accounts. The display name matches a maintenance firm that really works for the company. The text names a building from the website and asks finance to use a «corrected» IBAN today because the insurer changed bank details. The wording is smooth. Accounts calls the maintenance firm on the number from the contract, not the number in the signature. The firm sent nothing. The sample goes to the internal security mailbox and the payment stays on hold. That call, not a better spam filter, stops the case.

## What to do when a mail looks targeted

Do not reply with account numbers or attachments. Forward the sample to the address IT published, with headers if your mail client allows it. Finance should pause any payment mentioned in the thread until a second person confirms on a known channel. If the mail copied a real thread, warn the person whose name was abused so they can tell partners the message is fake.

Spear mail that only asks for a quick «yes» still counts. A short reply can be pasted into the next forged message. Break the loop by moving the check to phone or to an internal chat you already use for payments.

## After something slips through

When spear mail succeeds, review process as well as technology. Did four-eyes fail because one person was on sick leave? Fix cover instead of blaming an individual. A voluntary BACS report helps others when the domain is new.

Deloitte's 2026 survey of firms under two hundred fifty staff cites regular security training in sixty-five percent of small and eighty-two percent of medium companies. Your proof is attendance records and tickets, not slogans on a slide.
