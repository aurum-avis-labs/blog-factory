## Validation test metrics

Validation test metrics are the numbers you agreed to read before you launched: where traffic came from, how many people saw the page, how many completed one primary action, and what you paid per lead if you used ads. Conversion rate on the landing page is one line in that set. It is not a grade unless you defined the action, the audience, and the decision rule in advance.

This is for founders and innovation leads in Switzerland who already have or plan a [smoke test landing page](/blog/smoke-test-landing-page) or a [fake door test](/blog/fake-door-test) and need a short list of what to log. The wider menu of interviews, concierge delivery, and build-or-stop choices sits in [how to validate a startup idea](/blog/how-to-validate-a-startup-idea). Here the focus stays on measurement, limits, and what the dashboard cannot prove.

## Which validation test metrics to log

Split the readout into channel metrics and page metrics. Channel metrics explain whether the message reached the segment you targeted. Page metrics explain whether people understood the offer and took the action you chose. Four numbers carry the test: the primary action you named, the landing conversion rate for the window you set, cost per lead on paid traffic, and the decision rule you wrote before launch.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>One primary action in a sentence: booked call, waitlist with a follow-up question, application, not a grab bag of secondary clicks</li>
  <li>Sessions and users on the test URL, with source and medium so organic posts do not mix with paid clicks</li>
  <li>Impressions, clicks, click-through rate, and spend if you ran search or social ads</li>
  <li>Cost per lead for the primary action on paid traffic in the test window</li>
  <li>Landing conversion rate: primary actions divided by sessions on that page for the same dates</li>
  <li>Form drop-off from analytics or session recordings; mark warm contacts so they do not pollute the cold-traffic rule</li>
  <li>A fixed end date or sample size so you stop when the rule says stop</li>
</ul>

Connect analytics and session recordings before the first paid visit. Otherwise you cannot separate a weak headline from a broken form.

  <strong>5.65%</strong>

Eleven signups, five sessions longer than four minutes, three return visits, no credit purchases. One project, not a target for yours.</p>

## What landing conversion rate does not prove

A higher landing conversion rate on a waitlist does not show product-market fit, retention, or unit economics. It does not show that strangers will pay, that a marketplace has both sides, or that support and operations scale. It does not protect the idea: ideas as such cannot be protected under Swiss practice; execution and branding can ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)).

Comparing your rate to a blog post's "industry average" rarely helps. Audience, offer, friction, language, and whether traffic is warm or cold all move the number. There is no universal conversion benchmark worth treating as law for a Swiss validation test.

    </ul>

## A made-up example: four weeks on one primary action

A made-up example: a solo founder in Lucerne sells a compliance checklist to small trustee firms. They publish one German page, primary action is a 25-minute discovery call via a calendar link, secondary path is a four-field form. They write the decision rule before ads: continue positioning if at least six calls book from paid Google traffic in four weeks with stable job-title targeting, pivot if clicks are steady but nobody reaches the calendar, stop if cost per lead stays above what ten leads justify without a single cold call.

Week one: analytics live, CHF 120 spend, 840 sessions, two bookings from a newsletter mention, none from ads. Week two: they tighten the headline around "audit trail for client files", spend CHF 140, landing conversion on paid sessions rises to about two percent, four cold bookings. Week three: form drop-off clusters on "firm size"; they make that field optional. Week four: five more cold bookings, cost per lead about CHF 55 on paid traffic for the month. No shipped product yet. They log channel, sessions, and actions in one table, separate warm from cold traffic, and choose a concierge pilot over a repo. No industry benchmark was required for that call.

## Privacy and the test page

If you use analytics, heatmaps, or retargeting, Swiss practice expects you to inform visitors and to handle consent where profiling or cross-site tracking is involved. Put the notice on the page before ads start. This article is not legal advice.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Should validation test metrics include email open rate?</h3>
  <p>Only if email is the channel you are testing and you defined opens or clicks as part of the rule. For a landing page test, the primary action on the page matters more than newsletter engagement unless the hypothesis is specifically about email copy.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is a two percent landing conversion rate good?</h3>
  <p>Good is relative to your decision rule, your cost per lead, and whether the converters are strangers in your segment. Comparing to an invented benchmark is what this article tells you to avoid.</p>
</div>
