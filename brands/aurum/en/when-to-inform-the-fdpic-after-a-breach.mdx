After a **data breach**, the first question is rarely which form to use. It is: **must the FDPIC be informed now?** Art. 24 FADP requires notification when a data security breach is likely to pose a **high risk** to personality or fundamental rights, **as soon as possible**. The Swiss Act has no fixed 72-hour deadline like the GDPR. Statute: [Fedlex Art. 24](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de). Portal: [databreach.edoeb.admin.ch](https://databreach.edoeb.admin.ch).

This article is the decision aid before you click. Step-by-step process: [Report a data breach to the FDPIC](/security/blog/report-a-data-breach-to-the-fdpic). Not legal advice.

## What counts as a data security breach

Art. 5(h) FADP: personal data are accidentally or unlawfully lost, deleted, destroyed, altered, or disclosed or made accessible to unauthorised persons. Examples: mail to the wrong recipient, unencrypted lost laptop, ransomware, open exports. See [What counts as a data security breach](/security/blog/what-counts-as-a-data-security-breach).

## The test: likely high risk?

Not every incident must be notified, only those with a **likely high risk**. Orientation questions:

1. Which data types? Especially sensitive data raises risk.
2. How many people, how easily identifiable?
3. How easily can third parties misuse the data?
4. Which measures already help (encryption, quick recall, password reset)?

Uncertainty is not a reason to stay silent. The portal allows a **preliminary notification**; follow-ups are supported. The FDPIC usually contacts reporters within about 10 working days if more is needed, according to the portal text.

## When you usually notify

- Health or other especially sensitive data reached unauthorised persons.
- Large volumes of identifiers leaked.
- Ransomware with exfiltration of personal data.
- Prolonged unnoticed unauthorised access to customer systems.

## When you often document first

- A low-sensitivity appointment mail went to a wrong but trusted recipient and was deleted immediately.
- An encrypted device is gone, the key stayed safe, nothing suggests decryption.
- Internal misfiling without external exposure, fixed at once.

Still document internally so you can explain the decision later. Processors notify the controller, not the FDPIC themselves.

## Informing data subjects

Art. 24(4): inform the data subject when required for their protection or when the FDPIC demands it. That is a second decision beside authority notification.

## FAQ

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Does the GDPR 72-hour clock apply?</h3>
  <p>Not under the Swiss FADP. Here: as soon as possible for likely high risk. If you are also under the GDPR, assess both regimes.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Who notifies: company or IT vendor?</h3>
  <p>The controller notifies the FDPIC. The processor notifies the controller as soon as possible (Art. 24(3)).</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do we need a lawyer before notifying?</h3>
  <p>Useful for complex or large incidents. The portal is built for controllers; uncertainty justifies a preliminary report rather than months of delay.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>What helps long term?</h3>
  <p>Recurring awareness against misdirected mail and weak access habits. See [Data protection training for employees](/security/blog/data-protection-training-for-employees) and [Data protection awareness](/security/datenschutz).</p>
</div>
