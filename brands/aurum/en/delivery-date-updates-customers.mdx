## Keep customers informed about delivery dates

<p class="blog-lede">When a delivery date moves in your system, you draft a mail with the old date and the new one. A person sends it before the customer has to ask.</p>

The draft names the order, the last confirmed date and the new one. It does not invent a date, a cause or a tracking number. A key account is not left on the same template: a person who knows them sends, using the draft as the brief. A "where is my order" mail is answered from that same date, so the customer does not receive two stories.

How the order got into the system from an email is in [capture customer orders from email](/prozess-check/blog/capture-email-orders). This page starts after the order exists.

## The date has one home

The date you may tell a customer is the date a person has accepted in the system you already run for orders. Not the date in an old confirmation that nobody updated. Not the date a supplier mentioned in a mail that purchasing has not yet believed. Not a date a model suggests because the sentence would sound better.

If the system date is a placeholder, the first job is to make it real. Automating the mail on top of a fictional date spreads the fiction faster. Someone in planning or purchasing changes the date, with a reason the office can see. Only then does a draft exist.

If the program you already pay for emails the customer, or shows the date on a portal the customer actually opens, use that. A second mail that quotes a different export will disagree with it. We are not claiming a named system has that button. Look at the one you run.

## What the draft is allowed to say

The draft is short, and it is the same shape every time.

<ul class="blog-facts">
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Which order</span><strong>Your number and theirs</strong><p>Both, when you have both. A customer cannot match "the parts" to a line.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">What moved</span><strong>Old date, new date</strong><p>Taken from the system after a person changed it. Not a range you hope for.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">What did not</span><strong>Article and quantity</strong><p>Say so if they are unchanged, so the customer does not re-read the whole order.</p></li>
</ul>

Leave out the supplier's name, the machine that broke, and any promise about the cause, unless a person has approved that sentence for this case. A customer does not need your internal argument. They need a date they can plan with, and a person to answer if the new date breaks theirs.

The subject line carries the order number and the words that a date changed. Buried in a thread titled "Re: Re: question", it will be missed. One customer, one mail, even if several of their lines moved. A dump of every open order in the firm is not an update.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Notice the change</h3><p>Compare today's confirmed date with the date you last told this customer. Only a difference starts a draft.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Fill the draft from the system</h3><p>Order numbers, article, quantity, old date, new date. Empty field: stop, do not guess.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Mark key accounts</h3><p>A list a person maintains. Those drafts go to a named colleague, not to send.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>A person sends</h3><p>They read the draft. They send, they edit one sentence, or they hold it. The model does not press send.</p></li>
</ol>

## Key accounts are an escalation

A key account is a customer you have named, not a customer a model ranked. The list is short on purpose. For them, the draft is the note on the desk: what moved, by how much, which order. The person calls or writes in their own words, the same day. The draft is not mailed as-is under their signature without that look.

Decide the threshold in one sentence and keep it next to the list. For example: any move of more than a few days on a named account, or any move that crosses a date the customer has written into the order. The flow only flags. It does not decide that the relationship can absorb the delay.

Everyone else can receive the plain draft after a person has read a sample, once you trust that the fields are filling correctly. Trust is a week of reading, not a setting on day one. If a draft is wrong, the date in the system was wrong or the match to the customer was wrong. Fix that. Do not add adjectives.

## "Where is my order" uses the same date

A status question is not a new research project. The answer is the current confirmed date, the order number, and whether anything has changed since you last wrote. If you have no carrier milestone, do not imply a scan, a truck or a dock. "We do not have a shipment event yet. The confirmed date is this one" is a complete answer.

If the customer writes in French or Italian and your confirmation is usually in German, the stable sentences (order, date moved, date unchanged, we will write again if it moves) belong in a short glossary so the date does not get paraphrased into a different promise. How to keep that wording, with a person still responsible for the send, is in [multilingual customer communication](/prozess-check/blog/multilingual-customer-communication).

Reply in the thread they used. A fresh mail they do not recognise, plus a portal status they did not open, is how you get a second chase an hour later.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a small machining shop, three open customer orders for the same family of turned parts. On Monday, purchasing accepts that bar stock will arrive later than the date the system assumed. A planner changes one order from week 14 to week 16. The other two orders do not move. Their material is already in the rack.

The flow sees one changed date. It drafts a mail to that customer: their order number, your order number, the article, the quantity, week 14 as the previous confirmed date, week 16 as the new one, quantity unchanged. The customer is on the key-account list, so the draft goes to the sales lead, not to the outbox. She calls in the afternoon, uses the two dates as the brief, and writes one sentence afterwards so the file matches what she said. The other two customers receive nothing that day, because their dates did not change.

On Wednesday one of those two writes "where is my order". The reply quotes week 14, still the confirmed date, and says nothing has changed since the confirmation. It does not mention the other customer's delay. On Friday a further order, not a key account, moves from week 15 to week 16. A draft is created. A person in the office reads it, checks the numbers against the system, and sends it. Nobody is told a supplier name. None of these customers is a client of ours. The weeks are here so the rule is visible: only a changed, accepted date starts a message.

## When not to build this

Skip it when three customers are called by the planner as a habit and the date almost never moves. The call is the product. Skip a parallel mail when a portal the customer uses already shows the date you mean, and you have checked that they look at it. Skip the model entirely when the date in the system is typed on a Friday to make a report look calm. And skip any flow that would answer a claim for damages or a contractual penalty. That is not a delivery note. It is a conversation with whoever owns the account, and often with your adviser.
