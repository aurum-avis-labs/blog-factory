## Data protection for property managers in Switzerland

Data protection for property managers in Switzerland centres on tenant and applicant dossiers, not on generic customer CRM hygiene. Salary statements for affordability checks, ID copies, debt enforcement extracts, correspondence about noise complaints, and access logs all identify people. Under the [FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de) you need lawful grounds, retention limits, and security suited to how your team actually works on site and in the car.

This is for administration leaders in residential or mixed portfolios with tens to a few hundred employees across Switzerland. Orientation, not legal advice.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Applicant data deleted after a defined period if no lease is signed</li>
  <li>Tenant files segmented by object; cleaners and externals see only their buildings</li>
  <li>Secure channel for sending contracts; no ID scans over personal WhatsApp</li>
  <li>Processor list for maintenance software, portals, and credit agencies</li>
  <li>Finance trained on invoice IBAN changes per BACS guidance on business email compromise</li>
  <li>Visitor and contractor rules so mandate details are not discussed in lifts</li>
</ul>

## Cases that fit property work

A property manager leaves printed applicant files on the dashboard while inspecting a flat with a prospect. A caretaker photographs a tenant's open debt enforcement letter and sends it to a neighbour «to warn others». A portal breach exposes bank details used for rent refunds. A phishing mail to accounts payable swaps the IBAN on a supplier invoice for heating maintenance. A student flat mix-up publishes one tenant's phone number to the whole building chat. These are administration stories, not recruiter stories.

## What it is not

Not a guide to recruitment agencies storing CVs. Not mortgage advice. Not a full [DPIA](/security/blog/when-a-dpia-is-required-in-switzerland) manual; tenant self-service with biometrics still needs a project screen. Your own HR job applications follow different retention rules than tenant applicants.

## What to do first

Map where applicant PDFs live after rejection (CRM, mail, phone album, paper in the van). Test who can export the full tenant list from your software. Align with counsel on retention for resolved disputes. Train front desk and finance together on [security and privacy overview](/security) sessions that cover mail fraud and everyday data handling in German or English.

## A made-up example: applicant folder in the van

A made-up example: an administrator with 35 staff keeps rejected PDFs in a shared phone album «to save time». After a phone theft, photos include passports and salary slips. The owner stops the practice, moves to object-based folders with deletion after 90 days, and runs a one-hour session on phones and mail fraud. Documented changes, not a marketing claim.

## Software, access, and contractors

Caretakers need tenant phone numbers for emergencies, not full application files from unrelated objects. Mobile apps that bundle keys, alarm codes, and medical notes about residents blur purposes. Write a data sheet per building with the minimum fields.

Craftsmen see names on doorbells and invoices. NDAs help but behaviour matters: no photos of interiors with visible mail on tables in social media posts by subcontractors. Key lists with tenant names on tags are personal data; digital lock logs from pilots belong on the same risk list as the basement folder.

## Finance, heating, and owners

Ordering a report reveals financial data to a third party. Keep the legal basis and retention for each report copy. Do not forward PDFs to owners «for information» without checking mandate.

Supplier mail fraud changes IBANs on maintenance invoices. Finance needs a callback rule independent of the property manager who forwarded the PDF from the tenant mailbox thread. After accounts staff change, check mailbox forwarding rules; new hires often do not know old forwards.

Owner meeting minutes with names and flat numbers face the same access and retention questions as tenant files.

## Foreign applicants and shared flats

Foreign applicants often send passports and visa copies. Apply the same deletion period as for domestic applications, not «keep everything for later». Cleaning subcontractors should not take tenant lists off site. When sending service charge statements by mail, check whether the PDF exposes every flatmate's bank details. A wrong recipient is a breach like any other SME; clarify your internal high-risk path with counsel.

## Tenant rights and incidents

Tenants also request access and correction. Know where data lives: CRM, paper, mail, contractor apps. Use counsel for deadlines.

Mould reports or disputes may include health notes in expert reports; limit what owners receive.

Repair subcontractors often see more than the invoice shows. Brief them on site: no photos with visible post, no mandate details in the lift. A visitor book and contractor registration reduce «who was in the flat when» questions after an incident.

When owners ask for digital access to tenant portals, check the mandate and minimise: read access rather than a full export. Portals that trigger refunds belong on the same risk list as e-banking, because fraudsters target property-management mail.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>May we keep rejected applications for «the next vacancy»?</h3>
  <p>You need a clear purpose and retention limit. Indefinite pools of applicants are hard to justify; document a short period or fresh consent.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>May owners see all tenant correspondence?</h3>
  <p>Only what the management contract and law allow. Minimise forwarding full dossiers by mail.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>What if a phone with viewing photos is lost?</h3>
  <p>Treat as potential breach if IDs or financial data are visible. Document, contain, seek advice on notification to the FDPIC when high risk is likely.</p>
</div>

Once a year, one hour: new buildings, new software, new field staff. ## Access and erasure for tenants

Tenants can request access and correction. Keep a list of storage locations ready before the first request arrives. Deletion after a contract ends usually needs coordination with the archive and the owner; set the periods with counsel instead of improvising. Paper files in the basement and cloud folders count equally, so do not leave the search to IT alone.
