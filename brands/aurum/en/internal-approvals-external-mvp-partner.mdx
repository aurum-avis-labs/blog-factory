## Internal approvals for an external MVP partner

Internal approvals for an external MVP partner usually mean three parallel tracks before you sign: legal and data protection on contracts and personal data, IT or information security on access and suppliers, and finance on budget and payment terms. You collect the same facts for each track once, then tailor the packet. A green light lets you spend and share data; it does not prove the idea works.

This is for innovation leads and product owners inside a Swiss company who must show the board or an investment committee a test, not another slide deck. It is not for a full enterprise transformation RFP, and not for founders who already own the company and need no internal gate. Partner selection itself sits in [getting an MVP built for you](/blog/getting-an-mvp-built-for-you). Budget framing is in the related posts on approval and corporate venture building.

## What each gate actually asks

Legal wants to know who owns the code, who may process personal data, and what happens if the engagement ends. IT security wants to know where data live, who can log in, which subprocessors you add, and how secrets are handled. Finance wants a capped scope, a payment rhythm, and whether the spend is capex, opex, or a project code. Procurement may add a supplier form even when the amount is small.

None of these teams wants a product pitch. They want answers they can file.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Written scope: decision the MVP should enable, success measure, explicit out-of-scope list, and end date or phase gate.</li>
  <li>Intellectual property: assignment of custom code and designs to your company, licence for third-party libraries, and what stays with the partner only if they reuse a generic framework.</li>
  <li>Data protection: inventory of personal data in the build, hosting region, processor list, and signed processing terms with each cloud or analytics vendor. See [privacy requirements for a Swiss MVP](/blog/privacy-requirements-swiss-mvp) for the product-side checklist.</li>
  <li>Access and secrets: repository and cloud accounts in your organisation, named admins, no shared passwords, and a plan for API keys in the app.</li>
  <li>Security expectations: how the partner handles vulnerabilities, whether they will share a gap review or test summary, and what you will not ask for (a full ISO audit on week two).</li>
  <li>Exit: export of code, database, and documentation if you terminate; handover calendar; what happens to pilot user data.</li>
  <li>Commercial: fixed phase price or time-and-materials cap, change control, and invoice milestones tied to demonstrable paths, not ticket counts.</li>
</ul>

## Internal approvals for an external MVP partner: the packet

Put one short document in front of all approvers. One page of scope, one page of data and hosting, one page of IP and exit, and the draft contract or statement of work. Attach the partner’s company extract if procurement requires it. If personal data are involved, attach the processor list and mark which items are still unsigned with a dated plan to sign before production users.

<aside class="blog-callout">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Note</span>
  <p>Employee-written software belongs to the employer under Swiss law when it is created in the employment relationship. External authors keep rights until assignment. Your packet should show assignment language, not assume «we paid so we own it».</p>
</aside>

## A made-up example: six weeks before signature

A made-up example: an innovation manager in a mid-sized logistics firm wants an external studio to build a pilot portal for carrier self-service. Legal receives a one-pager: work emails and shipment references are processed, hosting in Switzerland or EU with named subprocessors, processing terms to be signed before the tenth live carrier. IT receives a diagram: Azure tenant owned by the firm, two admin accounts, partner gets a scoped contributor role, secrets in a vault, no production data in the partner’s personal laptop backups. Finance receives CHF 85'000 for twelve weeks with a stop gate at week six if fewer than five carriers complete the core path. Procurement gets a completed supplier form and proof of professional indemnity. The committee approves the phase, not «digital transformation». Signing waits only on two unsigned processor terms, with a hard date before marketing sends the invite link.

That approval does not show carriers will use the portal. It shows the firm can legally and safely run the test.

## What approvals do not prove

A signed contract and a clean supplier form do not validate demand, unit economics, or security of the final code. They do not replace user interviews or analytics after launch. They do not mean IT will automatically accept every future feature without a change request. They do not satisfy a bank-grade audit if you later pivot into regulated data.

<div class="blog-pair">
  <div>
    <h3>Run the full approval path</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Real users will log in and leave personal or business data</li>
      <li>The spend exceeds your discretionary limit</li>
      <li>You use corporate email, SSO, or internal APIs</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Keep the path lighter</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Interviews only, no production database</li>
      <li>A clickable prototype with fake data on the partner’s sandbox</li>
      <li>You personally fund a sub-threshold experiment outside company systems</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>This checklist is governance around a validation build, not legal advice. Refusal rules, sector law, and employment agreements can add steps. When the data are sensitive or the buyer is regulated, stop and involve counsel before you promise dates to the partner.</p>
</aside>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Can we start discovery before all approvals finish?</h3>
  <p>Often yes, if discovery uses no production data, no customer identifiers, and a separate NDA. Many firms still want legal to see the NDA first. Do not migrate real user records into a partner environment on a verbal okay.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Does IT need ISO 27001 from the partner?</h3>
  <p>Some questionnaires ask for it. For a time-boxed MVP, IT usually accepts a proportionate answer: hosting choices, access control, dependency updates, and a written security contact. Ask what evidence is mandatory for your tier of spend instead of sending a blank enterprise checklist.</p>
</div>
