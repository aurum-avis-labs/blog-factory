## Four questions before you pick a tool

You want process automation in an SME because the manual work is not going to shrink on its own. Someone exports, copies, follows up. ChatGPT is open. Copilot may be too. The Monday report still gets built by hand. Before you add licenses or commission a project, you need a filter for the first workflow. Four questions are enough.

The questions are plain on purpose. [BDO](https://www.bdo.ch/de-ch/medienstelle/2026/ki-adoption-die-kluft-zwischen-grossunternehmen-und-kmu-wachst) noted in 2026 that large companies already pull AI into the value chain, while many SMEs stay on individual apps. Their advice: a few concrete use cases, owned by management, not treated as an IT side topic. A few cases, in practice, means one that survives the filter.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Does it repeat on a weekly or daily rhythm?</li>
  <li>Does it hurt in time, errors, or a single owner?</li>
  <li>Does it have a clear start and end?</li>
  <li>Is the input already in mail, PDF, or a system you use?</li>
</ul>

### Does it repeat?

Count how often the process happens in a normal month. Daily or weekly is a candidate. Twice a quarter is usually a project, not operations.

Write down three things: who does it, on which weekday, and what happens if that person is ill or on holiday. If the answer is "then we wait", you have frequency plus a single point of failure.

Five Excel exports every Monday: that repeats. Switching the till system once: that does not repeat, even if it hurts.

### Does it hurt?

Hurt is time, errors, or a person without whom nothing moves. Ninety minutes every Monday until the numbers add up. Three follow-up questions a week because the wrong column landed in the sheet. The office manager who is the only one who knows which file is the real one.

You do not need a stopwatch study. If the process regularly sits until it burns, it hurts. If nobody wants to do it and it is still mandatory, it hurts. If a typo knocks over an invoice or a report, it hurts.

What is not enough: "we could make this nicer someday". That is discomfort, not a bottleneck.

### Does it have a clear edge?

A start and an end. An email with a PDF arrives, the relevant fields land in bexio or Abacus. A quote goes out, seven days later a follow-up draft is ready, or the status is dead. The process has an edge if you can say in one sentence when it is finished.

No edge: calming a difficult customer. No edge: turning the proposal until it feels right. No edge: "make the numbers look coherent for management" when the question in the room changes every week.

Without an edge you are trying to automate judgement. You will fail before anyone has picked a tool.

### Is the data already there?

Email, PDF, spreadsheet, folder, bexio, Abacus, Microsoft 365. The input has to exist somewhere already. You do not need a data warehouse. You need a source you can point at: this comes in, that should go out.

The [adesso GenAI Impact Report 2026 for Switzerland](https://www.adesso.ch/adesso-ch/whitepaper/genai-impact-report-2026-ch.pdf) names data quality and missing processes as the most common blockers, not the model. That matches office life. If the Excel columns change name every week, the PDFs live in the inbox one week and on a stick the next, and nobody will say which version counts, the process is missing. A different model will not fix that.

If all four answers are yes, you have a first workflow. If three are yes and the data wobbles, the work is tidy-up, not automation.

You can run this internally in fifteen minutes. Each person in management, ops or the office names one process that grates. Put the four questions next to it. What scores three yes answers and has an owner stays. The rest waits. No workshop, no tool comparison, just that ranking.

## What you should not automate first

One-off projects are the wrong start, even when they hurt. The office move, the new website, the data clean-up before year-end accounts: once that is done, you are left with something nobody touches again. There is no repetition.

Highly individual advice does not belong at the front either. The conversation where you walk a client through their numbers. The exception you make on purpose. The call that holds the relationship. A template or a checklist is often enough. It is not a first automation case.

And the chaotic process with no owner: if three people are "sort of responsible" and the files live in four folders, you will automate the mess. First a person who owns the process. Then run the filter again.

Doing ten things in parallel feels like a plan. Next week, still nothing that anyone actually uses.

## Five Monday exports, or calming a difficult customer

Two pictures, no company names.

Case A: every Monday someone pulls five exports from three tools, pastes them into Excel, deletes rows, checks totals, sends the file to two people. It takes just under an hour, longer when something is wrong. If that person is on holiday, the report waits, or someone else fights through the same paste job.

Case B: a customer is unhappy. The tone is sharp, the complaint may or may not be fair, and someone from management or sales has to hold the conversation. The conversation is different every time. What counts is judgement. Pasting does not help.

Filter on case A: it repeats, it hurts, it has an edge (exports in, one file out), the data already sits in the tools. Candidate.

Filter on case B: it does not repeat in the same shape, it hurts (in a different way), it has no edge, half the relevant information sits in someone's head. Not a first case.

If case A sounds familiar, two or three siblings often sit next to it: invoices that stall beside the accounting software, quotes that die in the inbox, reports from too many sources. Those three workflows often pass the same filter in an SME office.

## The tool comes after the process

Only once the process is clear is the tool question honest. Sometimes a Copilot license in Microsoft 365 is enough. The pain sits in drafting, summarising, sketching, and the work already lives in Word, Outlook or Excel. Then the next step is not a build. You use the license where the process already lives, with a clear rule about which data must not go into the chat.

Sometimes the process is glue between systems. Source A into system B, same shape every week. Then Copilot inside a document does little. The problem is not the sentence. It is the stitch between systems. Then you build a workflow. Which tool that is, you decide after the filter, not after a vendor pitch.

What you do not need here is a feature list of integration platforms. That comes once the case exists. And leave bexio or Abacus in place just because a gap sits beside them. Name the gap. Keep the software you already have.
