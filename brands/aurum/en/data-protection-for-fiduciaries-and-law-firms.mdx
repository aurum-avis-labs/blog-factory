## Data protection for fiduciaries and law firms

Data protection for fiduciaries and law firms hinges on mandate and client secrecy alongside the [FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de). Payroll for clients, beneficial owner lists, litigation papers, and tax working papers are personal data even when you think "this is business". Art. 26 FADP limits how much you may disclose when answering access requests; professional rules add another layer. Security mistakes (wrong attachment, open folder on a train) can breach both client trust and data protection.

This article covers trustees and attorneys in Swiss SME-sized firms, roughly 20 to 250 people, without a dedicated data protection officer. One article, two professions, shared patterns. Orientation, not legal advice.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Matter-centric folders; no client A files in client B's directory</li>
  <li>Encryption or secure exchange for identity documents and court filings</li>
  <li>Processor agreements for hosting, e-discovery, and dictation tools</li>
  <li>Clear desk when visitors tour the office; no mandate names on whiteboards</li>
  <li>Offboarding checklist when a fee earner leaves (devices, mail, tokens)</li>
  <li>Training on CEO fraud and invoice change fraud for finance and partners</li>
</ul>

## Cases that fit mandates, not generic HR

A fiduciary assistant attaches the wrong client's salary list to an email about "the usual monthly package". A junior lawyer forwards discovery PDFs to a personal webmail to "work faster at home". A partner keeps ten years of closed mandates on a USB stick without encryption "for emergencies". A client's competitor calls reception pretending to be counsel and asks for "confirmation of the account number". A cloud e-sign tool stores passports without your IT team listing it as a processor. Each scenario is typical for trust and legal work, not for a warehouse.

## Practical close in daily work

Partners should agree who signs off on new SaaS tools. Reception needs a script for "urgent transfer" calls and for callers who ask for mandate details. A combined day on [security and privacy](/security) works for reception, juniors, and partners: real mandate questions without client names in the slides.

## Mail and attachments as the weak point

Mandate work still moves by email. Outlook autocomplete sends privileged PDFs to homonyms. Large ZIP files bypass DLP if you never configured it. Train fee earners to pause before replying to "updated bank details" threads; BACS documents rising CEO and invoice fraud as external statistics ([CEO fraud in the weekly review](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/de/26w4-de)). Technical forwarding rule checks matter after any suspicious login on a partner mailbox.

## Physical files and court

Hearings still need paper bundles. Taxis lose bags. Scanning everything to an unencrypted USB for "convenience" duplicates risk. Locked cabinets in transit, clear rules for interns copying files, and shredders for draft pleadings are low-tech controls that auditors understand.

## Access when people move between teams

Litigation and tax teams sometimes share platforms but must not see each other's open matters. Role groups in document management beat folder discipline alone. When a trainee ends, remove access the same day, including mobile tokens for signing apps.

## Client due diligence data

AML identity copies are personal data with their own retention. Do not store them in the same informal folder as marketing leads. Processor agreements for verification platforms belong next to your hosting contract.

## Retention and repeat data

In fiduciary work the same tax packages return each year; deletion after purpose still applies to addresses and identity copies for individuals. In law firms, limitation and statutory retention need counsel. A signature line asking clients not to send mandate data unencrypted is a reminder, not a control on its own.

## Client questionnaires and diligence

When clients ask about processing, keep agreements ready. For new AI contract review tools, do not paste mandate text into public models without approval. That avoids a typical Friday-afternoon chat mistake.

## Avoid shared logins

One login for several juniors in the DMS leaves no clean access trail later. Prefer short-lived accounts with expiry dates. Partner mailboxes with a shared password can be an entry point for CEO fraud; a [password manager for business](/security/blog/password-manager-for-a-business) and separate accounts are the usual alternative to Excel lists on the server. Document exceptions in writing when a mandate briefly needs several people.

## Shadow IT and new tools

Partners should ask once a year which tools were bought without IT. In law firms that is often dictation apps and translators fed with mandate text. Each new cloud needs an entry on the processor list and a decision on whether US transfer matters. That takes about twenty minutes per tool and prevents surprises in a mandate audit.

## A made-up example: wrong payroll attachment

A made-up example: a fiduciary with 35 staff. The assistant picks client B's payroll PDF instead of client A's for the monthly send. The recipient reports within an hour. The partner stops further sends, documents time and file name, informs both clients after counsel, and avoids a public blame thread in the office. The next day: two-step approval for attachments above a size threshold and a training line "read the recipient list before send".

## Seven-day start for firms without a privacy office

Monday: agree matter folder naming with partners. Tuesday: mail rules for large attachments and encryption. Wednesday: SaaS list with mandate data; open processor agreement tickets. Thursday: reception script for urgent payment calls and identity checks on the phone. Friday: test offboarding form with a fictional leaver. Next week: mailbox forwarding audit. Date-stamp each step for client questionnaires.

When clients ask about [processing agreements](/security/blog/data-processing-agreement-under-the-fadp), keep signed contracts and sub-processor lists ready, not only a marketing brochure.

## Visitors and clean desk

Office tours often reveal mandate names on monitors or whiteboards. Before visitor groups arrive, switch to neutral backgrounds or lock screens. Interns get no access to open matters until confidentiality is confirmed in writing.

## Video calls and recording

Mandate meetings on Teams or Zoom need clear rules: who may record, where chat transcripts land, who deletes after the matter closes. Recording without consent is a risk of its own alongside the FADP. IT should review guest access and external sharing yearly.

<div class="blog-faq">

</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>What if a laptop with matter files is stolen?</h3>
  <p>Remote wipe, credential reset, breach assessment. Notify affected clients when required by law and professional rules. Insurer and counsel early.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do we need a processing register?</h3>
  <p>Firms with fewer than 250 employees are generally exempt, unless they process sensitive data on a large scale or engage in high-risk profiling. The exemption does not remove security duties or processor contracts.</p>
</div>
