## Send website form enquiries to your CRM automatically

You send website form enquiries to your CRM automatically by writing each submission into one record, with an owner, the moment it arrives, and by sending a short acknowledgement from the company address. A reminder goes to that owner if the record is still untouched after the time you chose. Spam is stopped before it becomes a customer. If you have no CRM, the same record is a row in a sheet the office actually opens. The form is not a second inbox that someone checks "when there is time".

Email that is not a form is a different pile. Sorting a shared mailbox is described in [sort emails in a shared inbox](/prozess-check/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). Chasing a quote after you have sent one is described in [quote follow-ups](/prozess-check/blog/quote-follow-ups). This page ends when a person has taken the enquiry, not when the sale is closed.

## Fields before tools

A form that is one big text box is an email with extra steps. Ask for the name, the email, and a choice: the service, the product, or "something else". Leave one short line for the detail. Required fields stop empty rows. The choice is what lets you assign an owner without reading a paragraph first.

Say, next to the button, who you are and what the message is for. When you collect personal data, the Act expects the person to be told at least who the controller is, how to reach you, and the purpose ([Art. 19 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). A link to a privacy page can carry the longer text. The sentence beside the form is what people actually see. Your adviser checks the wording if the form asks for anything beyond a business enquiry. Do not ask for a date of birth, a health note, or an ID number to send a price.

## Owner, acknowledgement, reminder

The owner rule is small. In a firm of one office, it is one person. If two people sell different services, the choice on the form picks the owner. If both could answer, pick one anyway and let that person pass it on. Two owners means nobody answers.

The acknowledgement is a few lines: we have the message, a person replies within the number of working days you really keep, and this address is monitored. Send it from the company mailbox, so the reply lands where the thread belongs. Do not let the text mention a price, a delivery date, or a yes. Those are the later conversation.

Set a status the owner changes: new, in conversation, quote sent, closed. If it is still new after the limit you chose, two working days is a common house rule, the owner gets one reminder. A second reminder can go to a backup person. After that, a human looks. Do not send the customer three automatic chasers. That is the follow-up on a quote, and it has its own page, after a quote exists.

What your CRM calls a "lead" and whether it accepts a form directly is specific to that program. We have not built those connections. In a check we look at the interface your program actually offers.

## Spam, duplicates, and a missing CRM

Bots fill forms. A field that people do not see, which a bot often fills, can drop the submission before it becomes a record. A message that is only a link can wait in a side list for a person once a day, instead of paging someone at night. During the first weeks, glance at what was dropped so a real enquiry is not in that pile. There is no reliable percentage to quote. Watch your own form.

If the same email sends the same text twice in a short time, keep one record and ignore the second. People double-click. Two rows for one request produce two owners and two acknowledgements.

A sheet fails when it lives on one laptop, or when people edit it offline and overwrite each other. Put it where the office already works, with the date and the owner visible. That is enough until the volume, or the hand-off to accounting, hurts.

## Who should leave the form as email

Leave it if you receive a few messages a month and the same person answers the same day. A flow will not beat that habit.

Do not automate a form that asks people to attach plans, CVs, or medical notes until you know where those files are stored and who can open them. The acknowledgement path above is for a business enquiry, not for a dossier.

Skip a marketing tool whose job is to score the visitor. This page is about not losing the message. A score is a different decision, and it needs the same care as any other automated judgement about a person.

## A made-up example: fifteen forms in a week

A made-up example: a firm of 18 people, not a client, with a contact form and no CRM. In a week, fifteen submissions arrive. Three fill the hidden field and never become rows. One is only a link and sits in the side list. The office lead checks that list on Friday and deletes it. Eleven rows land in a sheet: name, email, the service they picked, the free line, the date, the owner, the status. Two services have two owners. The acknowledgement says a person replies within two working days. On Thursday one row is still "new". The owner gets a reminder, replies, and sets the status to "in conversation". When a quote goes out later, the follow-up is the other process, not a second copy of this form.

No conversion rate is claimed. The sheet shows who still owes an answer. That was the missing part.
