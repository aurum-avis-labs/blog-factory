<p class="blog-lede">A developer account as a company means the legal entity, not a founder's personal login, owns App Store Connect and Google Play Console, appears as the seller, and can add teammates. You still need a real organisation, verification, and someone with authority to sign.</p>

## Developer account as a company

You register a company developer account when the product belongs to the firm and the App Store seller name should match the GmbH or AG in the commercial register, not a private individual. Google Play asks for an account type when you create the console profile.

This is for founders in Switzerland who already have a web MVP or wrapped app and are deciding whose name goes on the store before the first submission. For why Aurum ships deployable apps before measuring demand, see [why we build production-quality MVPs](/blog/why-we-build-production-quality-mvps). Submission and guideline 4.2 sit in [publish an app on the App Store](/blog/publish-an-app-on-the-app-store) and [App Store guideline 4.2](/blog/app-store-guideline-4-2).

## When a company account is worth the admin

A personal Apple enrollment suits some sole proprietors. Once a registered company will hold the code, brand, and revenue or work with external developers, a personal seller name clashes with company contracts. Agencies should not enroll in their own name; Apple expects the client organisation to enroll and add the agency to the team. More than one person uploading builds or managing TestFlight is another argument for the company account.

## Apple Developer Account for an organisation

For an organisation, Apple requires legal entity status (no trade name or branch alone), a D-U-N-S Number, a work email on the company domain, a public website on the organisation's domain, and a person with authority to bind the company. D-U-N-S Numbers are free in most jurisdictions per Apple’s enrollment page. Membership is 99 USD per year where listed ([enrollment support](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/)).

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Match names to the register</h3><p>Use the exact legal entity name from your commercial register entry, not a product nickname.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Resolve D-U-N-S before you start</h3><p>Search whether your entity already has a number; a new request can take days. Apple’s page describes lookup and request, not third-party expedite fees.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Prepare domain email and site</h3><p>A placeholder registrar page or only a social profile is unlikely to pass Apple’s website check.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Enrol on the web or in the Developer app</h3><p>Organisations complete verification before purchase; follow the email from Apple Developer Support.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Add developers, not a shadow owner</h3><p>Contractors get roles in your team; the organisation remains seller if you distribute on the App Store.</p></li>
</ol>

## Google Play with a business profile

Plan who owns the Google account tied to Play Console long term. Check the registration fee on Google’s signup flow before you budget; fees vary by account type and region and are not stated here.

Many new personal accounts need a closed test with at least twelve testers for fourteen continuous days before some production releases ([Play Console help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)). That rule is one reason to set up a stable company profile once Android is in scope.

## A made-up example: two founders before launch

A made-up example: two technical co-founders, not a client. Their GmbH sits in the register under a long legal name while the product brand is shorter. They request a D-U-N-S on the legal name, wait eight working days, and enrol with a director’s firm email. A contractor gets App Manager access instead of the director’s Apple ID password. Google Play uses a company account forwarded to finance. No store listing is live; the step only cleared admin blockers, not demand.

## What a company developer account does not prove

Verified seller identity is housekeeping, not validation. You can still fail review under 4.2 or stall in closed testing. Store access does not replace data protection paperwork or payment design.

If the question is still whether anyone books a call or the landing page converts, store accounts spend calendar time without measurement. If the hypothesis needs installs or mobile-only flows, accounts belong in the experiment early.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Can our agency use its Apple account and list our startup as seller?</h3>
  <p>No for App Store distribution. Apple expects your organisation to enroll and add the agency to your team. The agency’s name would be seller on their account, not yours.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do we need the company account before we build?</h3>
  <p>No. Xcode installs on devices for development without membership. Enrol when you are ready to distribute, use TestFlight with external testers at scale, or ship production builds you intend to measure.</p>
</div>
