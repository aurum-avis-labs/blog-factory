## Product Hunt launch for an MVP

A Product Hunt launch for an MVP is a scheduled day on [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/) where you publish a product page, ask your network and strangers to upvote and comment, and absorb a short burst of traffic to your site or app. It is a visibility event, not a substitute for watching people use the product or paying for acquisition you can repeat. Founders with an idea or pre-seed budget often treat a strong rank as proof; the useful read is narrower: did the right visitors understand the job, try the core action, and leave a signal you can log?

This page is for founders and innovation leads in Switzerland who already have something clickable, not for teams that only need another marketing channel on a mature product. If the open question is whether the build is honest enough to learn from, read [why we build production-quality MVPs](/blog/why-we-build-production-quality-mvps) before you optimise launch copy. Store submission and guideline 4.2 are separate gates, covered in [publish an app on the App Store](/blog/publish-an-app-on-the-app-store) and [App Store guideline 4.2](/blog/app-store-guideline-4-2).

## Why teams reach for Product Hunt early

The usual story is cheap reach. You have no ad budget, your LinkedIn network is small, and a friend says one good Product Hunt day "changes everything". Sometimes the spike is real: comments, newsletter mentions, a few hundred curious visits. The mistake is to treat that spike as a channel. Product Hunt ranks products for its own audience on its own clock. Tomorrow the homepage moves on. Unless you capture behaviour in your product and follow up, the launch is a memory, not a funnel.

Internal work at Aurum on [CitySage](/portfolio/citysage) looked similar: friends and family used the browser product, then a mix of Instagram, Product Hunt, and light Google Ads brought attention. It did not become a repeatable acquisition channel. That is a useful outcome to report honestly. It is not a reason to hide the test; it is a reason not to publish dashboard figures as if they were universal benchmarks.

## What you do before launch day

Treat the launch as one experiment with a written question, not as theatre.

<ol class="blog-flow">
  <li><strong>Ship</strong><p>A URL where a stranger can finish the core action without you in the room. A demo video alone is weak evidence.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Measure</strong><p>Analytics and session recordings on from the first visit, so launch traffic is not a blank spot in your log.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Prepare the page</strong><p>One sentence job, three screenshots or a short clip, maker comment that answers "who is this for?" without jargon.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Schedule</strong><p>Pick the day on Product Hunt, warn supporters once, and accept that most learning still happens on your domain, not on the platform.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Define stop rules</strong><p>What signup, activation, or reply rate would still leave the idea unproven? Write it before the upvotes arrive.</p></li>
</ol>

## What a Product Hunt launch can show, and what it cannot

<ul class="blog-facts">
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Can show</span><strong>Message clarity</strong><p>Comments and clicks hint whether tech-curious visitors grasp the job in one pass.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Can show</span><strong>Technical nerve</strong><p>A spike stress-tests signup, onboarding, and error pages you never saw in quiet testing.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Cannot show</span><strong>Retention</strong><p>Launch-day visits are not a habit. You need return sessions over weeks.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Cannot show</span><strong>Repeatable acquisition</strong><p>Rank and upvotes are not a paid channel you can tune like search or LinkedIn.</p></li>
</ul>

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>A Product Hunt launch does not prove product-market fit, willingness to pay, or that Swiss B2B buyers will adopt your workflow. It is one day of attention. If the only users who convert already knew you, log that and change the next test.</p>
</aside>

## Measurement and privacy on launch day

If you switch on analytics, heatmaps, or remarketing, tell visitors on the landing page before ads or Product Hunt traffic arrives. In Switzerland, what consent you need depends on how you use those tools, not on which channel sent the visitor. That is not legal advice; with several providers it is worth asking an adviser. Without measurement you will only see upvotes, not whether strangers fail or come back.

## Product Hunt versus measured acquisition

Product Hunt is good at collecting launch energy in one place. It is poor at answering "what does cost per lead look like when we target finance leads in Zurich?" That second question needs a landing page you control, stable targeting, and spend you can explain week by week. The twelve-week product validation package Aurum sells includes positioning, a landing page, and paid acquisition on Google and LinkedIn in weeks 5 to 6, then cost per lead, conversion, and drop-off through weeks 7 to 10. It does not promise a Product Hunt campaign. Use Product Hunt when you want a public milestone and qualitative feedback; use paid search or social when you need numbers tied to a segment.

## Who should launch on Product Hunt, and who should wait

<div class="blog-pair">
  <div>
    <h3>Reasonable timing</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>You have a live MVP, not a slide deck, and you can watch sessions tonight</li>
      <li>Your audience includes makers and early adopters who actually read Product Hunt</li>
      <li>You will follow every comment with a fix list, not a victory post</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Better to wait or skip</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Your buyer is a risk-averse B2B committee that never visits launch sites</li>
      <li>Onboarding breaks on mobile and you hope nobody notices</li>
      <li>You have no plan after launch day except "see what happens"</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

## Questions founders still ask

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do you need an app store listing before Product Hunt?</h3>
  <p>No. Many launches point to a web app. If you also ship iOS or Android, make sure the store build matches what you show on the page. A wrapped site that fails guideline 4.2 hurts both channels.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Should Swiss founders launch in English only?</h3>
  <p>Product Hunt is largely English. If your first paid validation is German-speaking SMEs, a PH launch is optional colour, not the main test. Match the channel to the segment you need evidence on.</p>
