## Prepare monthly payroll by closing the month's inputs

<p class="blog-lede">To automate payroll preparation, close five inputs before anyone opens the payroll program: hours, absences, staff changes, approved expenses, and a named person who has checked the exceptions.</p>

The program calculates the wage and files declarations when it is certified to do so. A side spreadsheet that "helps" the program usually creates a second wage.

This is for the office lead or owner in a Swiss firm where payroll already runs in a program. The painful hour is the week before the run, when timesheets, chat messages and a leaver's last day are still scattered. Setting up a new hire for the first time is a different job, in [automating onboarding](/prozess-check/blog/automate-employee-onboarding). This page is the monthly repeat.

## What belongs in the input pack

Fix a cut-off. For a monthly run, the second working day of the next month is often enough if people can still submit the last days of the period. After the cut-off, late hours wait for the next run. They do not land in a run that has already been checked.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Hours and overtime</h3><p>One source per person: the signed timesheet, or a fixed monthly salary with nothing to add.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Absences</h3><p>Type and dates. Holiday, sick, accident, military, unpaid. The program applies the rule. The office does not recalculate insurance.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Changes</h3><p>Entry, exit, workload, function, address, bank, withholding-tax canton. Recorded when they happen, not reconstructed on payroll day.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Expenses</h3><p>A closed, approved list for the period, or an explicit "none". Open receipts stay in the next month.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Exceptions</h3><p>A person looks at missing signatures, implausible hours and changes with no date. Then the run starts.</p></li>
</ol>

Hours in three places, paper, chat and a private workbook, are the usual failure. Pick one path for hourly staff and one flag for people on a fixed monthly wage with no variable hours. Overtime needs the same path, with a mark that a supervisor has seen it. An unsigned sheet is not zero. It is missing and stays on the exception list.

If leave is already requested digitally, the approved dates should be the same lines payroll needs. How a [digital leave request](/prozess-check/blog/leave-requests) is built is its own workflow. For payroll preparation, the only question is whether approved days arrive as closed rows.

## Master data and Swissdec, without double work

Swissdec's salary standard, ELM, is the electronic declaration from a certified payroll program. The association states that one transmission can serve the recipients you choose, and that entries and exits can be reported to the AHV compensation office, and withholding-tax mutations during the year, from that program. It works only if master data in the payroll program is current. The simplest approach is to record changes as they happen.

The monthly pack is not a second HR system. It is the list of changes that must be in master data before the run: a leaver's last day, a move from 80 to 100 per cent, a new address, a new bank account, a move that affects withholding tax. If a joiner's master record is missing on payroll day, the run waits. It does not invent one.

Do not email wage files to insurers, software vendors or Swissdec. The association asks firms not to. Transmission is the step inside the certified program, after inputs are clean. This page does not describe that click. Your vendor's support does.

## Absences, expenses and the exception list

An absence needs a type and dates that do not overlap another absence for the same person. Holiday, illness, accident, military service and unpaid leave are not the same input, because the program treats them differently. The office does not need to know the tariff. It needs the type the payroll program already uses, written the same way every month.

A person who was absent and also logged hours on the same day is an exception, not a silent average.

Expenses that belong on the wage are a closed batch: approved, for this period, in the currency and amount you will actually pay. A receipt photo without approval is not an input. If the batch is not ready, defer it and note that on the pack.

Automation can assemble the pack and flag gaps. It should flag, not guess. Useful flags:

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>A person with neither a fixed salary nor hours</li>
  <li>Hours above a threshold you set, or hours on a day already marked absent</li>
  <li>A leaver with no end date, or an end date and hours after it</li>
  <li>A joiner on the hours list but not in payroll master data</li>
  <li>Expenses still open after the cut-off</li>
</ul>

A person clears the flags or accepts them in a note. Then the payroll run starts in the program you already pay for. A parallel file that "looks like" the declaration is how figures drift.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: eighteen people, fourteen on a fixed monthly wage, four who report hours. The cut-off is the second working day. By then the four hourly sheets are in one form, each with a supervisor's name. One person left on the 20th. The end date was written when the letter was signed, so the pack shows the date and no hours after it. One address change is on the change list. Expenses for twelve people are approved. Two receipts arrived on the cut-off morning and are marked deferred. The office lead sees two flags: one sheet without a supervisor name, and one hourly total far above the person's usual month. She gets the signature and asks about the hours before the run. The payroll program calculates. Nobody retypes the fourteen fixed wages into a sheet to check the total beside the program.
