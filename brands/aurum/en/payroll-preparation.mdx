## Prepare payroll by collecting the month's inputs, not by retyping the wage

Prepare monthly payroll by closing five inputs before anyone opens the payroll program: hours and overtime, absences, staff changes, approved expenses, and a named person who has checked the exceptions. The payroll software calculates the wage and, if it is certified for it, sends the declarations. A side spreadsheet that "helps" the program usually creates a second wage.

This is for the office lead or the owner in a Swiss firm where payroll is already in a program, and the painful hour is the week before the run, when timesheets, chat messages and a leaver's last day are still scattered. Setting up a new hire for the first time is a different job, described in [automating onboarding](/blog/automate-employee-onboarding). This page is the monthly repeat.

## What belongs in the input pack

Fix a cut-off. For a monthly run, a date such as the second working day of the next month is enough, if people can still submit the last days of the period. After the cut-off, late hours wait for the next run. They do not get typed into a run that has already been checked.

The pack is short:

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Hours and overtime</h3><p>One source per person: the timesheet the supervisor has signed, or a fixed salary with nothing to add.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Absences</h3><p>Type and dates. Holiday, sick, accident, military, unpaid. The program applies the rule. The office does not recalculate the insurance.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Changes</h3><p>Entry, exit, workload, function, address, bank, withholding-tax canton. Recorded when they happen, not reconstructed on payroll day.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Expenses</h3><p>A closed, approved list for the period, or an explicit "none". Open receipts stay in the next month.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Exceptions</h3><p>A person looks at missing signatures, implausible hours and changes with no date. Then the run starts.</p></li>
</ol>

Hours that live in three places, a paper sheet, a chat and a private workbook, are the failure. Pick one path for hourly staff and one flag for people on a fixed monthly wage who have no variable hours. Overtime needs the same path, with a mark that a supervisor has seen it. An unsigned sheet is not zero. It is missing, and it stays on the exception list.

## Changes are not a yearly surprise

Swissdec's salary standard, ELM, is the electronic declaration from a certified payroll program. The association states that one transmission can serve the recipients you choose, and that entries and exits can be reported to the AHV compensation office, and withholding-tax mutations during the year, from that program. It also states that this works only if the master data in the payroll program is current, and that the simplest way is to record changes as they happen.

So the monthly pack is not a second HR system. It is the list of changes that must land in the payroll master data before the run: a leaver's last day, a change from 80 to 100 percent, a new address, a new bank account, a move that affects withholding tax. The first setup of a joiner, contract, access and the initial master record, belongs to onboarding. If that record is missing on payroll day, the run waits for the record. It does not invent one.

Do not email wage files to the insurer, to the software vendor or to Swissdec. The association asks firms not to. The transmission is the step inside the certified program, after the inputs are clean. This page does not describe that click. Your vendor's support does.

## Absences and expenses as closed lines

An absence needs a type and dates that do not overlap another absence for the same person. Holiday, illness, accident, military service and unpaid leave are not the same input, because the program treats them differently. The office does not need to know the tariff. It needs the type the payroll program already uses, written the same way every month.

A person who was absent and also logged hours on the same day is an exception, not a silent average.

Expenses that belong on the wage are a closed batch: approved, for this period, in the currency and the amount you will actually pay. A photo of a receipt that nobody has approved is not an input. If the batch is not ready, defer it and say so on the pack. Paying a half-finished claim, then reversing it, is more work than waiting a month.

How people photograph receipts and how a manager approves them is its own workflow. For payroll, the only question is whether that workflow has handed over a closed list.

## What a person still checks

Automation can assemble the pack and flag gaps. It should flag, not guess. Useful flags:

- A person with neither a fixed salary nor hours.
- Hours above a threshold you set, or hours on a day already marked absent.
- A leaver with no end date, or an end date and hours after it.
- A joiner who appears on the hours list and not in the payroll master data.
- Expenses still open after the cut-off.

A person clears the flags or accepts them in a note. Then the payroll run starts in the program you already pay for. The declaration to insurers and authorities, if you use ELM, is that program's step. Building a parallel file that "looks like" the declaration is how figures drift.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: eighteen people, fourteen on a fixed monthly wage, four who report hours. The cut-off is the second working day. By then the four hourly sheets are in one form, each with a supervisor's name. One person left on the 20th. The end date was written when the letter was signed, so the pack shows the date and no hours after it. One address change arrived by mail on the 12th and is already on the change list. Expenses for twelve people are approved and closed. Two receipts arrived on the cut-off morning and are marked deferred. The office lead sees two flags: one sheet without a supervisor name, and one hourly total that is far above the person's usual month. She gets the signature and asks the supervisor about the hours before the run. The payroll program then calculates. Nobody retypes the fourteen fixed wages into a sheet to "check the total by hand" beside the program.

## Who should skip this

Skip a workflow if the firm is a handful of fixed salaries, overtime is rare, expenses are nil, and the payroll person already runs a five-line list from memory without hunting through chats. The program's own checklist is enough. Skip it if you have no payroll program and someone calculates wages in a workbook. The first step is a proper payroll program, not an automated feed into a workbook. Skip it if a fiduciary runs payroll entirely from papers you already hand over on a fixed day, and that day is calm. Do not add a tool that duplicates their intake form.

The work that pays is the scattered input: hours in a chat, a leaver mentioned in a corridor, expenses still open when the run should start.

## When a Prozess-Check fits

A [Prozess-Check](/prozess-check) fits when that gathering is the recurring task: the same chase before every run, the same late sheet, the same change that reaches payroll a month late. You bring this one task. The meeting is 60 minutes on Microsoft Teams. You leave with a concrete case, whether it pays off, a price range and a rough duration. The hour does not commit you to a project.
