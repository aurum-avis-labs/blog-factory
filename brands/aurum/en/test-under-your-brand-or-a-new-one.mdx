<p class="blog-lede">You test under your brand when the buyer already trusts the firm and the test fits what you sell today; you test under a new brand when the segment, price, or promise would confuse existing customers or when a failed test should not stick to the core reputation.</p>

## Test under your brand or a new brand

The naming choice is not vanity. It decides who picks up the phone, whether procurement treats the pilot as "another product from the same vendor", and whether a weak landing page reads as "the whole company is pivoting". Most founders who already shipped a prototype in Lovable, Bolt, v0, Replit, Cursor, or Claude Code face this fork the moment they put the link on a paid page or send it to a buyer list.

This is for founders and innovation leads in Switzerland with something clickable who must decide what name sits on the URL and the invoice footer. For internal evidence packs, see [validate a new product idea inside a company](/blog/validate-a-product-idea-inside-a-company). For stage-gate next to lean loops, see [stage-gate vs lean startup](/blog/stage-gate-vs-lean-startup). ## A short decision path

<ol class="blog-flow">
  <li><strong>Segment</strong><p>Would your best existing customer recognise themselves in the headline without feeling mis-sold?</p></li>
  <li><strong>Risk</strong><p>If the test fails in public, does the damage land on revenue you already have?</p></li>
  <li><strong>Channel</strong><p>Will sales, support, or the website send traffic that expects the old catalogue?</p></li>
  <li><strong>Ownership</strong><p>Can you publish privacy text, billing, and support in the name you put on the page?</p></li>
  <li><strong>Exit</strong><p>If the test succeeds, do you want to merge, spin out, or keep a separate line?</p></li>
</ol>

Write the answers on one page before you register a domain or paint a logo. The test measures demand for a job, not love for a font.

## When the existing brand is enough

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>If the product extends what you already deliver</h3><p>A treuhand adds a client portal, or a logistics firm tests driver scheduling for existing shippers. The buyer expects your name on the contract.</p></li>
  <li><h3>If warm intros come from current accounts</h3><p>Your sales team will forward the link. A mystery name triggers "who are these people?" before anyone clicks.</p></li>
</ul>

Under the house brand you still need a falsifier: a metric strangers must hit, not polite feedback from people who already like you.

## When a separate name helps

<div class="blog-pair">
  <div>
    <h3>Favour a new brand</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>The price band or buyer role is far from today’s core offer</li>
      <li>Existing customers would assume a feature is included or guaranteed</li>
      <li>You test a consumer app while the firm is visibly B2B, or the reverse</li>
      <li>A public flop should not appear in trade press as "the company failed"</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Stay on the house brand</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>The pilot sells to the same budget holder with the same procurement path</li>
      <li>Trust is the main asset and the product is a thin wrapper on your service</li>
      <li>Legal and support are already set up for one trading name</li>
      <li>You will merge the test into the main site within one quarter if it works</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

A new name is not secrecy. It is labelling so the test can fail without rewriting the story of the whole firm.

## What the naming choice proves

<ul class="blog-facts">
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Proves</span><strong>Fit of channel</strong><p>Whether the audience you targeted will act under that label.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Proves</span><strong>Message clarity</strong><p>Whether the promise matches the segment when trust cues differ.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Does not prove</span><strong>Product quality</strong><p>A strong name can win a click while the workflow breaks.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Does not prove</span><strong>Legal clearance</strong><p>A free domain is not a trademark search.</p></li>
</ul>

Ideas as such cannot be protected ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)). A new name still needs a trademark search before heavy spend; one class at the IGE is CHF 450 for ten years on the [trademark FAQ](https://www.ige.ch/de/etwas-schuetzen/marken/faq).

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Brand choice is a test design decision, not proof of product-market fit. If both names produce weak behaviour, the segment, job, or offer is wrong, not only the logo.</p>
</aside>

## A made-up example: same tool, two labels

A made-up example: a Lucerne founder tests supplier onboarding in a vibe-coded app while the firm sells metal parts. Under the firm domain, procurement assumes the feature is in last year’s frame contract and clicks fall. Under a neutral subdomain, sessions rise but half of sign-ups are students. The pack compares pricing-section bounce, not vanity metrics. The next step is a sharper job title, not a victory parade for either name.

## Who should not spend weeks on naming

If cash is tight, pick the label that reaches strangers fastest, run one paid week, and log the result. If legal blocked a public test under the main entity, a new name does not remove that block. A repackaged website still faces [App Store guideline 4.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); naming will not save a thin shell.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Does a new brand need a new company?</h3>
  <p>Not always for a validation sprint. You may trade under one entity with a product name, or add a subsidiary later. Tax and liability are for your adviser; this article only covers what strangers see on the test surface.</p>
</div>
