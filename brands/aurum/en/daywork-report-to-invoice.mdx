## From daywork report to invoice in five steps

You get from daywork report to invoice without retyping in five steps: capture on the phone or photograph the paper the same day, put details into fixed fields (who, hours, material, job, signature), check in the office, create an invoice draft in the software you already use, approve. Automation handles the copy between step one and four. The office checks instead of typing.

For office managers and owners of trades and construction businesses that bill daywork or service calls on time and materials. Day report or daywork sheet (Swiss German offices: Regierapport or Tagesrapport), same route. Plus when your trade software app is enough, one example, when to leave it. ## Step 1: Capture where the work happens

Capture on site, same day, one route. A form on the phone, or paper the foreman photographs in the evening and sends to a fixed address. Both work. Three routes side by side (app, group chat, glovebox) do not.

The signature belongs in the same step. On the phone, the customer or the site manager signs on the screen; on paper, as before. What matters is that the name and date are legible. Initials nobody can match to a person will not help you when a question comes back.

Timing counts too. The [civil engineering office of the City of Lucerne](https://www.stadtluzern.ch/_doc/5291620), for example, states in its amendments to the SIA 118 standard that daywork reports must be written daily, signed by the contractor and submitted to the site management within 5 days. Reports submitted later, or not signed by the site management, are not accepted there. Which rules apply to you is set in your contract. With clients like that, a report that spends a week in the van is more than a delay.

## Step 2: The fields every report needs

A report can only be processed automatically if it always has the same fields. Decide them once, before you automate anything.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Date and job or site, with the number from your trade software</li>
  <li>Customer or client, and who signed on site</li>
  <li>Who worked, with hours per person, start and finish, and breaks</li>
  <li>Machines and equipment with hours used</li>
  <li>Material with quantity, unit and item, named as in your item list</li>
  <li>A short description of the work, so the invoice makes sense</li>
  <li>Daywork or included in the contract, where both happen on the same site</li>
</ul>

One field that often goes missing is material. Everyone remembers the hours. The two rolls of sealing tape and the small parts from the DIY store get forgotten. It helps if the receipt is photographed together with the report.

With a photo of the paper, AI does the reading: it recognises the handwriting, pre-fills the fields and matches "2 bags of cement" to the item in your list. Anything it cannot read with confidence is flagged, not guessed.

## Step 3: Check in the office, do not retype

The office checks the exceptions, not every report line by line. The automation puts each report with pre-filled fields into a review list and flags what stands out:

- The signature is missing or illegible.
- The hours overlap with another report from the same person.
- A material does not appear in the item list.
- The job cannot be found or is already closed.
- A field the AI could not read with confidence.

Reports with no flags get a quick look and a confirmation. For the flagged ones you decide: correct it, ask the foreman, or hold it back. The rates that apply are in the contract or your price list. Write those rules down once, and the automation checks them the same way every time.

## Step 4: The invoice draft in the software you already have

The draft is created where you write invoices today: in your trade software or your accounting package. The automation does not replace that software; it fills it. Whether that runs through an interface, an import or another function depends on the product. What your software offers in terms of interfaces is something we clarify in the Check.

Attach the signed reports to the invoice as a PDF. Whoever checks the invoice then has everything together and does not need to ask where a line came from.

## Step 5: Approve and keep track

One person approves the draft and sends the invoice. The related reports are then marked as billed. That way you can see every Monday which reports are still open and how old they are. With that list, a lost sheet of paper gets noticed before the hours on it are forgotten.

## When your trade software's app is enough

Your trade software's app is enough if everyone uses it every day. Many packages have one. Sorba, for example, offers a [day report app](https://www.sorba.ch/software/app/tagesrapport) for iOS and Android: record staff, machines and material, attach photos and digital delivery notes, send the report to the office with one click, and work offline on sites without signal. If every crew uses an app like that and material comes from the item list, you do not need extra automation for this part.

The gap usually sits next to it:

- Subcontractors and temporary staff bring paper because they do not work in your app.
- Photos and agreements end up in the group chat instead of the job file.
- Material from the DIY store is on a receipt, not in the report.
- Extra work is agreed verbally and written down later.

That is exactly where automation comes in. It brings the paper, the photos and the receipts into the same route as the reports from the app. The trade software stays as it is.

## An example: a plumbing firm with 40 reports a week

An example, not a client: a plumbing and heating firm with around 15 staff, three crews on building sites and two service engineers. Around 40 reports are written each week. The service engineers use the app that comes with the trade software; the site crews use paper. On Friday afternoon the office manager types up the paper reports and hunts through the group chat for photos of the extra work.

In the new process, the foreman photographs each paper report in the evening and sends it to a fixed address, along with the receipt if material was bought. The next morning the reports sit in the review list with pre-filled fields, next to the ones from the app. Three are flagged: a missing signature, an unknown item name, and two reports from the same fitter with overlapping hours. The office manager sorts out those three, confirms the rest, and the invoice drafts are ready in the trade software.

What stays the same: the prices, the daywork rates, the approval. What goes: the retyping and the searching through the chat. How many hours that adds up to in your business depends on how many reports there are and how long one takes today. We work that out in the Check with your numbers.

## When to leave it

Leave it if only a few reports come in each month. A clean form and a fixed day for invoicing will cost less than any automation. Leave it too if almost everything is billed at a fixed price and daywork is the exception.

Wait if every report looks different or the rates are not written down anywhere. Introduce a single standard form first and write the prices down. After a month with the same fields, an automation has something to hold on to.

If your crews already use the app without gaps, look for the manual work somewhere else. Which workflows are often worth it in an SME office is in [three workflows worth automating](/prozess-check/blog/three-workflows-worth-automating).
