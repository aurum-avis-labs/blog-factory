## Prepare the year-end stocktake from the system you already keep

Prepare the year-end stocktake by freezing a count list from the system that already holds your articles, counting each location once, and writing down quantity differences. The fiduciary, or whoever signs the accounts, sets the value. The count proves how many units you have. It does not replace the annual accounts.

This is for owners and office leads in a Swiss firm that keeps goods, spare parts or material, and that already has those articles in an accounting or inventory program, or in a list that acts as one. A pure service firm with no stock can skip the wall-to-wall count. It still has to show what sits on the balance sheet.

## What the accounts need from the count

The accounts need a reliable quantity, not a second valuation method written by the warehouse. Under the Code of Obligations, each figure in the balance sheet and the notes has to be shown by an inventory or by some other method. The federal SME portal states that duty for companies that keep accounts under those rules, and it points to article 958c paragraph 2. A physical count is the usual way to show goods. It is not the only way the statute allows.

Valuation stays with the person who prepares the accounts. If, on the balance-sheet date, the amount you could sell the goods for, after costs still to come, is below what you paid or what they cost to make, that lower figure has to go into the accounts. The statute calls those goods raw material, work in progress, finished goods and merchandise. A count sheet that invents a market price is outside the job of the person with the scanner.

The most common balance-sheet date is 31 December, and the balance sheet is a snapshot of that day. Goods that arrive on 2 January belong in the next year. Goods that left on 30 December do not belong in the count, even if the invoice is still open.

## Take the count list from the system, not from a fresh workbook

The count list is an export of what the system thinks is on hand: article, description, unit, quantity, location. If the articles live in a workbook that the warehouse trusts more than the accounting program, that workbook is the system for this job. A second file, opened in December and typed up from memory, splits the truth. That pattern is the same one described in [Excel as a shadow system](/blog/excel-as-shadow-system).

Before anyone counts, agree a cut-off. Book or note every receipt and every issue up to that hour, including goods in the van, on a job, at a customer, or in consignment that you still own. Units must match the article list. "Box" on the shelf and "piece" in the system is a false difference.

If the program can print or export that list, use the export. If it cannot, a dated copy of the article list with a blank counted column is enough. Do not retype the whole catalogue.

## Count once, on one path

One location, one person or one pair, one form. Paper the same day, or a form on a phone the same day. Two paths, a photo in a group chat and a sheet in a drawer, means the office counts twice and still misses a line.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>A count list dated to the cut-off, with article, unit and book quantity</li>
  <li>Every location, including the van, the site and stock you own that is not in the building</li>
  <li>A counted quantity filled in, including zero</li>
  <li>The name of the person who counted, and the date</li>
  <li>Damaged or unsellable goods marked, not quietly left out</li>
  <li>Open receipts that arrived before the cut-off but were not booked yet</li>
</ul>

High-value or easy-to-lose lines are worth a second look by someone who did not count them. The rest does not need two full passes. Unreadable handwriting is the same problem as a missing quantity. If the paper form is the path you keep, photograph it the same day and keep the photo with the list. The office should not retype a sheet that already had the article numbers on it.

## Reconcile quantities, then stop

The difference is counted quantity minus book quantity. Look for a cause before you change the system: a receipt not booked, an issue booked twice, a unit mismatch, goods in a van, scrap, or a real shortage. Only then does a person sign the quantity off.

Do not "correct" the difference by changing the price. A cheaper price hides a missing unit. The fiduciary needs the quantity you stand behind, the article, the location and a one-line reason where the figures do not match.

The stock figure that later shows up in a management report is only as good as this quantity. How that number is pulled into a Monday overview is a different job, covered in [reporting from five sources](/blog/reporting-from-five-sources).

## What you hand over

Hand over a stock list the fiduciary can file: article, unit, counted quantity, book quantity, difference, location, and a note on damaged goods. Say which items you own but do not hold, and which items you hold but do not own. Attach the count sheets or the export. Do not attach a valuation workbook.

A made-up example: a small workshop keeps filter cartridges in a program. The export says 40 pieces in the store. The count finds 36 on the shelf. Two are in the service van and still belong to the firm. Two are not there. The office books the van stock to a location and flags two pieces as a shortage, with the name of the person who counted. Nobody types a new sales price onto that line. The fiduciary later sets the carrying amount from the purchase invoices and the shortage.

## Who should skip a full count

Skip a wall-to-wall count when you have no goods to show: a consultancy, a pure services mandate, an office with only stationery. Skip it as the once-a-year rescue when you already reconcile quantities every month and the December run is only the last of those reconciliations. Do not skip it because the export "looks about right". An uncounted warehouse is a guess, and a guess is a weak inventory.

Also skip automation of the count itself when the list is twenty lines and one person finishes it in an afternoon. The work worth a workflow is the repeated part: building the list, chasing uncounted locations, and typing differences back in January after the fiduciary asks the same three questions.

## When a Prozess-Check fits

A [Prozess-Check](/prozess-check) fits when one task comes back every year, or every month: exporting the count list, chasing the locations that never answer, and typing differences into the program. You bring that one task. The meeting is 60 minutes on Microsoft Teams. You leave with a concrete case, whether it is worth doing, a price range and a rough duration. The hour does not commit you to a project.
