## Sensitive personal data under the FADP

If you search for **sensitive personal data under the FADP**, you usually need to know which files in a Swiss SME need stricter handling than a name and an email address. The revised Federal Act on Data Protection lists the categories in [Art. 5(c) FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en): health, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, and data on intimate life or racial or ethnic origin, among others. Genetic and biometric data used to identify someone count as well.

This note is for HR, office managers and owners in a firm with perhaps 20 to 250 staff. It is orientation, not legal advice, and not a full catalogue of obligations.

## Everyday office cases

<ul class="blog-facts">
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Health</span><strong>HR inbox</strong><p>Sick notes, accident reports, occupational health files, vaccination status if collected.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Beliefs</span><strong>Payroll or forms</strong><p>Church tax deductions, voluntary entries on diversity forms.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Unions</span><strong>Negotiation</strong><p>Membership lists when you negotiate with a union.</p></li>
  <li><span class="blog-kicker">Biometrics</span><strong>Access</strong><p>Fingerprint time clocks when templates are stored.</p></li>
</ul>

A made-up example: A fiduciary with 45 employees forwards a scanned sick note through the general inbox because the assistant "only wanted to pass it on quickly". The note is sensitive. The customer invoice in the same thread is not. Leaving both in one "Inbox" folder is the mistake, not mislabelling the invoice.

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>HR collects sick leave</h3><p>Every certificate and every email that mentions a diagnosis belongs in a restricted HR store, not in the managing office group chat.</p></li>
  <li><h3>Reception asks for the reason</h3><p>For attendance, "not in today" is enough. Asking about illness or religion on the phone creates data you do not need.</p></li>
  <li><h3>Fingerprint time tracking</h3><p>If the system stores templates, plan retention and access like health files, not like the petty-cash log.</p></li>
</ul>

## What is usually not "sensitive" on its own

A name, email, phone number, invoice amount, job title or customer number is personal data, but not in the special category, unless health or belief data is attached. A performance note ("good teamwork") without a health link stays outside the list. Add a disability or diagnosis in the same row and the row moves into the special category.

In payroll, salary structure is personal; the band alone is usually not sensitive. Supplementary health insurer details or line items with a health link often are. Team event photos follow usual image rules; religious holiday planning counts as sensitive only if you systematically store people's beliefs.

A customer complaint is not automatically health data. A medical certificate attached to the same email is.

## What an SME should do differently

Sensitive data mainly needs **less sharing** and **clearer folders** in daily work, not necessarily a new IT project.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Separate folders and permissions from "ordinary" HR; access only for people who need the file.</li>
  <li>No scans on personal phones or in group chats without a rule.</li>
  <li>Set retention periods and delete when they end.</li>
  <li>Before pasting into AI tools: use the same traffic light as for customer data; health and payroll stay red.</li>
</ul>

If you process sensitive data on a **large scale**, the exemption from keeping a full register of processing activities under [Art. 24 of the Data Protection Ordinance](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/568/en) may not apply even below 250 employees. What "large scale" means depends on context; a practice with many patient files every day is not the same as a trading firm that archives one sick note per year. Both still treat that one note carefully.

## Breaches, trust and GDPR overlap

Losing a laptop with health files, or emailing a sick note to the wrong person, makes it more likely the FDPIC will see a high-risk breach. Context on fines and personal liability: [FADP fines and personal liability](/security/blog/fadp-fines-and-personal-liability). Intent still matters for criminal fines; carelessness still damages trust with staff and clients.

EU customers often use "special categories" in contracts (GDPR Art. 9). The labels are similar; the assessment stays case by case. Whether the GDPR applies to your firm depends on whether you offer goods to people in the EU or monitor their behaviour there. A Swiss address on the invoice does not settle that.
