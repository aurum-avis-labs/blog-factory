## Personnel file contents and retention: what Swiss SMEs keep

Personnel file contents and retention depend on the employment relationship, the Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP), and statutory holds for accounting and social insurance. The FADP requires appropriate measures ([Art. 8 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)) and deletion when the purpose is fulfilled unless law requires longer storage ([Art. 6 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). Employee data may only be processed as far as the employment relationship requires ([Art. 328b CO](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1996/236/de#art_328_b)). This text is for owners, HR, and office staff in firms of about 20 to 250 people without a full-time data protection officer. It is orientation, not legal advice.

## What belongs in the active personnel file

Typical content for a Swiss SME, where it relates to employment:

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Employment contract, amendments, job description, home office or secondary employment rules if you use them</li>
  <li>Wage slips and withholding tax papers you keep in-house, not only at the fiduciary</li>
  <li>AHV, pension fund, and accident insurance registrations and annual wage statements you hold as employer</li>
  <li>Work accident and occupational disease files, correspondence with SUVA or insurers</li>
  <li>Working time records, holiday and overtime balances if you archive them internally</li>
  <li>Appraisals and objectives you shared or discussed with the employee</li>
  <li>Training evidence for safety, data protection, or sector duties</li>
  <li>Sick notes and medical certificates in the form you need for pay and absence</li>
</ul>

Do not mix into the same file: private correspondence, chats without a work link, customer data from an old sales role, or health notes never shared with HR. A full medical history has no place in the dossier unless law or insurance demands more; incapacity and duration are enough for pay and planning.

## Retention anchors instead of one blanket rule

There is no single official list that names every sheet and one month count for every SME. You work from legal anchors and common practice and write them into an internal table. Anchors fiduciaries and HR often cite in Switzerland (orientation, not legal advice):

| Document type | Practice anchor | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payroll accounting and wage-related vouchers | often about ten years after the financial year | Accounting and audit rules; originals may sit with the fiduciary |
| Wage slips in the personnel file | duration of employment plus archive period after exit | With a destruction date in archive, not forever in the active folder |
| AHV and pension fund papers | statutory proof duties during and after employment | Align with payroll |
| Work accidents | longer than ordinary HR notes | Accident insurance and insurer mail |
| Pre-hire applications | see neighbour article | Not the same period as the active contract |

After exit: close the active dossier, move to a leaver archive with a deletion or destruction date. Backups and shared mailboxes count: [sharing files that contain personal data](/security/blog/sharing-files-that-contain-personal-data) only with a clear role and expiry.

At many SMEs, wage slips and year-end payroll sit with the fiduciary. HR may still hold copies or summaries in the dossier. State in the table which system is authoritative and who may pull a copy for audit. Conflicting data in Excel and paper is a typical failure for access requests.

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>When only paper exists</h3><p>Locked cabinets, no files left in meeting rooms. Scans only with a scan policy and deletion for interim copies.</p></li>
  <li><h3>When HR software holds everything</h3><p>Limit export rights; leaver workflow with archive status and destruction date in the tool.</p></li>
  <li><h3>When managers keep "their" folder</h3><p>Clarify what must enter the central file and what moves to HR on termination, not in a director’s drawer.</p></li>
</ul>

## A made-up example: a table instead of folder chaos

A made-up example: a 55-person trades firm in central Switzerland keeps paper files and a small HR tool. HR and the fiduciary meet for an hour. They list ten document types, mark each as active, archive after exit, or held only at the fiduciary, and note destruction five years after exit for appraisals while wage vouchers follow the fiduciary period. Last year’s application folders are deleted after start, not copied into the dossier. IT restricts HR shares. No new software, only a one-page retention list on the intranet.

## Audit, access requests, and insurance

Auditors or insurers often want proof on pay, accidents, or training, not the full dossier with private margin notes. Provide excerpts with redaction. For FADP access requests, the same rule applies: only what you hold and what the person may see, not "export everything because it is faster".

Someone must own deletion and complaints even when HR is part-time. Leadership sets the line; HR or the office executes it.

Employees may request access under the FADP to data you hold about them. A "export everything from the CRM" dump is rarely needed; an ordered dossier speeds the reply. Internal notes you never showed the employee need review with counsel before release, not automatic inclusion.

Keep sick notes and certificates minimal in the file (duration, incapacity). [Medical certificates and the employer](/security/blog/medical-certificates-and-the-employer) covers the medical side in depth; here it is enough to treat health data as stricter than payroll lists.

## Who maintains the file when HR is part-time

In SMEs the same person often runs payroll, reception, and the "personnel file". Note in the memo who has access during absences and who runs deletions. External HR providers need a written order with purpose and deletion duty; your retention table applies to their exports too.

When someone leaves, handle it separately: archive the active dossier, switch off mail forwarding, remove HR tool access, set a destruction date. Do not forget server folders like "Staff_Archive" that nobody opens any more but that live on in backups.
