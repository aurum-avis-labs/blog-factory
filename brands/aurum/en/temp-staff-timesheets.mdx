## Process timesheets of temporary staff

You process timesheets of temporary staff on one weekly route: collect the hours from each client site, check them against the assignment, take the client's approval as a gate, then use those same approved hours for payroll and for the client invoice. Anything that does not fit goes to a person. The office stops typing the week twice.

This is for the office of a Swiss firm that employs people and lends them to client companies. The timesheet here is an Einsatzrapport: the hours of someone on your payroll, worked at someone else's site. It is not a trades daywork sheet. A daywork report bills a customer for a crew's day, including material. That route is in [from daywork report to invoice](/blog/daywork-report-to-invoice). The licence and the rules on passing candidate and worker data to a client are in [AI for recruitment and staffing agencies](/blog/ai-for-recruitment-agencies). This page is the Friday loop in between.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>One inbox for the week</h3><p>Every site uses the same route. Paper, a photo or a file, not three chats.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>The same fields</h3><p>Person, assignment, day, start, end, breaks, and who signs.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>The client signs</h3><p>No approval, no pay run and no invoice, unless that client's contract says otherwise.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Two outputs, one set of hours</h3><p>Payroll uses the pay rate. The invoice uses the rate you bill that client.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Exceptions to a person</h3><p>Overlaps, missing signatures, hours outside the assignment.</p></li>
</ol>

## Step 1: One route from every client site

Hours have to arrive in one place, for one week, on a day you name. A photo of the client's paper, an export from the client's time clock, or a short form your worker fills on the phone: any of these can work. What does not work is three routes at once. Some sites email a spreadsheet, some drop a photo in a group chat, some promise to sign "next Tuesday".

Pick the route per client and write it down. The worker or the site supervisor sends Friday's sheet to one address before Monday's payroll cut-off. A site that is always late belongs on the review list. That is not a reason to invent the hours.

## Step 2: Fields the payroll and the invoice both need

A sheet can be read automatically only if it always has the same fields. Agree them once, before you automate anything. They have to be enough for two different uses: what you pay the person, and what you bill the client.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Week, and the assignment or client number from your own system</li>
  <li>The person, named as in the payroll master, not a nickname from the site</li>
  <li>Client site, and the name of the person who signs</li>
  <li>For each day: date, start, end, and breaks of half an hour or more</li>
  <li>Hours that carry a supplement, only where the contract already names that supplement</li>
  <li>Expenses, with the receipt photographed next to the sheet</li>
  <li>A scheduled day with no hours, so an absence is not a missing sheet</li>
</ul>

The pay rate and the bill rate do not belong on the handwritten sheet. They belong on the assignment, where you already agreed them. The workflow looks them up. It does not invent a factor.

## Step 3: The client's approval is a gate

The client signs the hours before you pay them out and before you invoice them. Treat a missing or illegible signature as a stop, not as a detail to fix after the invoice has gone. If one client's written contract allows you to bill on your worker's record without that signature, follow that contract for that client only. Do not let a workflow guess which clients those are.

Timing matters because you are the employer. The person expects pay on your calendar. The client expects an invoice that matches the sheet they signed. Those two dates are rarely the same. The review list should show, on Monday, which sheets are signed, which are late, and which are blocked.

## Step 4: Payroll and the client invoice from the same hours

Approved hours are written once. Payroll reads them at the pay rate, with the supplements and absences your contract already defines. The invoice reads the same hours at the rate you bill that client, often a different figure, and attaches the signed sheet. If the two documents are typed in two sittings, they drift: a Saturday on the invoice that payroll never saw, or an expense paid out and never billed.

What your staffing or accounting software accepts as an import or an interface is something to clarify on the real file, not to assume. The monthly step after this week is clean is [preparing payroll inputs](/blog/payroll-preparation). This page stops when the week is agreed. It does not calculate social insurance.

Mark the hours as billed and as passed to payroll. Next Monday you can see what is still open, and how old it is, without opening every file.

## Step 5: What a person still checks

The office looks at the rows that fail a rule you wrote down. The rest get a short look and a confirmation.

- The signature is missing, or the name does not match anyone you know at that site.
- The same person has overlapping hours on two assignments.
- The hours sit outside the assignment: a different site, a day before the start, a day after the end.
- A supplement appears that the contract does not name.
- Start, end or a long break could not be read with confidence.
- The week, added up, sits past the weekly ceiling that applies to that activity. That is a flag, not an automatic refusal. Overtime and the right ceiling are a person's decision.

Write the rules once. The workflow applies them the same way every week. A person corrects, asks the site, or holds the sheet back.

## What the record has to show

[SECO](https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/arbeitszeiterfassung) states that Article 46 of the Employment Act requires employers to keep the records the enforcement bodies need. Duration, and the start and end of daily and weekly working time, including overtime and compensatory time, plus breaks of half an hour or more, must be visible under Article 73 of Ordinance 1. Simplified recording exists, under Articles 73a and 73b, only as an agreed exception. It is not the default for someone working a client's shift. A weekly maximum of 45 hours applies to industrial work, office staff, technical staff and large retail; 50 hours applies to other workers (Article 9 of the Act). Which ceiling fits a given assignment depends on the work. The sheet should make the check possible. It should not decide the ceiling by itself.

The employment contract already has to set working hours, wage, expenses and allowances ([Article 19 AVG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/392_392_392/de)). The timesheet records what happened. It does not replace that contract, or the written contract with the client. If the client is covered by a universally binding collective employment contract, Article 20 requires you to follow that contract's wage and working-time rules. A supplement the sheet mentions, and the contract does not, goes to a person. This is a practical reading, not legal advice.

## When the staffing software is enough

The staffing software is enough when every client site already delivers hours into it, the client approves them there, and payroll and the invoice are produced from that approval without a second typing. Many firms are halfway: the regular sites use the portal, and five other sites still send paper or a spreadsheet with different columns.

Automation earns its place on that remainder. It brings the photo and the odd spreadsheet onto the same review list as the portal hours. The staffing software stays the place where assignments, rates and invoices live.

## A made-up example: twelve client sites, one Monday list

A made-up example: a staffing office employs about 60 temporary workers, placed with twelve client companies. Most weeks, about 55 sheets come back. Eight clients export hours from their own clock. Four still use paper, photographed on Friday, sometimes cropped so the signature is missing. On Monday two people type the paper into the payroll tool, then type a similar set of lines into the invoices. The bill rate is not the pay rate. The differences show up when a client disputes a Saturday.

In the changed route, paper photos and the odd spreadsheet go to one address. On Monday the review list holds every sheet, portal rows included. Four are flagged: a missing signature, one person with hours at two sites that overlap, a night supplement the assignment does not mention, and a week past the ceiling the office has stored for that job. A coordinator resolves those four, confirms the rest, and the payroll file and the invoice drafts use the same hours. What goes is the second typing. How many hours that is in your office depends on how many sheets you handle and how long one takes today.

## When to leave it

Leave it if you lend only a few people and every client already signs in the same system you use for payroll. A fixed Monday for the exceptions will cost less than a new route. Leave it too if the sheets have no stable fields yet. Agree the fields, and use them for a month, before you ask a workflow to read them.
