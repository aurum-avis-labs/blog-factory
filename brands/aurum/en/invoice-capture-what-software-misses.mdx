Plenty of trustees and in-house bookkeeping teams already bought the first half. bexio, Abacus, Sage, DeepCloud: OCR on the document, a coding suggestion, often the bank match. Closing and exceptions stay with a person. That is as it should be. AI does not replace the trustee.

The work that still hurts every week sits *beside* the tool. An invoice as a mail attachment. A PDF from the energy portal, the mobile login, the supplier cockpit. An approval that lives in Teams or on paper. A match against a purchase order that sits in another system.

Three workflows in overview, including this one: [three workflows worth automating](/blog/three-workflows-worth-automating).

## What the software often already does

Photograph the incoming invoice or drop it in as a PDF, read fields, suggest tax and amount, suggest payment. If you already use the tool properly, do not throw it away. A second OCR "because AI" is usually waste.

The gap is the path *into* the tool. Five inboxes, three portals, an Excel clipboard because nobody has the interface. [Excel as a shadow system](/blog/excel-as-shadow-system) is often exactly that glue.

## A suggestion is not a booking

A model may suggest fields. The booking stays a human decision as soon as coding, period or input tax is unclear. Human-in-the-loop here means a queue to review, not "the AI posted it".

If every document tells a different story (a project bill with twelve exceptions), it is not a first automation candidate. Same filter as in [which process to automate first](/blog/which-process-to-automate-first): frequency, pain, a boundary, a file that already exists.
