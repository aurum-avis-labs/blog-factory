For an SME that only has customers in Switzerland and does not run a high-risk system, the EU AI Act is rarely the first lever. Day-to-day is the revised Data Protection Act, the cloud contract, and whether customer data lands in a private ChatGPT. That is in [ChatGPT and customer data](/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg). This piece stays short. It is not a law-firm reading.

The binding text and timeline sit with the [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai). If you supply into the EU, sell there, or run a system treated as high-risk there, you need your own counsel. A blog post does not replace that.

## When the Act still lands on the table

EU customers, an establishment in the EU, a product offered in the EU. Or a system that decides about people (credit, hiring, biometric identification). Then the Act is a project with a lawyer, not a blog paragraph.

For the Monday report from Excel and the invoice in the inbox, the practical list stays shorter.

## What you can still do without parsing the Act

An allow-list of tools. No customer data in Free accounts. An owner who may say what goes out. A human check on figures and personal data.

That is a team rule, not an EU commentary. That is what the [AI in Business workshop](/services/ai-in-business) is for. Building a single workflow remains the [Process Check](/prozess-check), one sentence, not a second button.
