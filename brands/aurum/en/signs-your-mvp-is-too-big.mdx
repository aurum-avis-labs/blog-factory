<p class="blog-lede">Signs your MVP is too big show up long before the backlog is "finished": you cannot name one behaviour you need strangers to repeat, every stakeholder added a must-have module, and the launch date moved twice without cutting scope. An MVP is sized for learning, not for the pitch deck version of year three.</p>

This is for founders who already have a prototype or a vibe-coded app and feel the gap between "almost ready" and "we could actually learn something". If you are still choosing between proof of concept, prototype, and MVP, start with [PoC vs prototype vs MVP](/blog/poc-vs-prototype-vs-mvp). This page is only scope: when the build is larger than the question.

## Signs your MVP is too big: the checks that hurt

An oversized MVP is not always a long feature list. It is often several **risks bundled into one release**, so a weak result does not tell you which assumption failed.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>You need two unrelated user types live on day one (for example buyers and sellers) before either side has a reason to return alone</li>
  <li>Payments, contracts, ratings, or compliance modules sit in v1 although the open question is still "will anyone complete the core job twice?"</li>
  <li>Success is defined as a dashboard of metrics instead of one primary loop you can read in analytics</li>
  <li>You added native apps, admin panels, or integrations because the template offered them, not because the hypothesis requires them</li>
  <li>Manual work is still cheaper than the code you are writing, but you keep coding because it feels more "real"</li>
  <li>You cannot explain the first session to a stranger in under a minute without listing exceptions</li>
</ul>

None of these mean the vision is wrong. They mean the **test is muddy**. You may ship on time and still not know whether to pivot. Not every extra element is bloat: production deployment and real auth belong in the build when you measure return visits, and one paid channel with analytics does when the hypothesis is reach and cost per lead. Documented gaps are different from a quiet "later".

Production quality and small scope can coexist. Confusing a large feature set with seriousness is the usual trap. [Why we build production-quality MVPs](/blog/why-we-build-production-quality-mvps) is about measurement integrity, not about shipping every module you can imagine.

## What cutting scope does and does not prove

A smaller MVP answers a **narrower question** with cleaner data. It does not prove market size, defensibility, or that your full roadmap is worth funding.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Removing features does not replace honest acquisition. If every tester is a friend, shrinking the app does not turn the pilot into market validation. Rename the stage instead of calling it an MVP.</p>
</aside>

Cutting scope **can** show whether one job is worth automating, whether strangers tolerate the friction you kept, and whether support load explodes on a thin slice. It **cannot** show network effects, full unit economics, or regulatory sign-off you deliberately postponed.

Ideas as such are not protectable in Switzerland; execution and brand are ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)). A smaller public pilot does not hide the concept. It buys a readable experiment before you fund the next layer.

## A worked example: KitchenCrew pilot scope

KitchenCrew is an internal venture on the GoldCrew backbone. The team **removed inventory from the pilot** on purpose. What stayed in the first live slice: suppliers, articles, chat or speech-to-text, order drafts, email, and spend tracking. The product was built in December 2025; a free pilot for feedback has run since April 2026.

That is a deliberate "MVP too big" correction in reverse: the full kitchen operations story could include stock, forecasting, and more. The open question for the pilot was whether restaurants would use the ordering loop and give usable feedback, not whether every back-office module existed on day one.

Acquisition matched the smaller ask. The warmest door was **conversations and events in Zurich**, not ads alone. A small Google Ads test in Zurich and Zug ran alongside; clicks did not yet turn into enough booked pilot calls. That is useful signal about channel fit, not a verdict on the whole category.

More context is on the [KitchenCrew portfolio page](/portfolio/kitchencrew). The lesson for founders is structural: when the pilot stalled on distribution, the team did not solve it by bolting inventory back in. They kept the hypothesis visible.

## Who should skip the "smaller MVP" lecture

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>If you have not proved the tech path</h3><p>Shrinking UX does not fix a broken integration. Time-box a proof of concept first.</p></li>
  <li><h3>If regulation blocks the core job</h3><p>Some products cannot legally test the real loop without certain controls. Scope still matters, but "cut everything" is not honest advice without your adviser.</p></li>
  <li><h3>If the board only funds a "complete" v1</h3><p>You may need a narrative document and a phased plan, not another weekend of features. Say what week one measures.</p></li>
</ul>

## Shrinking without losing the learning

Write one sentence: "The riskiest open question is …" Everything not needed to answer that sentence is a candidate to drop, automate manually, or fake with a wizard-of-oz back office for a fixed window.

[Prioritising MVP features](/blog/prioritize-mvp-features) helps once you agree you are in MVP stage. Until then, prioritisation meetings often add modules because silence feels like failure.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is a smaller MVP the same as a fake door?</h3>
  <p>No. A fake door tests demand with a thin surface. A smaller MVP still deploys a real loop for a narrow job, with instrumentation, so behaviour can repeat. Pick the tool to match the question.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>When is "too big" actually fine?</h3>
  <p>When each module maps to a separate metric you will read independently in the first month, and you have the capacity to support all of them. That is rare for a first release.</p>
</div>
