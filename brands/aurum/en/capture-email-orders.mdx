## Capture customer orders from email automatically

You capture customer orders from email by reading the message and its files, matching the customer and the article numbers, and writing an order draft in the system you already use. A confirmation goes out only after a person has cleared the lines that did not match. PDF forms, Excel sheets and short free-text emails all need the same fields. The copying is the part to automate. The matching that fails stays with a person.

This is for sales offices and order desks in Swiss B2B firms where customers still send the order as a file, not through a shop. It is the order case of a wider habit, typing the same facts twice, which is covered in [stop double data entry](/blog/stop-double-data-entry). Sorting which emails are orders at all sits one step earlier, in [sorting a shared inbox](/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails).

## What every order email has to contain

Every order, whatever the file type, has to yield the same fields before a draft is worth creating. Decide the list once, from the orders you actually receive, and write it down. The reading step looks for those fields and nothing else.

- Customer, matched to a number you already hold, not only to the name in the signature.
- Delivery address or site, when it is not the default address on the customer record.
- The customer's own order reference, so the confirmation and later the invoice can cite it.
- Article number as the customer wrote it, and the article number in your list once it matches.
- Quantity and unit. A quantity without a unit is not a complete line.
- Requested date, if the customer named one. If they did not, your usual lead time applies and the draft says so.

Excel is the easiest of the three when the customer uses a sheet you sent them, with fixed columns. A PDF of their own form is readable, but the layout differs by customer, so the first weeks are about learning those layouts, not about a perfect read on day one. Free text, the three-line email, is readable when the article numbers are in the text. It is a poor source when the customer writes "the usual" or "same as last month". That mail goes to a person, with the previous order beside it.

## How the draft gets into the system you already use

The draft belongs in the order system the desk already uses. The automation does not replace that system. It fills a draft the way a careful clerk would, and it stops where the clerk would also stop and ask.

If the mailbox is Microsoft 365, [Microsoft Graph](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/message-list-attachments?view=graph-rest-1.0) can list the attachments on a message. The least privileged permission on that page is Mail.Read. If the mailbox is Google Workspace, the [Gmail API](https://developers.google.com/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.messages.attachments/get) can fetch a specified attachment, with a read-only mail scope among the scopes Google lists. You are reading mail the office already receives. You are not asking the customer to change how they order.

What happens next depends on what that order system accepts. Some accept a file import. Some offer an interface. Some only have a screen. Which of those your program offers is something we clarify in the Check, against the product you actually run. We do not assume a named accounting or ERP package has an order interface until that product's own page says so.

Match customer and articles against your records, not against the spelling in the email. A customer can sign as a brand name and order under a legal name. Keep a small table of the names and article codes they use, maintained by the order desk, and let the match use that table first. New spellings fall through to the person, and a confirmed match can be added to the table so the next order is quieter.

Prices on the customer file are a check, not the price you adopt without looking. If the customer's PDF shows a unit price and your agreement shows another, the line is flagged. The draft keeps your price, and the note shows both numbers.

## Unclear lines stay with a person

An unclear line is not a failure of the tool. It is the reason a person still sits on the desk. Send the line to that person instead of guessing an article, a unit or a customer.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>If the match is unsure, the draft does not invent an article, a unit or a customer. The line waits. The rest of the order can still be prepared, if you have written that rule down.</p>
</aside>

Typical waits: no article code and a description that fits two items, a quantity with no unit, two customer records with the same name, a delivery address you have never used, or a price that does not match the agreement. Write, in one sentence each, what the desk does. Correct the line and release the draft. Ask the customer and hold the order. Split the sure lines into a draft and park the rest. The rule has to be the same on Monday and on Friday, or the automation and the person will contradict each other.

Do not send the confirmation until the draft is released. A confirmation that quotes a guessed article is worse than a short delay. The original file stays on the draft, so the person does not hunt through the mailbox.

Orders contain customer and commercial data. The mailbox already holds them. Do not paste the PDF into a free chat tool to "just read it". What belongs in which tool is set out in [ChatGPT and customer data under the Swiss FADP](/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg).

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a small wholesale office for installation parts, eight people, no in-house IT. Orders arrive at bestellung@ from about forty trade customers. A dozen of them send a PDF of their own form. Most of the rest send the Excel sheet the office issued last year, with columns for article, quantity and the customer's commission number. Two or three emails a week are free text from a foreman on site.

Each morning the flow reads the mailbox. Excel lines with a known article and a unit become draft lines. PDF lines are read into the same fields. Where the article text is "elbow 90" and two articles match, the line is marked and the draft is held. The free-text mail that says "same as Tuesday, plus six of the usual clips" is placed in a folder for the desk, with Tuesday's order linked.

The person on the desk opens the held drafts, not every email. She corrects the elbow, releases the draft, and the confirmation goes out with the customer's commission number on it. The original PDF stays on the order. Nothing in this example is a client, and it is not a claim about how many minutes you will save.

## When EDI with the large customers is the better answer

EDI is the better answer when a few large customers already send orders as structured messages, or when they require it. [GS1 Switzerland](https://www.gs1.ch/en/barcodes-standards/gs1-standards/share) describes electronic data interchange as the way to transmit transactional documents such as orders, order confirmations, delivery notes and invoices in a standardised format. It names EANCOM as the format for electronic orders, and it says that in Switzerland a further reduced subset is offered as IDEAL messages.

The GS1 Switzerland Ideal Message for orders, published in December 2022, is meant for day-to-day ordering. Its recommended practice is one purchase order for one delivery, in one location, at one time. That is a different job from reading a PDF. Both sides need the identifiers the message expects, such as location and item numbers, and a path that already speaks that format.

Use EDI for those customers and keep the email path for everyone else. Do not type EDI orders into the mailbox flow "as well", or you will create two drafts. Do not wait for every small customer to move to EDI. Most of them will keep sending a file. The email flow is for that long tail. A customer who only orders twice a year is not an EDI project.

## What to leave alone

Leave it alone if you receive a handful of orders a week and you already know each one by name. A shared checklist and a careful person will beat a flow that nobody trusts. Leave it alone if the order system has no import and no interface and nobody will type from a prepared screen. The reading still helps, but someone still keys the draft. Complaints, invoices and quotes stay out of this flow. This page starts when the message is already an order.
