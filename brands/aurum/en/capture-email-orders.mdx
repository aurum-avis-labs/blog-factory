## Capture customer orders from email: the short answer

<p class="blog-lede">Capturing orders from email means reading the message and attachment, matching customer and items, and writing a draft order in the system you already use. Unclear lines stay with a person until release.</p>

PDF forms, Excel sheets and short free-text emails need the same fields. The copying is what you automate. Lines that fail to match stay on the desk.

This is for sales offices and order desks in Swiss B2B firms where customers send files instead of ordering through a shop. It is the order case of [stop double data entry](/prozess-check/blog/stop-double-data-entry). Which emails are orders at all is one step earlier, in [sorting a shared inbox](/prozess-check/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails).

  <li><strong>Lines</strong><p>Customer, articles, quantities and the customer reference are pulled out.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Match</strong><p>Each line is matched to your customer and item records.</p></li>

  <li><strong>Confirm</strong><p>A person releases the draft. Then the confirmation can go out.</p></li>

## What every order email has to contain

Every order has to yield the same fields before a draft is worth creating. Decide the list once from orders you actually receive and write it down.

- Customer, matched to a number you hold, not only to the name in the signature.
- Delivery address or site when it is not the default on the customer record.
- The customer's order reference for confirmation and later invoice.
- Article number as the customer wrote it, and your number once it matches.
- Quantity and unit. A quantity without a unit is not a complete line.
- Requested date if the customer named one. If not, your usual lead time applies and the draft says so.

Excel is the easiest when the customer uses your sheet with fixed columns. Their PDF form is readable, but layouts differ from customer to customer, so the first weeks are about learning the layouts. Free text works when article numbers are in the message. When the customer writes "the usual" or "same as last month", that mail goes to a person with the previous order beside it.

## How the draft gets into the system you already use

The draft belongs in the order system the desk already uses. The automation does not replace it. It fills a draft the way a careful clerk would and stops where the clerk would ask.

If the mailbox is Microsoft 365, [Microsoft Graph](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/message-list-attachments?view=graph-rest-1.0) can list attachments. The least privileged permission on that page is Mail.Read. If the mailbox is Google Workspace, the [Gmail API](https://developers.google.com/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.messages.attachments/get) can fetch an attachment with a read-only mail scope. You read mail the office already receives. You are not asking the customer to change how they order.

What happens next depends on the order system: file import, interface or screen only. Which your program offers is clarified in the Check against the product you run. We do not assume an order interface until the vendor page says so.

Match customer and articles against your records. A customer may sign as a brand and order under a legal name. Keep a table of names and article codes they use, maintained by the desk. New spellings fall to the person; confirmed matches can enter the table.

Prices on the customer file are a check, not the price you adopt without looking. If the PDF unit price differs from your agreement, the line is flagged. The draft keeps your price and the note shows both numbers.

## Unclear lines stay with a person

An unclear line is not a failure of the tool. That is why someone still sits on the desk.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>If the match is unsure, the draft does not invent an article, a unit or a customer. The line waits. The rest of the order can still be prepared if you have written that rule down.</p>
</aside>

Typical waits: no article code and a description that fits two items, quantity with no unit, two records with the same name, an address you have never used, a price outside the agreement. Write one sentence each for what the desk does: correct and release, ask the customer and hold, split sure lines into a draft and park the rest. The rule must be the same on Monday and Friday.

Do not send the confirmation until the draft is released. A confirmation that quotes a guessed article is worse than a short delay. The original file stays on the draft.

Orders contain customer and commercial data. Do not paste the PDF into a free chat tool to "just read it". What belongs in which tool is in [ChatGPT and customer data under the Swiss DPA](/prozess-check/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg).

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a small wholesale office for installation parts, eight people, no in-house IT. Orders arrive at bestellung@ from about forty trade customers. A dozen send a PDF of their own form. Most send the Excel sheet the office issued, with columns for article, quantity and commission number. Two or three emails a week are free text from a foreman on site.

Each morning the flow reads the mailbox. Excel lines with a known article become draft lines. PDF lines map to the same fields. Where "elbow 90" fits two articles, the line is marked and the draft held. "Same as Tuesday, plus six of the usual clips" is not read; it sits in a folder for the desk with Tuesday's order linked.

The person on the desk opens held drafts. She corrects the elbow, releases the draft, and the confirmation goes out with the commission number. The original PDF stays on the order. Nothing here is a client, and it is not a claim about minutes saved.

## When EDI with large customers is the better answer

EDI fits when a few large customers already send structured messages or require them. [GS1 Switzerland](https://www.gs1.ch/en/barcodes-standards/gs1-standards/share) describes electronic data interchange for orders, confirmations, delivery notes and invoices in a standard format. It names EANCOM for electronic orders and a reduced IDEAL subset in Switzerland.

The GS1 Switzerland Ideal Message for orders, published in December 2022, is meant for day-to-day ordering: one purchase order for one delivery, in one location, at one time. That is different work from reading a PDF. Both sides need the identifiers the message expects and a path that already speaks that format.

Use EDI for those customers and keep the email path for everyone else. Do not type EDI orders into the mailbox flow as well, or you create two drafts. Do not wait for every small customer to move to EDI. The email flow is for the long tail. A customer who orders twice a year is not an EDI project.

## What to leave alone

If you receive a handful of orders a week and know each by name, a shared checklist and a careful person beat a flow nobody trusts. If the order system has no import and no interface and nobody will type from a prepared screen, reading still helps but someone keys the draft by hand. Know that before you start.

Complaints, invoices and quotes stay out of this flow. This page starts when the message is already an order.
