## Startup software development in Switzerland

Startup software development in Switzerland is a bucket term. Sometimes it means a first web app, sometimes an API other products will call, sometimes an internal tool a larger company wants built like a product. A useful map has three questions: what you are building, how long you want guided execution, and which expectations the local market puts on stability and data handling.

For the go-to-market slice, an MVP is still the usual entry. See [MVP development in Switzerland](/blog/mvp-development-switzerland) and [MVP cost in Switzerland](/blog/mvp-cost-switzerland). For who to hire (agency, studio, accelerator), read [venture studio vs agency](/blog/venture-studio-vs-agency). This page is the map of engagement types so those later choices are comparable.

If the thing you want is a first product you will learn from, the practical path we run is the [Product Validation Package](/services/mvp-validation) in Zurich: a structured MVP, not an open-ended body shop.

## Three common engagement types

| Type | You are buying | Stop when |
|---|---|---|
| MVP / validation | A tight scope and a launch you can measure | The decision the MVP was for is answered, or clearly not |
| Product extension | Ongoing work after people pay and stay | The roadmap is a queue, not a question |
| Special project | One migration, integration, performance, or security job | That job is in production and handed over |

**MVP / validation.** Scope is small on purpose. The goal is to learn, not to be finished on day one. You need a decision the build will answer: will this buyer pay, will they complete the core path, is the internal go or no-go now obvious. A partner who cannot say which decision the scope serves will fill the weeks with features. Our [Product Validation Package](/services/mvp-validation) is this type.

**Product extension.** Paying customers exist. The work is a roadmap, often mixed with people you employ. Expectations have already moved: availability, support, data you must not lose. The contract shape is usually a team-month or a retainer with a throughput you can point at, not a single fixed package from a blank page.

**Special project.** The product is not the question. A legacy system has to move, a payment or identity provider has to be wired in, or a security finding has to be closed. Price it as that job. Do not let it be sold as "the MVP" because the calendar is short.

Pricing follows the type. MVPs are often a package plus a written way to change scope. Ongoing work is capacity. A special project is a bounded deliverable. Two quotes with the same total and different types are not the same offer.

## A worked example, so the types stay distinct

A founder wants scheduling software for independent clinics. Still not a client of ours, just the sort of fork we see.

If no clinic has paid and the question is whether ten of them will book a slot this quarter, that is an MVP. The software has to be real enough that a clinic administrator can finish the path without the founder on the phone. It does not need every report the fifth clinic will ask for in year two.

If eight clinics already pay and the backlog is "they want a second location and a different invoice layout", that is product extension. The risk is no longer "does anyone care". The risk is breaking Tuesday morning for people who already depend on it.

If the same founder says the real pain is "the first contractor's database is a spreadsheet export and we need it in our own cloud before the contract ends", that is a special project. Mixing it into the MVP package hides both the learning goal and the migration risk.

Get the type in writing before you compare prices. Then the Zurich, Basel, and remote quotes are at least answering one question.

## Switzerland-specific planning

Not legal advice. This is product scope.

**Privacy.** Decide storage location, who can open production data, and how a user record gets deleted, before the first paying account. Swiss B2B buyers ask earlier than a generic US launch checklist implies. "We will add it after the seed round" often means a rebuild of the data model. For an MVP, the bar is: you know where it lives (commonly Switzerland or the EU), you can name the processors, and deletion is a path, not a promise in a slide.

**Languages and UX.** German, French, or Italian depending on who buys. That hits copy, support, error messages, and onboarding, not a translation file at the end. A German-speaking buyer who meets an English-only empty state will treat it as unfinished. If your first ten customers are in one language, ship that language properly and write down the second as later scope. Pretending to launch in three languages on an MVP budget usually means three thin ones.

**Operations.** Monitoring, backups, and a way to roll back. Once a clinic, a tenant, or an internal team relies on the tool, "it works on my laptop" is not an environment. Plan a staging system and a production system, and decide who may deploy. Swiss customers in B2B will ask who is on call. An MVP does not need a 24-hour desk. It does need a named person, and a backup you have restored once.

## MVP budget versus a product that has to stay up

Many teams price the first launch and then discover that paying users change the bill. Support, failed payments, access reviews, and the boring fixes around data integrity show up the week someone depends on the result. If you plan the MVP as a learning instrument, you can change mode afterwards: different budget, different team shape, a retainer instead of a package. If you plan the MVP as the finished company, the first quote will either be huge or quietly exclude the operations you will need in month four.

What comes after a real signal is covered in [product-market fit: how to find it](/blog/product-market-fit-how-to-find-it). Read that when you have users, not as a substitute for picking the engagement type now.

## How to choose a partner for the type you picked

Look for someone who will cut scope with you while the business model is still moving. Ticket clearance without a priority is how MVPs grow into unfinished platforms.

Ask each bidder to label their quote with one of the three types above. If they will not, you are not looking at startup software development. You are looking at hours.
