## What our own launches showed about a market test

**What our own launches showed about a market test** is that shipping and promoting a product teaches you faster than slides, but each channel answers a narrow question. Attention is not demand. Sign-ups without repeat use are not fit. A cheap click in Zurich is not a booked pilot call. Across [CitySage](/portfolio/citysage), [Holist-IQ](/portfolio/holist-iq), [KitchenCrew](/portfolio/kitchencrew), [Postology](/portfolio/postology), [do4me](/portfolio/do4me), [Inklets](/portfolio/inklets), and [Vemoir](/portfolio/vemoir), the useful read is which assumption broke, what you measured, and what you still cannot infer.

This hub is for founders and innovation leads who want honest patterns from real builds, not a scorecard invented after the fact. The discipline we use on client work is spelled out in [how Aurum Avis validates MVPs](/blog/how-aurum-avis-validates-mvps). Deeper posts on each lesson sit in [a launch is not validated demand](/blog/a-launch-is-not-validated-demand), [testing a niche product before revenue](/blog/testing-a-niche-product-before-revenue), [cutting pilot scope](/blog/cutting-pilot-scope), [when the model cannot finish the job](/blog/when-the-model-cannot-finish-the-job), and [marketplace MVP demand side](/blog/marketplace-mvp-demand-side).

## What a market test can show, and what it cannot

A bounded test can show whether strangers complete a risky step, whether a message pulls the right cohort, and whether friction sits in the product or on the landing page. It cannot show durable margins, a repeatable acquisition machine, or that you should raise a round. Treat every figure as local to that window and that audience.

## Launches that bought reach, not a channel

[CitySage](/portfolio/citysage) began as a weekend prototype for AI city tours, then moved to a mobile-first browser product with a globe, manual and AI itineraries, Mapbox, and sharing. Friends and family used it. A public launch with Instagram, Product Hunt, and light Google Ads produced attention. It did not produce a repeatable acquisition channel. Pin accuracy needed guardrails before we trusted the core loop. That outcome matches the point in [a launch is not validated demand](/blog/a-launch-is-not-validated-demand): publicity is a test of message and curiosity, not proof that strangers will keep coming back without paid push.

We do not publish CitySage impression, spend, like, or signup totals here. If you need them for your own planning, take them from your dashboards on the same dates, not from our story.

## Niche products before revenue

[Holist-IQ](/portfolio/holist-iq) was a weekend MVP for causal-loop diagrams with constrained agents, not open chat. Early signals were small but interpretable: 11 sign-ups, five sessions longer than four minutes, three return visits, two who hit the free credit limit, and no credit purchases. A paid campaign in the same period showed 5'293 impressions, 299 clicks, 5.65 per cent click-through, CHF 0.65 average cost per click, and CHF 194.81 spend. Friction sat on the mobile landing page and on credit purchase. The strongest pull was modelling political interrelations, as in [testing a niche product before revenue](/blog/testing-a-niche-product-before-revenue).

That pattern is useful for a board update. It is not revenue, and it is not product-market fit.

## Pilots where scope had to shrink

[KitchenCrew](/portfolio/kitchencrew) sits on the GoldCrew backbone. For the pilot we removed inventory and kept suppliers, articles, chat or speech-to-text, order drafts, email, and spend tracking. The build landed in December 2025; the pilot has run since April 2026, free in exchange for feedback. The warmest door was conversations and events in Zurich, not ads. A small email test reached 50 inboxes. Google Ads in Zurich and Zug showed a cost per click around CHF 1.80; clicks arrived, booked pilot calls did not follow at the rate we needed. We do not state a restaurant count; we only know what pilots volunteered under this scope.

That is a direct example of [cutting pilot scope](/blog/cutting-pilot-scope): fewer features, clearer ask, faster feedback on whether the remaining job is urgent.

## When the stack stops where the job continues

[Postology](/portfolio/postology) was built in 2024, before the company existed, as an internal tool. Text does not belong in the image model; overlays stay in the app. Instagram's API required a legal entity, so publishing stayed manual. About ten people joined a waitlist. We ran no active ads. The product remains internal, not a public launch. The lesson aligns with [when the model cannot finish the job](/blog/when-the-model-cannot-finish-the-job): the model can draft visuals, but distribution and compliance steps still need a human owner.

## Marketplace tests and the demand side

[do4me](/portfolio/do4me) is a client project for everyday help in Zurich, not an internal bet. In 2.5 days we shipped a landing page, web app, listings, profiles, categories, applications, chat, Google Ads, tag manager, Clarity, and QR and UTM links, then four days of feedback and one day of iteration. Payments, KYC, ratings, and disputes stayed out of scope on purpose; a native wrap was prepared but was not the first test. Seventeen students registered and four people posted jobs, mostly friends and family. That did not prove marketplace liquidity. Demand, not supply, was the bottleneck in [marketplace MVP demand side](/blog/marketplace-mvp-demand-side). Holding back payments was scoping, not legal advice.

## Apps that extend where the test lives

[Inklets](/portfolio/inklets) is a React app wrapped with Capacitor and released on the iOS App Store and Google Play, with a public site at inklets.app. Store submission is a real gate: Apple expects more than a repackaged website under guideline 4.2. A wrap still needs app-like function. We do not quote download counts or review stories here.

[Vemoir](/portfolio/vemoir) is a macOS app for Apple Silicon that transcribes meetings locally, without uploading raw audio to the cloud. It tests whether professionals trust on-device processing enough to change meeting habits, not whether a mobile web pilot converts.
