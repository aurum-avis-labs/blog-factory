## Data protection vs information security

Data protection vs information security is the split between lawful, transparent handling of personal data and the controls that keep systems confidential, intact and available. In a Swiss SME both matter under the revised FADP, but they answer different questions. Data protection asks whether you may process data, for how long, and what you tell people when something goes wrong. Information security asks whether passwords, backups and phishing defences work when someone targets finance on a Tuesday morning.

This comparison is for Geschäftsleitung, HR and the IT partner in a firm with about 20 to 250 staff. It is orientation, not legal advice.

## Where the FADP joins both

[Art. 8 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de) requires appropriate technical and organisational measures for data security. «Organisational» covers training, policies and who may export a payroll file. «Technical» covers encryption, firewalls and logging. You cannot satisfy Art. 8 with a privacy notice alone, and you cannot satisfy it with a firewall alone if staff paste customer lists into unapproved tools.

## Typical data protection work

Publish and maintain a privacy notice. Answer access requests. Keep processor agreements for cloud and payroll vendors, as in our guide on [data processing agreements under the FADP](/security/blog/data-processing-agreement-under-the-fadp). Decide whether a wrong email needs FDPIC notification. Run retention and deletion for HR and CRM. Train people on minimum necessary sharing and internal reporting.

## Typical information security work

Roll out MFA, patch servers, test backups, review admin accounts, run phishing awareness, define who may approve payment changes, and report cyber incidents on the internal path. Most SMEs are not subject to the BACS cyber reporting duty for critical infrastructure; they still need a response plan when ransomware locks the file server.

## Overlap that confuses teams

Ransomware is information security first and may become a data protection breach if personal data were exfiltrated. A phishing click sits in security training until credentials leak customer data. CEO fraud is process and culture; if customer IBANs were exposed, privacy documentation joins the folder.

## Who owns what in a small company

The owner or delegate sets risk appetite. HR owns applicant and employee files. Finance owns payment fraud process. IT or the external partner owns technical controls. Without labels, everything lands on «the person who does IT on the side». Splitting a privacy contact from a security contact helps, even if one person wears both hats part-time.

## Two trainings, one culture

Datenschutz training explains lawful bases, rights, safe email habits, and when to escalate to management about a breach. Security awareness training explains phishing, smishing, passwords, MFA fatigue, invoice fraud and internal reporting of cyber events. Auditors and insurers increasingly ask for both records.

Privacy training is often the gap after the 2023 FADP revision, even when MFA is already in place.

Aurum delivers both as live sessions of one to two hours after a short needs conversation, remotely or on site in Switzerland, in German or English. A combined day covering security awareness and datenschutz is the usual format when leadership wants one calendar block rather than two half-days months apart.

## When you need only one first

A firm with strong IT hygiene but no privacy notice or breach process should prioritise data protection. A firm with polished GDPR PDFs but shared passwords and no phishing reporting should prioritise security awareness. Most readers of this site need both within the same quarter.

## What neither training replaces

Penetration tests, ISO certification, 24/7 SOC, or a full-time CISO. Training does not write your processor contracts or restore backups. It aligns people with decisions technology already supports.

## ISO and NIST mapping

Customers may ask how awareness maps to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 control A 6.3 or NIST SP 800-50. Aurum can describe mapping on request; sessions do not certify the company.

## A made-up example: two gaps, one audit

A made-up example: a 45-person fiduciary passes an IT health check with MFA and backups. The auditor then asks for privacy training records and breach documentation. None exist beyond a 2018 slide deck. The next month they run datenschutz training for all client-facing staff. The quarter after, security awareness for everyone with mailbox access. Incident forms split «cyber» and «personal data» sections. The audit file shows two attendance lists and one combined management review. Neither session alone would have answered every question.

## Practical sequencing

Month one: privacy notice and processor contracts. Month two: datenschutz training. Month three: security awareness. Same year: test restore from backup and tabletop on wrong email plus phishing. Link everyday mistakes to [email sent to the wrong recipient](/security/blog/email-sent-to-the-wrong-recipient) and [reporting a breach to the FDPIC](/security/blog/report-a-data-breach-to-the-fdpic).

## Questionnaires from insurers and customers

Cyber insurers ask about MFA and backups. Corporate customers ask about training dates and breach history. One generic "we take security seriously" paragraph fails both. Split attachments: one page on technical controls from IT, one on datenschutz training attendance from HR.

## Supplier and EU customer questionnaires

EU customers pass security questionnaires down the chain even when NIS2 does not apply directly to your firm. Security awareness answers phishing and access; datenschutz answers processor contracts and retention. Treat them as two curricula, not one boilerplate PDF. A combined training day does not replace a written annual plan with measurement; that is a separate topic in the blog.

## Choosing external help

IT partners excel at tools; they rarely want to facilitate live behaviour training in German for finance and reception. A specialist session frees them to patch while staff ask about real processes.

## Phishing simulations vs datenschutz drills

Phishing simulations measure click rates; they do not replace instruction on access requests or breach escalation. Datenschutz training does not explain why MFA matters when a supplier changes bank details. If a customer asks for an "awareness programme", plan both and keep attendance lists separate so audits can see which curriculum ran when.

## After an incident

Teams often schedule only datenschutz refresher after a breach, or only IT drills after ransomware. If the event touched both personal data and systems, run both follow-ups in the same quarter so staff do not assume one department owns the whole story.

## Board reporting in one slide

Two bullets are enough: last datenschutz training date and last security awareness date, plus open actions (encryption gap, pending DPA). Directors want dates and owners, not statute articles.

## Remote work and mixed language teams

Home Wi-Fi and personal printers touch security. A customer call on speaker while family listens touches privacy. Combined training gives staff language for both where possible. English product names on tools are fine; briefings in the language of the warehouse or reception reduce mistakes on the same incident timeline.
