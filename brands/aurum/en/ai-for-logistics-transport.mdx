## AI for small logistics and transport companies

AI for a small logistics or transport company pays off on the office around the trip, not on the truck. Orders arrive by email, with a pickup, a delivery, a time window and a reference that someone retypes. A customer asks where the goods are, and the answer is a guess. A signed delivery note comes back as a photo, or not at all, and the invoice waits. Driver papers expire on a date that lives in a folder. Those are the jobs. Choosing a route on a map, or filing a road charge, is not this article.

This is for owners and dispatchers of Swiss firms with a few vehicles, carrying other people's goods. The transport software you already use stays. The question is what still happens in the inbox and on paper beside it.

## What the transport system already does

If every order is entered once, the driver sees it on the tour, the customer gets a status you trust, and the invoice is created from deliveries that have a signed note, there is nothing to rebuild on that stretch. Many small fleets are halfway there. The regular customers send orders in a portal. The rest send an email, a PDF, or a phone note the dispatcher writes down. The invoice is then typed again from the paper that came back.

Typing the same order twice, from the mail into the system, is the pattern in [stopping double entry](/blog/stop-double-data-entry). Here the fields are pickup, delivery, date, time window, reference, and a short description of the goods. A workflow fills them and parks what it cannot read. It does not invent a weight, a floor, or a tail-lift.

## Four jobs around one transport order

Four jobs repeat every day in a small fleet. Each ends in a record: an order, a reply, a reminder, an invoice that can wait for its proof.

### The order in the mail

A shipper writes: collect here, deliver there, by Thursday morning, reference on the pallet. Someone copies that into the system before a vehicle can be assigned. Sorting a mixed inbox is covered in [sorting a shared inbox](/blog/shared-inbox-sort-emails). A transport order adds fields the inbox does not have. The draft order shows pickup, delivery, window and reference, taken from the message. Missing fields stay empty. A person confirms before a driver is sent. Two interpretations of one address are not a thing to average.

### Where is the delivery

Customers ask the same question. The useful reply quotes the status you actually have: collected, on the tour, delivered, or not yet planned. It does not promise an arrival minute the tour does not show. If the driver has not reported, the draft says that, and names the person who will look. A guessed clock time, sent because the customer sounded urgent, is how you create a complaint. The proof that the goods were received, the signed note, is a separate piece of work. This article only says the invoice should wait for it.

### Papers the driver has to carry

Licence, medical certificate if you require one, permits for the vehicle: each has a date. A list that warns you thirty days before a date you entered is a reminder, not a judgement that the person may drive. You keep the rule. The workflow only reads the dates you stored and puts the due ones on Monday's list. It does not decide that a scan of a card is genuine.

### The invoice after the trip

A trip is billable when it was done, at the rate on the order or on the customer agreement, with the signed note attached or at least filed against the order. Deliveries without that note stay open. Partial deliveries bill what was delivered if the agreement says so, and they say what is still open. The price, the waiting time you will charge, and a refusal at the door stay with a person. The copying of lines from the note into the invoice is the dull part.

## What stays with dispatch

Whether to accept the order, which vehicle, a delay you will tell the customer about, and any damage or shortage: those stay with a person. A workflow may draft the order and the status reply. It should not send a truck, and it should not tell a customer the goods are delivered because a photo arrived that nobody has matched to the order.

[Which process to automate first](/blog/which-process-to-automate-first) fits a fleet as well as an office: the work repeats, it hurts on Monday, it ends in an order or an invoice, and the message is already in the inbox. A firm with a few trips a week, all from two customers who already use your portal, will not repay a new route for email orders.

## Where to start

Count one ordinary week: orders retyped from mail, status calls, delivery notes you chased, invoices held because the signature was missing, papers you almost forgot. If one of those dominates, that is the case.

If you want that one recurring task looked at with you, the [Process Check](/prozess-check) is an hour on it. You leave with a case and a price range, or you hear that it is not worth it.
