## Workshop or implementation: two different purchases

An AI workshop and an implementation are two different purchases that get stuffed into the same sentence: "we have to do something with AI". A workshop gives the team a shared language, shared rules, and one to three experiments they run themselves afterwards. Implementation, for us, starts with the Process Check: one recurring workflow, a written case, and a package size if you then want it built. Both are real offers. They answer different jobs.

This is for leadership and office leads in a Swiss SME, often 5 to 80 people, with little or no internal IT. You are choosing between an AI workshop for the company and AI consulting that ends in a built workflow. You are not choosing a prompt course, and you are not choosing a startup product.

## When a workshop fits

The [AI in Business workshop](/services/ai-in-business) is for leadership and specialist teams who already touch tools, or should, and do not share guardrails. The job sounds like this: everyone should be allowed to use a company chat or Copilot without dropping customer data into a private account.

You should leave with three concrete things, not a slide titled "AI strategy":

- Which tools are on the allow-list, and which stay off it for work data.
- Which data class may go where: public copy, internal notes with no people in them, personal data.
- One to three experiments the team will run itself in the following weeks, small enough to finish, with a person named for each.

A day like that fits when the visible problem is inconsistency. One person has a private ChatGPT account. Another was told not to. A third pastes client mail "just to draft the reply" because there is no agreed place to do it. Banning the tools moves that draft onto a phone. Buying seats for everyone without the page of rules leaves the same hole, now with an invoice attached.

The workshop is a poor fit when you already know the workflow and the only question is whether to build it. A room full of people will not assemble Monday's report by the end of the day. Training is not a connector between your inbox and the accounting tool.

An engineering team that wants agents inside a code repository is a different format: [Agentic Coding](/services/agentic-coding). This article is about the office.

## When the Process Check fits

The [Process Check](/prozess-check) is one recurring workflow. Sixty minutes online, a case (what, who, how often), and a package size if a fixed-price build is the sensible next step. You do not have to buy the build. Detail of what the hour includes and what it refuses is in [what the Process Check is](/blog/what-the-process-check-is).

Bring work that already has a shape: invoices that should land in the books, quote follow-ups that die in one inbox, a report glued from several sources, a spreadsheet that only exists because two systems do not talk. Copilot can still be the right answer in the middle of that. When a licence is enough, and when you need the transfer built, is covered in [which process you should automate first](/blog/which-process-to-automate-first).

The Check is a poor fit for "we need a shared vocabulary", "we need an allow-list", or "we want the board to see that we take AI seriously". Those are workshop jobs. It is also the wrong hour for a product idea or an MVP. Validating a product is a different offer. This cluster does not scope startups.

Sketch the fork on one company, still not a client story. Twelve people, a bookkeeper, and a shared inbox. On Monday someone rebuilds a report from the CRM, two sheets, and a PDF. On Tuesday a colleague pastes a customer complaint, name included, into a private chat to "make it nicer". The report is a Process Check topic: it repeats, it has inputs, it has an output. The complaint is a workshop topic: the team has no rule for personal data, so the draft went to the wrong place. Buying a workflow for the complaint does not write the rule. Running a workshop for the report does not move the figures next Monday.

## AI training versus implementation

People search this as AI training versus implementation, or as AI consulting for an SME. Training, in the useful sense, is the workshop: the same terms, the same allow-list, a few experiments, then the team works. Implementation is a bounded change to one workflow, after that workflow is named.

A catalogue of tools is neither. So is a prompt library that never touches the Monday file. If a proposal does not say which of the two jobs it finishes, you cannot compare it with the other quotes in the pile. Ask the vendor to point at the outcome: a one-page rule the office will actually use, or a workflow that runs with a person checking the result. If they answer with both, ask which one the engagement is for. Combining them into one invoice is how offices buy a day of slides and still copy the report by hand.

## If you think you need both

Sometimes both jobs exist, as in the sketch above. Do them as two decisions, in an order, not as one blurred project.

Start with the workshop if personal data is already leaving the company in private chats, or if nobody can say which tool is allowed. Building on top of that habit locks the habit in. A page of rules is cheaper to get wrong and fix than a package built on the wrong data path.

Start with the Process Check if one workflow is already clear, the data in it is allowed to sit in your existing tools, and the pain is hours, errors, or a single person who cannot be on holiday. You can write a short allow-list afterwards without waiting for a full day, if the only people involved are the ones who touch that workflow.

Do not buy a build "so the workshop has something to show", and do not book a workshop "so the build has change management". If you cannot say which sentence is yours, you are not ready for either invoice. Spend an hour internally: list the repeating work on one side, and the places client data currently gets pasted on the other. The longer column is the first purchase.

## What you are not buying

A product idea, a venture, or an MVP. That is outside this choice.

A legal opinion on the Swiss Data Protection Act. The workshop can set a practical traffic light with the team. It does not replace advice from someone who carries that duty for you.

A promise that every seat will "use AI" by Friday. A workshop can change what people are allowed to do. A build can change one workflow. A usage percentage is a different measurement from either result.
