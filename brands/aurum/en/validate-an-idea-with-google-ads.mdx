## Validate an idea with Google Ads

You validate an idea with Google Ads by sending paid search or Performance Max traffic to one landing page, measuring one primary action you defined before spend, and comparing the result to a written falsifier. Google supplies strangers with intent; your page supplies the offer and the ask. The test is whether enough of the right people take that action at a cost you can afford to learn from, not whether the headline sounds clever in a workshop.

This is for founders and innovation leads in Switzerland who already have a one-sentence hypothesis and a page live, and who need demand signal faster than organic search allows. For the wider validation menu, see [how to validate a startup idea](/blog/how-to-validate-a-startup-idea). For a lighter page-only test without campaigns, see [smoke test landing page](/blog/smoke-test-landing-page) and [fake door test](/blog/fake-door-test).

## How to validate an idea with Google Ads in practice

Run one campaign shape at a time. Mixing audiences, offers, and thank-you pages in week one usually tells you which image won, not whether the idea attracts buyers.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Fix the action before the account</h3><p>Choose one primary conversion: waitlist, application, calendar hold, or checkout. Secondary metrics can sit in analytics, but the falsifier refers to the primary action only.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Wire measurement on the real URL</h3><p>Tag manager, analytics, and the ad platform should see the same thank-you or event. Test with a manual visit before you switch spend on.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Match keywords to the promise</h3><p>Search campaigns work when people already type the problem. Broad «innovation» keywords attract curiosity, not necessarily your segment. Keep ad copy aligned with the headline on the page.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Set a budget cap and a stop rule</h3><p>Decide the maximum you will spend to learn, and what outcome would make you pause the idea. Write both down before the first click is billed.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Read behaviour, not vanity</h3><p>Record scroll depth, form abandonment, and device split in session tools. A high click rate with empty forms is a page problem, not proof of demand.</p></li>
</ol>

## What paid traffic can show you

Clicks from ads are evidence that a message attracted attention among people Google thought matched your targeting. A completed primary action is stronger: someone spent time and accepted friction. Repeat visits or return sessions after the first day are stronger still, because they are harder to explain as mis-clicks.

<div class="blog-pair">
  <div>
    <h3>Reasonable to infer</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Strangers reached the page and a measurable share tried the ask</li>
      <li>Which headline or keyword theme produced actions, not only clicks</li>
      <li>Whether mobile layout or form length blocks completion</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Not proven by ads alone</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Willingness to pay at a sustainable price</li>
      <li>Retention, referrals, or a repeatable acquisition channel</li>
      <li>That the idea is protectable: ideas as such cannot be protected ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden))</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

Internal product Holist-IQ ran Google Ads against an early web MVP. The campaign in the source material reached 5'293 impressions and 299 clicks, with total cost CHF 194.81 and an average cost per click of CHF 0.65. That bought learning, not revenue: eleven signups, five sessions longer than four minutes, three people who returned, two who hit the free credit limit, and no credit purchases. The team treated unclear mobile landing and credit purchase as friction to fix, not as proof the concept was finished.

<div class="blog-stat">
  <strong>CHF 194.81</strong>
  <p>Reported total Google Ads spend for the Holist-IQ validation campaign in Aurum's case material. Early signups and session depth are noted there; paid conversion to credits is not.</p>
</div>

CitySage also used Google Ads around a public launch, alongside other channels. The dashboards may show impressions and spend, but those figures are not published here until they are confirmed on the live accounts. The useful lesson is the same: attention arrived; a repeatable channel did not.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Google Ads can falsify a weak value proposition or a confusing page. It cannot tell you your margin, your sales cycle, or whether procurement will block a B2B buyer. Stop when spend hits your cap with zero primary actions from on-target traffic, or when actions come only from people you already know.</p>
</aside>

## A made-up example: search traffic for a narrow B2B tool

A made-up example: a founder in Bern tests software that turns supplier PDFs into structured order lines for small manufacturers. The hypothesis is that production planners search for a fix to manual retyping. They publish one landing page in German with a short demo video and a form that requests a company email and a sample PDF upload. Google Search ads use three phrase-match keyword groups tied to «PDF Bestellung erfassen» and similar wording, with a daily cap of CHF 40 for ten working days. Before launch they write: «If fewer than eight uploads arrive from paid traffic after CHF 400 spend, we interview planners instead of building OCR.» Analytics and session recording are live on day one. After six days they have 212 clicks, twelve uploads, and most abandonment on the upload step on mobile. They pause ads, fix mobile upload, and rerun for another CHF 200 rather than declaring victory on twelve files from mixed traffic.

## Who should skip Google Ads for now

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>If the problem is still vague</h3><p>Interview and [problem validation](/blog/problem-validation-before-building) come first. Paying for traffic to a vague page mainly buys ambiguous clicks.</p></li>
  <li><h3>If you have no falsifier</h3><p>Without a stop rule you will reinterpret any number as «early days». Write the rule while the account balance is still zero.</p></li>
  <li><h3>If legal or cookie setup is unfinished</h3><p>Swiss sites still need a clear information path for analytics and ads tags where required; retargeting and cross-site profiling raise consent questions under current EDÖB guidance. Fix the notice before you scale spend.</p></li>
</ul>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is a waitlist enough for validation?</h3>
  <p>It is a weak signal of interest, useful when paired with interviews. Treat it as step one unless your falsifier explicitly accepts email-only proof.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Should I use Performance Max or Search first?</h3>
  <p>Search is easier to read when you are testing wording tied to intent. Broader automated campaigns can work later, once the page converts and you know which outcome you are optimising for.</p>
</div>
