## Putting AI to work in the business, without a stable workflow

Putting AI to work in the business sounds like strategy. Day to day it often looks like this: ChatGPT or Copilot is open, and the Monday report is still copied together from five files. Everyone is "doing something with AI". Operations barely move.

That is not a criticism of people who use the tools. Translation and correspondence really are faster there. It is the gap between private use and a workflow the company can stand behind: same input, same output, a person who owns it.

A typical week in a Swiss SME makes the gap obvious. On Monday someone exports the CRM, opens two spreadsheets, downloads a PDF, and pastes until the workbook matches what management expects at 10:00. On Tuesday they use ChatGPT to translate a supplier mail and to soften a customer reply. Tuesday got shorter. Monday did not. The firm can honestly say it uses AI. It cannot honestly say a recurring workflow has changed.

## What "doing something with AI" usually means in an SME

The [AXA SME labour-market study](https://www.deepcloud.swiss/newsroom/artikel/ki-in-schweizer-kmu-wir-sind-bereit/), cited by DeepCloud, shows the jump: 22 percent of Swiss SMEs used AI applications in 2024, 34 percent in 2025. The most common jobs: translation (52 percent) and correspondence (47 percent). Automating a work step rose from 23 to 34 percent. Those are their figures, not our survey.

The Lucerne University of Applied Sciences [KMU Digital Pulse 2026](https://zenodo.org/records/22098334) is narrower for marketing: people who use AI mostly let it write. Text, translation, images. That job is crowded. Another prompt guide does not help operations.

[BDO](https://www.bdo.ch/de-ch/medienstelle/2026/ki-adoption-die-kluft-zwischen-grossunternehmen-und-kmu-wachst) described the 2026 split this way: large firms embed AI in value creation, many SMEs stay on single apps. Their advice: a few concrete cases, owned by management, not an IT side project.

The [adesso GenAI Impact Report 2026](https://www.adesso.ch/adesso-ch/whitepaper/genai-impact-report-2026-ch.pdf) adds measurement: the biggest hurdles they report are data quality and missing processes or governance, and many firms track whether staff *use* the tool, not whether a process got shorter or more reliable. Usage is not impact.

ChatGPT in daily work can be the right tool for a one-off sentence. It becomes a dead end when leadership counts seats and logins, then asks why the books, the follow-ups, or the Monday file still depend on one person's afternoon.

## Three signals that usage is not moving the work

<ul class="blog-scenarios">
  <li><h3>No owner</h3><p>Nobody may say when the workflow is done. AI stays a tab each person opens alone.</p></li>
  <li><h3>No recurring input</h3><p>Different files and questions every week. Drafting helps. A stable flow has nothing to hold on to.</p></li>
  <li><h3>No measurable output</h3><p>You track logins, not shorter cycles or fewer errors.</p></li>
</ul>

If staff paste customer data into private chats because there is no allowed option internally, that is a different problem: shadow AI in the office.

## Why the usual first steps fail

A strategy slide for the board names the topic and leaves Monday untouched. Ten pilots at once split a small office until nothing finishes, and nobody remembers which pilot was supposed to change a named workflow. A licence for everyone so "the company has AI" speeds up drafting inside the tab. It does not log into the other four sources. A prompt library never meets the report. People may even use the prompts, which will show up as adoption, while the copy-paste stays intact.

These steps are easy to put in a meeting. They leave the gap between a chat window and a workflow someone else can run when the usual person is away.

## What better looks like

Better, for a first pass, is small. One workflow. One owner who can say it is finished. One input that already arrives (an inbox, an export, a PDF). One output someone can check, on a rhythm you already have. You can tell it worked if that output takes less time or throws fewer errors, not if more people logged in.

That can still include a model. The model might draft a line, or pull a field, inside that path. The path is the point. Translation of a random mail is a fine use of ChatGPT. It is a poor definition of "AI in the company", because it never had an owner or a repeated output to begin with.
