Putting AI to work in the business sounds like strategy. Day to day it often looks like this: ChatGPT or Copilot is open, and the Monday report is still copied together from five files. Everyone is "doing something with AI". Operations barely move.

That is not a criticism of people who use the tools. Translation and correspondence really are faster there. It is the gap between private use and a workflow the company can stand behind.

## What "doing something with AI" usually means in an SME

The [AXA SME labour-market study](https://www.deepcloud.swiss/newsroom/artikel/ki-in-schweizer-kmu-wir-sind-bereit/), cited by DeepCloud, shows the jump: 22 percent of SMEs used AI applications in 2024, 34 percent in 2025. The most common jobs: translation (52 percent) and correspondence (47 percent). Automating a work step rose from 23 to 34 percent. Those are their figures, not our survey.

The Lucerne University of Applied Sciences [KMU Digital Pulse 2026](https://zenodo.org/records/22098334) is narrower for marketing: people who use AI mostly let it write. Text, translation, images. That job is crowded. Another prompt guide does not help operations.

[BDO](https://www.bdo.ch/de-ch/medienstelle/2026/ki-adoption-die-kluft-zwischen-grossunternehmen-und-kmu-wachst) described the 2026 split this way: large firms embed AI in value creation, many SMEs stay on single apps. Their advice: a few concrete cases, owned by management, not an IT side project.

The [adesso GenAI Impact Report 2026](https://www.adesso.ch/adesso-ch/whitepaper/genai-impact-report-2026-ch.pdf) adds measurement: many firms track whether staff *use* the tool, not whether a process got shorter or more reliable. Usage is not impact.

## Three signals that usage has no leverage

**No owner.** Nobody is allowed to say this workflow is ours, and it is done when X comes out. Then AI stays a tab each person opens for themselves.

**No recurring input.** Different files, questions, and recipients every week. Then there is nothing to clamp. Drafting can still help. A stable flow cannot.

**No measurable output.** You do not know whether the report finishes an hour earlier or whether fewer follow-up questions arrive. Without that, "we use ChatGPT" is a posture, not a result.

If staff paste customer data into private chats because there is no allowed option internally, that is a different problem. See [shadow AI in the office](/blog/shadow-ai-in-the-office).

## What does not work as a first step

A strategy slide for the board, with no workflow. Ten pilots at once. A licence for everyone so "the company has AI". A prompt library that never touches the Monday report.

Those are visible activities. They do not fill the gap above.

## The next step is a workflow, not a model

Filter first: does the work repeat, does it hurt, is it bounded, is the data already there? That is [which process you should automate first](/blog/which-process-to-automate-first).
