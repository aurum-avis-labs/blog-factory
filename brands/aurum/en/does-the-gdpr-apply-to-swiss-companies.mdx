## Does the GDPR apply to Swiss companies?

Does the GDPR apply to Swiss companies? Not by default for every firm in Zurich or Bern. Switzerland keeps the [FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en) as home law for processing with Swiss effects. The GDPR additionally applies when a Swiss controller offers goods or services to people in the European Union or monitors their behaviour there, under [Art. 3(2) GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679). A local plumber with Swiss-only customers is a different case from a SaaS vendor with German trials.

This article is for owners in SMEs with 20 to 250 staff who sell online, employ EU nationals remotely, or sign EU customer contracts. It is orientation, not legal advice. For a side-by-side law comparison, see [Swiss FADP vs GDPR](/security/blog/swiss-fadp-vs-gdpr). This page answers only the scope question.

## When FADP alone is often enough

Purely domestic B2B services without EU delivery, Swiss-only recruitment, and local retail without deliberate EU marketing often stay under Swiss rules only. That does not change because you have a German supplier or because one order arrived from Austria. Patterns matter more than a single invoice.

If you want to stay in that bucket, still revisit the picture when you hire remote staff in the EU, open a .de site, join a marketplace that lists EU customers, or run remarketing limited to EU countries. Each change can open Art. 3(2) without opening a branch in Munich.

## Offering goods or services to the EU

Indicators include delivery to EU countries, prices in euros alongside CHF, EU VAT references, marketing campaigns geo-targeted at Germany or France, and support in EU time zones advertised on the website. Copy such as «we serve clients across Europe» or named reference customers in Hamburg points more strongly than a Swiss-only reference list in Swiss German.

The [Swiss SME portal on the EU data protection regulation](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/de/eu-regelung-zum-datenschutz-neue-eu-verordnung) explains the bridge in plain language. Use it with counsel for your actual sales mix.

## Monitoring behaviour

Tracking EU visitors with cookies, fingerprinting, or behavioural ad networks can pull GDPR scope even without direct sales. «Monitoring» is broader than CCTV. If your marketing agency runs remarketing on EU IP ranges, ask what legal basis they rely on and whether a data processing agreement is in place.

A cookie banner on the EU subdomain only, not on .ch, is a common setup. Clarify with marketing whether tracking catches EU IPs. Whether purely anonymous analytics already count as «monitoring» is disputed in borderline cases. This article flags the question; it does not replace a cookie audit.

## When EU customers drive the paperwork

EU procurement teams often demand data processing agreements with GDPR wording even while your lawyer still tests scope. Contract pressure and legal scope do not always line up. Read group policies from a German parent carefully: corporate rules can push GDPR duties into a Swiss subsidiary that still starts from the FADP at home.

Some Swiss firms appoint an EU representative under GDPR Art. 27 when they have no establishment but target the EU. That is not a Swiss legal requirement but may be a GDPR one. Counsel decides.

## Employees and mixed processing

Hiring remote staff in Germany can create EU processing even if customers are Swiss. Payroll and HR files for that person may fall under the GDPR while the rest of the team stays FADP-primary. Split categories in your records of processing or risk register instead of one undifferentiated bucket.

## What changes in practice

When Art. 3(2) applies, you may need EU-facing privacy text, records of processing, vendor agreements with GDPR wording, breach paths within 72 hours for affected EU processing, and a lead supervisory authority in the EU for some incidents. Swiss notification to the [FDPIC](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/) still applies to Swiss processing with high risk, on the «as fast as possible» standard, not within 72 hours.

EU administrative fines and German Datenschutzbeauftragter expectations live in the GDPR world. Swiss criminal fines up to CHF 250'000 against individuals with intent stay on the Swiss side ([FDPIC penalties](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/de/strafbestimmungen)). Mixed teams benefit from training framed as after the FADP and GDPR without claiming certification.

## Incidents and sensitive data

EU scope changes breach clocks, not the need to document. What counts as a data security breach is a question for the incident in front of you.
