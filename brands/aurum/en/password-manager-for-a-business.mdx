## Password manager for a business: why teams outgrow the shared spreadsheet

A password manager for a business gives named users a shared vault for shop admin, social media, supplier portals, and legacy apps that still lack single sign-on. In Swiss SMEs with 20 to 250 staff, the usual failure mode is not weak personal passwords alone. More often it is a «Marketing2024» password in a Teams chat and an Excel file on a drive called «Passwords OLD do not use». That pattern feeds account takeover and [invoice fraud with a changed IBAN](/security/blog/invoice-fraud-changed-iban). A business vault replaces shared secrets with roles, audit logs, and offboarding that removes one person without resetting every password.

This comparison is not a vendor leaderboard with invented prices. It contrasts three habits: business password manager, shared team login, and spreadsheet or chat sharing. Password length rules live in [password policy for a small business](/security/blog/password-policy-for-a-small-business); payment dual control stays in [four-eyes principle for payments](/security/blog/four-eyes-principle-for-payments).

## What a business vault stores (and what it does not)

Keep personal employee vaults separate from company collections. Collections hold registrar, freight portal, LinkedIn admin, booking tools, and test environments. Each entry shows who last viewed it. Rotating a supplier password updates one record instead of re-messaging twelve people. Pair the vault with MFA on critical mailboxes as you [roll out MFA to staff](/security/blog/rolling-out-mfa-to-staff).

A made-up example: a 90-person industrial firm moves registrar, freight portal, and Instagram credentials into a company vault. When a marketing intern leaves, access to those three entries revokes without changing passwords the whole company uses on other tools.

## Shared team password: document it, then shrink it

Shops, franchise tills, and old postage machines still use one login. Record owner, last rotation, and compensating controls such as physical access limits. Each quarter, check whether a named account or vault entry is possible. Shared mailboxes for info@ are not the same as sharing the admin password; use delegated access where the platform allows.

  <p>A vault does not replace four-eyes on outbound payments. Finance e-banking should still use named bank users with dual authorisation, not a shared «finance» entry copied from a password manager note.</p>

## Spreadsheet and chat sharing: why auditors push back

Excel on SharePoint, Notes files, and pinned Teams messages create copies, mobile offline sync, and search indexing. Anyone with link access sees everything. After phishing, incident response cannot tell which human used a credential. Moving to a vault is as much about culture as software: stop posting resets in chats.

Report suspected compromise through [internal incident reporting](/security/blog/internal-incident-reporting) whether the leak came from a sheet or a vault with weak admin MFA.

## Properties to compare without naming vendors

Ask about encryption at rest and in transit, whether Swiss or EU hosting must appear in your processing agreement, SSO with your Microsoft tenant, and emergency access. If you cannot verify a feature on the vendor site at purchase time, omit it from your decision memo. Health-sector teams may combine vault use with broader checks in [data protection and security for a medical practice](/security/blog/data-protection-and-security-for-a-medical-practice); that article is about process, not picking a tool brand.

Run quarterly access reviews: managers confirm who still needs each collection. Test export once a year so vendor lock-in cannot become a blackout. Many Swiss SMEs prefer Swiss or EU hosting because customer questionnaires ask for it, not because every vault must sit in a particular country under the FADP. Record the decision in an IT memo, not only in procurement mail.

## HR, offboarding, and insurer questions

On exit day disable the vault account like mail. Review shared entries the person could export. Rotate secrets they routinely saw. Logs answer «who last viewed the supplier portal?» better than «we think we changed Instagram». HR and IT share a short checklist: account off, collections reassigned, export rights removed.

Insurers often ask how shared access works. Answer with a company vault, roles, documented offboarding, MFA on admins. «Excel on SharePoint» triggers follow-up questions. Test log export once a year and align retention with your privacy lead.

## Shared mailboxes and architecture

Where Exchange allows delegated access, do not store a vault password for info@. Name the IT partner and an internal owner together; no shadow admin should create collections that nobody reviews.

## Licences without a price league table

Count licences by active users, not every historical account. External project partners only for project duration. Budget extra helpdesk time in month one after rollout; reset requests usually fall afterwards. Too many collections without naming rules confuse people; prefer fewer, clearly named folders.

## Law and training

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is a password manager mandatory under Swiss law?</h3>
  <p>The [FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de) expects appropriate technical and organisational measures; form depends on risk. A vault is a common organisational measure when many shared credentials exist, not an automatic legal label. Orientation, not legal advice.</p>
</div>

Tools fail when people keep copying passwords to sticky notes. A live hour on vault basics, MFA fatigue, and payment fraud beats a link in an email. See [security awareness training](/security/security-awareness) for what the session covers.

As more apps move to SSO, shrink vault entries but keep break-glass and legacy lines. New hires get vault access on day one with only the collections they need, not every entry for interns. Plan one fewer shared login per quarter while legacy devices remain.

Use generated passphrases, not dictionary words. Rotate on exit and after incidents, not blindly every 30 days. Two short training slots for marketing and ops beat one long workshop, then Q and A with your IT partner.

Leadership does not need technical depth, but one line per quarter: open access reviews, exceptions with expiry dates, trend in shared logins. That is enough for insurer conversations without inventing metrics.

A shared password in chat is not a vault. When someone leaves, they still know it. A manager revokes access without broadcasting a new password to everyone else. After six months, check whether the number of shared logins is falling; one number is enough progress for leadership. Teams that keep the Excel list in parallel have only half-adopted the vault, and the audit trail is missing exactly where insurers ask.
