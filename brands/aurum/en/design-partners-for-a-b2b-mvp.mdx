## Design partners for a B2B MVP

**Design partners for a B2B MVP** are a small set of real companies that agree to use an early product in live work, give structured feedback, and tolerate gaps you would never ship to a broad market. You are not hunting logos. You are testing whether the workflow survives contact with their systems, their colleagues, and their patience. Three to six active partners who return each week beat twenty people who clicked "interested" once.

This is for founders and innovation leads in Switzerland who sell to other businesses and already heard the same pain in interviews. Design partners sit after problem discovery and before you pretend the MVP is finished. For landing pages, concierge tests, and pre-sales in the same funnel, see [how to validate a startup idea](/blog/how-to-validate-a-startup-idea). Here the focus is the co-build relationship, not how to scrape a long beta list.

## How to recruit design partners for a B2B MVP

Start from interviews where someone already described a recent episode, not from a cold list of "innovative" brands. A design partner needs a problem worth fixing, authority to run a pilot, and willingness to report what broke.

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Name the job and the falsifier</h3><p>Example: "Two logistics teams will route daily jobs in our app instead of email for four weeks." If nobody opens it twice, the workflow or the segment is wrong.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Offer a narrow pilot, not a roadmap vote</h3><p>One workflow, one team, clear start and end. Partners influence order within that scope; they do not get a backlog of every wish.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Make the cost explicit on both sides</h3><p>They spend time in onboarding and feedback calls. You ship fixes on a rhythm you can keep. Free access is fine if their time is the real payment.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Get something on paper</h3><p>A short pilot letter or order beats a verbal "we are in". Non-binding is common; scope, data, and confidentiality still belong in writing.</p></li>
  <li data-step="5"><h3>Stop adding partners when learning repeats</h3><p>More logos do not fix the same integration bug. Prefer depth with two firms over breadth with eight who ghost after week one.</p></li>
</ol>

## Design partners, beta testers, and your first hundred users

Beta testers often try a build once and send a survey. Design partners embed the product in a recurring task and meet you when it fails. A "first hundred users" goal is a distribution test; design partners are a product and workflow test. You may need both, but not at the same moment. If you still cannot describe the job in one sentence, more testers only dilute the notes.

## What design partners prove, and what they do not

Strong signals: the same team returns without you chasing, they route real data through the product, they escalate blockers inside their firm, and at least one person outside your network does all of that.

Weak signals: a kick-off workshop, a Slack channel that goes quiet, "we love the vision" with no login, or partners who are friends and will not say no.

Design partners do not prove that strangers convert from ads, that unit economics work, that every enterprise passes security review, or that the idea is defensible. Ideas as such cannot be protected; execution and branding can ([IGE on ideas](https://www.ige.ch/de/uebersicht-geistiges-eigentum/ein-leitfaden-fuer-innovative-und-kreative/idee/koennen-ideen-geschuetzt-werden)). They also do not replace a letter of intent or a paid pilot when procurement needs a budget line.

<aside class="blog-callout blog-callout--limit">
  <span class="blog-kicker">Limit</span>
  <p>Even engaged partners leave blind spots. One industry, one company size, or one champion who leaves can end the programme. Treat partner feedback as rich qualitative evidence, not as proof the market at large behaves the same way.</p>
</aside>

## A made-up example: handover checklist for facility managers

A made-up example: a founder builds software for facility managers who hand over buildings between contractors. After eight interviews, two mid-size service firms agree to design partnerships. Each names one site lead who runs weekly handovers. The pilot lasts six weeks. The product covers photo upload, open tasks, and a PDF export, nothing else. Partner A uses it on three handovers and asks for a field that maps to their ERP. Partner B tries once, then stops because the site lead went on leave and nobody replaced the habit. The founder ships the ERP field for Partner A, documents Partner B's drop-off, and does not add a third logo until both firms either repeat usage or exit with a written reason. The programme validated that one firm would change behaviour for a narrow checklist. It did not validate self-serve signup, pricing, or rollout across every canton.

## Who should skip a design partner round

<div class="blog-pair">
  <div>
    <h3>Who this is for</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>B2B founders with a clear buyer and a workflow you can pilot in weeks</li>
      <li>Teams that finished problem interviews and need live usage, not more opinions</li>
      <li>Innovation leads who must show the board behaviour, not slide interest</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Who should skip or delay</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Consumer products where cohorts and ads tests answer the risk faster</li>
      <li>Founders who have no champion with time and cover when IT asks questions</li>
      <li>Teams that want partners to avoid building anything testable</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>How many design partners do you need?</h3>
  <p>Enough to see the same failure twice or two different failures you can act on. For many Swiss B2B MVPs, two active firms plus one backup contact is a sane start. Scale the programme when usage patterns repeat, not when you want more names on a slide.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Should design partners pay?</h3>
  <p>They always pay with time. Cash is optional early on and can sharpen commitment. A symbolic pilot fee or a paid phase two works when finance needs a line item. Free access is not weak if attendance and data access are real.</p>
</div>
