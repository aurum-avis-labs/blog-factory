## When a DPIA is required in Switzerland

A data protection impact assessment under the Swiss FADP is required when processing is likely to result in a high risk to the personality or fundamental rights of data subjects. [Art. 22 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de) expects you to assess necessity, proportionality, and risks before you start or when you materially change a sensitive project. The question "when a DPIA is required in Switzerland" is not about filling fifty pages for every Excel list; it is about spotting projects where systematic monitoring, large-scale sensitive data, or new technology need documented thought.

This is for owners and the person who "owns" data projects in a Swiss SME with about 20 to 250 staff. You may face a new patient app, tenant portal, or HR analytics tool. This article explains triggers and first steps, not how to write the full assessment. Examples of sensitive categories are in [sensitive personal data under the FADP](/security/blog/sensitive-personal-data-under-the-fadp). Orientation only, not legal advice.

## Typical triggers in SME life

Large-scale processing of health data in a group practice merger, employee monitoring with keystroke logging, systematic scoring of tenants or job applicants without human review, combining camera footage with access logs, or rolling out biometric time clocks can push risk upward. A single shared mailbox for appointments is unlikely to need a formal DPIA; migrating that mailbox to a new US vendor without reviewing transfer risk is a different conversation.

The FDPIC may publish lists of processing that need prior consultation. Check current FDPIC material before you rely on blog summaries.

## What a DPIA is not

It is not a marketing document for your software vendor. It is not a substitute for a [data processing agreement](/security/blog/data-processing-agreement-under-the-fadp). It does not replace information duties toward staff or patients. It is not only a GDPR exercise: Swiss law has its own Art. 22 wording and consultation rules.

Exemption from a processing register for firms under 250 employees ([Art. 24 DPO](https://www.activemind.ch/gesetze/dsv/artikel-24/)) does not exempt you from impact assessment when risk is high.

## Who can keep the screening light

Firms with routine processing similar to peers, no systematic profiling, and no large sensitive datasets may document "low risk, no full DPIA" with short reasoning. That still belongs on file if a customer or insurer asks later.

## How long a DPIA takes in an SME

A screen often takes half a day with IT and the project lead. A full DPIA may need several sessions when many data types, subprocessors, and retention periods interact. Page count is not quality: decision, measures, and residual risk matter. Link retention work from [retention periods and a deletion concept](/security/blog/retention-periods-and-a-deletion-concept) when the assessment asks how long data stay.

## How this ties to breaches and fines

A DPIA does not prevent incidents. It should list residual risks and who acts if mail goes to the wrong patient or a laptop disappears. Swiss breach notification applies when a high risk to personality is likely, "as soon as possible", not on a fixed 72-hour EU clock ([EDÖB guidance](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/T64CAUyvAMcF/1_2%20Leitfaden%20des%20ED%C3%96B%20betreffend%20die%20Meldung%20von%20Datensicherheitsverletzungen%20und%20Information%20der%20Betroffenen%20nach%20Art.%2024%20DSG_DE.pdf)). Fines up to CHF 250'000 under the FADP target natural persons with intent; a signed DPIA is not a shield, but it shows you thought before launching.

## Roles inside a KMU without a DPO

The owner or practice manager owns the decision. IT partner supplies technical facts. HR joins when employee data mix with customer projects. External counsel reviews when the FDPIC consultation threshold may apply. Do not outsource the sign-off to a vendor sales deck.

## Who should skip reading further

Purely internal IT upgrades that do not change who sees personal data may only need change management. If you already have a counsel-run DPIA for your industry software and nothing changed except price, update the version number and move on.

## A made-up example: tenant portal before launch

A made-up example: a property manager with 120 employees plans a tenant self-service portal with defect photos, bank details for refunds, and ID uploads. The project lead lists data categories and retention. HR notes minors in student flats. IT notes US hosting for image compression. The owner schedules a half-day workshop: proportionality, alternatives, access roles, deletion after move-out. They produce a ten-page DPIA draft, add measures (shorter retention for photos, Swiss backup), and only then enable uploads. No vendor claimed "compliance"; the firm owns the sign-off.

## When customers ask before you start

EU clients sometimes request a "DPIA" by name in RFPs. Swiss law uses the same idea under Art. 22. You can answer with your screening outcome and a summary of measures even when the document is ten pages, not fifty. Do not copy a German template without checking Swiss consultation rules.

## Cameras and monitoring

Systematic CCTV at work can trigger a screen or require a full DPIA. Purpose and scope are covered in [CCTV at work in Switzerland](/security/blog/cctv-at-work-in-switzerland); the DPIA additionally asks about scale, automation, and alternatives.

## Register, DPA, and DPIA

Three different documents: the processing register lists ongoing processing, the [data processing agreement](/security/blog/data-processing-agreement-under-the-fadp) governs a vendor, and the DPIA assesses one high-risk project. Firms under 250 employees are often exempt from register duties, not from Art. 22 when risk is high. RFPs sometimes ask for all three; prioritise the screen before you go live with software.

## Records to keep

Store the date of the assessment, participants, conclusion, and residual risks. Update when the vendor changes subprocessors or when you add fields to a form. Auditors and insurers increasingly ask "show your thinking", not only policies on paper.

## This week without a big project

Block 45 minutes with the owner and IT: list running projects that introduce new personal data or new tools. Mark one as "high risk suspected". For the rest, one sentence "screen on date X: low" is enough. Keep the note in the same folder as your data processing agreements.

Train decision-makers in a [data protection session](/security/datenschutz) so they recognise when to call counsel.
