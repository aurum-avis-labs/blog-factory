## AI for trades businesses: automate the office work first

AI for trades businesses earns its keep where someone is still typing things up in the evening: in the office. Not on the building site, and not on the website. What you can automate is the office work between the site and your trade software: moving day reports across, ordering material, filing photos against the right job, preparing invoice drafts and flagging payments that are due. Costing, prices and promises to customers stay with you.

This piece is for owners and office managers of Swiss trades and construction businesses, roughly 5 to 50 people, working with trade software such as Sorba, Messerli or another package. It shows where the office work gets stuck, why the usual fixes do not close the gap, and what better looks like.

## Where the office work gets stuck

Office work gets stuck wherever the same information is written twice: once outside, once in the office. The trade software knows the job. What happens on site arrives on paper, as a photo in a group chat, or by phone.

| Task | How it often runs | What can run automatically | What stays with you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quotes | Copy the last quote, adjust the lines | Draft from a template and the customer record | Costing and price |
| Daywork and day reports | Paper in the van, typed up on Friday | Capture on the phone or a photo of the paper, fields pre-filled | Checking what gets billed |
| Material orders | A call or message to each supplier | One order draft per supplier from a single list | Approving the order |
| Scheduling | Whiteboard or Excel, changes by phone | Weekly plan from the jobs, sent to the crew | Who goes where |
| Invoices | Hunt down the reports, type in the lines | Invoice draft from checked reports | Approval and sending |
| Payment reminders | Go through open items when there is time | List of overdue items and a draft reminder | Whether and how to chase |
| Photo records | Photos on private phones and in chats | Filed against the right job, with the date | Which photos go to the customer |

Not every row applies to your business. Usually it is two or three, and one of them lands on the same person every week, late in the evening.

## Why the trade software alone does not close the gap

Trade software works with what is in it. The gap is the way in. Many packages have their own apps for this. Sorba, for example, offers a [day report app](https://www.sorba.ch/software/app/tagesrapport) that records staff, machines and material on site and sends the report to the office. If every crew uses an app like that every day, this part is solved.

In many businesses not everyone does. The subcontractor brings paper. The fitter who has been with you for twenty years prefers a pen. Material from the DIY store on the way sits on a receipt in the glovebox. The extra work was agreed verbally on site. The photos live in a group chat, on private phones. The software sees none of it until someone in the office types it in.

The second usual fix is a ChatGPT subscription. According to the [AXA SME labour market study, cited by DeepCloud](https://www.deepcloud.swiss/newsroom/artikel/ki-in-schweizer-kmu-wir-sind-bereit/), Swiss SMEs use AI most often for translation (52%) and correspondence (47%). That helps with a letter to the property manager. It does not get the report from the van into the invoice.

The third is another person in the office. That can be the right call. In smaller businesses, though, the office work is often done by the owner, in the evening, because the site takes the day.

## What better looks like

Better does not mean the office disappears. Better means each piece of information is captured once, where it happens, and the office checks drafts instead of retyping.

AI has a small, clear place in this. It reads a photographed paper report, matches "2 bags of cement" to the right item in your list, and flags what it cannot read with confidence. Ordinary automation does the rest: moving data to the right place, producing a draft, setting a reminder.

Material orders follow the same pattern as [KitchenCrew](/portfolio/kitchencrew), a product we built for restaurants: the team types a list into a chat, and an order draft is created for each supplier, which someone checks and sends. A kitchen is not a building site. But copying one list out five times for five suppliers is the same job.

## What applies to photos and customer data

Site photos often show more than the work: flats, names on doors, plans. Images like that do not belong in a free personal account of an AI service. What to watch for in Switzerland is covered in [ChatGPT and customer data](/prozess-check/blog/chatgpt-customer-data-swiss-dsg). Filing works the same as for paper: with the job, not on the fitter's phone.

## Who should not start yet

A three-person business writing five invoices a month usually does not need automation. It needs a clean template and a fixed slot in the diary. The same goes if every report looks different and nobody has decided which fields it must have. Then a single standard form comes first, automation after.

If your crews already use the app of your trade software every day, the manual work is somewhere else, perhaps in payment reminders or scheduling. Which workflows are often worth it in an SME office, trades or not, is in [three workflows worth automating](/prozess-check/blog/three-workflows-worth-automating).

## The first step

Take last week's reports and count: how many there were, who typed them up, how long it took, and how many days passed between the work and the invoice. Those four numbers show whether automation pays. Use the four questions in [which process to automate first](/prozess-check/blog/which-process-to-automate-first) to sort the other candidates.
