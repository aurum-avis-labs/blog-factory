## Swiss startup funding programmes

**Swiss startup funding programmes** for a small firm usually mean a mix of your own cash, selective non-dilutive schemes, and later angel or venture money if the story and the data support it. For an SME turning internal know-how into an app, a portal, or a narrow SaaS offer, sort programmes by stage, innovation story, and what you still need to prove. The biggest cheque is rarely the right first filter. This page is a checklist for that sorting pass, not a grant catalogue.

The reader is an owner or project lead in Switzerland with a concrete product direction. If you are still unsure what an MVP should cost, start with [MVP cost in Switzerland](/blog/mvp-cost-switzerland).

## What funding can and cannot prove

Non-dilutive money can buy coaching, prototypes, or runway while you learn. It does not replace interviews, deployed usage, or paid acquisition tests. A voucher does not tell you whether a buyer will renew, and venture capital interest in the Swiss market as a whole does not set the size of your round. Institutional investment reached about CHF 2.95 billion in 2025 according to the CMS note on the Swiss Venture Capital Report 2026, which says the market exists, not that your slide deck will close at a given valuation.

## The two national programmes most SMEs meet first

**Innosuisse Initial Coaching** offers a voucher of up to CHF 10'000 for up to twelve months. It targets start-ups up to five years old, or up to ten in life sciences, with an innovative, science-based idea. The money is for coaching and defined project work, not a blank cheque for marketing. If your product is a straightforward digitisation of an existing spreadsheet with no research or technology angle, assume you are outside the spirit of the scheme until an adviser says otherwise.

**Venture Kick** supports selected Swiss start-ups with staged non-dilutive funding. The public site describes support of up to CHF 150'000 across stages; confirm the current split on [venturekick.ch](https://www.venturekick.ch/) before you plan a budget line. The programme is competitive and expects traction, team, and a credible innovation story, not only a PDF.

## Same-day checklist before you apply

Work through the list in order. If you stop at an item, that is the answer for this month.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li><strong>Name the job the money must do.</strong> Separate build cost, validation spend (ads, pilots), and living runway. A grant that only covers coaching does not pay Azure hosting unless the programme explicitly allows it.</li>
  <li><strong>Match stage to instrument.</strong> Coaching vouchers suit structured learning and expert input. Larger Kick stages assume you can show progress, not only intent. Your own cash or revenue still funds the first deploy if no scheme fits yet.</li>
  <li><strong>Check age and form.</strong> Innosuisse counts company age from formation. A GmbH needs at least CHF 20'000 share capital fully paid in at incorporation ([KMU guidance](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/de/gmbh-haftung-stammkapital-gruendung)). Sole proprietorships and young GmbHs both appear in applications; read the current form.</li>
  <li><strong>Read reporting and IP clauses.</strong> Note deliverables, milestones, and whether the funder expects co-financing. Copy the deadlines into your calendar before you celebrate approval.</li>
  <li><strong>Prepare evidence, not adjectives.</strong> One-page problem statement, who you interviewed, what you built or deployed, and what you measured. Link to a live URL when you have one.</li>
  <li><strong>Plan time honestly.</strong> Applications and acquittals eat founder hours. If the team is two people and both are selling full time, a CHF 10'000 voucher can still be a net loss if it delays shipping.</li>
  <li><strong>Decide what you will not buy with public money.</strong> Legal opinions, patent filings, and brand agencies may be necessary, but they are rarely the core of a first validation story unless the programme asks for them.</li>
  <li><strong>Keep dilution for when the product question is narrower.</strong> Angels and funds want to see what you learned from real usage. A grant application is not a substitute for that narrative.</li>
</ul>

## A made-up example: portal for field technicians

A made-up example: a metalworking SME with eighteen staff wants a portal where field technicians log job status and photos instead of WhatsApp threads. The owner has CHF 25'000 set aside and wonders whether Innosuisse Initial Coaching should pay for external product coaching.

On Thursday they run the checklist. The innovation angle is process data and integration with their ERP, not a generic app idea, so they bookmark Innosuisse and read the science requirement carefully. They list three uses for money: four weeks of build, CHF 2'000 for a small LinkedIn test, and one day per week of the owner's time for customer calls. They see that the voucher cap is CHF 10'000, which covers coaching but not the full build, so they keep their CHF 25'000 for deployment and ads. They draft a one-page note with five technician interviews and a screenshot of a manual form. They decide Venture Kick is premature until ten technicians used a browser MVP for two weeks. They calendar two hours next Tuesday for the Innosuisse form and stop before inventing revenue forecasts they do not have.

The pass did not win funding. It stopped them from applying with a vague "digital transformation" paragraph and no test plan.

## Angels, banks, and your own balance sheet

Friends, family, and angels still fund many Swiss software projects when the product is testable. Banks rarely finance pre-revenue app experiments on favourable terms. Equity belongs after you can show what you learned from deployed users.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Do Swiss startup funding programmes pay for an MVP agency?</h3>
  <p>Sometimes indirectly through coaching or project budgets defined in the grant. Few schemes pay an open-ended agency rate list. Read the eligible cost lines and ask the programme contact in writing if unsure.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Must we be a university spin-off for Venture Kick?</h3>
  <p>No single label fits every cohort. The programme selects on innovation and team strength. Read the current criteria on the official site rather than assuming only ETH or EPFL teams qualify.</p>
</div>
