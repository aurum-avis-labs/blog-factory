## Digital delivery notes and proof of delivery

You process delivery notes and proof of delivery digitally in five steps: the driver photographs the signed note at the door, the picture is matched to the transport order, exceptions go to a person, the invoice is released only when the match holds, and a later dispute is answered from that archive. The office stops hunting through a group chat on Friday.

This is for a carrier, a shipper with its own vehicles, or a trader who delivers and bills the customer from its own note. It is not the buyer's job of matching a supplier's delivery note to a supplier invoice. That route is in [matching delivery notes to orders and invoices](/blog/delivery-note-invoice-matching). It is also not a trades daywork sheet, where a crew records hours and material on site. That route is in [from daywork report to invoice](/blog/daywork-report-to-invoice). Where the rest of a small fleet's office work sits is in [AI for small logistics and transport companies](/blog/ai-for-logistics-transport).

## Step 1: One photo, the same day, one address

The note is useless in the cab until Friday and useless as a cropped picture in a group chat. The driver photographs the whole page at the doorway, signature included, and sends it to one address you chose, or files it in the transport system if that is already the habit. Paper can stay in the folder for the period you agreed. The office works from the picture.

What does not work is three routes: some notes in the app, some in a chat, some still on the clipboard on Monday. Pick one. A note that never leaves the truck is an invoice you cannot defend.

## Step 2: The fields the match needs

The picture can be read only if you know which fields matter. Agree them once.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Your order or delivery number, written where the driver was told to write it</li>
  <li>Date, and the address if the order has more than one stop</li>
  <li>The recipient's name, as signed, even if the handwriting is only partly clear</li>
  <li>A signature that is on the page, not cut off by the photo</li>
  <li>Quantities received, and any shortage or damage written on the note</li>
  <li>The driver's mark, so you know who delivered</li>
</ul>

A workflow can pre-fill those fields from the picture and flag what it cannot read. It should not complete a missing order number from "the only delivery on that street". If the number is absent or ambiguous, the note waits for a person.

## Step 3: Match to the open order, or hold

The match is the order number first, then the date and the address. A clean match lands on the order as proof of delivery, next to the lines you planned to deliver. A note that names a quantity lower than the order is a partial delivery, not a failed match. It is marked as short, and a person decides whether you bill the short quantity or hold the whole delivery.

These cases stay on the review list:

- No order number, or two open orders that could fit.
- Signature missing or off the edge of the photo.
- The note is for a date or an address the order does not have.
- Damage or a refusal is written on the page.
- The same note arrives twice.

The person matches, asks the driver, or holds the invoice. They do not retype a note that already matched.

## Step 4: The invoice uses the order, the note proves it

The billable lines are the ones on the order that the note supports. The rate is the rate on the order or on the customer agreement, not a figure read off the customer's handwriting. The signed picture is attached or linked, so the customer and your office see the same page. Waiting time, an extra stop, or a refusal fee is a person's decision, written as its own line, not guessed from a scribble.

Create the draft in the software you already use to invoice. Whether that is an import or an interface depends on the product. Until you have looked at the file, do not assume it. Lines that were typed from the note into the invoice, then typed again into the accounts, are the gap. Mark the order as billed only when the invoice is approved. A draft you delete should leave the note unmatched, so you can bill it later.

## Step 5: Disputes from the archive, not from the chat

A customer writes three weeks later: we did not receive the second pallet, the signature is not our person, the carton was crushed. The answer starts from the archived picture and the order it was matched to. The draft quotes the date, the name on the note, and what was written about quantity or damage. It does not argue. A person sends it, and decides whether you credit, redeliver, or stand by the note.

A signature on a photo is the record you agreed to keep. It is not, by itself, a court judgement, and this page does not say it is. If the customer says the name is wrong, someone who knows that doorway looks at the picture. The workflow finds the page. It does not decide the claim.

## When the driver's app is enough

The app is enough when every delivery is confirmed in it, the signature is on the order, and the invoice will not go out without that confirmation. You do not need a second reader for notes that never exist on paper. The gap is the customers and the subcontractors who still sign paper, and the photos that land in a chat instead of on the order. Bring those onto the same list as the app confirmations. The transport system stays the place where the order lives.

## A made-up example: thirty doors, one Friday list

A made-up example: a carrier with six vans makes about thirty deliveries on a weekday. Regular customers are confirmed in the transport app. About ten stops a day are still paper, photographed into a group chat, sometimes without the top of the page where the order number sits. On Friday the office invoices from the tour list and attaches a note when someone can find the photo. Two disputes a month start with "we never got a signature".

In the changed route, paper photos go to one address the same day. The next morning the review list shows each note beside an order where the number could be read, and four without a match: a missing number, a signature off the photo, a quantity two cartons short, and a note whose address is not on any open order. A dispatcher clears those four. The matched deliveries become invoice drafts with the picture attached. The short delivery is billed for what the note records, because that is the rule this carrier wrote down. What goes is searching the chat. How long that took depends on how many notes you handle and how long one takes today.

## When to leave it

Leave it if every delivery is already signed in the system you invoice from. Leave it too if drivers will not photograph the page. A workflow cannot match a note that stays in the cab. Agree the one address, use it for two weeks, and look at how many photos are complete before you connect them to the invoice.
