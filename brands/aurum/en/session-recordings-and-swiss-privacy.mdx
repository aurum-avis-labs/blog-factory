Under the Swiss FADP that is usually processing of personal data, not a free pass because the tool calls it "analytics". ## Session recordings and Swiss privacy in one sentence

Replay tools store a film of the browser session: clicks, navigation, form focus, and errors. Vendors often mask password fields and credit card boxes by default; they do not automatically know your contract PDF upload, health questionnaire, or payroll field is sensitive. The Swiss FADP applies to personal data about identifiable people ([Art. 5 FADP](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/de)). A session tied to an account, an IP address, or a rare combination of pages can still be personal data even when you never asked for a name on that visit.

Transparency is statutory. Art. 19 FADP expects the controller to tell people what is processed, for which purpose, and who receives data, in a form they can understand. Session replay belongs in that story if you switch it on for real users, including pilot cohorts you email by hand.

## What founders use recordings for in an MVP test

In weeks five to ten of a structured validation programme, you care where people stall: unread headlines, off-screen buttons on mobile, forms that clear on refresh. Replay answers "what did they try?" faster than a late survey. Heatmaps complement GA4 events; they do not replace a tracking plan. A short list after fifty sessions is often enough to pick one fix before the next ad iteration. That is product learning, not a substitute for consent design, enterprise contracts, or locking admin panels replay must never see.

## Swiss privacy angles you should not hand-wave

**Privacy notice.** If the MVP is public, you need a notice that matches reality. Session recording belongs next to analytics and hosting, with the vendor named. The security article linked above walks through Swiss notice content; this page does not duplicate it.

**Purpose and proportionality.** Process only what you need for the test. Full replay on every page including logged-in account settings is harder to defend than replay on the landing funnel and the first-run wizard. Sample rates and URL exclusions are product settings with privacy impact.

**Sensitive domains.** Health, children, finance, and employee monitoring each raise questions this article will not resolve. If your MVP touches those categories, assume replay is off by default until an adviser has read the flow.

**Cross-border processing.** Many replay vendors store data outside Switzerland. Transfers and subprocessors belong in the notice and often in a data processing agreement.

**Cookies and similar technologies.** Switzerland has no blanket "accept all cookies" rule. The EDÖB guide distinguishes consent cases (high-risk profiling, cross-site tracking, retargeting) from information and objection rights elsewhere. Replay is not retargeting, but align banner, notice, and tag manager. When in doubt, ask your adviser rather than copying a German GDPR template.

## What session recordings prove and do not prove

Replays prove that some sessions hit a concrete obstacle: a confusing label, a broken back button, a form error nobody reported. Combined with funnel events and a handful of interviews, they sharpen the next pivot.

They do **not** prove that strangers want the job done, that they will pay, or that you have product-market fit. Fit needs repeated use, retention, or payment signals interpreted together, as set out in [how to find product-market fit](/blog/product-market-fit-how-to-find-it). A beautiful replay of someone leaving after ten seconds is still a leave.

They do **not** prove your backend is safe or replace sample size. Three angry replays are a hint; pair them with traffic volume and event denominators from your analytics posts.

## Habits that keep the test defensible enough to ship

Configure masking before you share the link. Block replay on `/admin`, staging, and internal tools. Keep retention to the ad sprint, not "forever". Exclude authenticated screens until you know what appears on them. Tell pilot users you use replay; keep findings out of public channels with visible email addresses.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is Microsoft Clarity allowed in Switzerland?</h3>
  <p>Swiss law does not publish a list of allowed vendors. Clarity is widely used, including in structured validation builds that pair GA4 with replay. Whether you may use it for your MVP depends on notice content, contracts, data categories, and transfers. The question to ask your adviser is whether your specific setup is documented and proportionate, not whether a blog said yes.</p>
