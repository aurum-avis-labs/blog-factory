## Cold outreach for validation

**Cold outreach for validation** is contacting people who do not know you yet, with one narrow question about how they already handle a problem, so you can decide whether to build, pivot, or stop. It is not a mail merge for demos, not a LinkedIn growth hack, and not proof that paid acquisition will work. You send a small batch, read the replies like data, and stop when the next message would only repeat what you already heard.

This piece sits next to the wider menu in [how to validate a startup idea](/blog/how-to-validate-a-startup-idea). Warm introductions and waitlists are different channels. Here the focus is strangers, Swiss B2B habits included, and what a founder without a sales team can do in a week without burning the list.

## What one batch should learn

Pick one sentence you want evidence for. Examples: «Teams still reconcile supplier invoices in Excel every month» or «Site managers do not trust mobile forms without offline mode». Every message should make that sentence easier to accept or reject. If the reader only learns your product name, you pitched too early.

Log four fields per contact: segment tag, channel, reply type (story, referral, no, silence), and whether they offered a next step with cost (call, data sample, paid pilot). Silence after a polite nudge is also a data point when the list was on-target.

## A repeatable cold outreach sequence

<ol class="blog-steps">
  <li data-step="1"><h3>Build a list of thirty, send ten</h3><p>Thirty names you can defend as fitting the segment. Send ten personalised opens. Keep twenty in reserve so you can adjust the question after the first replies.</p></li>
  <li data-step="2"><h3>Research one concrete detail per person</h3><p>A public post, a job title change, a project page. One line that shows you did not copy a template blindly.</p></li>
  <li data-step="3"><h3>Ask about the last time, not the future</h3><p>«How did you handle X last month?» beats «Would you use a tool for X?» Invite a short reply; offer a 15-minute call only if they ask.</p></li>
  <li data-step="4"><h3>Stop the batch and write a half-page memo</h3><p>Three recurring stories, two blockers, one referral. Decide whether the next batch needs a new segment or a new question.</p></li>
</ol>

## Email, LinkedIn, and Swiss caution

In Switzerland, unsolicited **advertising** email to people who have not agreed to hear from you is risky. Treat **consent** as the safe default: only email addresses where the person opted in, or where your adviser confirms a narrow B2B exception applies. This article does not quote statute text; rules change and facts matter.

LinkedIn and industry forums are common for founder outreach. Stay inside each platform’s terms. Do not export connections into a bulk tool and pretend it is «research». If you store names and replies, you handle personal data: keep the sheet minimal, delete when the batch ends, and say in the first message why you are writing.

Phone cold calls can work in some trades; they are harder to scale and harder to log. For early validation, written outreach usually leaves cleaner notes.

## A made-up example: outbound after friend interviews

A made-up example: a founder targets finance leads at Swiss companies with 30 to 150 staff. Friends said expense reporting was «annoying». Ten strangers get a LinkedIn note, not email, because nobody opted in to mail.

The note mentions a public post about their audit season, then asks how receipts reached accounting last quarter. Four people ignore it. Three send one-line answers («still PDFs by email»). Two share a short story about a failed tool rollout blocked by IT. One offers a call with a colleague who owns the process map.

The memo says: pain is real, IT gate is common, no price talk yet. The founder does not send the remaining twenty the same text. She tightens the segment to companies that already use a named accounting cloud and runs a second batch of ten. She still has no product demo. The useful signal is the repeated workaround and the referral, not the four silent profiles.

## What cold outreach does not prove

A friendly reply is not a purchase. A call is not a pilot contract. A cluster of «we use Excel» stories does not show that strangers will pay your price or finish onboarding. Cold outreach also does not replace a landing page test when the question is simply whether anyone clicks «request access» from an ad.

It does not prove you can hire sales later, that CAC will stay low, or that Swiss enterprises will skip security review. Those need other experiments.

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>How many messages before I change the hypothesis?</h3>
  <p>After one batch of ten on-target sends plus one polite follow-up, you should see either repeating stories or a clear «wrong segment» signal. If every reply is polite vagueness, your question is probably too broad or too promotional.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Should I attach a deck or link to a waitlist?</h3>
  <p>Not in the first touch for validation. A link turns the conversation into marketing metrics you cannot interpret. If they ask what you are building, answer in two sentences and return to their workflow.</p>
</div>

<div class="blog-faq">
  <h3>Is cold outreach the same as getting your first 100 users?</h3>
  <p>No. Discovery batches are small and qualitative. Scaling to a hundred active users usually needs product, positioning, and channels you measure separately, as in [getting your first 100 users](/blog/get-your-first-100-users). Use outreach to sharpen who those users might be.</p>
</div>

## When the validation package fits

The [validation package](/services/mvp-validation) fits when cold outreach has pointed to a segment and a painful workflow, and the open question is whether people use real software and whether acquisition can be measured. Weeks one to four put a deployed product in their hands, not a slide. Weeks five to twelve add positioning, a landing page, paid traffic on Google and LinkedIn, and up to three pivot loops, ending in a Go, Pivot, or Stop recommendation plus what the data still cannot prove.

If you are still choosing the segment or the first question, stay with small batches and a written hypothesis. Book the package when you want to trade build time for behaviour you can record, not when you only need ten more polite replies.
