## Request and compare supplier quotes

<p class="blog-lede">You compare supplier quotes when the same line list goes to several suppliers, every reply lands in one table, and missing lines stay visible until someone asks or decides.</p>

Purchasing here means you write the request, you read the replies however they arrive, and you award the order. The table does not choose alone, and the lowest number is not a decision.

This is not chasing quotes you sent to your own customers, covered in [how to follow up on quotes](/prozess-check/blog/quote-follow-ups). A standing price file you update in bulk is different again, in [import supplier price lists](/prozess-check/blog/supplier-price-lists). Here the quote is a one-off answer to a request you wrote.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>One request, the same lines, to each supplier you actually ask</li>
  <li>Reply deadline, delivery place and the unit you buy in</li>
  <li>Replies read into one table, whatever the file looked like</li>
  <li>Missing lines and odd units marked, not guessed</li>
  <li>A person awards the order</li>
</ul>

## The same request to everyone

Comparison only works if everyone answered the same question. Write the request once. Per line: your description or article number, quantity, unit, and anything that changes the price (grade, dimension, finish). Name the delivery place and the date by which the quote must arrive. The date you want the goods is separate.

If you ask three suppliers, all three get that document. A phone call with a shorter list counts only if you put the list in writing and the others receive it too. Otherwise drop the call from the comparison.

The request can come from a template so last month's customer name does not sit in the footer. Fields and a check before sending: [generate documents from templates](/prozess-check/blog/generate-documents-from-templates). The comparison starts when replies return.

Store the request file with the date in the name. Suppliers rarely reuse your file name. You need one folder that holds the request and the replies.

If a reply arrives after the deadline, file it, mark it late, and decide whether it still belongs in the table. A late quote without a mark distorts the comparison you closed on the due date.

## Read replies that do not match

One supplier fills your Excel. One sends a PDF with their article numbers and a pack price. One emails three sentences and a total. All three can sit in one table if you read onto your lines, not onto the supplier layout.

Your line stays the row. Price, unit, article number, lead time and the supplier's note sit in columns under their name. A total in the email is not a unit price. If you cannot split it, the line says "total only". It does not belong in the unit-price column.

Net or gross: the same rule in every column. In Switzerland, purchase prices in quotes are often net plus VAT. If one column mixes gross and one net, the comparison is worthless until you convert or ask. A price without a stated discount is incomplete. Note it. Do not invent the discount from last year's list. If a quote line contradicts a current price list, show both numbers. A person decides whether it is a special price or a stale list.

## One table and the gaps

One row per line you asked for. Suppliers are columns.

| Column | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Your line | Description, quantity and unit from the request |
| Price | Net unit price in CHF, or empty |
| Unit as quoted | Piece, pack, hundred |
| Lead time | What the reply actually said |
| Gap | Missing, total only, or unit differs |

A line with no price is a gap, not a zero. Zero would win a quick "lowest price" glance and would be wrong. The word "missing" in the gap column is readable without colour coding.

If a supplier offered a substitute, note it in the cell. Do not replace your line with their description. Extra lines you did not ask for sit under the table until you widen the request on purpose.

Pack size is a frequent false win. A price per box of ten looks cheaper than a unit price until someone divides. If the reply does not state the pack, the unit column stays empty.

Freight, packaging and "small order surcharge" belong on their own lines or in an "extras" column, not hidden in the unit price. One supplier who includes delivery and one who names it separately can look alike until you read the extra line. If the quote says "ex works" or "from stock", note who pays transport. If two lines in different replies seem to mean the same item, resolve it in the cross-reference before you compare prices. Otherwise you compare different articles.

## The decision stays with a person

Price, lead time, whether the item matches what you specified, and whether delivery to site is possible are several facts. If you want weights, write them visibly on the table and show the arithmetic. A hidden formula nobody can explain is not a purchase. The table prepares the comparison. It does not send the order or declare a winner. Missing lines stay missing until a person asks, substitutes or drops them.

The order must trace to the reply: file, date, lines. Store that with the order. Next year you can see whether you chose the cheap line or the line that fitted.

Do not write awarded prices silently into standing purchase prices for every article. A one-off quote is not a new price list. If the price should stick, that is the work in [import supplier price lists](/prozess-check/blog/supplier-price-lists), deliberate and logged.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a small interior fit-out firm needs ironmongery for one flat. Twelve lines with quantities and units go to three suppliers, with a reply date. One fills the sheet. One sends a PDF with their own numbers. One says two hinges are unavailable and quotes the other ten as one total.

The table has twelve rows. Excel fills price and unit throughout. The PDF matches nine rows through the cross-reference. Three rows wait because the article text fits two hinges. The email has "total only" on ten cells and "missing" on the hinges. The buyer asks the third supplier for line prices, reads the substitute note on one hinge, and orders the rest from the first supplier because the lead time fits the site. The firm and numbers are invented.

After the award the folder stays: request, all replies, the table with gaps, and a note of who placed the order. Without that set, next year you repeat the argument about whether the price was "cheaper before".

## What to leave alone

Skip it when you buy the same article monthly from the same supplier and a current price list already covers it. Skip it when the "comparison" is a legal tender under public procurement rules: different procedure, different duties. Skip it when you only ever ask one supplier: there is nothing to compare. A request template may still help.
