## Request and compare supplier quotes

You request and compare supplier quotes by sending the same list to each supplier, reading the replies even when they come back as different files, and placing every line in one table. Missing items and units that do not match are flagged. A person chooses. The flow does not award the order, and it does not treat the lowest number as the answer.

This is purchasing. It is not the work of chasing quotes you have sent to your own customers, which is covered in [how to follow up on quotes](/blog/quote-follow-ups). A standing price file from a supplier, updated in bulk, is a different job again, in [import supplier price lists](/blog/supplier-price-lists). A quote here is a one-time answer to a request you wrote.

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>One request, the same lines, to each supplier you actually ask</li>
  <li>A reply deadline, a delivery place and the unit you buy in</li>
  <li>Replies read into one table, whatever the file looked like</li>
  <li>Missing lines and odd units marked, not filled in</li>
  <li>A person awards the order. The table does not</li>
</ul>

## Put the same request in front of each supplier

Comparison only works if the suppliers answered the same question. Write the request once. A line is your description or your article number, the quantity, the unit, and anything that changes the price: a grade, a dimension, a finish. Say where it has to be delivered and by when you need the reply, not by when you wish the goods would appear.

If you ask three suppliers, all three get that document. A phone call to one of them, with a shorter list, is not a third quote. Either put the shorter list in writing and send it to the others, or drop the phone call from the comparison.

The request itself can be a document from a template, so last month's customer name does not linger in the footer. How templates, fields and a check before sending work is in [generate documents from templates](/blog/generate-documents-from-templates). The comparison starts when the replies come back. The template only keeps the request honest.

Keep the request file with a name that includes the date. The replies will not use your file name. You need one place that still ties them to this ask.

## Read replies that do not look alike

One supplier returns your Excel with a price column filled in. One sends a PDF of their own form, with their article numbers and a pack price. One writes an email with three sentences and a total at the bottom. All three can be compared, but not by laying the files side by side.

Read each reply onto your lines, not onto the supplier's layout. Your line stays the row. The supplier's price, their unit, their article number, their lead time and their comment land in columns under their name. A total at the bottom of an email is not a line price. If you cannot split it, the line is "total only" and it does not sit in the unit-price column as if it were comparable.

Net or gross has to be the same decision on every column. A price that still needs a discount you have not been given is incomplete. Note that. Do not invent the discount from last year's list. If a current price list exists and a quote line contradicts it, show both numbers. The quote may be a special price. The list may be stale. A person sees the difference. The table does not pick one in silence.

## One table, and the lines that are missing

The table has one row per line you asked for. Suppliers are columns. Add only the facts you will use to decide. A useful set:

| Column | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Your line | The description, quantity and unit you asked for |
| Price | Net unit price in CHF, or empty |
| Unit as quoted | Piece, pack, hundred, so a mismatch is visible |
| Lead time | What the reply actually said |
| Gap | Missing, total only, or unit not the same |

A line with no price is a gap, not a zero. Zero would win a "lowest price" glance and would be wrong. Colour is optional. The word "missing" in the gap column is what a second person can read without the colour.

If a supplier offered a substitute, put it in a note on that cell. Do not replace your line with their description, or the other columns no longer refer to the same thing. If they added an item you did not ask for, park it under the table. It is not a row in the comparison until you decide the request should have included it.

Pack size is a frequent false win. A price per box of ten looks cheaper than a price per piece until you divide. The unit column is there so that division happens before anyone talks about who is cheaper. If the reply does not say the pack, the cell stays empty.

## The decision stays with a person

Price, lead time, whether the item is actually the one you specified, and whether the supplier can deliver to the site are not one number. If you want a weighted score, write the weights down where the reader of the table can see them, and show the arithmetic. Do not hide a ranking in a formula nobody else can explain. Even then, a person awards the order. A score is a view. It is not a purchase.

The order that goes out should be traceable to the reply: which file, which date, which lines. Store that with the order. Next year, when the same part is expensive, you can see whether you chose the cheap line or the line that matched.

Do not write the awarded prices back into the standing purchase prices for every article. A one-time quote is not a new price list. If the award should become the ongoing price, that is a separate, deliberate update, of the kind described in the price-list page. Mixing the two means a special price for one site becomes the cost on the next unrelated quote you send to a customer.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: a small interior fit-out firm needs ironmongery for one flat. The buyer writes twelve lines, with quantities and units, and sends the same sheet to three suppliers, with a reply date. One fills the sheet. One sends a PDF using their own numbers. One replies that two hinges are out and quotes the other ten as a single total.

The table has twelve rows. The Excel reply fills a price and a unit on each. The PDF reply matches nine rows through the cross-reference the firm already keeps, and three rows wait because the article text fits two hinges. The email reply does not get split into unit prices. Those ten cells say "total only", and the two missing hinges say "missing". The buyer asks the third supplier for a line price, accepts a substitute on one hinge after looking at the note, and orders the rest from the first supplier because the lead time fits the site. No score picked that. The firm and the numbers are invented.

## What to leave alone

Leave it alone when you buy the same article from the same supplier every month and a current price list already covers it. Asking for a quote each time adds delay and teaches nobody anything new. Leave it alone when the "comparison" is a legal tender under public procurement rules. That is a different procedure, with its own duties, and this page does not describe it. Leave it alone when you only ever ask one supplier. There is nothing to compare. A template for the request may still help. A comparison table will not.
