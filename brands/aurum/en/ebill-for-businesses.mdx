## eBill for a business: channel, not a new approval rule

For a Swiss business, eBill is a channel: supplier invoices arrive in online banking or in your invoicing software in a structured form. The check before you pay stays with you.

A business can receive supplier invoices through eBill, and send its own invoices once a network partner connects you. SIX runs the infrastructure for the banks. The service does not decide whether goods arrived, and it does not replace whoever may release a payment. What often disappears is retyping a PDF from the mailbox into the bank. What remains is matching to the order and the delivery note when you buy goods.

This is for the office that pays suppliers and sometimes wants customers to pay without a mail attachment. How accounting captures bills is in [invoice capture](/prozess-check/blog/invoice-capture-what-software-misses). Releasing after a check is in [invoice approval](/prozess-check/blog/invoice-approval-workflow). Matching to order and delivery note is in [matching delivery notes](/prozess-check/blog/delivery-note-invoice-matching). eBill is the pipe between them.

## How it differs from email and PDF

SIX describes the difference this way: an eBill invoice appears in online banking, where you check it and release it for payment. An invoice by email lands in the mailbox, and someone transfers the document or its details into the bank. That transfer, the payment instruction, and filing in the accounts is the manual work eBill can skip for billers who offer it.

A file in the bank can still be wrong: wrong amount, duplicate, invoice for goods you never received. Release in online banking is a payment instruction, not goods receipt.

## Two ways to receive

**Online banking only:** If the bank offers it, authorised staff see the firm's invoices, release them for payment or reject them. Several people can work on the same invoices on the account you choose. They can register the firm with further billers so later invoices from those billers arrive as eBill without hunting for a PDF each time. Settings such as standing approval are adjustable. SIX describes standing approval as a maximum or exact amount without a fresh click. Use that only for billers and amounts you would pay without looking. On a supplier who changes quantities, that is how a wrong invoice gets paid. Online banking alone is not your archive: file what accounting needs even when payment runs in the bank.

**Hand-off to software:** The bank passes the invoice into the invoicing software, often as a file transfer with PDF. Booking and payment follow your existing process. SIX says that path does not require a new process. Not every bank offers the service, and setup differs. Start with the business adviser at the bank. We have not built an eBill connection. Unclear software behaviour is a question for the provider or, in a check, for what the bank actually delivers. If your program already captures supplier bills, the question is whether eBill only changes the source or whether you approve twice.

## What you still approve

Authorised people view, check, release or reject. That is payment approval, not delivery approval. When you buy goods, the order and the delivery note still have to match before someone releases in the bank, as they did when the PDF sat in the mailbox. The upside: payment details are already in the bank. The risk: releasing because the screen looks complete.

A rejected invoice becomes a task for the person who owns the supplier, with the reason. A workflow can open that task when the status is rejected. It should not release on your behalf or set a standing approval.

Credit notes and reminders can travel on eBill. SIX lists single invoices, instalments, donation requests, reminders, notices and credit notes. A notice can be an invoice with no pay button, or a note about a credit. Treat notices as information. Do not pay them. If you already dunning customers who owe you, a reminder you receive as a customer belongs on the open supplier item, not in a new inbox rule that pays it. A rejected invoice with no task is a silent no: the supplier waits while the bank shows "rejected".

## Sending: network partner, not a new button

To send eBill to customers you need a network partner. SIX: the partner connects you to the infrastructure; you ask them for a price. Certified partners are listed on [ebill.ch](https://www.ebill.ch/de/home/business/faq.html). Private customers pay no eBill fee. That does not mean your debtors have switched. They must be reachable on eBill. Keep the PDF invoice for everyone who is not, until your debtor list shows otherwise.

Do not turn off mailbox invoices on day one. Run both channels until the customers who matter actually pay via eBill and you see those payments in the accounts without a manual match. A second channel nobody reconciles is a new kind of missing receipt. If you only track debtors in your head, you notice at month-end.

## Direct debit is a different switch

eBill Direct Debit is a pull payment SIX introduced in June 2025. It is not the same setting as standing approval. SIX: standing approval is capped at a maximum or exact amount; the direct-debit mandate is not limited by amount. The customer authorises in advance; the biller submits the amount. That is contract and credit decision, not an office shortcut. Read the current description in the SIX FAQ before anyone signs.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: an office pays about thirty supplier invoices a month. Twelve billers already offer eBill; the bank shows them in online banking. Two people may release up to a limit the owner set; above that, the owner waits. One of the twelve is a goods supplier: match to the delivery note before release in the bank. Eighteen suppliers still send PDF by email on the existing capture path. Nobody deletes the PDF for eBill ones: the software path should deliver a file you can archive. Standing approval only for one telecom biller with a stable monthly amount, not for the goods supplier.
