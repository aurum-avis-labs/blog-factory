## eBill for a business is a channel, not a new approval policy

eBill, for a Swiss business, is a way to receive a supplier invoice in online banking or in the invoicing software, and a way to send your own invoices if a network partner connects you. It is run by SIX for the banks. It does not decide whether the goods arrived, and it does not replace the person who may release a payment. A PDF that still has to be typed into the bank is the channel it replaces. The check on the invoice is the channel it does not replace.

This is for the office that pays suppliers and, sometimes, wants customers to pay without a mail attachment. Reading invoices that the accounting program already captures is in [invoice capture](/blog/invoice-capture-what-software-misses). Releasing a bill once it is checked is in [invoice approval](/blog/invoice-approval-workflow). Matching it to an order and a delivery note is in [matching delivery notes](/blog/delivery-note-invoice-matching). eBill is only the pipe.

## How it differs from an invoice in the mailbox

SIX describes the difference this way: an eBill invoice arrives in online banking, where it can be checked and released for payment. An invoice by email arrives in the mailbox, and someone has to transfer the document or its details into the bank. That transfer, the PDF, the payment part, the copy filed in the accounts, is the work eBill is meant to skip for the billers who offer it.

It does not skip the question "is this invoice right?". A file that lands in the bank can still be the wrong amount, a duplicate, or a bill for goods you have not received. Release in online banking is a payment instruction. It is not a match against the order.

## Two ways to receive

You can receive in online banking, with no extra system, if the bank offers it. Several employees can be allowed to see the firm's invoices, release them for payment on the chosen account, or reject them. They can also register the firm with further billers, so later invoices from those billers arrive as eBill. Settings such as a standing approval can be adjusted. SIX describes a standing approval as a maximum amount or an exact amount that may be paid without a fresh click. Use that only for billers and amounts you would pay without looking. A standing approval on a supplier who changes quantities is how a wrong invoice gets paid.

Or the bank passes the invoice into the invoicing software you already use, as a file transfer, and you also receive the PDF. Booking and payment then follow the process you already have. SIX says that path does not require you to invent a new process. It also says not every bank offers the service, and the setup is different at each bank. Start with the business adviser at your bank. We have not built a connection into eBill. If your program's side of it is unclear, that is a question for the software provider or, in a check, for what the bank actually delivers.

## What you still approve

Authorised staff can view, check, release or reject. That release is the approval of payment, not the approval of the underlying delivery. If you buy goods, the order and the delivery note still have to match before someone should release, just as they did when the PDF sat in the mailbox. The nice part of eBill is that the payment details are already in the bank. The dangerous part is releasing because the bank screen looks complete.

A rejected invoice should be a task for the person who owns the supplier, with the reason, not a silent no. A workflow can open that task when the status is rejected. It should not release on your behalf, and it should not set a standing approval.

Credit notes and reminders can also travel on eBill. SIX lists single invoices, instalments, donation requests, reminders, information notices and credit notes. A notice, an Avisierung, can be an invoice with no pay button, or a note about a credit. Treat a notice as information. Do not "pay" it. If you already have a dunning process for money customers owe you, a reminder you receive as a customer is the mirror image: it belongs on the open supplier item, not in a new inbox rule that pays it.

## Sending is a network partner, not a button you already own

To send eBill to your customers you need a network partner. SIX says the partner connects you to the infrastructure and that you ask them for a price. The list of certified partners is on ebill.ch. Private customers pay no eBill fee. That does not mean your customers have switched. They still have to be reachable on eBill. Keep the PDF invoice for the ones who are not, until you know otherwise from your own debtor list.

Do not turn off the mailbox invoice on day one. Run both until the customers who matter actually pay via eBill, and you can see those payments in the accounts without a manual match. A second channel that nobody reconciles is a new kind of missing receipt.

## Direct debit is a different switch

eBill Direct Debit is a pull payment, introduced by SIX in June 2025, not the standing approval above. SIX describes the standing approval as capped at a maximum or an exact amount, and the direct-debit mandate as not limited by amount. The customer authorises collection in advance. The biller submits the amount. That is a credit decision and a contract, not an office shortcut for paying every invoice without looking. Do not turn it on to avoid the approval step this page is about. Read the current description in the same SIX FAQ before anyone signs a mandate.

## A made-up example

A made-up example: an office pays about thirty supplier invoices a month. Twelve billers already offer eBill, and the bank shows those twelve in online banking. Two people in the office may release amounts up to a figure the owner set. Above that figure, the invoice waits for the owner. One of the twelve is a goods supplier. Those invoices are matched to the delivery note before anyone releases them in the bank. The other eighteen suppliers still send a PDF by email. Those stay on the existing capture path. Nobody deletes the PDF for the eBill ones either: the software path is supposed to deliver a PDF you can file, and the online-banking path is not your archive. A standing approval is on for one telecom biller with a stable monthly amount. It is not on for the goods supplier.

## Who should skip the project

Skip it if the bank does not offer business eBill. There is nothing to automate until they do. Skip it if you already receive into the accounting program through the bank and a person still approves there. A second release in online banking means two yeses, or one yes in the bank that bypasses the match. Skip it if you pay a handful of invoices a month and the PDF path is calm. The channel is a convenience, not a duty.

The recurring task worth describing is the split book: some invoices in the bank, some in the mailbox, and no rule for which ones may be released before the delivery is checked.
