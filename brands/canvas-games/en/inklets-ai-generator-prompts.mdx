## Prompt length and why it stays short

The roughly **eighty character** limit is a design nudge, not a punishment. Short prompts force:

- One subject instead of a crowded scene.
- One or two visual anchors the model can honor.
- A mood word that reads at small sprite size.

Long lore paragraphs dilute results and slow mobile sessions. Think trading card description, not fan fiction.

## Sketchbook-safe prompt recipe

Use this repeatable template:

**Subject + anchor + mood**

Examples that test well in play sessions:

- "Cardboard box kid, blue shirt, curious eyes"
- "Sleepy paper bat with tiny fangs"
- "Green forestling with leaf hood, grumpy"
- "Barrel courier, rope belt, determined"

Notice what is absent: copyrighted names, real celebrity likenesses, brand logos, and paragraph backstory.

## What to avoid in prompts

Keep prompts sketchbook-safe and store-safe:

- **No real people** or identifiable public figures.
- **No copyrighted characters** from film, comics, or other games.
- **No hate, slurs, or explicit content.**
- **No readable text requests** on the character ("write HELLO on shirt" often fails style anyway).
- **No ultra complex scenes** ("dragon riding a bike while juggling").

The generator interprets; it does not guarantee literalism. Lean into style-consistent results rather than exact portrait fidelity.

## Iteration without burning session time

When a result misses:

1. Change **one variable** (shirt color, mood word, species).
2. Regenerate instead of stacking five new ideas at once.
3. Keep anchors that worked; swap only the weak part.

If two iterations still miss, simplify the subject ("a box kid" beats "a box kid who is also a wizard chef").

Swap mood words until the silhouette reads from far away.

## Prompt workshop for groups (ten minutes)

Try this before a match night:

1. Everyone writes one prompt on paper without showing the others.
2. Read prompts aloud; vote which three would be funniest to see on the same page.
3. Generate winners first so lobbies start with recognizable silhouettes.
4. Ban "inside reference only" phrases unless the whole group shares the joke.

The workshop respects the eighty-character limit and keeps prompts sketchbook-safe. It also prevents one player from dominating creation time while others wait in matchmaking.

## After you equip: first match habits

Generated Inklets behave like any climber in a lobby: test jump height on the first safe platform before you chase coins. Prompt-made characters sometimes have wider silhouettes than catalog skins, so give yourself one screen of calibration before risky detours.

If the silhouette feels too busy at distance, regenerate with fewer accessories and keep the mood word. Readable friends beat detailed lore in three-player matches.

## Honest limitations

Prompt-based creation trades **slider precision** for **open vocabulary**. Jawlines are not adjustable; interpretation is.

Generated climbers must still match **sketchbook style** to sit next to Voidling, Barrel Courier, and other official Inklets. Weird is welcome; photoreal is not the goal.

If you need exact cosplay fidelity, catalog skins in the Sketchbook may serve you better than prompts.
