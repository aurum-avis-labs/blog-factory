## Why prompts stay near eighty characters

The limit is a design nudge, not a punishment. Short prompts push you toward:

- One subject instead of a crowded scene.
- One or two visual anchors the model can honor at phone scale.
- A mood word that reads on a small silhouette.

Long lore paragraphs dilute results and slow a couch session. Think trading-card blurb, not fan fiction. If you need collection rules and coin unlocks, those live in the [Inklets Sketchbook guide](/blog/inklets-sketchbook-guide); this page stays on wording.

## Sketchbook-safe prompt recipe

<ul class="blog-checklist">
  <li>Stay near eighty characters</li>
  <li>One subject, not a cast list</li>
  <li>No real brands, celebrities, or copyrighted mascots</li>
  <li>No hate, slurs, or explicit requests</li>
</ul>

Repeatable shape: **subject + anchor + mood**.

Examples that read well in lobbies:

- "Cardboard box kid, blue shirt, curious eyes"
- "Sleepy paper bat with tiny fangs"
- "Green forestling with leaf hood, grumpy"
- "Barrel courier, rope belt, determined"

Skip copyrighted names, celebrity likenesses, brand logos, and paragraph backstory.

## What to avoid (and what the model will not fix)

Keep prompts store-safe and multiplayer-safe:

- **Real people** or identifiable public figures.
- **Copyrighted characters** from film, comics, or other games.
- **Readable text on the body** ("write HELLO on shirt" often fights the style).
- **Ultra-busy scenes** ("dragon on a bike while juggling").

The generator interprets; it does not guarantee literalism. Aim for a silhouette friends recognize from across the table, not portrait fidelity.

## Iterate without burning the whole evening

When a result misses:

1. Change **one variable** (shirt color, mood word, species).
2. Regenerate instead of stacking five new ideas at once.
3. Keep anchors that worked; swap only the weak part.

If two tries still miss, simplify ("a box kid" beats "a box kid who is also a wizard chef"). Wide silhouettes sometimes need one calibration jump at the start of a match before you chase coins.

## Prompt seeds by party theme

| Theme | Prompt seed |
|---|---|
| Cozy | "Soft blob with scarf, sleepy smile" |
| Chaotic | "Spiky doodle gremlin, mischievous eyes" |
| Object humor | "Teacup with legs, nervous" |
| Nature | "Moss pebble with tiny acorn hat" |
| Retro desk | "Paperclip hero, bent into wings" |

Swap mood words until the shape reads from far away. In a ten-minute group workshop, everyone writes one prompt on paper, reads them aloud, and generates the three the table would most enjoy seeing on the same page. That keeps the eighty-character habit and stops one person from hogging creation time while others wait in matchmaking.

## Honest limits of prompt-based characters

Prompt creation trades **slider precision** for **open vocabulary**. You cannot nudge jawlines pixel by pixel; you regenerate.

Results still need to match **sketchbook style** beside Voidling, Barrel Courier, and other official Inklets. Weird is welcome; photoreal is not the goal. If you need exact cosplay fidelity, catalog skins in the Sketchbook may fit better than prompts.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long can an Inklets AI character prompt be?

About eighty characters. Shorter, concrete sentences outperform long descriptions.

### Is the AI Inklet Generator free?

Yes, inside the free game on the App Store and Google Play. Optional cosmetics may exist; check in-game shop labels before you buy.

### Can I edit a generated Inklet manually?

Expect regeneration and unlock cosmetics rather than pixel editing. Details can change between updates.

### Will other players see my prompt text?

They see the resulting climber in matches, not necessarily the sentence you typed.

### Can I name my Inklet anything?

Follow in-game naming rules and safe language policies.

### Do generated Inklets persist?

They are meant to live in your Sketchbook alongside other unlocks.

### What if generation fails moderation?

Revise the prompt to remove banned content and try again. Keep descriptions original and friendly.

### Where do coins fit?

Runs award coins for Sketchbook unlocks; see the [Inklets Sketchbook guide](/blog/inklets-sketchbook-guide).

### Can I reuse the same prompt every season?

You can, but small tweaks keep silhouettes distinct in a long-term collection.
