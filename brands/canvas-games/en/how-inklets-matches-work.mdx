## How an Inklets match works

An Inklets match is a short survival climb: three players share one sketchbook page, hazards stack while black ink rises from below, and the last Inklet standing wins. There is no finish banner and no solo score trance; every jump is relative to the other two climbers. Inklets is not released yet: it is planned for iOS and Android, with the waitlist at [inklets.app](https://www.inklets.app) and a lighter browser sample at [game.inklets.app](https://game.inklets.app). This post walks the flow from main menu to lobby countdown to elimination for readers who already skimmed [what is Inklets](/blog/what-is-inklets) but do not want the product tour yet.

## Before the match: menu, loadout, and your Inklet

Opening Inklets should land you one tap from Play. The main menu surfaces:

- **Your active Inklet** (base character, earned skin, or AI-generated oddball).
- **Loadout slots** fed by the Sketchbook: skin, trinkets, page background, platform style.
- **A clear path to create** a new Inklet via short text prompt when you want a fresh climber.

Loadout choices are cosmetic and readable. They repaint the page and the climber, not hidden combat stats. If collection depth is your question, [inside the Inklets Sketchbook](/blog/inklets-sketchbook-guide) is the tour; this post stays on match flow.

Swap skins until the lobby countdown if you are indecisive; short sessions punish long prep.

## Matchmaking shape: three-player lobbies

Launch mode is **competitive last-player-standing climbing** with **three players** per lobby. That cap is deliberate:

- **Readable chaos.** You can track two rivals on a phone screen without losing platform language.
- **Stable netcode.** Vertical climbers fail when eight ghosts jitter on narrow ledges.
- **Social fit.** Three friends with phones is a pub table default; empty huge queues are not the goal.

You can invite friends when those flows are live; random matchmaking depends on who is online in your region. For quiet hours, invite one or two people you know rather than waiting on an empty queue.

Compare party-size discovery in [three-player mobile games](/blog/three-player-mobile-games) if you are building a game night list.

## Lobby: rules you see before the drop

The lobby is the contract screen. Everyone should see:

- **Who is climbing** (Inklet silhouettes or names).
- **Win condition copy:** last Inklet standing when ink or elimination removes the other two.
- **Countdown** short enough to prevent settings paralysis.

No hidden rulebooks mid-match. If you entered from a Doodle Jump background, note the difference from racing: there is **no shared finish line**. [Doodle Jump multiplayer](/blog/doodle-jump-multiplayer) explains Lima Sky's racing answer; [Inklets vs Doodle Jump Race](/blog/inklets-vs-doodle-jump-race) compares sprint versus survival directly.

## On the page: jump, dodge, grab, outlast

When the countdown hits zero, all three Inklets land on the same living page. The loop is simple verbs, messy outcomes.

### Jump and climb

Platforms wobble, slide, stick, spike, or snap. Auto-jump or short action jumps (depending on build tuning) keep thumbs friendly. Your job is horizontal reads first, vertical panic second.

### Dodge interference

Ink smudges drift, page monsters wander, and **chaos events** reshuffle safety when the match needs tension. Dodging is not only spikes; it is predicting social knockback when three climbers fight for the same spring.

### Grab coins

Coins sit off the safe line on purpose. They fund Sketchbook unlocks after the match; see [how coins and unlocks work in Inklets](/blog/inklets-coins-unlocks-loop) for economy detail. In-match, coin grabs are bets: detour sideways while rivals climb the safe column.

### Outlast the ink

Black ink rises from below, swallowing the sketchbook. Trailing climbers see the deadline; leading climbers feel pressure not to greed a detour. Category-level explanation of rising kill planes lives in [rising floor mechanics in mobile climbers](/blog/rising-floor-climbing-games).

## Elimination and win moments

A climber leaves the match when ink (or other elimination rules active in that build) consumes them. When two are gone, the remaining Inklet wins.

Matches aim for **minutes, not hours**:

- Enough time for comeback arcs.
- Not enough time for battle pass homework.

That pacing is why Inklets sits in [last-one-standing mobile games](/blog/last-one-standing-mobile-games) comparisons without pretending to be a battle royale.

## After the match: coins, Sketchbook, rematch

Post-match flow ties back to collection:

- **Coins bank** toward unlocks, stamps, and rare entries.
- **Sketchbook** receives new cosmetics when thresholds hit.
- **Rematch** stays one or two taps away because the genre lives on "one more run."

## How Inklets matches differ from solo vertical jumpers

Solo Doodle-style games ask, "How high can I score?" Inklets asks, "Can I still be on the page when you are not?"

Shifts you feel in the first three matches:

- **Relative lines** beat absolute height.
- **Coin greed** punishes you when ink catches the pack.
- **Readable rivals** make chaos events social, not random.

Skill habits from [how to get better at vertical jumpers](/blog/how-to-get-better-vertical-jumpers) transfer; multiplayer adds glance discipline.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many players are in an Inklets match?

Three players per lobby for competitive last-one-standing climbing.

### Is there a finish line?

No. The win condition is outlasting the other climbers when ink or elimination removes them.

### Can I play Inklets solo?

The planned launch focus is multiplayer survival. Solo practice may exist in demos or modes over time; the browser sample is the place to check the feel now.

### How long does a typical match last?

A few minutes, long enough for reversals, short enough for a commute.
